Caching speeds up a website by temporarily storing parts of it on the visitor's device. When someone visits a webpage, the website's server sends not just the page they requested but often many of its components as well, like images, scripts, and stylesheets. If the visitor returns to the same page, instead of re-downloading all these elements, the cached version of those parts can be displayed instantly from the local device storage. This significantly reduces the time it takes for the page to load. Future visits are faster because the browser can simply retrieve the saved information from cache, rather than waiting for it to be fetched again from the server over the network. Caching generally benefits both visitors by providing a smoother experience and the website by cutting down on the server's workload and improving the site's overall performance.
