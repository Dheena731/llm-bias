A draft literature review should be 2000 words or less. It should truly review the literature; it should not simply summarize it. You should highlight key points and discuss trends and relationships.

Your review should not be a single 2004-word document. It should likely be a series – at least a summary and a discussion.

Your review should not be a list of references. It should be a set of assertions, with evidence, that you have evaluated. You should not simply read and write down what others have said; you need to think critically about what you read and determine what you think about it.

Your review should thoroughly examine the literature. It should go beyond just the papers you have actually read; it should also consider papers you have not read but have cited papers you have read. When citing papers you have not read, you should not only cite the paper but also provide a citation to the citations you have. If a paper you have read cites a paper you have not read, you should also cite that paper.

Your review should be well organized. It should have an introductory paragraph and body paragraphs. The introductory paragraph should introduce the topic and state the conclusions you have drawn. The body paragraphs should make and support your assertions. The last paragraph should be a conclusion – it should not simply repeat the introduction.

Your review should discuss important papers in detail. The more important and influential a paper, the more detail you should provide.

Your review should discuss newest papers in detail. You should cite the most recently published papers in your review; you should also discuss them in detail.

Your review should not be in the form of journal articles. It should not follow the style of a journal paper, which is typically longer, formal, and more thoroughly cited. Instead, it should be in the form of a white paper or a grant proposal.

When writing your review, you should consider how you will submit it. If submitting it as a journal publication, you should follow the journal’s rules. If submitting it as a white paper or a grant proposal, you should follow those rules instead.

Your journal should use a professional tone. It should avoid slang.
