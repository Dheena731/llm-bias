An API rate limit refers to the restrictions placed by a service provider on how many requests can be made to its Application Programming Interface (API) within a certain timeframe. These limits are designed to prevent abuse of the service, such as spamming or overloading the system, and to ensure fair use among all users. The limits typically dictate the number of requests that can be made in a minute, hour, or day, and exceeding them can lead to temporary blocking of further requests until the limit period resets. API rate limits help maintain the stability and security of the service by managing the volume of requests and preventing excessive server load.
