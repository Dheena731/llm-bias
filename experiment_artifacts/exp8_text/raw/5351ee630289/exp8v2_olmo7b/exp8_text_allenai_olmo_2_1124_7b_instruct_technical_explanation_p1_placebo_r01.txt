Latency in web applications refers to the delay between a user's action, such as clicking a link or pressing a button, and the point at which the requested data begins to be transmitted from the server to the client. This lag is crucial because it affects the overall user experience and can significantly impact satisfaction and retention. 

Here are several reasons why latency matters:

1. **User Experience:** In today's fast-paced digital world, users expect immediate responses. Slow loading times can lead to dissatisfaction and confusion, as users may think the site is malfunctioning or not loading properly.

2. **Bounce Rate:** High latency is a leading contributor to heightened bounce rates. Users are prone to abandon a slow-loading site, resulting in fewer page views and decreased engagement.

3. **Conversion Rates:** For e-commerce and other transactional websites, prolonged latency can lead to lost sales. If a potential customer is met with slow response times, they might opt for a competitor's faster service.

4. **SEO Implications:** Google and other search engines often prioritize sites with better user experience metrics, which include load times. Slow pages can be penalized in search rankings, reducing a site's visibility and potential traffic.

5. **Mobile Experience:** With the surge in mobile users, latency affects a significant portion of the audience. Mobile networks often have higher latency than wired connections, making this issue even more critical for mobile-optimized web applications.

6. **Perceived Reliability:** A site with low latency is generally perceived as more reliable and professional. High latency can imply poor management and technical proficiency.

In summary, latency matters because it directly influences the end-user experience, affects conversion rates, impacts SEO, and affects user perception of a website's reliability and professionalism. Modern web applications must strive to minimize latency to ensure a positive and efficient user experience.
