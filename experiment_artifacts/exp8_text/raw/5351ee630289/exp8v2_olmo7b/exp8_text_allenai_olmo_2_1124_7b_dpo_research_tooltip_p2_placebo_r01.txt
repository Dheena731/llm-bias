A confidence interval in a chart represents a range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter with a certain level of confidence. For instance, a 95% confidence interval means that if we were to draw many samples and calculate an interval for each, we would expect 95% of those intervals to capture the true parameter. It gives us a sense of the reliability of a sample statistic in estimating the population parameter. Note that a confidence interval does not mean that there is a 95% chance the interval is correct; rather, it indicates the long-run frequency of capturing the true value when repeated sampling is done. This tool helps statisticians and analysts understand the precision and potential error in their sample data.
