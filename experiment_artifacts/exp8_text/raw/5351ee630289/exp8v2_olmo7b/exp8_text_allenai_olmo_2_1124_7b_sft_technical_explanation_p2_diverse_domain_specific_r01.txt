A database index is a performance optimization technique that improves the speed of data retrieval operations. It achieves this by creating a searchable and sorted copy, or a "book index," of a table's columns. This allows the database to quickly locate the exact row or rows that match the search criteria, without having to scan the entire table. Consequently, this process reduces the time required to perform complex queries and operations, like range searches or joins, on large tables. However, it is important to note that while indexes speed up data retrieval, they can slow down data modification operations such as INSERT, UPDATE, and DELETE, since these require updating the index in addition to modifying the table. Therefore, they should be used judiciously and only when the benefits of faster read operations outweigh the potential performance costs on write operations.
