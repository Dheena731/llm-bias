Helping others to help themselves

Our work as fundraisers is not just about receiving support. We must also add value to the work that we are doing. We must have a purpose greater than ourselves.

Here’s where the theory of the gift economy comes in: When we support others, we are supporting them to succeed and to help others, too. It’s like a ripple in a pond: When one person gives, they receive; when they receive, they give; and so on. It’s a cycle of giving that perpetuates generosity and builds back up our communities.

And that’s a gift from you, too. You deserve to keep on giving.

What is a gift economy?

Here’s a quick recap:

A gift economy is a type of social structure that is based on the tenet that human and social capital, such as knowledge, expertise, love, and time, are the most important things in life.

The theory of the gift economy states that as money is used, it creates social and economic divisions, such:

  • Rich and poor,
  • Haves and have-nots,
  • Givers and takers,
  • Privilege and underprivilege,
  • 1st- and 3rd-world countries.

Money, therefore, is not the most important thing: It is social capital.

In a gift economy, money does not matter, and everyone has equal access to the resources of the world.

The gift economy requires that each person is responsible for giving the gifts that they have and receiving those gifts that they do not.
