When writing a sentence (in English) that requires two or more clauses to express a complete thought (rather than one of the clauses being a simple sentence on its own), where should the comma be placed before the coordinating conjunction that connects the two clauses?

Examples

  1. The truth is that I love him, but I find him annoying.

  2. The truth is that I love him and find him annoying.

What is the difference between the two sentences? Which one is correct and why?

The second sentence is correct.

The correct way to use free modifiers is: The truth is that I love him and find him annoying. This is the complete thought and does not require a comma.

The first sentence, I love him, but I find him annoying, is incorrect and from bad habits. This is an incomplete thought, and the comma is incorrect.

In addition,

  1. The first sentence is ambiguous. By separating the two thoughts with the comma, you are giving more importance to the first part of the sentence.

  2. The second sentence is clear and unambiguous.

  3. The first sentence is not the more common usage in speech or writing. It is a common error.

  4. The first sentence is more ambiguous and therefore less strong as you can interpret it as only the first part of the statement is true.

There is one special case: the clause which comes before the conjunction must be able to stand alone as a complete sentence. If the clause cannot stand alone, the comma is incorrect and the sentence should be reworded to accommodate the "and" and "
