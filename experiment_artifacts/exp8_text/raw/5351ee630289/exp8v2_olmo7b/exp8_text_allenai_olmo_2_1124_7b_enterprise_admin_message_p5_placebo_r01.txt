Write to impress. Use big words, complex sentences, and flowery language to demonstrate your superiority. Don't worry if people don't understand you — they can always ask.

Write to sell. Assume that readers are not familiar with your offer or product, and that the more information you provide, the better. Give them every possible reason to buy.

Write to tell everything. If you have a story to tell, explain every detail, no matter how obscure or irrelevant.

Write to educate. You're a professional, and people need to hear from you. Give them all the information you have, whether they want it or not.

Write to argue. Explain how your opinion is superior to all others, and that those who disagree with you are ignorant or evil.

Write to narrate. People love a good story. Pick a topic, introduce the characters, and spend ten pages describing their lunch.

Write to proselytize. Your beliefs are the only way to truth and prosperity. Share them with the world.

Write to offend. If something offends you, write about it angrily, and insult everyone who disagrees with you.

Write to confuse. Cram as much information as possible into a single sentence. Challenge people to read it without making mistakes.

Write to show off. Pity for those who have no clue about your vocabulary, so show them all the words you know.

Write to get published. Write for the sake of writing, and the page count, and the byline. Nobody reads the dictionary, but it has more pages than anything you'll ever write.
