Problem: PHILADELPHIA—Four years after declaring an early end to his time as mayor, Frank Rizzo was widely considered a political has-been when he ran for City Council president in 1980.

Yet he swept the field on Election Day.

Mr. Rizzo, who died in 1991 at age 76, won 10 council races with more than half of the vote and stepped down after one term as the first black council president of a city whose population was then 40% black.

The 1980 election was a turning point in the city’s fight over race and politics. It occurred in the aftermath of a federal race-stirring investigation that eventually led to Mr. Rizzo’s resignation after the fatal shooting of a black teenager by a white police officer.

The election also marked the beginning of the end of perhaps the most tumultuous period in the city’ s modern history. The Rizzo era, as it came to be known, was marked by racial tension and police brutality.
