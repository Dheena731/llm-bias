Brett, that's great. We're just adding time pressure to help you get to the point. You're going to have to cut it down. And also... It's a road closure.

Here we go:

Parkland Road will be closed off from 8am to 4pm on Tuesday, for roadwork. Please use the following alternate routes: George Street from 8am to 1pm and then Craven Street from 1pm to 4pm.

A bit too long, eh? Closer:

Parkland Road will be closed from 8am to 4pm on Tuesday, for roadwork. Use George Street from 8am to 1pm and then Craven Street from 1pm to 4pm.

Now that's a proper public notice.

So, why is this a good notice? And why do some of Brett's earlier attempts fail? Let's look at the implicit contract between the writer and reader of these notices.

The contract

The public notice has a contract with the reader that it is short, timely and accurate. If the notice fails to deliver on any or all of these points, then it is a poor notice and the reader should ignore it.

It is short

The notice has to be short because it is written in a situation when the reader has to decide if it is worth reading. Perhaps it is in the middle of a commute and the reader has to make a decision about what to do. Perhaps the reader is browsing and wants to find out if there is anything interesting.

In either case, the reader does not want to waste time and will only read the notice if it is short.

It is timely

The notice has to be timely because the business regulations vary across locations. Where I am, a public notice about road closures has to be written ten days in advance. So in this case, the notice can be written ten days in advance and then left on the notice board until the actual closure happens.

On the other hand, if the closure is not announced ten days in advance, then it is treated as an emergency closure and the business owner has to pay a fine. In this case, the public notice should have been written as part't of the emergency closure notice.

It is accurate

The notice has to be accurate because the business owner relies on the notice to tell the reader about the closure. The reader will try to follow the notice and therefore the business owner must be clear about what the reader will be able to do.

If the notice is inaccurate, then the reader might try to do something that is not allowed and the business owner will be partly responsible for that. The reader might sue the business owner. The business owner might pay a fine.
