When you see a confidence interval in a chart, it refers to a specific range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter, such as the mean, with a certain degree of confidence. Typically, a 95% confidence interval means that if you were to draw many samples and calculate the confidence interval each time, about 95% of those intervals would contain the true population parameter. This interval provides a range of values that help researchers understand the precision of the estimated mean and how much variability can be expected in the data.
