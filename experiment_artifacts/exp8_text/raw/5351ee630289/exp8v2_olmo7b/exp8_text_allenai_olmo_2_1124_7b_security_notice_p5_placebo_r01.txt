Writing good notes

Here is a talk on writing notes by Edward Tufte.

Getting feedback

Take your notes to a classmate or to your teacher’s office and have them read them. Spot check by reading the notes back to yourself. There are a number: 5-minute mini-reviews, 20-minute medium-long reviews, 1-hour long reviews. The best person to ask is the person who took the notes. The best way to write notes is to take them, so don’t be afraid to ask: “Did you understand my notes?” Another good option is to fallow up with an email to your classmate.

After taking notes

You will need to go back to the notes, and reorganize them. Go back to your notes and organize them into a title page, the date taken, the name of the course, the name of the professor, and the name of the main topic. Make the first line of the title page the title, name, and date.

Now you can start to organize your notes by each section. Look at the outline of the course. Many courses have a structure to their lectures. Good teachers lecture from a set of notes. They have a topic. They have a subtopic. They have an example, and then they come back to their main topic. If your notes do not have this structure, write it in.

Now create a table of contents. Look back at your notes and write down a line for each section. Then write down a line for each subsection. Now write down a line for each sub-subsection. Now write down a line for each example.

Now go back to your notes and go through them, and create a table of contents. Create a page for each section, and a subsection, and a sub-subsection. Identify the main topic and write it at the top of the page. If you have a subsection and a sub-subsection, create four columns. If you have an example, create two columns. Write down this example at the top of its column.

Now go back to your notes and write down the main points for the section. Start with the main point, and then the main point’s subsections, and then the main point’s sub-subsections, and then the example. Write down these points in order, with the main point on top, and the sub-subpoint underneath it, and then the example underneath this.

Now write down a name for each of these points. Write down what the point is called. Write down the definition. Write down how to use it. Write down how to apply it. Write down how it connects to other points.

Now go back and read your notes. Did you leave out anything? If you left out something, go back and add it. Read over your notes again. Now go back to the beginning and start to make corrections. If you have time, go back and read them again.

Reading your notes

Reading your written notes allows you to go back and look at them a second time. You can review them; you can go back and review the main points you wrote down. You can revise them; you can go back and modify them, change them, add to them, remove redundant parts, or reduce them.

The last step is to create a review page. Place the title of your main topic at the top of the page, and then write down the main points you covered, and then the main points underneath this. You can also add the subsections and the sub-subsections.

In addition, you can use a mind map to visualize your main topics and subtopics. This allows you to see them in a different way. It lets you see the connections between them.
