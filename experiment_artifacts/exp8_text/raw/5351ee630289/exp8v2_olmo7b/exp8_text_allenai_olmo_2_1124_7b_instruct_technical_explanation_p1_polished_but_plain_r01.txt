Latency is a critical factor in web applications because it directly impacts the user experience. Essentially, latency refers to the delay between a user's action, such as clicking a link, and the moment the requested content loads on their screen. When latency is high, users are forced to wait longer for pages to load, videos to buffer, or actions to take effect. This waiting time can be frustrating and may lead users to abandon the site or service, ultimately resulting in lost traffic and potential revenue.

Moreover, high latency can diminish the overall perception of an application's performance and reliability. In a highly competitive digital landscape, where consumers are accustomed to instant gratification, slow loading times can quickly make a web application seem outdated or incapable compared to its faster counterparts. This can lead to negative reviews, reduced customer satisfaction, and even harm to a brand’s reputation.

Consequently, prioritizing low latency is essential for maintaining user satisfaction, ensuring ongoing engagement, and sustaining the growth of web applications. Developers and service providers must invest in optimizing server infrastructure, leveraging content delivery networks (CDNs), and implementing efficient coding practices to minimize latency and deliver a smoother, more responsive user experience.
