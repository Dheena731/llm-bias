A missing-value marker is a special code or symbol placed in a statistical dataset to indicate that a specific value is missing or not available for a given data point. This marker helps data analysts or predictors to recognize and handle these missing values appropriately during analysis or modeling, as failing to do so can lead to incorrect conclusions or biased results. The choice of marker, such as a numerical placeholder (like "NaN" for Not a Number in programming), an alphabetic code (like "NA" for Not Available), or even a more complex identifier, depends on the context and conventions of the field conducting the analysis. The existence of these markers is crucial for ensuring the integrity and accuracy of statistical processes and models.
