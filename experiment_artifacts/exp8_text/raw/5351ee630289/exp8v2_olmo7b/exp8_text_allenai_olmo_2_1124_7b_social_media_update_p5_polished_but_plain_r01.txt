You've probably heard about CAPTCHA by now. At its core, it's a kind an interactive challenge that a user has to solve to prove they're not a robot. For example, below is a CAPTCHA that you've probably seen before:

It's important to understand what CAPTCHA is and isn't. It's a good tool for determining whether a human being is interacting with a website, but it's not a good tool for determining account ownership. There are all sorts of methods that malicious actors can use to bypass CAPTCHA, and even a large CAPTCHA provider like Google is unable to effectively block all of them.

This is why we've never recommended using CAPTCHA for account verification purposes. If you need to confirm that a human being is interacting with your site, then we recommend using an email confirmation prompt instead.

We recently worked with our partner SecureData to help their customers implement an encryption strategy called "data at rest encryption" (DARE). It's a powerful strategy that's often used by credit card companies to protect the data stored in a database by encrypting it. You've probably used it many times before without even realizing it.

Here's how it works: the data is encrypted and decrypted before it ever goes into a database. This means that even if the database gets compromised, the data remains secure.
