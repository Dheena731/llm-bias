Research Question:  What are the attitudes about genetically modified foods (GM foods) among American parents and the public and how are they influenced by attitude change communication?


A large number of researchers around the world have conducted studies about attitudes towards GM food and its impact on consumer’s attitudes and behaviour. A few researchers have explored the influence of media coverage, advertisements and formal communication on consumer attitudes. However, there is a dearth of research on how specific communication programmes affect attitudes of GM food consumers. Also, most research on consumer attitudes and behaviour towards GM food has been conducted in Western countries, with the majority of them located in the United States. In contrast, there is a dearth of research in developing countries, especially among consumers, farmers and traders of GM crops and their perspectives on GM food.

The proposed research project will examine how communication can be used to promote or reduce acceptance of GM food among farmers in various countries in Africa. The research will be conducted in Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Sudan and South Africa. The research will also examine how information campaigns can be used to improve understanding of genetically modified foods and their safety, and to address misconceptions and misrepresentations of such foods.

The study will also explore what types of campaigns are more effective in influencing attitudes and behaviour towards GM food than others. The study will also examine the impact of the media coverage, advertisement and formal communication on consumer attitudes about GM food.

A comparative study will also be conducted among a sample of American consumers and parents of children, aged between 3 to 18 years old, in the United Bookworm Café, a chain of bookstores in the United States.
