Latency is a critical factor in web applications as it directly impacts user experience by determining how quickly users receive responses from a server. When a user interacts with a webpage, they expect instantaneous feedback. For example, when they submit a form or click a button, the action should be reflected immediately on the page. Latency is the delay in transmitting this information. 

High latency can lead to a slow loading time for web pages, sluggish interactions, and a poor perception of the application's responsiveness. Users may perceive an unresponsive application as unreliable or poorly designed. This can lead to increased abandonment rates, where users lose patience and navigate away to a competing website. Additionally, high latency can strain server resources, as requests take longer to process and deliver responses, potentially affecting the overall performance and scalability of the application.

For businesses and developers, mitigating latency is essential to maintain user satisfaction and engagement. Techniques such as optimizing server responses, utilizing content delivery networks (CDNs), and employing load balancing can significantly reduce latency. By focusing on minimizing latency, developers ensure a smoother, more engaging experience for end-users, which can translate to higher conversion rates and customer retention. In summary, latency matters because it affects user satisfaction and business outcomes directly, making it a key consideration in the design and operation of web applications.
