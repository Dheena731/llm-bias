The confidence interval in a chart is a range of values that likely contains the true population parameter, such as the mean, with a certain level of confidence. For instance, a 95% confidence interval means that if we were to repeat the sampling process many times, we would expect the interval to contain the true value 95% of the time. The width of the interval indicates the level of certainty; the wider it is, the less precise our estimate of the true value is. Confidence intervals help us understand the variability and reliability of statistical estimates based on sample data, allowing for a better interpretation of findings in research and analysis.
