Do not use the first person (“I”, “me”, or “my”), e.g. “I state that …”.

Do not use the third person (“he”, “she”, or “they”), e.g. “The author states that …”.


This assignment asks you to write a short (1-2 page) review of a peer-reviewed literature review. Your goal is to provide a brief and helpful academic feedback about the review’s strengths and weaknesses.

  1. Read the literature review for the week, and focus on one, primary research question that the article addresses. You may use the first section of the article for guidance. You may also use the abstract and the conclusion if you want to focus on the primary research question.

    Tip: Focus on the research question that is most central to the article. You may also want to focus on only one paragraph or section of the article.

  2. Write a short feedback note. This is a short, academic summary of the strengths and weaknesses of the literature review. Be positive and helpful. Do not be mean or overly critical. You may refer to your own research experience (e.g. you have done research on the topic, or you are a TA), but be careful not to spend too much time on your own work.

    Tip: Consider the original research question and how the literature review answers it. Review the studies and descriptions of the studies (and their methods) and how they are grouped, and review the conclusions drawn from the studies and how they are grouped. Then think about what is missing. Does the literature review leave the reader wanting more information? Does it give the reader too much information? Does it address all of the research questions?

  3. Submit your response as a PDF document. Please include your name as the author.
