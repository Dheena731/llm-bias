Here is a short explanation of why latency matters in web applications:

Latency is the delay before a transfer of data begins following an instruction for its transfer. In web applications, latency can refer to the delay between a user clicking a link or a button and the moment the page starts to load.

Latency matters in web applications for several reasons:

1. User experience: High latency can cause frustration, slow user experience, and a poor overall impression of your service.
2. Search Engine Optimization (SEO): Google and other search engines use the speed of your website to determine its ranking. A slower site could achieve lower rankings and fewer visitors.
3. Lost revenue: If your site takes too long to load, users may abandon the cart and leave your site without making a purchase or signing up for a subscription service.
4. Reduced conversion rates: High latency can result in reduced conversion rates due to your users’ inability to find what they’re looking for quickly, or an overall poor user experience.
5. Decreased competitiveness: Not only can high latency affect conversion rates and user experience, but it can also make your site appear less professional and less competitive, which can impact your search engine rankings.

In summary, latency matters in web applications because it affects the user experience, can negatively impact search engine rankings, decrease conversion rates, and make your site appear less professional. Reducing latency is crucial for a successful web application.
