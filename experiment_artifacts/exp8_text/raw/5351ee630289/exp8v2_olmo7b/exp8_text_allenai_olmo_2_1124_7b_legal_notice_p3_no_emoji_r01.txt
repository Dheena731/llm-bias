Cookie usage on a website can seem a little scary. There are a lot of legal requirements and regulations to manage and comply with when using cookies. The best thing to do is to be as upfront and transparent as possible when it comes to cookies. Balancing this with a good UX is not always a simple task, so it’s best to be as concise as possible and provide information in a logical order.

This is how to write a short legal-style notice about cookie usage on a website.

  • To get you started, here is a sample notice:

    Our website uses cookies to provide you with a good experience. This notice applies to all the pages on our website.

  • Explain what cookies are

  • Explain why cookies are used

  • Explain why cookies are necessary

  • Explain what cookies are not

  • Explain how to reject cookies

  • Link to more information

  • Date of last review

  • The text can be as long or short as you like

  • How long can the notice be?

    This notice can be as long or short as you like. We recommend that it is at least two lines long, and ideally four or five. If it is over ten lines it is probably too long.

  • What should be included?

    You should explain that the website uses cookies and why. Typically, cookies are used to:

    • remember your preferences when you come back to the site;
    • make the website easier to navigate;
    • improve your experience when using the website;
    • provide relevant information, such and promotions, or special offers.

    In addition, you should explain how to reject the cookies. Finally, you should give a link to further information and an indication of when the notice was last reviewed.

  • How should the notice look?

    It is best to include the notice in a prominent place on the website. This is typically the top right corner or bottom of the website. It works to have the notice as a background image that is repeated throughout the site, but you don’t have to do this.

  • What are the alternatives?

    You can also choose to place the notice at the bottom of all the pages on the website, but this may be too intrusive for the user experience.
