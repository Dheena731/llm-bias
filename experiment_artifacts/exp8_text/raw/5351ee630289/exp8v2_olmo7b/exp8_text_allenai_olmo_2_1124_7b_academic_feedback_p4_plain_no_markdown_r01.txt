Suggested process

  1. Give students the prompt.
  2. Have students independently work in small groups to develop their poster draft.
  3. In the next session or two, have students present their poster draft to the class.
  4. In the following session(s), have students give feedback on their peers' poster drafts. Have students create and use rubrics to evaluate their peers' work.
  5. Have students create their poster rubric based on the feedback on their peers' poster drafts as well as the poster prompts.
  6. In the next session, have students write their feedback note based on the prompt.
  7. In the next session, have students polish their poster.
  8. Have students present their posters to the class.

What should be included in a poster

The poster should be a brief explanation of the process students went through to complete the project and the results of that process. The poster should be structured such that it can tell a story.

Most of the story will be that process, but the most important part of the story is what was learned as a result of that process. The presentation of that learning is what should be included in the poster.

The poster should include:

  • The essential question(s) posed by the project.
  • What it means for a question to be essential.
  • The research question(s) that were used to guide the project work.
  • What was the significance of the project?
  • What were the design constraints of the project?
  • What were the process steps used to guide the project work?
  • What were the process steps used to guide the presentation of the work?
  • What was the evidence of the work?
  • What was the student learning that resulted from this work?
  • How is that student learning manifested in the project?
  • How do the student learning and the results of that learning align with the intended learning?
  • What resources did the students use to guide their work?
  • What were the patterns of student learning throughout the project?
  • What were the setbacks encountered throughout the project?
  • How did setbacks influence the path of the project?
  • How did the setbacks serve to enhance learning and the results of that learning?
  • How did the setbacks impact the presentation of the work?
  • What was the most important lesson learned?

The poster should be a single-page document that can be read by anyone who understands English.
