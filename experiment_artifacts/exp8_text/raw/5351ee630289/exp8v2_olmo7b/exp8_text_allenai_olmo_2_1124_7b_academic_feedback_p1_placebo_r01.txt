The goal of this exercise is to write well. To fail well, do the opposite.

The subject of your work will be a literature review draft from a course at your school. The audience for your notes will be the course professor as well as your peers.

Your notes should be in the form of an annotated bibliography with a paragraph of text for each paper.

You are writing to an audience that is familiar with the subject of the papers, and that is also familiar with the format of the annotated bibliographies you are writing about. Your goal is to help that audience understand what the paper is about, its contribution to the relevant literature, its strengths and weaknesses, and any other relevant information.


There are two ways to get feedback on a draft of a literature review:

  1. Ask your peers to read your work and give you feedback.

  2. Write an email to your course professor asking for feedback. That email should include a link to your work (e.g., Google Docs).

A good way to request feedback from your peers is to bring in a hard copy or email them a link, and ask them to meet you somewhere quiet to discuss.

When getting feedback from your peers, be sure to ask them to be specific. Don't just ask "how is this?" Be precise and ask them to point out things that are confusing or unclear.

When getting feedback from your course professor, do the same.
