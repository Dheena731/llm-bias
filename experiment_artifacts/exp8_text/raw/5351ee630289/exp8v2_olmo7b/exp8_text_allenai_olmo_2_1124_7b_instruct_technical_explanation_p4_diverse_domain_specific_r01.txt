Caching is like a well-stocked kitchen at a bustling restaurant. When a website receives thousands of requests for its pages (much like diners ordering meals), serving up fresh ingredients each time would slow things down. That's where caching comes in. It allows the restaurant (or your website) to prepare certain dishes (or pages) ahead of time and store them in the kitchen (in the cache) for quick service. The next time someone orders that dish, the cook simply pulls out the pre-prepared meal from the fridge (the cache) instead of waiting to cook it from scratch. This speeds up your website significantly because it doesn’t have to generate each page from scratch every time someone visits. The result is a swift, smooth experience for website visitors, enhancing customer satisfaction and potentially increasing engagement and conversion rates.
