A commercially operating anaerobic sewage sludge digester installed at the wastewater treatment plant in San Francisco, California, is presented and discussed. In the first part of the paper a brief introduction on anaerobic digestion providing the basis for the investigation into anaerobic sewage sludge treatment is given.

Later, the installation and its different components are described and the plant operational condition and process parameters are presented. The flow chart and a process control chart for the anaerobic digestion of sludge are given in the form of schematic diagrams.

Sewage sludge is a very valuable material, which can be treated to a good quality product by anaerobic digestion. This digestate can be stabilized for up to six years under anaerobic conditions and is a good plant nutrient.

The application of anaerobic technology in improving the quality of sewage sludge is an important subject in wastewater treatment. Gas produced by this process is of great value. In addition, the anaerobic treatment of sewage sludge can lead to lower nitrogen and phosphorous loads in the effluent, and the solid material remaining can be used as a soil amendment.

The use of biological processes in wastewaters and sludge is discussed, with emphasis on anaerobic processes. A brief characterization of anaerobic processes is provided, and their applications in wastewater treatment are discussed.

A short account of the anaerobic conversion of substances yielding methane in the sludge of municipal wastewater treatment plants is given. Anaerobic digestion processes are widely used for the recovery of methane from organic wastes.

A mix of organic and inorganic waste is preferably used to avoid an excessive accumulation of ammonium and phosphorous. For anaerobic digestion, this organic waste fraction is completely degraded to methane and carbon dioxide by microorganisms under anaerobic conditions and in the complete absence of oxygen.

In an alternative method, the organic fraction is converted to carbon dioxide and methane by mixed and pure methanogenic bacteria. The inorganic fraction in the wastewater sludge is mainly microorganism cells, mineral salts and trace elements. This is a chemical-physical process, and anaerobic digestion is a biological process.

The first step involves the hydrolysis of the complex organic waste by hydrolytic microorganisms, followed by the second step of chemically converting the monomer sugars to acetate, formate and carbon dioxide, as well as the reduction of ammonium and nitrate to nitrite, which is then further reduced to ammonium.

The successive conversion of acetate, formate and carbon dioxide by methanogenic bacteria under anaerobic conditions results in gaseous methane and carbon dioxide. The methane is recovered either by a two-phase separator or by the extraction of the gas.
