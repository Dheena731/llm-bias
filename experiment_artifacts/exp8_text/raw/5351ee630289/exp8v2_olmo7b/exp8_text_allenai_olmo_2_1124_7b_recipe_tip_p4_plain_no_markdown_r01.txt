Tip  Plan out lunches for the week ahead of time. Pick a day or two each week to plan your lunches and make sure you have the ingredients you need. Check out the pantry and fridge and make a grocery list of any missing items. Once you have your ingredients, make up a batch of lunches to last the week. That way you won’t feel so rushed when it comes time for lunch because you’ll already know what you’re going to make!


A good tip is one that fills a skill gap for users. Your tips should be concise, focused on a single idea, and useful.

Your tips should be informative and useful. If a tip could apply to many different audiences, it may be too broad. Try to add some context for your tip by sharing personal experiences. For example:

  • “Create a spreadsheet of all your passwords. This way, if you forget a password, you have a safe place to look it up.”
  • “I keep a grocery list on my refrigerator door. That way, I can check it every time I go to the store to make sure I’ve got everything I need.”


Tips are not how-to guides or tutorials; they are suggestions to help users solve a specific problem.

Tips should not be long or detailed; they should be straightforward and to-the-point. However, if you feel additional information is necessary to make your point, you can write that information within the text of the tip. Avoid unnecessary and redundant details. Avoid “fluff” words. Simply write what you have to say, quickly and concisely.

If you need to write a lot of text to explain what you are doing or why, you may need to expand your tip into a how-to guide.

If you need to include extra information such details about how to find something or what exactly something means, you may need to expand your tip into a how-to guide.

Answering questions like “How to” or “What is” or “What does” are a good indication you are writing a how-to guide, not a tip.

If you are writing a how-to guide, see the How-to Guides Guide for useful, general guidelines for writing how-to guides.
