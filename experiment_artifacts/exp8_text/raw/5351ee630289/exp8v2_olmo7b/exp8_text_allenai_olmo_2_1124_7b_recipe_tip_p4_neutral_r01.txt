Try these games with children from age 5-7 (or younger, if you like). These are just a few ideas. You can make up your own games too, as long   as they meet all the goals of this lesson.  You'll need playing cards and dice for these games. For the dice game, don't use a pair of dice. Use one die instead in some of your games (for younger children).  Say the word: "I'm going to the market, and I'm going to buy some (blank)!" Name a food or something else you can buy at a market. Have all the children stand up and repeat this sentence together. Then, have them sit down on the floor. The last child to sit down has to do a silly dance or sing a silly song.  Ask the children to stand up in a circle and put their hands in the middle. Put an imaginary ball in each child's hand. Say, "I'm going to the market and I'm going to buy a (blank)!" Name any fruit or vegetable. Then, ask each child to carefully pass the imaginary ball to someone else in the group. It can be anyone. When you say “go,” the children pass the ball left or right. The goal is to get the ball back into their hands. When you say “stop,” they need to stop passing the ball. If it’s in someone's hand when you say “stop,” they are out of the game. The last person left is the winner. For older children, try this instead: Say, "I'm going to the market and I'm going to buy some (blank)!" Name any animal. Each child needs to make that animal's sound. This continues until the last child makes the sound. The last child out of the game sits in the center (or doesn't have to stand up for the game).  Keep putting the word “market” at the end of several sentences, like this: "I went to the park today and I saw a (blank). I also bought (blank) at the market." Name random words that have nothing to do with the market. The point is to get children to think quickly and try to come up with the right word fast. Try this for children of all ages. Play a CD of music from the market and ask children to sing along and to understand the lyrics. You can also play fun market tunes on the piano for the children.  If you're able to get recordings of children at the market, that's a great way to have children learn too. Bring in a recorder or a video camera and make some recordings to listen to at home.  Try listening to your children as they sing. If they're singing about the market, then they know the lyrics.
