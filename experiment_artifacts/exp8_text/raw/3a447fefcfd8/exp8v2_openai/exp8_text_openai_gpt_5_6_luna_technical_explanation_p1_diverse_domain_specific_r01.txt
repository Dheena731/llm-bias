Latency matters because every click in a web application often waits on a chain of work: a browser request, authentication, database queries, downstream APIs, and rendering. A 300 ms delay in one service may become seconds when several requests run serially, especially on a mobile connection. Users experience that delay as a stalled search, a form that seems not to submit, or a checkout they may retry.

Latency also affects system behavior, not just perceived speed. Slow requests occupy server workers and database connections longer, increasing contention and causing more timeouts under load. Tracking percentiles such as p95 and p99 is important because a small group of users—often those on distant networks or slower devices—can encounter failures even when the average response time looks acceptable.
