Card transaction fees have been subject to significant changes in the recent past due to evolving regulations, technological advancements, and economic considerations. Here's a brief overview of these changes:

**Historical Overview:**
Traditionally, card networks like Visa and Mastercard have levied transaction fees on every card payment. These fees were used to cover network expenses, like maintaining the infrastructure, security, and processing power needed to process large volumes of card transactions. However, these fees have traditionally been quite high.

**Recent Changes:**
1. **Ongoing Regulation Push:** A tide of consumer and industry pressure has been exerted towards reducing these fees. This is partly due to the growing popularity of cashless payments and the desire to encourage more sustainable and environmentally friendly financial practices.
   
2. **Interchange Fees (Reform):** One of the key changes is the reduction of interchange fees, which were notoriously high and often opaque. The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) has also contributed to improving the security of payment card data, which in turn has led to a reduction in associated fees.

3. **Volume Discounts:** Some networks now offer volume-based discounts to businesses and retailers who process a significant number of transactions. These discounts can significantly reduce the fees paid per transaction.

4. **Minimum Fee Reductions:** In many cases, networks have started to cap the lowest minimum fee they charge, ensuring that even small transactions do not incur exorbitant fees.

5. **Freemoney Initiatives:** In some regions, like Europe, initial efforts to reduce card transaction fees were successful, leading to the establishment of "freemoney" or "zero-friction" payment products designed to reduce barriers to entry for small businesses and consumers alike.

6. **International Adoption of Fees Reductions:** As global expansion of the digital economy continues, many countries are adopting or revisiting similar fee reductions across borders, reflecting a global trend towards fairer and more consumer-friendly payment practices.

The reductions in card transaction fees are part of a broader effort to balance the needs of the payment processors with the needs of merchants and consumers in the digital economy. These changes aim to create a more economically sustainable payment ecosystem, making it more attractive for small businesses to accept card payments and encourage greater adoption of digital payments. 

It's important for merchants to familiarize themselves with these changes, as they can directly impact their bottom line and the overall health of the e-commerce and retail sectors.
