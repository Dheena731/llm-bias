Before you start, there are a few things you might want to consider in terms of writing style.

  • Are you addressing an audience that is professional food writers? If so, it's best to follow the rules of the journal, newspaper or magazine you're writing for.
  • If you're writing for a website or blog, you're more likely to be writing for a general audience, and you can adapt the style to suit your audience.
  • Generally, it's a good idea to avoid using slang, acronyms or abbreviations, as they are likely to confuse or offend non-experts.

Here are a few style tips, with examples:

  • Avoid colloquial language. For example, instead of saying, "It is good to be full," say, "You are satiated."
  • The best way to write about food and eating is with a clear, simple style. For example, "The food was delicious, and the service was excellent." Maintain a high level of clarity throughout; aim to avoid the use of long sentences and complex words, and try not to overuse adjectives and adverbs. Avoid using modal verbs (will, should, could, etc.) and unnecessary prepositions. Instead of, "We had a lovely meal prepared by the chef," say, "The meal was delicious."
  • A great way to write is to use a simple, clear sentence structure, with clear, simple words. Here is a great example:

    It is good to be full. You are satiated when you feel full.

    1. It (is good to) What is the subject of the sentence?
    2. to be full What is the predicate?
    3. You are satiated when you feel full.

    1. You are satiated.
    2. Satiation: "The state of being satisfied, content, or pleased; satisfaction." (Oxford Dictionary)
    3. What you have eaten or experienced. (Oxford Dictionary)

    Let me give you another example, to show you how to use it:

    The food was delicious, and so was the service.

    1. The food was delicious.
    2. The food was delicious.
    3. What you have eaten or experienced. (Oxford Dictionary)
  • If you would like to look at examples of great food writing by people who are more widely respected in the profession, check out the OUP website. They give a full list of great food writers, and provide links to their work.

  • Emphasis can be achieved through the use of italics, bold, or headings. However, only the first use of the bold or italics option is allowed: any use of heading will result in a "missing code" error.
  • Don't write in the present tense, unless you are writing a food diary or current event (rather than a more general article).
  • When writing a food diary, it's a good idea to use an active voice, rather than a passive voice.
