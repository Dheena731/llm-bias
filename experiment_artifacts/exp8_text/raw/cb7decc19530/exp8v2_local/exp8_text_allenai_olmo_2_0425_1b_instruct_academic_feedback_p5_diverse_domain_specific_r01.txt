In an essay, the absence of citations, especially for direct quotes or research references, distorts the credibility and depth of the argument. For instance, if a student writes about the impact of social media on teenage mental health and asserts that "a study by Smith & Johnson (2020) found that social media use correlates significantly with Anxiety Disorder symptoms," the omission of these references undermines support for the claim. In academic disciplines where accuracy and integrity are paramount, the inclusion of cited sources not only demonstrates thorough research but also strengthens the author's thesis by anchoring it in empirical evidence.

Furthermore, references that are missing can lead to misunderstandings, as readers may interpret unsupported assertions as facts or opinions merely because they are not properly cited or documented. In a domain as important as psychology or science writing, where the quality of understanding can depend as much on the veracity of the information as it does on the clarity of the argument, the importance of acknowledging and properly citing sources cannot be overstressed. This brief comment touches on the practical implications of citation care in academic and research contexts, emphasizing the role of citations in maintaining academic integrity and the overall perception of the author's work.
