A change to card transaction fees likely occurred as part of efforts to manage costs or to adopt more sustainable financial practices. Card networks, such as Visa and Mastercard, often negotiate fee structures with payment processors and card issuers to align with industry-wide standards and to accommodate new regulations and technological advancements.

Typically, small transaction fees are a cost that merchants incur when accepting payments via card. These fees are the costs that merchants pay for processing card transactions, including the risk of chargebacks and the costs associated with card networks Like Interchange fees (which are fixed costs for card-present transactions), card validation costs (when the cardholder's bank confirms the transaction), and transaction fees (charged to merchants for each card transaction).

The new change might aim to reduce overall costs for merchants, potentially enabling them to pass on savings to customers or reinvest these savings in operations, marketing, or innovation. Alternatively, the change could be part of a broader strategy to promote more sustainable or environmentally-friendly payment methods, such as contactless payments or digital wallets.

Understanding the specifics of the change requires consulting a press release or official communication from the respective payment association or the issuing card networks, as the details can vary and the motivations behind such policy shifts can have broader impacts on the economy and consumer behavior.
