A missing-value marker, also known as a placeholder or an interpolation marker, is a symbol used in data visualization, statistical analysis, and data preprocessing to indicate the presence of missing or "unknown" data points. Its primary purpose is to prompt analysts to decide how to fill or handle these data points, depending on the context and the nature of the data. Common methods of filling missing values include interpolation (calculating a predicted value based on nearby data points) and deletion (removing the data point).

When you hover over a missing-value marker in a chart or table, a tooltip typically appears to explain its purpose and the action that can be taken (e.g., "Interpolate Missing Values" or "Remove Missing Observations"). This tooltip not only aids in understanding the concept but also guides analysts in making strategic decisions regarding the treatment of missing data in their analysis.
