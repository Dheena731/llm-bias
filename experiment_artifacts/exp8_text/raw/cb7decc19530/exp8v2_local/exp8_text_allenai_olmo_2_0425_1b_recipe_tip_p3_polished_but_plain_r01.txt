What is a short cooking tip? It's an expression of knowledge that is helpful to other cooks. Don't use long-winded recipes or videos that don't work, but instead give a simple, clear, and nice cooking tip that may help the reader make their dish better than before. For example:

Cook your rice in a covered pot at a medium heat until all the water is absorbed.

Don't use emojis or formatting, and don't link to other recipes or web pages. Instead, use plain prose to share your knowledge. For example:

To make plain rice, cook it in a covered pot on the stove top for about 15 minutes until the water is almost fully absorbed. If you want it fluffy you can use more rice and water, and cook it more slowly for a longer time. But it's best not to overcook it.

If a recipe has a lot of ingredients, and it was written by a professional cook who is experienced and knowledgeable about food, you may be able to get some clues from the ingredient list. For example:

As a professional chef, I use 14 cups of flour, 6 cups of cold water, 1/4 teaspoon of yeast, and 4 cups of hot water in this recipe.

But short cooking tips are intended for more casual cooks and beginners, so they should be as simple as possible. Avoid providing a lot of condensed instructions unless they are very simple, such as "wash your hands". The bulk of the information should be in the tips themselves, written in plain text and devoid of formatting or decoration.

Do not use large amounts of punctuation marks like exclamation points, question marks, and so on. They aren't necessary and can be confusing. Use simple, clear, and plain words. Use the right words. For example:

If you follow my recipe, the dish will have a great flavor and texture. It will improve a lot if you use a lot of cloves and cinnamon.

Be careful not to use too much of the "magic words" like "magic", "ninety-nine percent", "cook", "peel", and so on. These are not magic, they are just words that are often used in cooking when something interesting happens. They are not interchangeable with the words "and", "to", "when", "so", "then", "unless", "or", "except", "but", and so on. Use these words sparingly.

Don't use a lot of big words. Keep your tips short, simple, and clear.

Do not spend time trying to make your writing look nice or fancy. It's fine to use some formatting. But make sure it's not too complicated or confusing.

Be careful with synonyms. Do not use the same word in the same sentence to mean the same thing. For example:

For my recipe, you need a small plate, a fork, and a knife.

You need a small plate, a fork, and a knife for my recipe.

If you want to use a lot of synonyms, make sure that they are very similar. Otherwise, they will sound really strange. For example:

For my recipe, you need a small plate, a fork, and a knife.

You need a small plate, a small fork, and a small knife.

Be careful with the words "when" and "between", as these words are often used in cooking to indicate time and space. For example:

Cook the rice as directed, but watch it carefully, as it will be done when it turns white and fluffy.

Cook the rice between 15 and 20 minutes, depending on how fluffy you want it.

When the rice is done, it turns white and fluffy.
