Try to ensure that the tip is scintillating in all three of (1) the senses, (2) the sound and rhythm of the language, and (3) the emotion it evokes.

Things to Avoid

  • Don't use imperative sentences (e.g., "Use a spoon.")
  • Don't use questions (e.g., "What do you need to do?").
  • Don't be ambiguous (e.g., "The recipe expects 2 cups of flour").
  • Don't be annoyingly cute (e.g., "Try it, you'll like it!").
  • Don't use contractions (e.g., don't).
  • Don't use an exclamation point (e.g., "Try it, you'll like it!").
  • Don't use passive voice (e.g., "The recipe expects 2 cups of flour").
  • Don't use colloquialisms (e.g., "What am I supposed to do?").
  • Don't use gross humor (e.g., "Try it, you'll like it!").
  • Don't use passive humor (e.g., "You'll like it!").
  • Don't use double negatives (e.g., "Don't try it, you won't like it").
  • Don't use any punctuation other than periods, commas, semicolons, colons, and question marks.

Tips for Writing Effective Tips

Tip 1—Write in Active Voice

When we say that something should be cooked, the action is performed by the user.

  • DO NOT—Cook in a pan.
  • DO—Cook in a pan.

Tip 2—Be Specific

Most people want instructions that are very specific. We’d rather know what we need to do than be told to “follow the instructions.”

  • DO—Add 1 cup of chopped onions to the recipe.

Tip 5—Remember the Fruits of the Spirit

  • Love – How often do you give someone a hug or a smile?
  • Joy – How often do you laugh?
  • Peace – How often do you listen to a song that makes your heart sing?
  • Thankfulness – How often do you say “thank you?”
  • Kindness – How often do you give a hand or a hug?
  • Gentleness – How often do you behave in a way that makes someone feel good?
  • Self-control – How often do you resist the urge to talk?

You don’t need to use every single one of those phrases in your recipes. Those are just examples to get you started.
