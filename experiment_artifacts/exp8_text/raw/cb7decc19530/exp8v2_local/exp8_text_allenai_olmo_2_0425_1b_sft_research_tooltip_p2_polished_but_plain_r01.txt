A confidence interval (CI) is a range of values within which the true value of a population parameter is believed to fall with a specific level of confidence. For instance, when analyzing a stock's expected market value, a 95% confidence interval means that if we were to repeatedly sample from the population and compute the mean, we would expect 95% of those sample means to fall within the interval. A confidence interval thus provides a range of plausible values for the population parameter, offering a range of estimates rather than a single point estimate, while maintaining a certain level of confidence.
