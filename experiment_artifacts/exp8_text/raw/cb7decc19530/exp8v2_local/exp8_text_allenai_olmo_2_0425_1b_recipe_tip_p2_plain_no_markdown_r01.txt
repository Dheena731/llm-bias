The best way to practice is to write a tip on a piece of paper (or in a notebook) and then write the same tip in your public space.

A good tip is a short paragraph, typically 60 to 80 words (about 2 to 3 typed lines). You can copy and paste your tip.

Your tip can be:

  • A method for doing something

  • A warning

  • A joke or anecdote

  • A warning

  • A hint

  • An adventure

  • A solution to a problem

  • A recipe

  • A how-to

  • A wish or a prayer

  • An interesting fact

Tips can be short or long. They can contain a single short sentence or a few longer sentences, but they must contain everything you need to know. You should try to write tips that appear in your head, not tips you read online.

Keep your tips short. If you can't write a tip, try just saying the thing as a sentence. It's not a bad thing to have a long tip, but if you really find yourself struggling to say something you should stop and think.

If you have to explain a tip, don't make it a lesson. It's a good idea to explain a tip as part of a larger story or as part of a larger lesson.

You can write tips while making a checklist. You can write them on a sheet of paper. You can write them in your public spaces, your notebook or a personal memory.

Each tip should be a complete sentence with a single subject and a verb. Each tip should not have more than one word that is not a complete sentence.
