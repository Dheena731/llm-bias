C.  Get the Reader’s Attention

If you have found the right audience (or made the right call when writing to the wrong one), get their attention with the thesis statement. A well-written thesis statement captures the essence of the whole article in clear words. It is a single concept that answers your readers’ question and shows them the direction you will be going in your article.

Avoid passive voice; instead, use active voice where possible. The active voice is more engaging and makes your writing more vivid. It is a good practice to avoid making statements that you can prove wrong in a few days’ time—unless you are sure about them.

E.  Make It Interactive

Let us assume your readers are all lawyers who are experts in civil law. Using the same topic, let us see what kind of dialogue between two characters would make better reading than a “static” story.

You could try writing in a similar manner to something like a legal thriller. What if one of your readers, who is not an expert in a particular topic, tries to figure out the topic from what you did share. If you have left out things that could have been easily made clear to them, they will face a challenge. And, if it is possible, try to provide a link to an online resource for them to get a little more information.

D.  Use an Interesting Premise

An interesting premise is something that readers’ will want to read further. It can be derived from any of the points that you made during the first three steps. If you can find the right combination, you will have a win-win situation.

An interesting premise can be a question that readers are anxious to answer. It can be a statement that readers are curious to know more about. It can be a fact that readers would want to know. It can be a statement that readers would sympathize with or one that they would want to read more to understand the whole story.

Readers want to find answers to their questions. They want to feel empathy for the characters. They want to find a satisfactory ending to their read. They want to see how the “what if” scenario becomes real to them. If you can make your readers want to find out more, you would have done a great job.

F. Make It Self-Explanatory

We already know the knowledge gap between expert readers and non-expert readers. It is the main reason for them to not be able to understand your article. You will have no chance of getting your readers’ attention, and you would not have met your objective of making your article ready for a wide readership.

In such a situation, you will have to make your article self-explanatory. You can do this by using simple language, avoiding technical jargons and undefined terms, and making your point clear. You would need to add visuals and diagrams to make your article self-explanatory. One tip is to keep your language simple, as complex language can confuse your readers.

G.  Use a Coherent Structure

You have outlined why your article should be ready for a wide readership. So, you need to decide on a structure that will allow your readers to understand what you are trying to convey. And, you need to ensure that this structure remains coherent throughout your article. You need to plan your structure carefully and try using a logical order for the main points. You can use the topic sentences to effectively present your ideas in an organized manner.

The topic sentences act as small summaries of the main points you plan to present later in the article. In a way, they are the “topic bridge” that connects earlier paragraphs to the later ones. They also draw a conclusion from the previous paragraph. That is why they are called “topic sentences.”
