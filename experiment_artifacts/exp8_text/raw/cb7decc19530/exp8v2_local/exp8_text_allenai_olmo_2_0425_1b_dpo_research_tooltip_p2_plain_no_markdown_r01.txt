In a chart, a confidence interval offers a range of possible values for a population parameter, based on a sample. Imagine you're trying to find out the average height of all schoolchildren in a city. You take a random sample from that population and measure the heights of 100 students. Instead of just guessing the average height might be, you define a range—let's say 56.25 to 61.75 inches. That range forms your confidence interval. It indicates how accurately you can say the true average height of all schoolchildren in the city falls, given the sample data. A 95% confidence interval means you trust this range to include the true population average about 95% of the time. This tool helps you gauge the precision of your data and decision-making based on it.
