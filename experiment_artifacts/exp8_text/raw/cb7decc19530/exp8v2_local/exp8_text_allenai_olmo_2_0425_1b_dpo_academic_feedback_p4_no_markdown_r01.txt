**Feedback Note on Conference Poster Draft**

The conference poster draft is mostly well-organized and provides a clear overview of the research presented at the conference. Here are some suggestions for improvement:

1. **Title and Abstract**: The title is clear and concise. The abstract could be enhanced by including more specific details about the research question or hypothesis, the novel findings, the implications for the field, and the methodology used. Consider wording that captures the essence of the work and positions it within the context of existing research.

2. **Visual Design**: The use of white space and typography is effective in guiding the reader's eye through the poster. Ensure that all main elements are easily readable and that the call to action is prominent without being overpowering.

3. **Image Quality and Placement**: The images are a good start but could be of higher quality or provided in a larger resolution. Ensure they complement the text and are not overcrowded. The placement of images and text needs to enhance the poster's visual impact without interrupting the flow of information.

4. **Consistency**: The overall tone and style should be consistent with the conference's branding. The draft should reflect the sophistication and scientific rigor of the research.

5. **Keywords and Tags**: Including targeted keywords and tags will improve the poster's visibility and searchability on research platforms and in search engines.

6. **Follow-up Feedback**: Consider initial feedback loops with colleagues or peers in the field. Collaboration can often refine the poster to better represent the work and resonate with the intended audience.

With these adjustments, the poster can effectively communicate the significance of the research and lay the groundwork for engaging discussions at the conference.
