Don't be weird. Essays should have a clear thesis statement, be accessible, and demonstrate a basic understanding of the subject matter. This is especially important for a road safety essay, so you do not want to come off as pompous or arrogant. Be concise, and use good grammar and spelling.

Stay on topic. Do not, at any point, bring up unrelated subjects, such as your favorite band's latest album or a film that you saw in the theater the other day. Essays need to stick to the subject at hand, so be sure that your work is well-polished and insightful.

Provide evidence when necessary. Do not simply assert that, say, driving is dangerous. Provide concrete examples to back up your arguments, and include statistics or the experiences of others as needed. Your reader will be more likely to buy your arguments if you can provide hard data to support them.

Don't try to be flashy. Public safety is not the time to show off. Keep your work simple and direct, and don't make it overly complicated. This is a good time to call upon professional experience, but do not try to outdo your peers with sensationalist headlines or shocking claims.

Identify influential individuals and organizations. To what extent do these people and organizations influence public policy on safety? What are their strengths and weaknesses? How do they apply their resources and expertise?

Include relevant sources. Be sure to reference any article, book, or website that you have used to support your arguments. This will help to demonstrate your research and understanding of the topic. Use APA style, unless otherwise specified.
