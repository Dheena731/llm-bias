An API rate limit is a restriction placed on an application's access to an API to control the volume of requests made over a given period. It's a mechanism used by service providers to manage the demand on their servers and to prevent abuse or overloading. When an API is under high usage, it might impose a cap on how many requests an application can make per hour, day, or another predefined time frame. This can protect the service and ensure reliability for users who rely on it, as well as help manage data consumption and prevent unexpected surges in traffic that could overwhelm the system. Rate limits come in various forms, such as throttling, which is more gentle and allows for controlled increase in requests, and hard limits, which are stricter and directly limit the number of requests allowed.
