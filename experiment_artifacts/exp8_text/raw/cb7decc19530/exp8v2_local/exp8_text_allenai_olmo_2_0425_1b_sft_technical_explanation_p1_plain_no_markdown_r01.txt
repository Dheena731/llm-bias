Latency refers to the amount of time it takes for a request to be sent from the user's device to a server, and for the server's response to be received and processed back to the user's device. In the context of web applications, latency can significantly impact the overall experience. High latency can lead to a cumbersome and frustrating user experience, making navigation and interaction more difficult and less efficient.

For example, consider an online shopping cart that requires multiple steps, such as filling out forms, clicking "Add to Cart," and then clicking "Checkout." If one of these steps takes a long time to complete, the entire process can feel slow and cluttered, even if the individual steps themselves are not complex.

In addition to affecting the user experience, high latency can also lead to increased stress, frustration, and potentially negative reviews of the application, which could harm its reputation and potentially affect its revenue generation.

To reduce latency, web developers can consider optimizing the design and architecture of their applications, using more efficient programming languages, and implementing asynchronous data processing, among other strategies.
