You should also keep the following in mind when writing your critical review:

  • State clearly what the article is about. Are you reviewing a new study, a comparison study, a survey, or a case study? Do you have a preference for one method over another? Discuss the strengths and weaknesses of the study, and interpret the results.
  • Give a clear and brief explanation of the research process. Explain what kind of research was conducted, what evidence was used, and for what purpose.
  • Evaluate just the research process, rather than the article's conclusions. For example, did the researchers address all the questions about the research process discussed in the introduction? Did they use one method over another? Did they include a sufficient number of participants? Were the statistical methods reliable? You can also evaluate the quality of the study's evidence. For example, were their results statistically significant? Did they perform careful, unbiased interpretation of their results?
  • Discuss the strengths and weaknesses of the study. Did the data fit the researchers' goals? Did the researchers follow accepted guidelines?
  • Discuss other studies or articles that you have found while conducting your research. What do you think about the methods and conclusions?
  • Finally, do not include your personal opinions about the research or its results. You should not use the article as a platform to advertise or promote your own opinions or research. Instead, use the article to help guide your research.

Don't worry if you are not able to write a complete review. Just make sure you address the main points, and use the article as a guide to help you write a more polished academic review.
