In recent months there has been a noted shift in card transaction fees for businesses. Previously these fees were often seen as a significant burden adding to the cost of doing business. However the recent changes reflect a balancing act between financial institutions and businesses as they seek to encourage more digital transactions for environmental and economic reasons.

Under the new system, while some fees have remained unchanged, there is an intention to make transactions slightly more affordable. This change is part of a broader strategy to promote cashless transactions and digital payments which are deemed more environmentally friendly and reduce the need for physical cash handling. These new fees are usually conveyed as a charge per transaction rather than a flat rate, which aims to make digital payments appear more competitively priced compared to the previous higher fees.

Businesses are encouraged to adopt contactless payments or digital wallets more widely as a result of these new fees. This shift is expected to stimulate an increase in digital transactions, aligning with the goals of the financial institutions who benefit from the decline in cash usage and the associated cost savings from reduced handling of paper money.
