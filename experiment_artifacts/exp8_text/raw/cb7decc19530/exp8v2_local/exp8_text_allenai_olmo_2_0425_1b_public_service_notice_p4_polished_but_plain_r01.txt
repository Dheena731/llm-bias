Address any potential hazards that could affect public safety. For example, if the weather forecast predicts below-freezing temperatures, warn that roads may be slick, and give the name and address of a phone number where people can call for further information. If you know how to drive, deal with the weather, and have the equipment to stay safe, your readers will not be surprised by your advice. By the same token, if you do not know how to drive and will not be able to stay safe in case of severe weather, do not attempt to address it in your public notice. You may wish to include a statement that you will not be responsible if someone gets hurt because you did not follow public safety advice.

Include a link to the city’s website where the public can learn more about the heat advisory and other emergency preparedness information.

Respond to emergencies as they happen. As you write, keep the local emergency response team informed of changes in the weather and any other relevant concerns. Send them any information about unusual circumstances that were not included in the public notice or prompts that you received from the emergency response team.

Your public notice should include a direct phone number where the public can ask questions. Add the following information under the phone number:

Information that is only useful to emergency responders and may not be useful to the public should go here. For example, if you were responsible for installing a sprinkler system, you may want to include that information. Also, if you are responsible for providing water to the local fire department, you may need to include that information. It is not necessary to include this information if you cannot or will not provide it.

The public should call this phone number before they use any other phone number. For example, if there is a local 911 number that people should use, put that number first in the public notice.

Add a sentence to the end of the public notice that says, “Should You Be Concerned? If You Are, Call [number] for further assistance.”

The next time the city issues a public notice, add a note to your public notice about the previous notice. For example, “Be sure to be aware of the latest heat advisory.”

On a regular basis, send a revised public notice to your subscribers, saving the first version as part of the archived copy of your public notice. On the day you post a revised version, send an email to your subscribers with the following information:

When you put out a public notice, you are not required to do anything specific. The information that you provide in your public notices is crucial to your community. I appreciate you taking the time to do it right. If the information you provided in your public notice will not be useful to your public, please let me know and I will make the necessary changes. If you have questions about anything in your public notice, email me.

If you maintain a website that is intended to be useful to the public, you may want to put the public notice with your website. In that case, post the public notice at the top of your website. Add a sentence to the top of the notice that reads, “In case you can’t find the public notice, here it is.”
