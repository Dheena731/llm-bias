What’s next?


Most other web pages are designed to be read as a single unit in a web browser. An HTML page is a single cohesive document, one that can be read from start to finish with ease. By contrast, the layout of your poster will vary as it changes between different parts of your poster, as you highlight different sections depending on the way you are explaining something, or addressing different questions. As a result, the way you read your poster needs to be different too. By separating your sections, you are guiding your audience to directly focus on the parts of your poster that are most relevant and interesting to them.

Hack the poster, cheat on the timeline

The goals of the poster are to give your audience a high-level overview of information that they can read without having to read the full paper, and to help them to understand the main points of your research to a level that they can quickly inform their own decision-making. So you are going to use a lot of tricks to help your audience get through the poster quickly. One way to help your audience remember what is in your poster is to highlight different sections and labeling them with words and symbols. Another way is to use your own sketches to help people quickly identify what your poster is talking about.

As a result, you are going to want to include a lot of symbols and notation in your poster. But don’t include so many that your poster is unreadable. You will want to keep your page count to a minimum, and try not to use too many symbols and acronyms.
