Do not write a full essay. Instead, write a short essay that provides a positive or negative response to the draft literature review. This should be a time of no more than 300 words. For example, you could follow the format below:

  • a positive response: good, great, yes, yes yes GREAT JOB! Keep up the good work, you’re doing it right. You show great insight and skill. I was impressed by the range of topics covered.
  • a negative response: We know that you can do better. In this writing, you neglected to provide sufficient detail or statistical data to demonstrate any connections to your topic. Your arguments are weak due to a lack of evidence. 
  • a mixed response: This is a good start, but you need to tie your results to the research question more clearly. Read the research question aloud to see if you can identify an area where you can make a connection when you read on. 

Be sure not to use quotes or examples from the drafts you submit. Instead, write your own sentences that summarize the main points of each draft you submit.

Note that the feedback will be used to update your final draft, so make sure to include the feedback numbers in your final draft to show where each response came from.

Tips from the College Writing Center:

  • Review the feedback. Make sure to read the feedback numbers at the top of each paragraph. This will help you understand where you fall short in the feedback.
  • Give a summary of the main points of each draft. This will help you relate to each draft and make sure you've covered the main points in the feedback.
  • Read the feedback again. The feedback will keep you on track as you revise your paper and get feedback on your final draft.
