How to Storing Fresh Herbs

Fresh herbs are a gardener's dream, offering a burst of flavor in just a pinch. To ensure they stay fresh and flavorful, follow these tips:

* **Utensil Essentials**: Use clean, dry, and sharp knife, cutting blade, and deep bowl for trimming herbs.
* **Rinsing**: Thoroughly rinse herbs under cold water right after trimming. If you're using leaves with a high water content (like parsley or cilantro), pat them dry immediately with a paper towel.
* ** Storage Containers**: Use airtight jars, glass containers, or plastic bags for storing herbs. Avoid plastic as it can release harmful chemicals into food. Glass or ceramic are better options for long-term storage.
* **Prevent Mold**: Trim any excess leaves and store in a dry place away from direct light and excessive heat. Keep in mind that some herbs like basil and thyme are prone to mold if not stored correctly, so consider their storage needs when planning your inventory.
* **Make Individual Clusters**: For compact storage, tie clusters of herbs together with string, leaving about an inch at the end for ventilation. Store in a cabinet or a jar with a lid.
* **Check Regularly**: Fresh herbs can often be used within a week of harvesting. If they start to show signs of wilting, they're ready for use, and you just need to prune them for the next batch.

By following these simple storage practices, you can enjoy a continuous supply of fresh, flavorful herbs without sacrificing fresh taste in your cooking.
