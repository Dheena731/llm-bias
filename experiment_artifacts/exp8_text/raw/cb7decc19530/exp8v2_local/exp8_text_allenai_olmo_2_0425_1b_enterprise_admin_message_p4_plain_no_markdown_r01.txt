About quarterly reviews

Periodically, your nonprofit receives quarterly access reviews from the nonprofit sector. They provide a report on the state of your nonprofit's financial reporting, governance, and business management. They also make recommendations for the future and recommend who should be next in line to lead your nonprofit.

  • They provide a report on your financial reporting and governance.
  • They recommend who should lead your nonprofit and how to improve your future governance.

The purpose of this post is to help you better understand what an access review report is, why it's needed, and what it's for.

Why do nonprofits need access reviews?

Nonprofits are subject to all sorts of regulatory and jurisdictional requirements. Depending on the jurisdiction, there may be requirements for financial reporting, audit requirements, public reporting, or other requirements. These requirements can sometimes be confusing, and that's why nonprofits are required to do quarterly access reviews.

Access reviews are assessments carried out by a third party. The third party evaluates the nonprofit on the state of its financial reporting, governance, and business management. This is important because the nonprofit will be subject to future regulatory requirements and assessments if it falls short. The third party will be able to provide a report as well as a recommendation on how the nonprofit can improve.

What should the report include?

The report should be comprehensive and detailed. Know the requirements for your jurisdiction. Typically, the report will be about a month old by the time it is received. This means that you may receive it as late as 3 months after the end of the previous fiscal year.

Here are the types of information that the report should contain. The report should include a summary, recommendations, and supporting documentation. Here is a sample report:

  • Summary

    The report is a summary that includes key findings and recommendations. The summary is typically just a handful of pages.

  • Recommendations

    The report presents recommendations to improve how you manage your nonprofit. These recommendations will be based on the reviewer's assessment of your nonprofit.

    The recommendations may include any of the following:

    • Areas of improvement, including areas that should be prioritised.
    • Basic procedural improvements, such as organisational changes.
    • Relevant external changes, such as passing a vote of no confidence in the board.
    • Supplementary information, such as financial data. The report may also include supporting documentation, which is in addition to the above.

  • Supporting documentation

    The report will include supporting documentation, such as financial statements and other data. The information in the supporting documentation may be referenced in the The report typically contains supporting documentation, such as financial statements.

    Some of the documentation that you need to include in your access review report includes the following:

    • Power of attorney
    • Board minutes
    • Copy of bylaws
    • Relevant legislation
    • Relevant audit reports
    • Relevant financial statement
    • Financial reports
    • Other supporting documents, such as financial data and supporting documentation, as required by the jurisdiction.
    • The report may also include supporting documentation from your board of directors.

  • Acknowledgements

    Any acknowledgements will be at the end of the report, just before the report is closed. The report will acknowledge the third party who carried out the assessment and recommend who should be next in line to lead your nonprofit.
