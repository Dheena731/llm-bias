When preparing a notice, it is advisable to:

  • preserve the original wording wherever possible;
  • retain all the relevant detail;
  • use language and style that will meet the needs and expectations of all readers.

If writing a manual to provide the right information in an easy and straightforward way, the warning should have the same type as the alert.


These are instructions which have an immediate effect, for example opening a window or locking a door. They warn of risks or dangers, such as “Do not use the stairs”, “Do not touch the hot plate”, “Do not fly alone in an aeroplane”.

Hints and Tips

These are information which indicate an action that should be taken. For example, “In case of emergency, call 999”, “Remove the plug from the electrical socket” and “In the unlikely event of a power failure, turn off the gas at the main switch”.

Warnings and Accidents

These are warnings that something could go wrong, such as “Warning! The engine is not working properly, do not start the car. Call us on 111 or 999”, “Do not open this door”, “Do not drive when drunk”.
