4. Write a good note. People take notes for different reasons, and they also take notes at different speeds. Your note must be relevant to the discussion, and should not make any unfounded claims, nor introduce any new ideas. Your note should be concise, but not too short. It should be recognisable as your note, but not too easy to recognise as a distraction. Use this paragraph to consider how to break up your note and get it into the right format.

5. Write an introduction. The introduction should give the necessary context, but you should not introduce any new ideas. If it was a relevant note then it should be included in your discussion. It should summarise the main points, and introduce a few ideas that you’ll explore in more detail during the discussion. It should be as clear and concise as possible, and should not contain unnecessary jargon or filler.

Your introduction should be read by the other participants and the people who assigned the discussion to you. It should illustrate why the discussion is relevant to them, and be concise. Ensure that you have included any relevant IFRS literature.
