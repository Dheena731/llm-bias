An API rate limit is a safeguard put in place by a service provider to ensure they do not become a victim of abuse or have their resources drained. Essentially, it's a maximum number of requests you can make within a certain time frame, beyond which you will start to incur penalties, such as increased costs, slower service, or even temporary or permanent bans.

Think of it like a door limit on a garage: if too many cars try to park all at once, the attendant may refuse entry or declare the lot full. In the context of APIs, the "doors" are API calls, and the "car owners" are the clients making those calls. The limit acts as a preventative measure to maintain the integrity and reliability of the service for all users, encouraging fair usage and preventing service disruptions.
