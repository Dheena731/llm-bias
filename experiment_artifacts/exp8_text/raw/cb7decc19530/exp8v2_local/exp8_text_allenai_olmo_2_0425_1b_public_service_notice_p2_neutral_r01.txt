Of course, sometimes you just can't avoid a road closure, in which case it will be much better to have a plan in place to make sure you can cope. For example, you might need to find an alternative route to a destination that's a bit further. Or you might need to buy some extra time in order to make up a lost hour. Whatever the reason, you will need to have an option in place to allow you to carry on driving, even if the road is closed.

Our advice is to make sure you keep a note of the road closure status at the time you leave the site, and leave yourself a bit of time to make your way back. If you are relying on a GPS system that relies on accurate and constantly updating maps, you may need to make sure you allow extra time if there is a gap in the GPS coverage.

Rest areas

Stop over for a spell, and you might get a chance to take a look at the rest areas. You might find interesting information about the area, or you might just find some interesting sights that can be photographed.

It's also a good idea to spend a little time checking out the facilities in the rest area, especially any services like a bathroom, a shower, a toilet, and food and drink. These can be useful if you are stuck in the cold overnight.

If you can't stop, at least take a look at the signposts for the surrounding areas. You may even be able to spot a few of the up-coming holes in the road that are about to be closed.

It's also a good idea to spend a little time checking out the nearby hills and rivers. These can be good places for spotting wildlife, including bears and moose, and they can also be good places for spotting birds.
