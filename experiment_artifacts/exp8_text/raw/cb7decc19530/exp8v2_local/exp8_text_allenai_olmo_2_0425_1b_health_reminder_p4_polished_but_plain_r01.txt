Don't worry about the style of the writing. All you need is prose that is readable.

Before beginning

Give yourself a little bit of time to get into the writing. Choose an easy task that you enjoy and put it on your calendar. It might be a list, or it might be a blog post or video. You also might want to experiment with writing the one thing that is stumping you the most. It might be a new idea or a new skill you want to learn. Whatever it is, put it on your schedule and leave yourself a few hours to work on it.

Even if you have a cool idea, do not try to write everything all at once. Write small chunks of text at a time, always remind yourself that you can edit your draft later, and try to keep your writing focused on the task at hand.

Your writing practice will build momentum. Small successes will inspire you to keep going, because the chances of succeeding will be much higher than if you try to write everything all at once.

Keep a writing log

Many people keep a journal, but journals are not exclusive to writers. You can also keep a list of your writing tasks on a whiteboard or in a notebook.

Writing a text or a task list on a whiteboard or paper is an excellent way to make sure that nothing falls through the cracks of your schedule. A writing log makes it easier to catch your writing lapses and helps you build more writing into your life.

Just write

If you are stuck with an idea, don't spend time trying to come up with a perfect title or trying to write a catchy paragraph. Just write something down and then come back and revise it later.

This is the first step to building a habit of writing. You must simply get in the habit of putting words on the page.

If it helps, you might try writing in a different color of pen than you normally use. Try to keep a consistent habit of doing some writing each day, every day.

Habits are easy to start. It's the easy part that is hard. When you stop and have to think about what to write, you'll have a harder time actually starting.

Once you have started writing, you will notice that you will eventually write something useful. A practice of writing will bring you positive feedback, which will increase the odds that you will keep writing.

Practice writing simple sentences

You don't need to know more than one type of sentence. Practice writing simple sentences that contain two ideas. You might want to keep every sentence to two words, or you might want to try to stretch those words out to create longer sentences.

There are many ways to structure sentences, but the simplest ones are these:

  • Simple sentences have one independent clause.
    • My name is Jane.
  • Compound sentences have two or more independent clauses.
    • I like spicy food.
    • I don't like spicy food.
  • Complex sentences have more than one independent clause.
    • I like spicy food.
    • I don't like spicy food.
    • The first independent clause is a complete thought, but the second and third are not.
    • I like spicy food, but I don't like spicy food.
    • My new house is red.
    • My new house is not red.
    • My new house is red and not red.
    • My new house is not red and is not red.

These are very simple sentences, but they force you to focus on what you want to say. You will soon notice how many times you use one of these ideas in real life.
