A confidence interval is a range of values that is likely to contain an unknown population parameter with a certain level of confidence. For example, if we calculate a confidence interval for the average height of all adults in a city, we're saying that if we took many samples of that size from the population and calculated a confidence interval for each one, a certain percentage of those intervals (like 95%) would likely contain the true average height of all adult city dwellers. This concept is crucial in statistics as it helps us understand the precision and uncertainty of our estimates.
