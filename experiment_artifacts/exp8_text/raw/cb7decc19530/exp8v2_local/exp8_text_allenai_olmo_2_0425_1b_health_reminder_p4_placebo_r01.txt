If there are several important points, combine them into one paragraph. Do not spread them over the page, unless the structure of the document is clearly marked. A sentence or two may be split between the pages.

Use short concise sentences, with the main idea in the first sentence; subordinate sentences should follow. Do not use passive voice, that is, do not use a form of “be” or “to be” in the first position of a sentence. Pay attention to the logical sequence of ideas.

Use a short quote or paraphrase from an authoritative source to illustrate your point or give extra information.

If the article has received criticism, respond to it. If you did something wrong, say you are sorry and explain why. If the criticism is not justified, do not try to defend the position or make excuses, but do not attack the criticism; there is no place for a “myopic” article in the book.

If the article contains several errors, point them out, but do not present the criticism as a separate issue. The author is allowed to make mistakes, but it is left to the reader to decide whether the article is good or not.

The author, just like everyone else, is entitled to his own opinion. Do not persuade the reader to adopt yours, because this is your opinion. If you miss the point of the article, politely express your disagreement and counter-arguments.

After each article, add a summary of the main concepts.

If possible, leave a link to the source of the article in the references.

If you received some ideas from other authors, quote them correctly, remembering that they are yours.
