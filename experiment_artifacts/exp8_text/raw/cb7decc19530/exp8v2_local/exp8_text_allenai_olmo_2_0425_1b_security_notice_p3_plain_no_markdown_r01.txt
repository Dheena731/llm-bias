Include the budget range, and any specific details, such as whether you would be providing accommodation or not.

Give a specific deadline. On the short end, you should be able to say “today” or “by tomorrow," and on the longer end, you should give at least a week to respond.

Write it down. This will help you remember to write the letter, and you should have it handy when you’re ready to send it to the agency.


You might be asked to provide a letter of recommendation; letters of recommendation are a bit different than letters of employment history. A letter of recommendation is your opportunity to recommend someone for a job or other position. You can write your recommendation in the following way:

  • Greet the recipient by name
  • Provide a brief introduction, including your personal relationship with the applicant
  • Write several paragraphs about why you feel the recipient would be a good fit for the job or position
  • Sign off with professional and polite language
  • Format your recommendation letter as a business letter, with a business letterhead and the correct header.

If you’re applying for a job, you should ask the job posting who to send the recommendation letter to. Most letters of recommendation are for employment, but you may also ask for a letter of recommendation if you’ve been asked to serve on a committee or attend an event or conference.

Best Practices

The letter of recommendation is an opportunity for you to provide some personal insight into someone else’s ability to succeed. You should always be truthful in your letter. If you’re not sure about a detail, try to find the answer and incorporate that detail in your letter. The goal is to give the recipient a good sense of who you think they would be, not who you know they are.

You should always proofread your letter. Check for errors in spelling, grammar, and punctuation; do not use a word processor to proofread. Many word processors include a spelling checker and a grammar checker that are helpful, but a human eye can see mistakes that a word processor or spell checker can’t.

Letters of recommendation can be professional and informative, or informal and personal. In your letter, you should be polite, positive, and friendly, though you might want to be direct in the course of your letter. Try to give the recipient some insight into how they might be a good fit for the position or circumstance. If you can give examples of how you and the recipient have worked together, that can go a long way.
