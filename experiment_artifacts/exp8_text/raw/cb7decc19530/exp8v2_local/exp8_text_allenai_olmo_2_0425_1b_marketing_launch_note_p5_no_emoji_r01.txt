Use Two Words

Two words. These are the minimum amount of vocabulary needed to join in a conversation.

A slight exaggeration, but still a guideline to stay on. While two words may not seem like much, they are enough to convey nuances of meaning, and to say things that a native speaker would say in just two words. And if you only have two words, you are definitely saying something!

A subtle example. Imagine you are in a foreign country and you hear someone say "I lost my keys." And you hear another person say "I lost." Both may mean the same thing, but there is a nuance of meaning there. The person who lost their keys may have lost them because they were misplaced, which is very different than the person who lost them because they were stolen.

That is what those two words are for: to convey nuances of meaning.

Don't Fulfill the Expectation

As it turns out, those two words are not enough. You can say something like "Oh, I've lost my keys." That makes it sound like you know what happened to your keys.

But a native speaker would not take that as a joke. They would say "I've lost" with a little smile. They would be saying "You know, I was just saying that I lost my keys in the park, but I'm sure I'll find them soon." A native speaker would take that as an expression of doubt or a slight sense of humor.

You want to say something more like, "I've lost my keys." That would be more like "So, I lost my keys. I'm sure I'll find them soon."

But even that may be too subtle. You can say something a bit more direct, like "I've lost my keys." That would be like the person who lost their keys saying, "I lost my keys in the park. I'm sure I'll find them soon ." But in that case, it might actually work out for you if you are in the park and you say, "I've lost my keys."
