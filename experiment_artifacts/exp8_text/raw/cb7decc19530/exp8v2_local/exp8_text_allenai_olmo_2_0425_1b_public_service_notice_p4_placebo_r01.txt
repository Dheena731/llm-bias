A heat advisory is a public service announcement intended to protect the general public from the heat index or UV index. Heat advisories are issued by local government authorities in the event of an excessive heat event. Unless otherwise specified by the local government, the heat advisory only applies to the affected area and may not be used to refer to the entire state.

Heat advisories are issued to warn the general public about the extreme risk of heat-related health hazards and to encourage them to take preventative measures. The heat advisory period may be extended to two days or more if weather conditions continue to pose a risk to public health.

If you're unable to follow the heat advisory closely, here are some tips to stay safe during a heat emergency:

  • Stay out of the sun as much as possible
  • Drink plenty of water or other electrolyte-replacing drinks
  • Wear loose-fitting, lightweight, lightweight clothing
  • Take cool showers or baths
  • Apply sunscreen that blocks both UVA and UVB rays
  • If you go outside, wear a hat that shades your face and neck
  • Avoid strenuous activities such as heavy exercise or heavy manual labor
