In this post we are going to look at how the research question can be clarified. Before we get started we should make it clear that we are not looking into the meaning of research questions (that is a topic for another post), but how to make a research question more answerable and definitive.

We are going to look at this from two different points of view. The first is that of the researcher who is asked to work on a research question that is not very clear. The second is the point of view of the reviewer who is reading a paper that makes no sense and is very unclear.

The first point of view is that of the researcher who is thinking about how to run an experiment and how to answer the question. The second point of view is that of the researcher who is asked to write a review of that paper.

The first paragraph of your paper should have the explanation of what you did. You want to explain it to someone else who has not been involved in the experiment and read the paper. Seems like a silly task? Well this is exactly what would happen if this paper was submitted to a journal where you would have been asked to write the review yourself. This is the first step in writing a review, to be able to explain your work in a way that someone else can follow it.

exercise: If you are writing a review that seems to be unclear, right now the only thing you can do is to ask someone to read the paper and yourself go back and read it multiple times. If something seems unclear you may want to clarify it. It is always easier to clarify a question before you write it down then later on. I mean you may change your mind, but you will have made sure that your wording is clear. If you are not clear with your wording, then you know that it will be very hard for the reviewer to understand what you found in the experiment. This is the main reason why clarity is so important. You do not want to confuse yourself or someone else with your wording. If you are not sure of the clarity of a question then ask someone else to read it and then ask them to rephrase it if it is unclear.

Now let’s move on to the second point of view. When you are reading a paper which is unclear and that is one of the things you have to keep in mind when reading a paper. If you are not sure of a question, you should not jump to another question. You should always try to clarify the question that is being raised before you move to another question. This is not always easy because you never know what you will find in an experiment and you may not be familiar with the jargon that is used.

Please note that this is a warning and not a directive. It should not be a rule that you need to follow. You are allowed to make a comment that you do not understand if you want to. However, when you are writing a paper you should take into account that you are not writing for yourself only. Many researchers read your paper as well and they may not be able to understand what you found. I mean if a lot of people do not understand what you found then you should ask yourself if you have done a good job.

A final note is that the review process is not that easy and that you are not always sure of the answer to a research question. However, you should not base this on whether it is a difficult question or not. The more difficult a question is the more likely it is that you have a hard time answering it. This is because the more difficult it is the more it can be explained by chance. This question I think is more difficult than this is one because the results are not always clear.
