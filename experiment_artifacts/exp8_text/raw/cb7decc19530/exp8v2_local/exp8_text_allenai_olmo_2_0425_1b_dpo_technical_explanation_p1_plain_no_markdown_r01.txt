Latency matters in web applications because it directly impacts the user experience. When a website requires a long time to load, even a simple task like clicking a button, users can become frustrated and move on to another site. The perception of latency is not just about how fast a page loads but also about how consistently and smoothly it loads. Distinguishable latency can lead to broken animations, freezing pages, or even errors, which can turn a potentially smooth user journey into a frustrating one.

In an age where real-time interactions are the norm, such as chat apps, video conferencing, and even simple scrolling through an extensive list of results on a search page, the milliseconds that can be shaved from loading times have a significant impact. Each millisecond counts because it translates to a faster response time for users and can influence their decision to return to the site or engage with the app again.

Moreover, high latency can diminish brand reputation, especially if the issue is rooted in the website's infrastructure rather than the user's connection or device. It can also affect user trust, as a consistent poor experience can lead users to doubt the reliability and integrity of the website. Ultimately, careful consideration of latency factors can help ensure that web applications deliver a seamless, responsive, and engaging experience.
