Be hyperlinked only if the destination is the same for every user. For example, let’s say you write an essay about unfolding time, and you want to link the idea of time to a Wikipedia page. The link should go to the Wikipedia page for time, not the page for “unfolding time.” Otherwise, there are no benefits to the reader, and unless you are a Wikipedia editor, no one will have an opportunity to update or correct the link.

Write copy that is larger than the headline but smaller than a full-sized A4 sheet of paper. Read this guide on the design of social media posts for more information about the styles and sizes of posts.

Don’t use a personal voice in your content. Only you know how to write the way you do, so don’t use your personal voice when writing content for a social media account. You should always use the professional voice for writing social media posts, where appropriate.

To avoid people writing content just to get likes or shares, keep all the posts you make relevant to your brand. Write posts that people will be interested in reading. For example, a post on a recent holiday would be more likely to generate likes and shares than one about planning next year’s holiday.

Be known for something other than sharing links.

When communicating with your followers, be clear, concise, and true to the tone of your brand’s voice. In today’s society, we are bombarded with social media posts from all over the world, and we expect you to write posts that are:

  • easy to read
  • easy to access and understand
  • contain relevant and accurate knowledge and information
  • deliver value
  • have a point or a purpose
  • share interesting, useful, and original information.

Like in most jobs, there is a hierarchy in writing content. Some people are good at writing social media posts because they have a natural knack for writing. However, a large part of being a social media writer is being able to write well.

The process of writing social media posts is similar to writing copy for other applications. However, you should note that social media leads to a lot of cutting and pasting. You’ll find that your social media posts become repetitive. This is because people use the same expressions over and over again. For example, you could write articles about different aspects of time in a variety of ways. Focus on one aspect of time and write a couple of posts about that aspect, then compare, contrast, or discuss different aspects of time. It is also useful to write short posts about the same topic, so you can write a post about a random idea or activity that you enjoy in the same way that you write about another random idea or activity that you enjoy.

For example, if you love skiing, write a post about skiing, then a post about watching a movie, then a post about some interesting fact about a famous ski resort.

Post regularly, but don’t be a robot.

Post at regular intervals, but don’t leave your followers wondering when to expect the next content. They will unfollow you if your posts are too sparse or you simply don’t post enough. If you’re not sure when to post, start by posting once per week. This gives you a good baseline and lets you readjust how often you post if necessary, so you can display more or less content than you’re used to. You can also use a schedule to help with your regular posts.

It is important to post when you have a lot of content to share. For example, if you’re expecting an important event, post about that event. At other times, you can share more casual content.
