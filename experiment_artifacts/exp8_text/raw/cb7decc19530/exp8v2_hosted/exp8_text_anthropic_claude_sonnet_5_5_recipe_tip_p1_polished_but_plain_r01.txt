For a weeknight dinner that comes together quickly, start by heating your pan while you prep, not after. A skillet that is properly hot before the food touches it sears chicken, shrimp, or vegetables in minutes, giving you browned edges and real flavor instead of the pale, steamed result that comes from a lukewarm pan. Cut everything into small, even pieces so it cooks at the same pace, and dry your ingredients with a paper towel before they go in, since surface moisture is what keeps food from browning.

Once the main ingredient is cooked, leave the flavorful browned bits in the pan and use them to build a quick sauce. Add a splash of stock, wine, or even water, scrape up the bits with a wooden spoon, and finish with a knob of butter, a squeeze of lemon, or a spoonful of mustard. In the time it takes to boil a pot of rice or pasta, you will have a dinner that tastes far more considered than the effort required.
