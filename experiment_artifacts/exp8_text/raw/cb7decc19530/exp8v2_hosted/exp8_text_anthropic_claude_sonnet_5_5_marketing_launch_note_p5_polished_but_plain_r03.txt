Today we are releasing the first episodes of Everyday Fluency, a podcast for people who want to learn a new language without feeling buried by grammar tables and vocabulary lists.

Each episode runs about fifteen minutes and is built around a single real-life situation, such as ordering at a café, asking for directions, or making small talk with a neighbor. We begin with a short dialogue at natural speed, then slow it down and walk through it phrase by phrase. Along the way we explain why native speakers say things the way they do, point out common mistakes, and share small cultural details that textbooks tend to skip. Every episode ends with a brief practice section in which you are invited to pause, respond aloud, and then hear a model answer.

The show is designed for beginners and early intermediate learners, though we have heard from more advanced listeners that the pronunciation segments and cultural notes are worth the time. You do not need any special materials. A commute, a walk, or a few quiet minutes with your headphones is enough.

We are launching with six episodes in Spanish, with French and Japanese to follow over the coming months. New episodes will arrive every week, and each one will stand on its own, so you can start anywhere and still follow along.

If you are new to podcast-based learning, our suggestion is simple. Listen once for the general sense, listen again while repeating the phrases out loud, and return to the episode a few days later to see what has stayed with you. Short, repeated exposure tends to work better than a single long session.

Thank you for listening and for sharing the show with anyone who might enjoy it. We would love to hear which situations you want covered next, so please send us your requests and questions. Your feedback will shape the episodes to come.
