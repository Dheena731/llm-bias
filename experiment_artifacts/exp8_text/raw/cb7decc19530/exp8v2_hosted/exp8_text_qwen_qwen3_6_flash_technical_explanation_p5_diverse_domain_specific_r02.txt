An API rate limit is a boundary condition enforcing a maximum request throughput against a specific identity (API key, IP address, or tenant). It functions as a governor, typically implemented via a token bucket or sliding window algorithm to track calls against a time-decaying quota. When the reservoir empties before the refill interval expires, the gateway rejects subsequent requests immediately, returning a `429 Too Many Requests` status code, often paired with a `Retry-After` header specifying the required pause duration.

From an engineering perspective, this is a hard contract, not a soft recommendation. Clients must instrument request loops with exponential backoff and randomized jitter to mitigate thundering herd collisions when capacity resets. Rate limits serve three primary operational purposes: they isolate noisy neighbors in multi-tenant environments, apply backpressure to protect downstream services from queue saturation, and enforce provisioning tiers aligned with billing structures. Breaching the limit doesn't merely degrade latency; it risks immediate credential revocation and constitutes a direct violation of SLA boundaries.
