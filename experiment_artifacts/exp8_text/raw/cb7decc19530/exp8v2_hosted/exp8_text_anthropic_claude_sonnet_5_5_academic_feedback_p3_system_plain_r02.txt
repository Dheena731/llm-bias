Reviewer comment on the research question

The manuscript's central research question is difficult to identify and, where it can be inferred, remains too vague to evaluate. The introduction states that the study "explores the relationship between digital engagement and well-being among young people," but this phrasing could describe almost any of several different studies. It is unclear whether the authors are asking if digital engagement predicts well-being, whether it causes changes in well-being, or whether the association differs across subgroups. Each of these questions would require a different design, a different analytic strategy, and a different standard of evidence.

The key constructs are also left undefined. Digital engagement is used interchangeably with screen time, social media use, and online activity, and well-being is sometimes treated as life satisfaction and at other times as the absence of depressive symptoms. Because the research question never specifies which operationalization is intended, I could not judge whether the chosen measures actually address it. The hypotheses in the later sections also do not map cleanly onto the stated aim. Two of them concern moderation by gender, which is never motivated in the introduction.

I would ask the authors to state a single, focused research question in one or two sentences near the end of the introduction, to define the main constructs precisely, and to indicate whether the aim is descriptive, predictive, or causal. They should then revise the hypotheses, methods, and discussion so that each follows directly from that question. Until this is done, it is not possible to assess whether the conclusions are supported by the data. The underlying topic is worthwhile, and I believe a sharper framing would substantially improve the contribution of the paper.
