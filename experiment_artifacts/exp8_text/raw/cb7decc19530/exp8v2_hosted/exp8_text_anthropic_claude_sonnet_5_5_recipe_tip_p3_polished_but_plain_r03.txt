To keep rice from turning sticky, start by rinsing it. Place the grains in a bowl or fine mesh strainer and wash them under cold water, swirling with your fingers, until the water runs mostly clear. This removes the loose surface starch that causes grains to clump together as they cook.

Next, measure your water carefully. For most long-grain white rice, a ratio of about one and a half cups of water to one cup of rice works well, though it is worth checking the package, since varieties differ. Too much water leaves the rice soggy and gluey.

Once the water comes to a boil, stir once, reduce the heat to low, and cover the pot with a tight lid. From that point, resist the urge to stir or lift the lid, because stirring releases more starch and breaks the grains. When the rice has cooked for about fifteen minutes, remove the pot from the heat and let it sit, covered, for another five to ten minutes. Finally, fluff the rice gently with a fork rather than a spoon, which lets steam escape and keeps the grains separate and light.
