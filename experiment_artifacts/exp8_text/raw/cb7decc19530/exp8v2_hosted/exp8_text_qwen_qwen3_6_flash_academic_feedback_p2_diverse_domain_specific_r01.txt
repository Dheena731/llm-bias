Your methods section outlines the workflow, but several omissions will block replication and invite reviewer pushback. Clarify immediately whether your reported *n* reflects independent biological replicates or technical repeats—this distinction dictates how your statistics are interpreted. For the Western blot, list the primary antibody catalog number, host species, working dilution, and validation evidence (e.g., knockout control or independent literature); without lot-level tracking, reviewers cannot assess signal reliability. In the qPCR subsection, name your reference genes, show stability metrics (GeNorm M or NormFinder V), and confirm that individual amplification efficiencies were calculated rather than defaulted to 100%. Your statistical plan names two-way ANOVA but skips assumption checks (Shapiro–Wilk, Levene’s) and any adjustment for multiple comparisons. Finally, replace the bare procedural listing with brief justification for key choices: why this cell line, why these culture conditions, and how batch effects were controlled. Methods should read as defensible decisions, not just a protocol copy-paste.
