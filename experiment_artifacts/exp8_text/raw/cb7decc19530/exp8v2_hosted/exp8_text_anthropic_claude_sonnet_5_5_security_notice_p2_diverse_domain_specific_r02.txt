**NOTICE: Fake payroll emails, Oct 14–16**

**To:** All staff
**From:** IT Help Desk

Since Monday, about 40 of us have received an email titled "Action required: updated direct deposit form." It looks like it comes from Payroll. It doesn't. The link opens a page that imitates our Microsoft sign-in and collects whatever you type.

One person entered their password on Tuesday and called us within ten minutes. We reset it, and nothing was accessed. That is how this should go.

**How to spot this one**
- The sender's address ends in *@hr-payrol1.com* (that's a number 1, not an L). Ours end in *@harlowsupply.com*.
- Payroll will never ask you to sign in through an email link to change banking details. Those changes are made in person or through the HR portal you already use.
- The message says your next paycheck will be delayed if you don't act today. Real payroll problems don't arrive with a deadline attached.

**What to do**
- Click **Report Phish** in the Outlook toolbar. Please don't forward it to coworkers to ask if it's real, because that spreads it.
- If you clicked the link, **call ext. 4417 right away**, even if you didn't type anything. If you did enter your password, we need to reset it before anyone else uses it. Nobody will be in trouble for calling.
- If a vendor emails to say their bank account has changed, call them on the number in our records, not the one in the email, before Accounts Payable changes anything.

We'll also be blocking the sender's domain today, but new ones appear faster than we can list them, so your own check matters.

Questions: helpdesk@harlowsupply.com or ext. 4417.
