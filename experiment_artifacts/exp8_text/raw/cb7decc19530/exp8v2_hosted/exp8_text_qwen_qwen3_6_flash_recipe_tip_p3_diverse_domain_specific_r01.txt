Scrub, don't soak. Vigorously massage the uncooked rice with cold water in a fine-mesh strainer to dislodge the powdery surface starch, rinsing only until the runoff clears. That initial cloudiness is concentrated amylopectin responsible for gelatinous clumps; physical abrasion removes what passive swirling leaves behind. After cooking, fold in a small amount of neutral oil while the rice is hot to coat individual grains, creating a lipid barrier that keeps them distinct as they cool.
