**Salt the pasta water, then steal a mug of it before draining.**

Most weeknight pasta falls flat because the sauce and noodles never actually meet. Fix that by finishing the pasta in the pan, not on the plate.

Here's the move: while the spaghetti is a minute shy of done, scoop out a mug of the cloudy cooking water. Drain the pasta and dump it straight into the skillet where your sauce is simmering, whether that's garlicky olive oil with chili flakes, a jar of marinara you're doctoring, or just butter and black pepper. Add a splash of the pasta water and toss over medium heat for a minute or two. The starch in the water emulsifies with the fat, so the sauce turns glossy and clings to every strand instead of pooling at the bottom of the bowl.

Add the water a few tablespoons at a time. It's easy to loosen a sauce and hard to tighten it back up. Finish with a handful of grated Parm, which will thicken things again, and you've got dinner in about twenty minutes with one pot and one pan to wash.

**Bonus:** Use water that tastes like mild seawater. About a tablespoon of kosher salt per 4 quarts is enough. It's the only chance you get to season the pasta itself.
