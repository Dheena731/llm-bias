**Feedback on your poster draft**

Thanks for sharing the draft. Here are my main comments.

**Main point:** A viewer walking past should be able to tell within about ten seconds what question you studied, what you found, and why it matters. Check whether the title and a single headline sentence do this. If the main finding is buried in the middle, move it up and make it the most prominent element.

**Structure:** Arrange the content so the eye moves in a clear order, usually top-left to bottom-right. Each section should have a short heading that states its purpose. Cut any section that does not directly support the main finding.

**Text:** Reduce body text to short bullet points or brief phrases. A poster is read standing up, and long paragraphs will be skipped. Define any abbreviations the first time they appear, even if you think your field knows them, because some viewers will be from neighboring areas.

**Figures:** Make the main figure the largest visual element. Label axes and units clearly, use a readable font size (ideally legible from about two meters away), and make sure the figure's takeaway is stated in its caption or title.

**Consistency:** Use one font family, a limited color palette, and consistent alignment throughout.

**Next step:** Read the draft aloud as if you were explaining it to a visitor in two minutes. Note where you pause or skip ahead, and revise those spots first.
