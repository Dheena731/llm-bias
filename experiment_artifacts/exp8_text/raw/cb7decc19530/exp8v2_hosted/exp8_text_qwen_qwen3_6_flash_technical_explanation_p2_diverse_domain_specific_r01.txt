A database index is a supplementary data structure that maps column values to their physical row locations, enabling the query planner to resolve filters, joins, and sort operations without full table scans. Most relational engines implement indexes as B-tree variants, though hash, bitmap, and expression-based structures exist for specific access patterns. By maintaining sorted or hashed pointers to disk blocks, an indexed lookup typically reduces I/O complexity from O(n) to O(log n), but this comes at the cost of additional storage and write amplification: every `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, or `DELETE` must also traverse and rebalance the index tree. Effective indexing targets high-selectivity columns, aligns with actual predicate distributions, and leverages the buffer pool cache. Poorly chosen or redundant indexes fragment storage, increase checkpoint duration, and can force the optimizer into less efficient plans, making them a deliberate trade-off rather than a universal performance fix.
