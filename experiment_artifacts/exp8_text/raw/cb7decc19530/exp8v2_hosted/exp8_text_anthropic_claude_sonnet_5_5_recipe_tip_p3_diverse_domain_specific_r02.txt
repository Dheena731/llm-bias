**Rinse until the water runs nearly clear.**

Sticky rice is mostly a surface-starch problem. Put the dry rice in a bowl, cover it with cold water, and swirl it hard with your fingers. The water will turn milky. Pour it off through a fine strainer, then repeat three or four times, until you can see your fingers through the water. Long-grain varieties like basmati and jasmine need this most.

Two small adjustments help further:

- **Use less water than the package says.** For long-grain white rice, try 1 part rice to 1¼ parts water in a covered pot. Packages tend to overshoot.
- **Don't lift the lid, and don't stir.** Stirring breaks the grains and releases more starch. Once the water is absorbed, take the pot off the heat and leave it covered for 10 minutes. The steam finishes the cooking and firms up the grains. Then fluff with a fork, not a spoon.

If the rice still clumps, you probably have a short-grain variety, which is bred to be sticky. Switching to basmati will fix it.
