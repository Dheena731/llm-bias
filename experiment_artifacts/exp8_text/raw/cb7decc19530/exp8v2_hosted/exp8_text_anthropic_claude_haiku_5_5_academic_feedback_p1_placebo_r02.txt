**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall assessment:** The draft shows a good grasp of the sources and a clear interest in the topic. Its main weakness is that it reads as a sequence of summaries rather than a critical synthesis. The review needs to make an argument about the state of the field that leads directly to your research question.

**Main point:** The review currently moves from one study to the next, describing each in turn. Readers learn what each author found but not how the findings relate to one another, where they agree or conflict, or why they matter for your project. Reorganize the review around themes or debates (for example, competing explanations, methodological approaches, or contested definitions), and use each source as evidence within those themes.

**Specific suggestions:**

1. **Open with a framing paragraph.** State the scope of the review, the main debates in the field, and the structure you will follow. This tells the reader what to look for.
2. **Compare and contrast sources.** Where two studies reach different conclusions, explain why. Differences in sample, method, context, or definition are often the most useful points in a review.
3. **Evaluate, don't just describe.** For key sources, comment on the strength of the evidence, its limitations, and how far its findings can be generalized. Phrases such as "this finding is limited by..." or "this supports the claim only if..." show critical engagement.
4. **Make the gap explicit.** The argument for your study depends on identifying what the literature has not yet established. At present, the gap is implied but not stated. Write a short paragraph that identifies the gap specifically and explains why it matters.
5. **Connect the review to your research question.** Each section should move the reader toward the question your study answers. If a section does not contribute to that path, consider shortening or removing it.
6. **Revise the conclusion.** It should summarize the synthesis and state the gap, not repeat the individual summaries.

**Priorities for the next draft:** (a) restructure by theme, (b) add explicit comparisons between sources, and (c) state the gap in one clear paragraph. Once these are in place, the review will be much stronger, and the strengths already present in your source selection and reading will be far more visible to the reader.
