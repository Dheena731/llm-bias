The research question as stated, "How do social media platforms affect young people's wellbeing?", needs substantial clarification before the study can be evaluated. "Social media platforms" covers a wide range of services with very different designs, from image-sharing and short-video feeds to messaging apps and professional networks, and the manuscript never specifies which of these are of interest or why. "Affect" is similarly open: it does not indicate whether the authors expect a causal effect, an association, a moderating influence, or simply a descriptive pattern. "Wellbeing" is also undefined, and the paper's measures (self-reported life satisfaction, depressive symptoms, and time spent online) point to several different constructs that the authors appear to treat as interchangeable. Finally, "young people" spans a developmental range that may respond quite differently across early adolescence and late adolescence, yet the sample description does not indicate which subgroup the question targets. I would ask the authors to restate the question in a form that names the platform type, the outcome, the direction or nature of the hypothesised relationship, and the population, so that readers can judge whether the design and analysis are capable of answering it.
