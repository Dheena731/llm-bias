**Treat soft herbs like cut flowers, and hardy herbs like linen.**

Cilantro, parsley, dill, and mint do best standing upright in a jar with an inch of water. Trim the stem ends first. Drape a loose produce bag over the top and refrigerate. Change the water when it clouds, usually every few days. Parsley and cilantro handled this way last two weeks or more. The exception is basil, which blackens below about 50°F, so leave it on the counter in a glass of water, out of direct sun.

Rosemary, thyme, sage, and oregano hate moisture. Wrap them in a barely damp paper towel, slip them into a zip bag with the air pressed out, and keep them in the crisper. If the towel feels wet, swap it. Wet is what turns thyme to slime.

Don't wash any of them until you're ready to cook. Surface water speeds rot more than almost anything else.

For the tail end of a bunch that's starting to flag, chop it and freeze it in olive oil in an ice cube tray. Drop a cube into a pan to start a sauté or braise.
