Treat tender herbs (parsley, cilantro, mint) like cut flowers: trim the bottom half-inch of the stems, stand them upright in a glass with an inch of water, and tent a plastic produce bag loosely over the leaves before putting them in the fridge. 

The non-negotiable exception is **basil**. Cold temperatures trigger rapid enzymatic browning, rupturing the cell walls and turning the leaves black overnight. Keep your basil jar strictly on the counter at room temperature, out of direct sun.

For woody perennials (rosemary, thyme, sage, oregano), surface moisture is the primary catalyst for mold. Run them through a salad spinner until completely dry, then roll them in a dry paper towel and seal them inside a glass container or zip-top bag in the crisper. The paper buffers humidity without trapping standing condensation against the leaves.
