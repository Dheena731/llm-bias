Rinse until the runoff runs clear—this strips the free starch that rapidly gelatinizes into surface glue. Weigh your components instead of cupping: 180 g long-grain white rice to 240 g water removes the variance that volume measures introduce. Bring to a rolling boil uncovered, then immediately drop to the minimum simmer, seal tight, and leave the lid alone. When the cooking time ends, pull the pot off the heat and let it rest undisturbed for ten minutes. That dead period finishes the internal hydration cycle without mechanical agitation. Loosen the grains with a wooden spatula or chopstick; a metal spoon wedges into the bed, compresses the structure, and forces residual moisture back onto the surface layer.
