Caching improves website speed by saving copies of files and data in a temporary storage area closer to the user. When you first visit a site, your browser downloads elements like images, stylesheets, and scripts. It stores these items locally so that on future visits, the browser can load them immediately without requesting them again from the distant server. This process cuts down on data transfer time and reduces the processing load on the web server. Essentially, caching acts like a shortcut, allowing the computer to grab information from a nearby spot instead of fetching it over the network every single time, resulting in much faster page loads.
