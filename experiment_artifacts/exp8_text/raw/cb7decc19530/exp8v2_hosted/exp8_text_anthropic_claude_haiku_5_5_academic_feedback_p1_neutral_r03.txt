**Overall impression**

The draft shows a clear grasp of the core debate between studies reporting improved well-being from flexibility and those highlighting isolation and blurred work-life boundaries. The organisation into thematic sections works well, and the writing is generally clear and concise. The main weaknesses are in critical synthesis and in the justification for the research gap.

**Strengths**

- The thematic structure (autonomy, social isolation, work-life boundaries) gives the review a logical flow.
- You engage with a good range of recent empirical work, including several longitudinal studies.
- Your summaries are accurate and generally well paraphrased.

**Areas for improvement**

1. **Move from description to synthesis.** Many paragraphs proceed study by study ("Smith (2019) found X; Jones (2021) found Y"). Group findings around the points of agreement, disagreement, and the methodological reasons for the differences. For example, do the studies that find negative effects rely mostly on cross-sectional surveys?

2. **Evaluate the evidence.** Comment on sample sizes, measurement of well-being, and the generalisability of findings, especially where studies draw on single-country or single-sector samples.

3. **Strengthen the research gap.** The conclusion states that "little research exists on hybrid working," but the review does not show this. Either provide evidence from the literature that hybrid arrangements are under-studied or narrow the claim to what you can support.

4. **Check conceptual definitions.** "Well-being," "flexibility," and "remote work" are used interchangeably in places. Define these terms early and apply them consistently.

5. **Citation balance.** The section on social isolation relies heavily on one research group. Include independent replications or contrasting perspectives.

**Minor points**

- Some sentences are long and contain multiple claims; splitting them would improve clarity.
- Check that all in-text citations appear in the reference list and vice versa.
- The introduction spends a full paragraph on the history of the internet, which is not needed here.

**Recommendation**

Revise and resubmit. The foundation is solid, and the main task is to turn a well-organised summary into an argued synthesis that ends with a clearly justified research question. I am happy to review a revised draft or to discuss how to structure the synthesis section.
