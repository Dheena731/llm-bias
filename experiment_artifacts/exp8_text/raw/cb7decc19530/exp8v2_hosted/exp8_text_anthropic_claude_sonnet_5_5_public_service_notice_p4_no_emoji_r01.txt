**PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT**

**Issued by:** [City/County] Office of Emergency Management
**Effective:** [Day, Date], 11:00 a.m. through [Day, Date], 8:00 p.m.

**What to expect:** Daytime temperatures are forecast to reach 98 to 104 degrees Fahrenheit, with heat index values up to 110 degrees. Overnight lows will offer little relief.

**Who is most at risk:** Older adults, infants and young children, people with chronic health conditions, outdoor workers, and anyone without access to air conditioning. Pets are also vulnerable.

**Safety recommendations:**
- Drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if you do not feel thirsty.
- Stay indoors in air conditioning whenever possible.
- Limit strenuous outdoor activity, especially between noon and 6:00 p.m.
- Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing.
- Never leave children, pets, or vulnerable adults in a parked vehicle, even briefly.
- Check on neighbors, relatives, and friends who live alone.

**Cooling centers:** Free cooling centers will be open during advisory hours at public libraries, community centers, and senior centers. For locations and hours, visit [website] or call [phone number].

**Know the warning signs:** Heat exhaustion may cause heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, or headache. Move to a cool place, loosen clothing, and sip water. Heat stroke is a medical emergency, with symptoms including a body temperature above 103 degrees, confusion, hot and dry skin, or loss of consciousness. **Call 911 immediately.**
