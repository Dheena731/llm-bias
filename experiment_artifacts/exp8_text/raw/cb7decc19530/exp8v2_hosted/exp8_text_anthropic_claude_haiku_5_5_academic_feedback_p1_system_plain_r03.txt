Thank you for sharing your draft literature review. The organizing question of the review is clear and well motivated, and the opening paragraphs place the topic within a recognizable debate, which helps the reader understand why the review matters. Your selection of sources is broadly appropriate, and you have included several recent studies alongside the foundational work, which shows awareness of how the field has developed.

The main weakness is that the review currently summarizes sources one after another rather than synthesizing them. Many paragraphs move from one author to the next without indicating where the studies agree, where they conflict, or how they build on one another. I would encourage you to organize the review around themes or competing positions, so that each section makes an argument about the state of the literature. Where two studies reach different conclusions, explain why, whether because of differences in method, context, or theoretical framing. This shift would turn the review from a catalogue into a critical account.

The review would also benefit from a clearer statement of the gap it identifies. At present the reader must infer what remains unresolved, and the concluding section does not yet connect the synthesis back to your own research question. A short paragraph near the end stating what the existing literature does not yet establish, and how your study addresses that limitation, would considerably strengthen the piece.

Finally, attend to consistency in citation format and to a few sentences that rely on vague qualifiers such as "many scholars" or "it is often argued." Naming the scholars or specifying the evidence would make your claims more precise and more persuasive. With these revisions, the review should be in a strong position for submission.
