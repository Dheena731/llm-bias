**Cook one grain and one sauce on Sunday, then change the middle all week.**

Make a big pot of something sturdy, like farro, brown rice, or barley, and a jar of one sharp sauce, such as a lemony tahini, a ginger-scallion oil, or a mustardy vinaigrette. That's all you cook ahead. Each day, add whatever is cheap or already in the fridge: a can of chickpeas, a soft-boiled egg, leftover roast sweet potato, shredded rotisserie chicken, a handful of arugula.

Three details that keep it from getting dull by Wednesday:

- **Pack the sauce separately**, or at the bottom of the container with the grain on top. Soggy grain is the main reason desk lunches get abandoned.
- **Add something crunchy at the last minute**: toasted seeds, cucumber, a few pickled onions. Keep these in their own small jar so they stay crisp.
- **Reheat the grain with a splash of water**, covered, for 90 seconds. Cold farro is fine, but microwaved dry rice turns to gravel.

You end up eating five different lunches from about 30 minutes of cooking, and nothing in the fridge needs to be eaten by a deadline except the cooked chicken, which should go first.
