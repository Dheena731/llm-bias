**Feedback note on your poster draft**

**Main point:** A visitor should grasp your central message within about 10 seconds, from a few feet away. Check your draft against that test first.

**What to check:**

1. **One clear takeaway.** State your main finding or argument in a single sentence near the top, in large type. If you can't point to that sentence, add or sharpen it.
2. **Title.** It should say what you found or asked, not only the topic. It should be readable from several feet away.
3. **Reading order.** Use clear sections, such as background, methods, results, and conclusion, arranged so the eye moves naturally, usually left to right and top to bottom. Number the sections if the path isn't obvious.
4. **Less text.** Cut paragraphs to short phrases or bullet points. Let figures carry the results, and aim for body text that is legible from about 1.5 meters (5 feet).
5. **Figures.** Each one needs a short title or caption stating what it shows, labeled axes, and readable fonts. Remove any figure that doesn't support your main point.
6. **Conclusions.** They should match the evidence you show, with no claims that the results above don't support.
7. **Contact and credits.** Include your name, affiliation, and a way to reach you, plus funding or acknowledgments if required.

**Suggested next step:** Print the draft at reduced size or view it zoomed out. Whatever you can't read or understand at that scale is probably too small, too dense, or not essential.
