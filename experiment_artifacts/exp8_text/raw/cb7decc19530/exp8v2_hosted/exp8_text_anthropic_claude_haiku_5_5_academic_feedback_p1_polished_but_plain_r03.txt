Dear Maya,

Thank you for sending the draft of your literature review on remote work and employee wellbeing. You have clearly done a substantial amount of reading, and the review is already well organised around the three themes you identified: autonomy, social isolation, and boundary management. The opening section sets out the scope of the review clearly, and your use of recent empirical studies alongside the foundational work on job demands and resources gives the argument a solid base.

The most significant issue is that the review currently reads more as a sequence of summaries than as a synthesis. Many paragraphs move from one study to the next, describing findings without indicating how they relate to each other or where they disagree. Consider opening each section with the central claim or tension in the literature, then using the individual studies as evidence for that claim. Where studies conflict, for example where some find that flexibility reduces stress while others find it increases work intrusion into home life, explain what might account for the difference, such as sample composition, measurement of wellbeing, or national labour context.

A related point concerns methodology. You often report that a study found a particular effect, but you rarely comment on its design. Given that much of the evidence on remote work comes from cross-sectional surveys conducted during the pandemic, readers will want to know how far those findings can be generalised. A sentence or two on the strength of the evidence for each major claim would make the review considerably more critical and credible.

Finally, the conclusion should return explicitly to your research question. At present it restates the themes but does not identify the gap your study addresses. I would suggest stating clearly what remains unresolved, particularly the question of whether the effects of boundary management differ between hybrid and fully remote arrangements, since this is the rationale for your own project.

Overall, this is a promising draft. If you can shift the structure from summary toward argument and tighten the link to your research gap, the next version should be in strong shape. I am happy to look at a revised section of the synthesis if that would help.

Best wishes,
