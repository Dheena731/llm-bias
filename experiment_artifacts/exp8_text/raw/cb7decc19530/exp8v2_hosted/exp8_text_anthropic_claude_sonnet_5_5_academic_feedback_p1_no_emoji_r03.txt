**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall assessment**

This draft shows solid familiarity with the field and a clear commitment to covering the relevant sources. The writing is generally competent, and the sections on theoretical background are especially well developed. The main weakness is that the review currently reads as a sequence of summaries rather than an integrated argument. With revision, it could become a much stronger foundation for your research.

**Strengths**

- The scope of reading is broad, and you draw on both foundational and recent work.
- The opening section sets out key concepts clearly and defines terms consistently.
- Your summaries of individual studies are accurate and concise.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Most paragraphs describe one study after another ("Smith (2018) found... Jones (2020) argued..."). Organize the discussion around themes, debates, or questions instead, and place sources in conversation with one another. Where do they agree, conflict, or rely on different assumptions?

2. **Critical evaluation.** The draft rarely assesses the quality of the evidence. Consider the methodological limits of key studies, the populations or contexts they cover, and whether their conclusions are fully supported. Evaluative comments need not be lengthy, but they should be present.

3. **Identification of the gap.** The conclusion gestures toward "further research" but does not state precisely what is missing or why it matters. Make the gap explicit and show how your project responds to it. The review should lead the reader logically to your research question.

4. **Structure and signposting.** The section headings are broad, and transitions between sections are abrupt. A brief introductory paragraph outlining the organizing logic, along with linking sentences between themes, would improve coherence.

5. **Currency and balance.** The sources lean heavily on work from one region and one theoretical tradition. Consider whether alternative perspectives or more recent publications should be included, or explain why they are excluded.

**Minor points**

- Reduce long direct quotations in favor of paraphrase with analysis.
- Check citation formatting for consistency throughout, including the reference list.
- Some sentences are overly long; breaking them up would improve readability.

**Suggested next steps**

Begin by mapping the sources into three or four themes, and write a one-sentence claim for each that expresses what the literature collectively shows. Then rebuild the draft around those claims, ending with a clearly articulated gap. I would be glad to review a revised outline before you redraft in full.
