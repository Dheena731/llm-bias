**Reviewer comment:**

The central research question, "How does social media affect adolescent well-being?", is too broad to guide the study design. "Social media" covers many platforms and uses, and "well-being" is not defined. Depending on the reader, it could refer to depression, self-esteem, life satisfaction, or sleep. The question also doesn't specify a population beyond "adolescents," a term that spans a wide age range with different developmental stages.

As written, I cannot tell what the authors would count as a positive or negative effect, or what result would count as evidence against their hypothesis. I recommend the authors (1) specify the outcome construct and how it will be measured, (2) name the platform use behaviors of interest (e.g., time spent, passive versus active use), (3) define the age range and setting, and (4) state the expected direction of the relationship. A question such as "Among 13- to 16-year-olds in the UK, is daily active social media use associated with higher self-reported depressive symptoms over one year?" would give the study a testable focus.
