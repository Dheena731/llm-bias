Thank you for sending your draft. The reading you have done is evident, and the sources are relevant to your topic. The main issue is that the draft currently reports the literature rather than arguing from it. Each paragraph summarises one source or group of sources in turn, so the reader finishes without a clear sense of what the field knows, where it disagrees, or what remains unresolved.

**Priority revisions**

1. **Organise by theme or question, not by source.** Group the literature under two or three themes that bear directly on your research question. Each section should open with a claim about the state of the field, then bring in the sources that support, qualify, or challenge that claim.

2. **Synthesise within each paragraph.** Where you cite several studies, state how they relate. Do they agree? Do they use different methods that lead to different conclusions? Does one study's limitation matter for another's finding?

3. **State your research gap explicitly.** The review should end by identifying what the literature has not addressed and why that gap matters for your study. At present, the reader must infer this.

4. **Connect the review to your question.** Make clear at the start what question the review is answering, and at the end how the findings lead to your study's design or hypotheses.

**Minor points**

- Check that each citation is accurate and follows your required referencing style.
- Remove evaluative phrases such as "very important" or "interesting" unless you explain why.

**Overall**

The foundation is sound. A reorganisation around themes and a clearly stated gap will turn this from a catalogue of sources into a critical review. I am happy to look at a revised outline before you draft the full section.
