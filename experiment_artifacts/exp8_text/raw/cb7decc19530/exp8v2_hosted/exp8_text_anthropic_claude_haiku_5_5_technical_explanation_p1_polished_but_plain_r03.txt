Latency, the delay between a user's action and the application's response, shapes how people experience a web application more than almost any other technical factor. When a page takes too long to load or a button takes a noticeable moment to respond, users feel friction even if they cannot name the cause. Research on user behavior has repeatedly shown that abandonment rises as load times grow, and even small delays can reduce engagement, conversions, and return visits. For businesses, this translates directly into lost revenue and weaker customer loyalty.

Latency also affects perceived quality. An application that responds quickly feels reliable and well built, while one that lags invites doubt about whether the underlying system works properly. Users often judge trustworthiness by responsiveness alone, so delays can damage a brand's reputation before any feature is even evaluated.

Beyond the user experience, latency influences search visibility and operational efficiency. Search engines consider page speed when ranking results, so slow pages can be harder for prospective visitors to find. Internally, high latency in backend services can cascade into timeouts, retries, and overloaded infrastructure, increasing costs and the risk of outages. Addressing latency therefore improves not only how the application feels but also how well it performs as a whole.
