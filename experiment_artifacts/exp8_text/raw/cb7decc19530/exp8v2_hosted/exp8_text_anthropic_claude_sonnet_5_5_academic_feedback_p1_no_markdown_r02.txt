Feedback on Draft Literature Review

Overall impression

This is a promising draft. You have clearly read widely, and your topic is well chosen and relevant to the field. The main weakness is that the review currently reads as a sequence of summaries rather than a sustained argument. With restructuring and sharper critical engagement, it could become a strong foundation for your project.

Strengths

Your coverage of the key sources is thorough, and you have identified several important recent studies that are often overlooked. The writing is generally clear, and your introduction sets out the scope of the review effectively. I also appreciated your brief discussion of methodological differences in the second section, which hints at the critical stance the rest of the review could adopt.

Areas for development

1. Synthesis. Most paragraphs describe one study after another (for example, "Smith argues... Jones finds..."). Try organizing the material around themes, debates, or gaps instead of around individual authors. Ask what the studies collectively show, where they agree, and where they conflict.

2. Critical evaluation. At present you report findings without assessing their quality. Comment on sample sizes, methodological limitations, theoretical assumptions, and the contexts in which the research was conducted. This will help you justify your own approach later.

3. Research gap. The review does not yet lead clearly to your research question. In the conclusion, state explicitly what remains unresolved in the literature and how your project responds to it.

4. Structure and signposting. Section headings are somewhat generic. Use more descriptive headings and add short transitions between sections so the reader can follow the logic of your argument.

5. Sources and referencing. Several claims rely on secondary citations or textbooks where primary studies are available. Please also check for inconsistencies in your citation format, as a few in-text references do not match the reference list.

Next steps

Begin by drafting a one-page outline that lists three or four central themes and the sources that belong under each. Then revise two or three paragraphs to practice moving from summary to analysis, and send them to me before you rework the full draft. I am happy to meet to discuss the revised outline.
