In web applications, latency refers to the delay between when a user initiates an action (e.g., clicking a button, submitting a form) and when the server responds with the result. This delay can have significant consequences on the user experience, particularly in domains where real-time feedback is critical.

Take online trading platforms, for instance. A latency of just 50 milliseconds can mean the difference between executing a trade successfully or missing the market window. In this context, even a small delay can lead to lost opportunities and financial losses. Similarly, in online gaming, latency can cause disconnection from the game, delayed reactions, or even cause the player to miss important events.

In e-commerce, latency can impact conversion rates and user satisfaction. A slow-loading checkout process can lead to cart abandonment and lost sales. Furthermore, in applications with real-time data updates, such as live sports scores or stock prices, latency can make the data appear stale or outdated, undermining the user's trust in the application.

In summary, latency matters in web applications where speed and responsiveness are critical to the user's experience and the application's purpose. Even small delays can have significant consequences, and optimizing latency is essential for delivering a seamless and engaging user experience.
