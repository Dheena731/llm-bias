PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY

The city health department has issued a heat advisory for all residents, effective from noon on Thursday until 9 p.m. on Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees Fahrenheit, and overnight lows will remain above 78 degrees, offering little relief from the heat.

Residents are urged to stay in air-conditioned spaces whenever possible and to limit time outdoors during the hottest part of the day, generally between 11 a.m. and 6 p.m. Anyone who must work or exercise outside should take frequent breaks in the shade, wear light and loose-fitting clothing, and drink water regularly, even if they do not feel thirsty. Alcohol and sugary drinks should be avoided because they can speed dehydration.

Please check on older neighbors, young children, people with chronic health conditions, and anyone without reliable air conditioning. Never leave children, elderly persons, or pets in a parked vehicle, even for a short time, as temperatures inside can become deadly within minutes.

Cooling centers will be open at the public library, the community recreation center, and the senior services building from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. each day of the advisory. Free water will be available at each location, and transportation assistance can be arranged by calling the city information line.

Warning signs of heat illness include heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps, and a rapid pulse. If someone shows confusion, stops sweating, or loses consciousness, call 911 immediately and move the person to a cooler place until help arrives.

Further updates will be shared through the city website, local radio, and the city emergency alert system. Thank you for helping keep our community safe.
