Salt your chicken thighs the night before, then pat them dry before they hit the pan. Skin that's been dry-brined in the fridge for even four hours crisps up faster and stays tender inside, and you skip the twenty-minute wait for a seasoning to "take." Set the thighs skin-side down in a cold cast-iron pan, turn the heat to medium, and leave them alone until the fat has rendered and the skin releases on its own, about eight to ten minutes. A pan that starts cold gives the fat time to melt out instead of scorching.
