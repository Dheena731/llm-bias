**Caching speeds up a website by storing copies of its files in a temporary, easy-to-reach location.** 

Normally, when you visit a website, your browser has to request every image, script, and piece of text from the site’s main server—which might be located thousands of miles away. 

With caching, once those files are downloaded the first time, a copy is saved locally (like on your computer's browser or a nearby server network). When you revisit the site or click to a new page, your browser simply loads the saved files from this local "short-term memory" instead of downloading them all over again. 

**Think of it like keeping a book you use every day on your desk, rather than walking to the library every time you need to read a page.** This drastically reduces data travel time, lowers the workload on the website's server, and makes pages load almost instantly.
