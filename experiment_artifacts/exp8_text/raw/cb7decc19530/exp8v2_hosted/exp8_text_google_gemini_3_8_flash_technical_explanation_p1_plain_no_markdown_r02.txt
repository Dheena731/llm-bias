Latency refers to the delay between when a user performs an action, such as clicking a button or requesting a webpage, and when the application responds. In web applications, low latency is critical because people perceive even tiny delays. When an application responds immediately, it feels responsive and trustworthy, which keeps users engaged. Even a fraction of a second of delay can cause noticeable friction and frustrate visitors.

Beyond user perception, latency directly impacts business outcomes. Slower loading times lead to higher bounce rates, meaning visitors leave the site before interacting with it. For commercial platforms and digital services, this drop in engagement translates directly into lost sales, lower conversion rates, and decreased revenue.

Latency also plays a significant role in search visibility. Major search engines consider page speed when determining rankings, meaning that slower websites often receive less organic traffic. Furthermore, modern web applications often rely on several background requests to communicate with databases and external services. If the baseline latency is high, these small delays multiply quickly, causing complex features to feel sluggish. Keeping latency low is essential for delivering a seamless experience and ensuring an application remains competitive.
