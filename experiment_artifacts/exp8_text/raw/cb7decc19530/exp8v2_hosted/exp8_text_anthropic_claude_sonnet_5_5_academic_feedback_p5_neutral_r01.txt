**Comment on Missing Citations**

Several claims in this essay rely on specific facts, statistics, or the ideas of other authors, but no sources are cited. For example, the statements in the second and fourth paragraphs read as established findings, yet readers have no way to verify them or to find the original research. Without citations, the argument feels less credible, and the essay also risks suggesting that borrowed ideas are your own, even if that isn't your intent.

To strengthen the essay, add in-text citations wherever you quote, paraphrase, or draw on outside information, and include a matching reference list at the end. Be sure to follow the required citation style (e.g., APA, MLA, or Chicago) consistently. Doing so will lend authority to your claims and show that your argument is grounded in solid evidence.
