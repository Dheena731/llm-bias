A Heat Advisory Is In Effect for the County

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for the county from late morning through 8 p.m. today. Temperatures are expected to reach the low 100s, and the heat index, which accounts for humidity, may feel as high as 105 degrees in some areas. Conditions like these can lead to heat exhaustion and heat stroke, especially for older adults, young children, people who work or exercise outdoors, and those with chronic health conditions.

Residents are encouraged to limit time outside during the hottest part of the day, drink water regularly even if they do not feel thirsty, and wear lightweight, light-colored clothing. Never leave children, pets, or anyone who depends on you inside a parked vehicle, as interior temperatures can rise dangerously in a matter of minutes. Check on neighbors and relatives who live alone, and make sure they have a way to stay cool.

Cooling centers are open at the county library, the community recreation center, and the senior center during regular business hours. Anyone experiencing dizziness, confusion, nausea, rapid pulse, or a body temperature above 103 degrees should move to a cool place and call 911 immediately.

For updates, residents can follow the National Weather Service and county emergency management channels. The advisory will remain in effect until conditions improve.
