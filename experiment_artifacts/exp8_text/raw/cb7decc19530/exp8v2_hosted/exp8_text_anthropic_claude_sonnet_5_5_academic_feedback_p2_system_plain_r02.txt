Thank you for submitting your methods section. Overall, it is a solid draft. You have clearly explained what you did, and the order of the procedures follows the logic of your research question, which makes it easy to follow. Your description of the sample and the setting is also a strength, since a reader gets a good sense of who took part and why they were chosen.

The main area for improvement is replicability. A reader should be able to repeat your study from this section alone, and at present several details are missing or vague. For example, you mention that participants completed a survey, but you do not say how long it took, how it was administered, or where the items came from. Likewise, when you describe your analysis, name the specific tests or procedures and the software you used, and explain why you chose them over alternatives. A sentence of justification for key decisions, such as your sample size, exclusion criteria, or coding scheme, will make the section more persuasive.

I would also encourage you to address limitations in the design where they affect how the results should be read. You do not need to apologize for them, but acknowledging, for instance, that your sample was a convenience sample shows the reader that you understand the boundaries of your claims. Check as well that you describe ethical safeguards such as consent, anonymity, and approval from the relevant board.

Finally, some passages drift into reporting results or interpretation, especially near the end. Keep those for the results and discussion sections so the methods stay purely descriptive. A light edit for tense would also help. Past tense works best throughout.

With these revisions, the section will be clear and credible. I am happy to look at a second draft, and if you send me the text itself I can give more specific comments line by line.
