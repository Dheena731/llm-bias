Thank you for sharing this draft. It shows a solid command of the relevant sources, and the opening section does a good job of establishing why the topic matters. The prose is generally clear, and your summaries of individual studies are accurate and fair. The main task now is to move from reporting what each author found to constructing an argument about what the field collectively knows, where it disagrees, and what remains unresolved.

At present, the review is organized largely study by study, so that each paragraph introduces a new author and describes the methods and findings. This makes it difficult for the reader to see the larger shape of the literature. I would encourage you to reorganize the middle sections around themes, debates, or competing explanations, and to bring studies into conversation with one another within each paragraph. For example, where two articles reach different conclusions, consider whether the difference stems from sample, method, or theoretical framing, and say so explicitly. Doing this will show your own analytic judgment, which is what distinguishes a review from an annotated bibliography.

Related to this, the draft would benefit from a more visible evaluative voice. Phrases such as "Smith argues" and "Jones found" are useful, but they should be followed by some assessment of the strength or limits of the evidence. Are the findings consistent across contexts? Do the methodological choices constrain what can be claimed? A few well-placed critical observations will strengthen the credibility of your review considerably.

The framing of the gap is the other area I would develop. Your conclusion gestures toward under-researched areas, but the gap is stated in general terms and is not clearly tied to the preceding discussion. Try to show how the thematic analysis leads inevitably to a specific question or problem, and then connect that question to your own project. A reader should finish the review understanding exactly why your study is needed and what it will add.

On a smaller level, watch for heavy reliance on direct quotation, which could often be paraphrased more economically, and for some inconsistency in citation formatting. Transitions between sections could also be tightened so that each one signals how it builds on the last.

Overall, this is a promising foundation. With a more thematic structure, sharper critical commentary, and a clearer link between the literature and your research question, it will become a much stronger piece. I would be glad to look at a revised version.
