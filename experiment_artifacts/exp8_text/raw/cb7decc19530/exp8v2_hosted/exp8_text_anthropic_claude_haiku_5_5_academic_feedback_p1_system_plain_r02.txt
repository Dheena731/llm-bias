Thank you for sending the draft of your literature review. You have assembled a useful body of sources, and the opening section does a good job of situating the question within the wider debate on remote work and organizational commitment. The writing is generally clear, and your use of recent empirical studies gives the review a sense of currency.

The main weakness is that the review is organized by source rather than by argument. Several paragraphs follow the pattern of summarizing one study after another without saying how each contributes to the question or where it disagrees with the others. I would encourage you to restructure the middle sections around two or three themes, such as the mechanisms linking autonomy to commitment or the conflicting findings on supervision and trust. Under each theme, compare what the studies found, explain why they may differ in method or context, and identify what remains unresolved. This will make the gap your study addresses much easier for a reader to see.

Your critical evaluation also needs strengthening. Descriptions of sample sizes and methods appear, but you rarely assess whether the designs support the conclusions drawn. Note where studies rely on cross-sectional self-report data, where samples are drawn from a single country or sector, and where measurement of the key constructs varies. A short evaluative comment after each theme would give the review analytical weight.

Finally, check the citation format for consistency, particularly in the reference list, where several entries are missing page numbers or publication details. The concluding paragraph should state the research gap more explicitly and link it directly to your research questions. With these revisions, the review should move from a competent survey to a well-argued foundation for your study.
