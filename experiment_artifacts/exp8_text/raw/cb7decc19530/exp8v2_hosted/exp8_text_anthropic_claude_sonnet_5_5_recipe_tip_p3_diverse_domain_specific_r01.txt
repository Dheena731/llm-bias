**Rinse until the water runs clear, then rest the rice before fluffing.**

Sticky rice is mostly a surface-starch problem. Put the rice in a bowl, cover it with cold water, and swirl it with your fingers. The water will turn milky. Drain it and repeat three or four times, until the water is only faintly cloudy. A fine-mesh strainer works too, but the bowl lets you agitate the grains more thoroughly.

For long-grain rice like basmati or jasmine, use a ratio of about 1 part rice to 1½ parts water. The usual 1:2 ratio on the bag tends to overshoot once the rice has been rinsed and is carrying a little residual water.

When the rice is done, leave the lid on and take the pot off the heat for 10 minutes. This lets moisture redistribute through the grains so the bottom layer doesn't turn gluey. Then fluff with a fork, using a lifting motion rather than stirring, which breaks grains and releases more starch.

If you still want more separation, toast the dry rinsed rice in a teaspoon of butter or oil for a minute before adding water. The fat coats the grains and helps keep them apart.
