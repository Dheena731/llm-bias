**Feedback on your draft literature review**

**Main point:** A literature review should do more than report what each source says. It should organize the research into an argument that shows what is known, where studies agree or conflict, and what remains unresolved, so that your research question follows from it. Please check the draft against this standard first.

**1. Synthesis over summary.** If paragraphs move through sources one at a time ("Author A found… Author B found…"), reorganize them by theme, debate, or method. Each paragraph should make a claim about the literature and use sources as evidence for it. Compare studies directly: where do they agree, differ, or rely on different assumptions?

**2. A clear gap and rationale.** The review should end by stating what the existing work leaves unanswered and why that matters. If your research question or aim doesn't follow clearly from this, strengthen the link.

**3. Critical evaluation.** Comment on the strength of the evidence, such as study design, sample, or scope, where it affects how far a conclusion can be trusted. Avoid treating all sources as equally authoritative.

**4. Scope and selection.** State briefly how you chose the sources and what you excluded. Make sure the key works in your area are included, and that older foundational studies are balanced with recent ones.

**5. Structure and clarity.** Use an introduction that sets out the organizing logic, headings or transitions that show how sections connect, and a conclusion that summarizes the state of knowledge. Define any specialist terms at first use.

**6. Citation accuracy.** Check that every claim is attributed correctly, that quotations are used sparingly, and that the reference list matches the in-text citations and follows your required style.
