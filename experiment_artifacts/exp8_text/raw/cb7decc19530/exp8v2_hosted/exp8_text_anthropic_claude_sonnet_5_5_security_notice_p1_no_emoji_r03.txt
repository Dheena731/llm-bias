**SECURITY NOTICE: Password Reuse**

**To:** All Employees
**From:** Information Security Team
**Subject:** Do Not Reuse Passwords

**Overview**
Reusing the same password across multiple accounts is one of the most common causes of account compromise. When a password is exposed in a breach at one service, attackers routinely try it on other platforms, including corporate systems. This practice is known as credential stuffing.

**Required Actions**
- Use a unique password for every work account.
- Never use your work password for personal accounts, such as email, social media, or shopping sites.
- Create passwords of at least 14 characters, using a passphrase if that is easier to remember.
- Store passwords in the company-approved password manager rather than in notebooks, spreadsheets, or browsers.
- Enable multi-factor authentication wherever it is available.

**What to Avoid**
- Adding a number or symbol to an old password (for example, "Summer2024!" becoming "Summer2025!")
- Sharing passwords with colleagues
- Reusing passwords after a required reset

**If You Suspect a Problem**
If you believe a password has been exposed or reused, change it immediately and report it to the IT Help Desk at security@yourcompany.com or ext. 5555. Prompt reporting helps us contain risks before they affect company systems.

Thank you for helping keep our organization secure.

*Information Security Team*
