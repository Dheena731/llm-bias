Latency, or the delay between a user's action and the application's response, is a critical factor in the overall performance of web applications. When a user interacts with a web application, they expect a timely and responsive experience. However, if the application takes too long to respond, the user may perceive it as slow, unresponsive, or even broken.

In reality, latency is a major contributor to user frustration and abandonment. Research has shown that a delay of just a few hundred milliseconds can significantly impact user engagement and satisfaction. For example, a study by Amazon found that a 100 millisecond delay in loading a product page resulted in a 1% reduction in sales.

Furthermore, latency can also have a significant impact on business metrics. A slow application can lead to lost sales, reduced customer loyalty, and decreased revenue. In today's fast-paced digital landscape, where users are accustomed to instant gratification, even a slight delay can be a major obstacle to success.

To mitigate the effects of latency, web applications must strive for optimal performance and responsiveness. This requires careful attention to server-side processing, network latency, and client-side rendering. By minimizing latency, web applications can provide a seamless, high-quality experience that keeps users engaged and loyal.
