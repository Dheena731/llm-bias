Thank you for sending your draft literature review. I should say that I am writing this as a model note, since no specific draft came with the request, so please adapt the comments to the points that fit your text.

The strongest feature of your draft is the evident breadth of reading. You have clearly engaged with a wide range of sources, and your summaries are generally accurate and clearly written. The opening section also does a good job of establishing why the topic matters and where it sits within the wider field.

The main weakness is that the review is organized mostly source by source, so it reads as a series of summaries rather than a developing argument. A literature review should be structured around ideas, debates, or methodological approaches, with individual studies brought in to support the points you are making. I would encourage you to identify three or four central themes and regroup your material under them. Within each theme, say where scholars agree, where they disagree, and why the disagreement matters. At the moment your own voice appears mainly in the introduction and conclusion, and it needs to be present throughout.

Related to this, the review would benefit from more critical evaluation. You tend to report findings without commenting on the strength of the evidence, such as sample size, research design, context, or possible bias. Even a sentence or two on the limitations of a key study would make your assessment more convincing. Where studies reach conflicting conclusions, consider whether differences in method or setting might explain the divergence.

The review also needs a sharper link to your own research. The final section identifies a gap, but the gap is stated in general terms. Say precisely what is missing, why it matters, and how your research question responds to it. The earlier sections should then be shaped so that this gap emerges naturally from the discussion.

On smaller matters, check that your citation style is consistent, since there are some variations in formatting and a few references in the text that do not appear in the bibliography. Watch for long quotations, which can usually be paraphrased more effectively. Some paragraphs also run long and would be clearer if split around a single main point.

Overall this is a solid foundation. With restructuring around themes, more critical commentary, and a more clearly defined gap, it could become a persuasive review. I would be glad to look at a revised version, and I am happy to meet to talk through the new structure beforehand.
