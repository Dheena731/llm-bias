Latency is the delay between a user's action—such as clicking a button or requesting a page—and the web application's response. Minimizing this delay is critical for several key reasons:

* **User Experience and Retention:** Humans perceive delays as small as 100 milliseconds. When an application feels sluggish, users become frustrated and lose their train of thought. If a site takes several seconds to respond, users are far more likely to abandon it entirely.
* **Conversion Rates and Revenue:** Speed directly impacts the bottom line. E-commerce platforms repeatedly find that even a 100-millisecond reduction in latency leads to measurable increases in completed sales, while slight increases in delay lead to abandoned carts and lost revenue.
* **Search Engine Optimization (SEO):** Search engines, particularly Google, use performance metrics like Core Web Vitals to rank websites. Applications with high latency are penalized in search results, reducing organic traffic.
* **Compounding Architectural Impact:** Modern web applications rely on multiple microservices, third-party APIs, and database queries. A small amount of latency at each step compounds quickly, leading to significant bottlenecks across the entire system.

In short, low latency makes an application feel reliable and responsive, directly driving user engagement, customer trust, and business success.
