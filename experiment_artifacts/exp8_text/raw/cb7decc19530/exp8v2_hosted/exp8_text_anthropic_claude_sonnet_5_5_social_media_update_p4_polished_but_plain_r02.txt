Here is a calendar tip that has saved me more time than any productivity system: schedule your tasks, not just your meetings. Most of us use a calendar app to record appointments, then keep a separate to-do list for everything else. The trouble is that a to-do list tells you what needs doing but never when it will happen, so important work gets pushed aside by whatever is loudest that day.

Instead, create a calendar event for each task that matters, and give it a realistic duration. If a report will take ninety minutes, block ninety minutes. When you see your day laid out this way, you immediately notice when you have promised yourself more than the hours allow, and you can adjust before the day begins rather than at nine at night.

One more small habit makes this work better. Put a few open blocks on your calendar as buffer time, and label them something like "overflow." When a task runs long or something unexpected lands in your lap, you have a place to move it instead of watching your whole schedule collapse.

Try it for one week and see how your days feel.
