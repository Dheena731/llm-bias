**Subject: Eleven of you reused a work password on a site that got breached**

**From:** Information Security
**To:** All staff

Last Tuesday, a recipe and meal-planning site that some of us evidently use for personal accounts published its user database. We checked it against our directory, and 11 work email addresses appear in it. For 6 of those, the leaked password was identical to the one on their work login.

No one got in. Our sign-in alerts caught two failed attempts from a residential IP in another country, and multi-factor authentication stopped them. Without MFA, those would have worked.

**What we've done:** Reset all 11 accounts. If that was you, you've already had an email from us. It wasn't a mistake on your part to receive it, and no one is in trouble.

**What we need from everyone by Friday, 5 p.m.:**

1. Make sure your work password isn't used anywhere else. If you aren't sure, change it.
2. Install the company password manager (Help Desk will do it in about ten minutes, or you can self-serve from the Software Center) and let it generate the new one.
3. Check your personal email at haveibeenpwned.com. If it shows up, change the passwords for any accounts that share a password with something here.

Reuse is the problem, not weak passwords. A strong password used in two places is only as safe as the weaker of the two sites.

Questions go to security@yourcompany.example. Please don't reply-all.
