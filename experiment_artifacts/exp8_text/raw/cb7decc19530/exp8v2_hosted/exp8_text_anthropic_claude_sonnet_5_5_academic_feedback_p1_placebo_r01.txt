**Feedback on your draft literature review**

**Main point:** A literature review should do more than report what each source says. It should combine the sources into an argument about what is known, where the evidence disagrees or is thin, and why that leaves your research question worth asking. If your draft reads as a series of paragraphs, each describing one study or author in turn, the most important revision is to reorganize it around ideas rather than around individual sources.

**Priorities for revision**

1. **Organize by theme or debate.** Group sources by the question they address, the approach they take, or the position they defend. Within each group, say how the sources relate: where they agree, where they conflict, and why they might differ, for example because of different methods, contexts, or definitions.

2. **Evaluate, don't just report.** For key sources, comment on the strength of the evidence, the limits of the method, and how far the conclusions can be generalized. Avoid treating all sources as equally reliable.

3. **State the gap explicitly.** Close the review by saying what the literature leaves unresolved and how your project responds. A reader should be able to see why your question follows from the review.

4. **Make the scope clear.** Say briefly what the review covers and what it leaves out, and why. Check that the most relevant and influential work on your topic is included, not only the most convenient sources.

5. **Use clear paragraph structure.** Open each paragraph with a sentence stating its point, and use your own voice to lead. Use quotations sparingly, and make sure every claim drawn from a source is cited consistently.

6. **Define key terms.** If you use specialized vocabulary, define it at first use and apply it consistently throughout.

**Suggested next step:** Write a two- or three-sentence summary of what the literature collectively shows and where it falls short. Then check that each section of your review supports that summary. Any section that doesn't should be revised or cut.
