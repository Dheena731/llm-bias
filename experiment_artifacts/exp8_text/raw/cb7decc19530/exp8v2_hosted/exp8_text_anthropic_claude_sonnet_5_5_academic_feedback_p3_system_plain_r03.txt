Comment on the research question

The manuscript does not state its central research question clearly enough for me to judge whether the study answers it. The introduction moves between several related aims: understanding why employee turnover varies across firms, testing whether remote work policies reduce it, and evaluating the role of managerial support. These are distinct questions that call for different designs, and the paper never says which one is primary or how the others relate to it. The abstract frames the work as an exploration of "factors influencing retention," which is too broad to guide the analysis or to be falsified.

This vagueness carries through to the rest of the paper. The hypotheses in the third section do not follow from a single question, and the variables in the models seem chosen for availability rather than for a stated purpose. As a result, it is hard to tell whether the null findings on remote work count as evidence against a claim or are simply one more result in a long list. The discussion then draws conclusions about managerial practice that the design cannot support.

I would ask the authors to state one specific question in a sentence or two near the end of the introduction, specifying the population, the outcome, and the relationship of interest. If they intend the paper to be exploratory, they should say so openly and temper the causal language accordingly. Secondary questions can stay, but they should be labeled as such and tied to the main one. Once the question is sharpened, the authors should revisit the hypotheses, the model specification, and the conclusions to confirm they all address the same problem. I think the underlying data may be valuable, but at present the contribution is hard to identify.
