**Feedback on Draft Literature Review: Urban Heat Vulnerability & Policy Response**

Your draft accurately catalogs recent studies on municipal cooling interventions, but it currently functions as a sequential summary rather than a synthesized argument. The empirical coverage of green infrastructure effectiveness is disproportionately weighted toward environmental engineering literature, leaving out the sociological and political-economic critiques of deployment patterns. Integrating scholarship on “green gentrification,” land tenure instability, and procedural fairness in siting decisions would prevent the review from reproducing the very technocratic blind spots it seeks to examine.

The theoretical scaffolding leans heavily on Ostrom’s adaptive capacity framework, which treats vulnerability as a tractable optimization problem. This obscures how historical zoning, disinvestment, and infrastructural legibility continue to generate heat exposure regardless of contemporary planning metrics. Consider anchoring the review in critical urban geography’s work on path dependency and state capacity, which better accounts for why identical policy toolkits yield divergent neighborhood outcomes.

Methodologically, you treat all cited studies as comparable evidence, yet they operate on mismatched scales and data regimes. Several rely on county-level census proxies for exposure, while others deploy block-group thermal imaging or community sensor arrays. Distinguishing between administrative convenience sampling and granular, temporally explicit measurement would clarify where the literature genuinely advances versus where it repeats methodological constraints. Update the citation base beyond 2018 as well; the shift toward open-source remote sensing and participatory monitoring has substantially revised baseline assumptions about exposure heterogeneity.

Restructure the review along three analytical nodes: (1) measurement validity and spatial resolution limits, (2) policy instrument design versus implementation friction, and (3) competing definitions of “resilience” across engineering, planning, and community advocacy traditions. Replace paragraph-by-paragraph source description with direct comparative critique, especially where studies employ incompatible operationalizations of risk or equity.

With tighter theoretical alignment and deliberate synthesis over enumeration, this will move from source inventory to a functional conceptual map.
