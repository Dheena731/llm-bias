Thank you for sharing your draft literature review. I have not seen the text itself in this exchange, so what follows is a model note covering the points that most often matter at this stage. Please adapt it to your own draft as you see fit.

The strongest feature of a good literature review is a clear line of argument, and this is where I would focus first. At present the draft reads largely as a sequence of summaries, with each source introduced, described, and then set aside. A reader finishes each paragraph knowing what a given author found but not why it matters for your question. I would encourage you to organize the review around themes, debates, or methodological tensions rather than around individual studies, and to open each section with a claim of your own that the sources then support, complicate, or contest.

Related to this, the review needs more critical engagement. You identify what previous researchers concluded, but you say little about the strength of their evidence, the limits of their samples or methods, or the assumptions behind their framing. Where studies disagree, say so directly and offer a view on why they might differ. This analysis is what will show your reader that you are evaluating the field and not merely reporting on it.

The gap you are aiming to fill should also emerge more explicitly. By the end of the review, a reader should be able to see precisely what is unresolved or understudied and why your project is a logical response. Right now that rationale is implied rather than stated, and a short concluding section that draws the threads together would help considerably.

On sources, the range is generally sound, but I would check that you are relying on recent work alongside the foundational texts, and that you are not leaning too heavily on a few authors or on secondary accounts where the original study is available. Please also check citation formatting for consistency, as there are several small variations in the reference list.

Finally, the prose is clear in most places, though some paragraphs run long and a few transitions are abrupt. Tightening topic sentences and adding brief linking statements between sections will improve the flow.

With these revisions, I think the review will become a much more persuasive foundation for your study. I am happy to look at a second draft or to discuss any of these points in person.
