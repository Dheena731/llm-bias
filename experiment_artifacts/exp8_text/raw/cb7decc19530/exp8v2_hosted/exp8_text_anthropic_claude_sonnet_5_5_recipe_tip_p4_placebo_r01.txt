**Tip: Cook once, assemble all week.**

On the weekend (or whenever you have a free hour), prepare a few basic components instead of full meals:

- **A grain or starch:** rice, quinoa, or roasted potatoes
- **A protein:** baked chicken, hard-boiled eggs, or a pot of beans
- **A vegetable:** roasted or raw, such as carrots, broccoli, or chopped peppers
- **A sauce or dressing:** something simple that changes the flavor

Store each component in its own container in the fridge. Each morning, or the night before, combine one item from each group into a lunch. Mixing different sauces or swapping the protein keeps the meals from feeling repetitive, and you skip the daily decision of what to make.

**Helpful guidelines:**
- Most cooked foods keep well for about 3 to 4 days in the fridge. If your week is longer, freeze a portion of the protein or grain and thaw it overnight in the fridge.
- Keep crunchy or fresh items, like raw vegetables and leafy greens, separate until you eat so they stay crisp.

Spending an hour on preparation means that on busy days, lunch takes only a few minutes to put together.
