Most personal finance spreadsheets fail at two points: sinking funds and mid-month reality. 

Either the sheet is too fragile—break one cell reference when adding a row and the entire annual summary throws `#REF!`—or it’s an over-engineered SaaS clone with 30 tabs and brittle Google Apps Scripts you have to re-authorize every month.

**The Cashflow Ledger** is built around actual cash allocation rather than retroactive guilt:

*   **Painless CSV Staging:** Paste your raw bank export into a single staging tab. Categorization maps from a clean lookup table based on your historical vendor names—no third-party aggregators dropping their bank sync every Tuesday.
*   **True Accrual for Irregular Expenses:** Quarterly taxes, car registration, and annual software renewals are treated as monthly liabilities, so unspent cash doesn't masquerade as disposable income.
*   **Hard-Coded Balance Checks:** A persistent reconciliation cell flags when your manually recorded cleared balance drifts from your actual account total, catching duplicate entries immediately.
*   **Zero Scripts:** Every view uses standard array formulas, native data validation, and basic `SUMIFS`. It runs fast on mobile, doesn't ask for access to your Google account, and won't break if Google updates their Workspace security policies.

It’s an opinionated, unflashy file for people who want to allocate their money in ten minutes on Sunday and close the tab.
