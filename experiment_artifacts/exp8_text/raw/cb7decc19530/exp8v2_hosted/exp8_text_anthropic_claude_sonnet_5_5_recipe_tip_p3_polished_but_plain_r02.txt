To keep rice from turning sticky, start by rinsing it. Place the grains in a bowl or fine mesh strainer and rinse under cold water, swishing with your hand, until the water runs mostly clear. This washes away the surface starch that causes clumping. Next, measure your water carefully. For most long-grain white rice, a ratio of about one and a half cups of water to one cup of rice works well, though basmati often needs slightly more. Once the water comes to a boil, stir once, reduce the heat to the lowest setting, cover tightly, and leave the lid alone for the full cooking time, usually around fifteen minutes. Stirring during cooking releases more starch and makes the rice gluey. When the time is up, remove the pot from the heat and let it rest, still covered, for five to ten minutes so the steam can finish the job. Finally, fluff the rice gently with a fork rather than a spoon, lifting and separating the grains instead of pressing them together.
