An **API rate limit** is a restriction that caps the number of requests a client can make to an API within a specific time window (e.g., 100 requests per minute or 10,000 per day). 

It's implemented by API providers to protect their servers from overload, ensure fair access among all users, and prevent abuse or accidental traffic spikes. When you exceed your limit, the API typically rejects further requests with a `429 Too Many Requests` status until the time window resets or your allocation refreshes. Developers handle this by implementing exponential backoff, caching data, or batching requests to stay within the allowed thresholds.
