Season roasted vegetables in two stages for the best flavor. Before roasting, toss them with a generous coating of oil, plus salt and pepper, so the seasoning clings evenly and the edges caramelize rather than dry out. Use enough oil to give each piece a light sheen, and spread the vegetables in a single layer with a little space between them so they roast instead of steam. Once they come out of the oven, hot and slightly glossy, finish them with the brighter and more delicate flavors that would burn at high heat: a squeeze of lemon juice, a splash of good vinegar, a handful of chopped fresh herbs, or a sprinkle of flaky salt. Delicate spices and minced garlic are best added in the last ten minutes of cooking or right after roasting, since they turn bitter if they spend the whole time in a hot oven. This simple pattern of seasoning early for depth and finishing late for freshness works for nearly any vegetable, from carrots and broccoli to squash and Brussels sprouts.
