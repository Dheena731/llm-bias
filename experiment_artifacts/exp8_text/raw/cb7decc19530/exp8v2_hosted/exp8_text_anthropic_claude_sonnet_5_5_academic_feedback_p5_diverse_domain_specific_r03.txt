**Comment on citations (paragraph 2 onward):**

Your argument depends on three claims that currently have no source: that Snow's map "convinced the authorities," that 616 people died, and that the Lancet "dismissed" his theory. The first is the one that worries me most. The Board of Guardians of St James's did order the pump handle removed, but the General Board of Health went on favoring miasma, and Henry Whitehead's later work on the index case is what made the water argument hard to ignore. "The authorities" blurs two very different bodies, and a citation would force you to say which one you mean.

For the death toll, name the report you took it from. Figures for Broad Street vary depending on whether the count comes from Snow's own tables or the Cholera Inquiry Committee's. Also, the Whitehead quotation in your conclusion has no page number, and I couldn't find it in the edition you list.

Before resubmitting, go through each paragraph and ask where each sentence came from. If the honest answer is "a lecture" or "I remember reading it," go back and find the source. Use the Chicago footnote style from the syllabus, not parenthetical author-date.
