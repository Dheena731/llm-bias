Latency is the delay between a user taking an action and the web application responding to it. In modern software, this measurement directly dictates the boundary between an experience that feels seamless and one that feels broken. Human perception registers delays as brief as one hundred milliseconds as an unnatural lag, interrupting cognitive flow and creating subtle friction. When interfaces hesitate, users become frustrated, lose focus, and often abandon tasks altogether.

The consequences of this friction extend far beyond user sentiment into core business metrics. Even fractional increases in latency reliably depress conversion rates, reduce retention, and drive visitors toward faster competitors. Major search engines also incorporate page speed into their ranking algorithms, meaning sluggish performance directly reduces organic discovery. Furthermore, in modern distributed architectures, individual network delays compound across internal services and database queries, rapidly degrading overall system throughput. Low latency is not merely an engineering preference, but a fundamental product requirement that governs trust, user satisfaction, and commercial viability.
