Latency represents the time delay between a user performing an action and the web application returning a response. This metric is vital because it dictates the perceived responsiveness and overall quality of the user experience. Users expect near-instant feedback; when latency is low, interactions feel fluid and intuitive, fostering satisfaction and trust. High latency, however, introduces friction that disrupts this flow, leading to frustration and annoyance. Studies indicate that even minor increases in delay can significantly raise bounce rates, as users are likely to abandon a slow application in favor of faster alternatives. Furthermore, sluggish performance can negatively affect conversion rates and revenue, since potential customers may lose patience during critical tasks like checkout or sign-up. Excessive latency can also trigger technical failures, such as connection timeouts, which prevent core features from functioning properly. Ultimately, managing latency is essential for maintaining engagement, ensuring reliability, and meeting the modern standard for instant digital communication.
