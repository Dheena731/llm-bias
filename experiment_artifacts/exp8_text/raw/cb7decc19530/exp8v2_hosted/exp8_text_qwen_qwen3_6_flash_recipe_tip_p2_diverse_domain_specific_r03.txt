Separate tender and woody herbs before they see the fridge. Stand cilantro, parsley, and dill in two inches of cool water with the ends freshly snipped, then loosely cover the foliage with a vented container or inverted bowl to maintain humidity without sealing out oxygen. Lay rosemary, thyme, and oregano flat on a single fold of parchment, slide them into the warmest zone of your refrigerator (typically the door or upper shelf), and leave them unwrapped in plastic. Condensation trapped against waxy or resinous cuticles accelerates fungal growth and breaks down volatile oils, so only rinse what you plan to use within twelve hours. Refresh the standing herbs' water every third day to inhibit bacterial film.
