**The Secret to Silky, Non-Sticky Rice: The Importance of Oil and Resting**

As a chef in a high-volume sushi restaurant, I've found that the key to achieving silky, non-stick rice lies in the ratio of oil to rice, as well as the resting time after cooking. Here's the trick: when cooking Japanese-style short-grain rice, use a ratio of 1 tablespoon of oil (such as sesame or canola) per 2 cups of rice. This will not only prevent the rice from sticking together but also enhance the flavor and texture.

After cooking, let the rice sit for 5-10 minutes, covered with a clean, dry cloth. This resting time allows the starches to settle, making the rice less sticky and easier to handle. As the chef, I also use a gentle folding motion to separate the grains, which helps to break up any clumps and prevent stickiness.

By incorporating these two simple steps, you'll achieve a perfectly cooked, non-sticky Japanese-style rice that's perfect for sushi, donburi, or as a side dish.
