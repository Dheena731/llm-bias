The central research question is not stated clearly enough for me to evaluate whether the study answers it. The introduction moves between several related aims: understanding why participation declined, testing whether the new outreach program had an effect, and describing differences across neighborhoods. Each of these would call for a different design and a different standard of evidence, yet the manuscript never says which one is primary. As a result, the hypotheses, the outcome measures, and the conclusions seem to belong to different studies.

This ambiguity has practical consequences. If the question is causal, the authors need to explain how they separate the effect of the program from other changes occurring over the same period, and the current comparison group does not do this convincingly. If the question is descriptive, the causal language in the discussion should be removed, and the analysis should focus on patterns and their uncertainty rather than on mechanisms. Several key terms are also left undefined. For instance, it is unclear whether "participation" refers to enrollment, attendance, or sustained engagement, and the results change meaningfully depending on which is meant.

I recommend that the authors state a single primary question in one or two sentences near the end of the introduction, specify the population and time frame it applies to, and define the main outcome precisely. Secondary questions can then be presented explicitly as exploratory. The methods and results sections should be reorganized to follow this structure, so that a reader can trace each analysis back to the question it addresses. With that clarity, the dataset could make a useful contribution, but at present the framing prevents a reader from judging what the findings actually show.
