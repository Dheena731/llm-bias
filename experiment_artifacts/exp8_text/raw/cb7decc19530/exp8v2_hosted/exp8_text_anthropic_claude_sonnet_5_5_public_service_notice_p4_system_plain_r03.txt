PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY

The city health department has issued a heat advisory for all residents, effective from noon on Thursday until 9 p.m. on Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees Fahrenheit, and overnight lows will remain in the upper 70s, offering little relief. When humidity is factored in, it may feel as hot as 110 degrees.

Prolonged exposure to this kind of heat can lead to heat exhaustion and heat stroke, which can be life-threatening. Older adults, young children, pregnant women, people with chronic illnesses, and those who work or exercise outdoors are at the greatest risk.

Residents are urged to drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if they do not feel thirsty, and to avoid alcohol and sugary drinks. Stay indoors in air conditioning whenever possible, and limit strenuous outdoor activity to the early morning or late evening hours. Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing and a hat when outside. Never leave children, pets, or vulnerable adults in a parked vehicle, even for a short time. Please check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and anyone living alone.

Cooling centers will be open at the public library, the community recreation center, and city hall from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. each day of the advisory. Free water will be available at each location, and transportation assistance can be arranged by calling the city information line at 555-0142.

Warning signs of heat illness include heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, headache, muscle cramps, confusion, and hot, dry skin. If you or someone near you shows these symptoms, move to a cool place, drink water, and apply cool cloths to the skin. If symptoms are severe or the person becomes confused or loses consciousness, call 911 immediately.

For updates, visit the city website or tune in to local radio and television stations.

Issued by the City Health Department and Office of Emergency Management.
