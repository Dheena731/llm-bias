PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT

The city's Office of Emergency Management has issued a heat advisory for all neighborhoods, effective from 11:00 a.m. Thursday until 9:00 p.m. Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees Fahrenheit, and high humidity will make it feel as hot as 110 degrees. Overnight lows will remain in the upper 70s, which limits the body's chance to recover from daytime heat.

Prolonged exposure to these conditions can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Older adults, infants and young children, people with chronic health conditions, outdoor workers, and anyone without reliable air conditioning are at greatest risk. Residents are asked to check on neighbors, relatives, and friends who may be vulnerable, especially those who live alone.

To stay safe, drink water regularly throughout the day, even if you do not feel thirsty, and avoid alcohol and heavily sweetened drinks. Wear lightweight, loose-fitting, light-colored clothing. Schedule outdoor work, exercise, and errands for early morning or after sunset, and take frequent breaks in shade or air conditioning. Never leave children, older adults, or pets in a parked vehicle, even for a few minutes, as interior temperatures can become deadly very quickly.

Watch for warning signs of heat illness. Heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps, and weakness may signal heat exhaustion, and the person should move to a cool place, sip water, and rest. Confusion, a body temperature above 103 degrees, hot and dry skin, fainting, or a rapid pulse may signal heat stroke, which is a medical emergency. Call 911 immediately and try to cool the person with cool cloths or water until help arrives.

Cooling centers will be open at public libraries, community centers, and senior centers during regular business hours, with extended hours until 9:00 p.m. on Friday and Saturday. Transportation assistance is available for residents who cannot travel on their own by calling the city information line at 311. Residents may also call 311 for updates on this advisory and for details on cooling center locations.

Please share this notice with family, neighbors, and coworkers. The advisory will be updated if conditions change.
