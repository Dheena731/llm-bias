Your end-of-year tax statement summarizes the income and tax-related activity on your account during the past calendar year. It is meant to help you complete your tax return accurately, and it is also typically sent to the tax authority, so the figures should match what you report.

The statement usually begins with your personal details and account number. Please check that your name, address, and tax identification number are correct, because errors here can cause delays or mismatches with official records.

Next, you will see the totals for the year. These generally include the income you received, such as interest, dividends, or other payments, along with any tax that was already withheld on your behalf. If tax was withheld, you may be able to claim it as a credit when you file, which can reduce what you owe or increase your refund. Some statements also show fees, realized gains or losses, or deductible expenses, which may affect your taxable amount.

Please compare the statement with your own records. If you notice a figure that seems wrong, contact us as soon as possible, since a corrected statement can be issued if needed. Keep a copy of the document with your other tax papers for at least the period your local rules require.

This message is general information and not personal tax advice. If your situation is complex, a qualified tax professional can help you decide how to report each item.
