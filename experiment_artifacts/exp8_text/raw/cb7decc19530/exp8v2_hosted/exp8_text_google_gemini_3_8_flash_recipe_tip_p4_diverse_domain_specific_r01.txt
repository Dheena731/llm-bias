**Build for cold-storage degradation, not the fresh plate.** 

Most home-prepped lunches fail by day three because refrigeration dulls acidity and ruins delicate textures. Skip chicken breasts and white rice, which turn chalky and stiff after 48 hours. Instead, batch-cook ingredients with structural staying power: braised chicken thighs, farro or lentils (which don’t seize up when cold), and shredded raw crucifers like cabbage or kale that won't wilt in dressing. 

The critical step: pack a small jar of something high-acid—quick-pickled shallots, pepperoncini, or a sharp lemon-mustard vinaigrette. Splashing acid over cold fats and starches immediately before eating cuts through the "refrigerator taste" without needing a microwave.
