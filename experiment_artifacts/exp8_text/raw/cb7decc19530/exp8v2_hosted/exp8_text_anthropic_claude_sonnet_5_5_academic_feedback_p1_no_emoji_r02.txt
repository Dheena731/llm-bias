**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall assessment**

This draft shows a solid grasp of the core sources and a clear interest in the topic. The review is well researched, and the prose is generally readable. At present, however, it reads more as a sequence of summaries than as an argument about the state of the field. The main task for revision is to move from describing individual studies to synthesizing them.

**Strengths**

- The range of sources is broad and appropriately current, with a good balance of foundational and recent work.
- Your summaries are accurate and concise, which suggests careful reading.
- The introduction states the scope of the review clearly, and the section headings help the reader navigate.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Many paragraphs are organized around one author at a time ("Smith argues... Jones found..."). Try organizing around themes, debates, or methodological approaches instead, and place sources in conversation with one another. Where do they agree, conflict, or rest on different assumptions?

2. **Critical evaluation.** The draft rarely assesses the strength of the evidence. Consider commenting on sample sizes, methodological limitations, theoretical blind spots, or the generalizability of findings. This will help establish your own analytical voice.

3. **Identifying the gap.** The conclusion does not yet state clearly what is missing from the existing literature and how your project responds to it. A review should build toward a justified research question, so make this link explicit.

4. **Structure and transitions.** The movement between sections feels abrupt in places. Brief framing sentences at the start and end of each section, explaining why the next topic follows, would improve coherence.

5. **Citation and style details.** Check for consistency in citation format, and reduce long direct quotations in favor of paraphrase, reserving quotations for wording that is essential.

**Suggested next steps**

- Create a simple matrix of your sources against key themes to reveal patterns and tensions.
- Rewrite one section thematically as a trial, then apply the approach to the rest.
- Draft a one-paragraph statement of the gap and use it to guide the conclusion.

With these revisions, the review should become a much stronger foundation for your project. I am happy to look at a revised section if that would be useful.
