The main area for improvement is replicability. A methods section should contain enough detail that another researcher could repeat your study without needing to ask you questions. At the moment, some key choices are stated without explanation or specifics. For example, describe how participants or samples were selected, state the exact instruments, materials, or software versions used, and say how you handled missing or unusual data. Where you made a decision, such as choosing a particular test or threshold, add a sentence explaining why it suited your research question.

I would also suggest separating what you did from what you found. A few sentences drift toward interpreting results, which belongs in later sections. Keep this section focused on design, procedure, and analysis. Finally, check your tense and voice. Past tense is standard for describing completed work, and a few passages shift between tenses.
