**Areas for improvement**
1. **Justify your choices.** You explain what you did, but not always why. For example, say why you chose this instrument, sampling approach, or analytic technique over alternatives, and note any limitations that follow from those choices.
2. **Increase replicability.** Include specifics a reader would need to repeat the study, such as software versions, scoring procedures, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and the time frame for data collection.
3. **Clarify the analysis plan.** State which tests or analytic steps address each research question or hypothesis, and name the criteria you used (for example, significance thresholds or coding procedures).
4. **Address ethics and validity.** Mention consent, approval, and data handling, as well as steps taken to ensure reliability and validity.
5. **Tighten the writing.** Use past tense consistently, avoid first-person narrative where your discipline discourages it, and cut details that do not affect interpretation of the results.
