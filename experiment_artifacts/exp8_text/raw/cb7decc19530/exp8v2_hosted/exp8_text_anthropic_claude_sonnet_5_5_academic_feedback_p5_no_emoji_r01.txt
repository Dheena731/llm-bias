**Comment on Missing Citations**

Several claims in this essay rely on statistics, research findings, and paraphrased ideas, but no sources are given for them. For example, the statements about economic trends in the third paragraph and the quoted opinion in the conclusion have no attribution. Without citations, readers cannot verify your evidence, and your argument loses credibility. It may also raise concerns about academic integrity, even if the omission was unintentional.

To fix this, add in-text citations in the required style (APA, MLA, or Chicago) wherever you use another author's data, wording, or ideas. Then include a matching reference list or bibliography at the end. Review your draft for any statement that is not common knowledge or your own analysis, and make sure each one is traceable to a source. Doing so will strengthen your argument and show that you have engaged carefully with the existing research.
