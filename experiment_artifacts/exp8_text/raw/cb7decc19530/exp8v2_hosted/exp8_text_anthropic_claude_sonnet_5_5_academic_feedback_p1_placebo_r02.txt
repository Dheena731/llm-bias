**Feedback on your draft literature review**

**Main point:** A literature review should do more than report what each source says. It should synthesize the sources into an argument that shows where the field stands and why your project is needed. When a draft is organized source by source ("Smith found… Jones found…"), the reader is left to work out how the studies relate and why they matter. Revise with that in mind.

**What to check and revise**

1. **State the purpose early.** Your introduction should give the research question or problem and explain how the review will address it. Every section should then serve that purpose.
2. **Organize by theme, not by source.** Group studies by the questions, methods, or debates they share. Each paragraph should make a claim, such as "Studies disagree on X because of Y," and use the sources as evidence for it.
3. **Compare and evaluate.** Note where sources agree, where they conflict, and what might explain the differences, such as sample, method, or context. Comment on the strength of the evidence, not just its existence.
4. **Identify the gap explicitly.** End with a clear statement of what remains unresolved or understudied and how your work responds. Readers should not have to infer this.
5. **Use quotations sparingly.** Paraphrase and keep your own analysis in the foreground. Cite each source accurately and consistently.
6. **Check scope and currency.** Make sure you include the key foundational works and recent work on your topic, and say why you excluded any major strand.

**Next step:** After revising, try summarizing the review in three sentences: what is known, what is contested or missing, and how your project responds. If that is hard to do, the structure probably needs more work. I'm happy to look at the next version.
