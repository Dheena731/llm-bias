Thank you for sharing the draft of your poster. I like that the central question is clear and that the visual flow moves from background to methods to results in a logical order. A viewer walking past should be able to tell within a few seconds what you studied and why it matters, and your title and opening panel mostly accomplish that.

My main suggestion is to cut the amount of text. Several sections read like paragraphs lifted from a paper, and most people will not stop to read them at poster-viewing distance. Try trimming each section to the two or three sentences that carry the argument, and let the figures do more of the work. The background in particular could be shortened to a brief statement of the gap your work addresses.

The figures are the strongest part of the poster, but they would benefit from larger fonts for axis labels and legends, and from consistent colors across panels so that a given group or condition looks the same everywhere. Each figure should also have a short, plain-language headline above or beside it that states the takeaway, so readers do not have to work out the point themselves.

The conclusions section should be one of the most prominent parts of the poster, and at the moment it is easy to overlook. Consider placing it in a clearly marked box, limiting it to a few key findings, and stating the practical or scientific implication directly. Limitations and next steps can be brief, but including them will invite useful conversation.

Finally, check the balance of the layout. A bit more white space between sections would make the poster feel less crowded, and a small contact detail or QR code in a corner would help people follow up. With these changes, I think the poster will be much easier to absorb and discuss. If you send me the specific text or layout, I would be glad to give more detailed comments.
