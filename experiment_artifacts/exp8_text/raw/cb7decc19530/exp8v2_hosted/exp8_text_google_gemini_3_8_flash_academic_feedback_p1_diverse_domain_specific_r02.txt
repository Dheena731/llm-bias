Elena,

The draft is moving in the right direction, particularly where you trace the move from classical Burdett-Mortensen equilibrium models to modern AKM-style establishment-level rent-sharing. That narrative arc works well. 

However, the current draft still leans too heavily on chronological summary rather than conceptual tension. A few specific areas to address before the next iteration:

1. **Disentangle Structural Concentration from Search Frictions:** In Section 2.3, you tend to treat local labor market HHI (e.g., Azar et al.) and institutional frictions (non-competes, commuting tolerances) as interchangeable proxies for wage markdown. They aren't. Group the empirical literature by the *source* of the firm’s wage-setting power: does the elasticity arise from thin market geography, or from information asymmetry and job-to-job mobility constraints? 
2. **Missing Friction on the Rent-Sharing Mechanism:** Your discussion of Card, Heining, and Kline (2013) assumes sorting accounts for the residual variance, but you need to engage with the domestic outsourcing critique (Goldschmidt & Schmieder, 2017). If the fissured workplace is driving the collapse of the establishment premium at the bottom of the distribution, that alters the policy implications of the wage-setting elasticities you review in Section 2.5.
3. **Trim the Product-Market Tangent:** The four pages on retail price pass-through (pp. 14–18) derail the pacing. Unless you explicitly link consumer elasticity back to downstream labor-demand elasticity via a formal production function, reduce this to a footnote and a single framing paragraph.
4. **Setup for Your Empirical Strategy:** The literature review should build an analytical runway for your IV in Chapter 3. Right now, you discuss the limitations of shift-share instruments in abstract terms without directly signaling the specific identification threats (e.g., correlated local demand shocks) that your design intends to solve.

Let’s focus our meeting on Thursday around restructuring Section 2.3. Please bring a one-page outline showing how you plan to reorganize the papers around the underlying elasticities rather than publication dates.
