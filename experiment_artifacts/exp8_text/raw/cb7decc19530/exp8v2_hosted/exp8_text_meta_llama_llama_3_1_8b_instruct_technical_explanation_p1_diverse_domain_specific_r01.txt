In web applications, latency refers to the time it takes for a user's action to be processed and a response to be delivered. This might seem like a trivial concern, but the reality is that even small delays can have a significant impact on user experience and ultimately, business outcomes.

In online commerce, for instance, a latency of just 100 milliseconds can lead to a 7% decrease in sales. This is because users are impatient, and any delay in loading a product page or submitting an order can cause them to abandon their transaction. Similarly, in online gaming, latency can affect a player's ability to react to events on the screen, making the experience less enjoyable and potentially affecting their competitive edge.

The reasons for this sensitivity to latency are twofold. Firstly, web applications rely heavily on user interaction, and any delay in responding to that interaction can create a sense of disconnection and frustration. Secondly, the very nature of web applications – real-time and asynchronous – means that users expect instant gratification, and any delay can undermine that expectation.

In this context, reducing latency is not just a technical optimization, but a strategic imperative. By minimizing latency, web applications can improve user satisfaction, increase conversion rates, and ultimately, drive business success.
