Feedback on Draft Literature Review

Overall impression

This draft shows a solid grasp of the core literature and a clear interest in the topic. The sources are generally relevant and current, and your writing is mostly clear and readable. The main weakness is that the review currently reads as a sequence of summaries rather than a sustained argument. With some restructuring and sharper analysis, it could become a much stronger foundation for your project.

Strengths

Your coverage of foundational studies is thorough, and you have included a good range of recent work. The opening section orients the reader well and establishes why the topic matters. Your summaries are accurate and show that you have read the sources carefully.

Areas for development

1. Synthesis over summary. Many paragraphs describe one study after another (for example, "Smith found... Jones argued..."). Try organizing paragraphs around themes, debates, or questions instead, and bring several sources into conversation within each paragraph. Ask where authors agree, where they conflict, and why.

2. Critical evaluation. At present, most studies are presented as equally credible. Comment on methodological strengths and limitations, sample characteristics, and the extent to which findings can be generalized. This will also help you justify your own choices later.

3. Identifying the gap. The review does not yet make clear what is missing from existing research. Your final section should state explicitly what remains unresolved and how your project responds to it. Right now the link between the literature and your research question is implied rather than argued.

4. Structure and signposting. The section headings are helpful, but transitions between sections are abrupt. A brief introductory paragraph previewing the organizing logic, and short linking sentences between sections, would improve flow.

5. Citation and style details. Check for consistency in citation format, and avoid long direct quotations where paraphrase would serve better. Some sentences are wordy and could be tightened.

Suggested next steps

Begin by outlining the three or four main themes in the literature and reassigning your sources to them. Then revise each paragraph so that it opens with a clear analytical claim, supported by evidence from multiple sources. Finally, rewrite your conclusion to articulate the gap and your contribution.

I would be glad to look at a revised version once you have restructured the draft. You have a good base to build on, and the changes suggested here should make the argument considerably more persuasive.
