**Subject:** Notes on Chapter 2 draft (Lit Review: Microplastics in Benthic Biota)

Julian,

This draft shows clear progress on cataloging the empirical work, but the chapter currently reads as a sequential summary rather than an evaluative framework. It needs restructuring before it can serve as a foundation for your methodology. A few targeted issues to address:

1. **Analytical sorting over chronological cataloging:** In Section 2.3, you move author-by-author through ingestion rates in estuarine amphipods. Group these studies by exposure route instead (water-column suspension vs. porewater/sediment-associated). The central tension in the current literature isn't *who* found microplastics first; it's the discrepancy between idealized laboratory concentrations (spherical virgin polystyrene at $>10^4$ particles/L) and field-recovered weathered fibers. Emphasize that mismatch explicitly—it justifies your planned sampling design.

2. **Methodological caveats are missing:** You cite Van Cauwenberghe et al. and several subsequent bivalve uptake assays without interrogating their digestion protocols. A significant portion of the pre-2019 papers you lean on used high-concentration $\text{HNO}_3$ without verifying polymer degradation via $\mu\text{-FTIR}$ or Raman. You cannot treat those recovery rates as directly comparable to recent enzymatic or KOH digestions. Add a dedicated sub-section evaluating how analytical recovery techniques have bounded the reliability of older baseline data.

3. **Trophic transfer:** The transition between benthic primary consumers and secondary demersal predators (Section 2.5) drops off abruptly. Integrate the gut-retention and egestion half-life models—specifically looking at how gut residence time varies with sediment organic matter content. If sediment organic carbon outcompetes synthetic polymers for ingestion, say so; right now, you treat plastic availability as independent of sediment geochemistry.

Cut the narrative overview of global plastic production in the introduction down to two paragraphs. Assume your committee knows the macro context; we need to see your command of the benthic partitioning mechanics.

Bring an updated thematic outline to our supervisory meeting on Thursday.
