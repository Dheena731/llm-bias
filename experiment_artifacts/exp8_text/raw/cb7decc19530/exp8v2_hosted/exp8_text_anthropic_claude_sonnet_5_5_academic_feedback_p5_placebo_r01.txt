**Comment on Missing Citations**

Your essay makes factual claims, statistics, and references to other people's ideas without telling the reader where they came from. Without sources, readers cannot check your evidence, and your argument loses credibility. Unattributed ideas or wording can also look like plagiarism, even when that was not your intent.

To fix this:

- Cite every statistic, specific factual claim, direct quotation, and idea that came from another author.
- Use one citation style consistently (for example, APA, MLA, or Chicago), whichever your course requires.
- Include a reference list or bibliography that matches your in-text citations.
- Common knowledge, such as widely known facts, does not need a citation. If you are unsure whether something counts, cite it.

Once your sources are properly credited, your argument will be more persuasive and easier to trust.
