**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall impression**

This draft shows a solid command of the relevant sources and a clear effort to cover the breadth of the field. The prose is generally clear, and the section headings help orient the reader. At present, however, the review leans toward summary rather than synthesis. With some restructuring and a more critical stance, it could become a much stronger foundation for your project.

**Strengths**

- The range of sources is good, and you draw on both foundational works and recent studies.
- Your account of the major theoretical frameworks is accurate and accessible.
- Citation practice is consistent, with only minor formatting slips.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Many paragraphs move through studies one at a time ("Smith (2018) found... Jones (2019) argued..."). Try organizing paragraphs around themes, debates, or points of disagreement, and use the sources as evidence within those discussions. Ask what the studies collectively show, where they conflict, and why.

2. **Critical evaluation.** The review rarely comments on the quality or limitations of the work cited. Consider methodological weaknesses, sample limitations, or unexamined assumptions, and note where findings rest on thin evidence.

3. **Identifying the gap.** The conclusion gestures toward a gap in the literature but does not state it precisely. Make the link explicit: what remains unresolved, why it matters, and how your research question responds to it.

4. **Structure and flow.** The transitions between sections are abrupt, and the third section overlaps with the second. A short framing paragraph at the start, outlining the logic of the review, would help the reader follow your argument.

5. **Scope and focus.** Some material, particularly the discussion of historical background, is only loosely connected to your research question. Consider condensing it to make room for deeper engagement with the most relevant studies.

**Minor points**

- Avoid overly strong claims such as "it is well established that" unless supported by multiple sources.
- Check for inconsistent tense when reporting findings.
- Several long paragraphs would read better if divided.

**Suggested next steps**

Begin by creating a simple map of the themes and debates across your sources, then reorganize the draft around that map. Revise the final section to state the gap and your contribution in two or three clear sentences. I would be glad to look at a revised version.
