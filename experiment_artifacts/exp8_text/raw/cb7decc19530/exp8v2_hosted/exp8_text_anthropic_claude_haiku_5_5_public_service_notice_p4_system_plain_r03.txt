A heat advisory has been issued for the city and surrounding areas beginning this afternoon and continuing through Thursday evening. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach the mid-to-upper 90s Fahrenheit, with heat index values approaching 105 degrees, making outdoor conditions potentially dangerous for extended periods.

Residents are urged to limit time spent outdoors during the hottest part of the day, typically between 11 a.m. and 6 p.m. When outside, wear lightweight, light-colored clothing, use sunscreen, and take frequent breaks in shade or air-conditioned spaces. Drink water regularly even if you do not feel thirsty, and avoid alcohol and caffeinated or sugary drinks, which can contribute to dehydration.

Never leave children, older adults, or pets in parked vehicles, as interior temperatures can rise rapidly and become life-threatening within minutes. Check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and anyone who lives alone, and make sure they have a way to stay cool. Those who do not have air conditioning are encouraged to visit a public cooling center. Information on locations and hours is available from the city's emergency management office.

Watch for warning signs of heat-related illness, including heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, muscle cramps, and confusion. If someone shows signs of heat stroke, such as a high body temperature, hot dry or damp skin, rapid pulse, or loss of consciousness, call 911 immediately.

For updates and additional guidance, residents should monitor local news outlets and the National Weather Service. This advisory will remain in effect until conditions improve.
