**Comment on missing citations**

Your essay makes several factual claims, statistics, and references to other people's ideas without telling the reader where they come from. Without sources, readers cannot check your evidence, and your argument loses credibility. Unattributed material can also look like plagiarism, even when that is not your intent.

To fix this:

- Add a citation whenever you use a fact, figure, quotation, or idea that is not your own or common knowledge. This applies to paraphrases as well as direct quotes.
- Use one citation style consistently (for example APA, MLA, or Chicago) and follow the one your instructor requires.
- Include a complete reference list or bibliography that matches your in-text citations.
- If you are unsure whether something counts as common knowledge, cite it.

Once your sources are credited, your argument will be much stronger and easier to trust.
