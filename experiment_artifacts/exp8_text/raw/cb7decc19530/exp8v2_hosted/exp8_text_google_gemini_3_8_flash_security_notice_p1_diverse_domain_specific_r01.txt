**Security Notice: Credential Stuffing & Password Reuse**

**The threat vector:**  
When an external service is breached—whether it’s a niche vendor, an industry forum, or a personal shopping app—those email-and-password pairs are immediately fed into automated credential-stuffing tools aimed at our SSO, VPN, and GitHub gateways. 

If your corporate login matches a password you use anywhere else, an external database breach becomes our internal incident. Even complex passwords fail when they exist in plaintext on someone else's compromised server.

**Operational requirements:**

* **Complete isolation:** Your corporate identity password must never be reused for external tools, SaaS pilots, or personal accounts.
* **No "variations":** Appending a year, an exclamation mark, or the platform’s name to a shared root string does not defeat modern cracking masks. 
* **Vault generation:** For services that do not support corporate SSO, use 1Password to generate and store random 20+ character strings. Do not rely on memory.
* **External work accounts:** If you register an `@ourdomain.com` address for an external vendor or research portal, use a distinct vault-generated password.

SecOps continuously cross-references active directory hashes against public and dark-web credential dumps. If an active corporate password matches an exposed credential hash, the account will be automatically revoked and locked pending IT verification.

Direct questions or vault provisioning requests to the `#ask-infosec` Slack channel.
