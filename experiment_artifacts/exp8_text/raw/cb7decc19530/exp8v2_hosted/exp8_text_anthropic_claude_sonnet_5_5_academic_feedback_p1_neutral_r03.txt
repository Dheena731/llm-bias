**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall impression**
This draft shows solid familiarity with the field and a clear effort to cover the key sources. At the moment, though, it reads more as a summary of individual studies than as a synthesis. The next revision should focus on building an argument about what the literature shows, where it conflicts, and where it falls short.

**Strengths**
- The scope is well chosen, and the sources are relevant and mostly current.
- The writing is generally clear, and the section headings help orientation.
- You engage with some foundational works, which gives the review credibility.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Many paragraphs describe one study after another ("Smith found… Jones found…"). Organize them around themes, debates, or methodological approaches instead, and compare the studies directly. Where do they agree, and why do they differ?

2. **Critical evaluation.** Assess the quality of the evidence, not just its content. Consider sample limitations, methodological weaknesses, theoretical assumptions, and generalizability.

3. **Clear gap and rationale.** The review should build toward a specific gap or tension that justifies your research question. Currently the link between the literature and your project is implicit. State it explicitly in the introduction and conclusion.

4. **Structure and signposting.** Add a short roadmap at the start, and use topic sentences and transitions to show how each section contributes to your overall argument.

5. **Source balance.** Check for over-reliance on a few authors or on review articles. Include primary studies and, where relevant, dissenting or alternative perspectives.

6. **Citation and style.** Make sure formatting is consistent and that every in-text citation appears in the reference list. Reduce long direct quotations in favor of paraphrase.

**Suggested next steps**
- Create a table or concept map of the main themes and the sources linked to each.
- Rewrite one section as a model for synthesis, then apply the approach throughout.
- Draft a concluding paragraph that states the gap and connects it to your research aims.

**Summary**
This is a promising foundation. With stronger synthesis, more critical engagement, and a sharper link to your research question, it could become a persuasive and cohesive review.
