Latency in web applications refers to the time it takes for data to travel between a user and a server, and it directly influences the overall success of a digital platform. When latency is high, users experience noticeable delays between an action, like clicking a button, and the application's response. This sluggishness disrupts the user's workflow, leading to frustration and a perception that the service is broken or untrustworthy. In competitive sectors like e-commerce, delays measured in mere fractions of a second can result in abandoned shopping carts, reduced engagement, and significant losses in revenue. Latency also plays a major role in search engine visibility, as modern search algorithms prioritize fast-loading websites, penalizing slower pages with lower search rankings. Furthermore, for modern web tools that rely on real-time interaction, such as collaborative document editing, chat services, and financial trading platforms, low latency is a strict operational requirement rather than a luxury. Minimizing latency is therefore essential for retaining visitors, protecting revenue, and delivering a smooth digital experience.
