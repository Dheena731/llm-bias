Before you start cooking on a busy weeknight, take five minutes to prep your ingredients and set out everything you need. Chop vegetables, measure spices, and pull out your pans and utensils so the stove is the only thing left to manage. This habit, often called mise en place, keeps you from scrambling mid-recipe and prevents overcooking or burning while you hunt for the garlic. A simple trick that helps even more is to start the rice, pasta water, or oven preheating first, since those take the longest, and then prepare the rest of the meal while they work. With a little planning, a dinner that could take an hour of frantic effort will feel calm and come together in about thirty minutes.
