PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY

Issued by the County Office of Emergency Management

A Heat Advisory is in effect from 11:00 a.m. Thursday until 9:00 p.m. Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach 98 to 103 degrees Fahrenheit, with heat index values up to 108 degrees. Overnight lows will remain in the upper 70s, offering little relief.

Who is most at risk: Older adults, infants and young children, people with chronic health conditions, outdoor workers, and pets.

What you should do:

- Drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if you do not feel thirsty.
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces whenever possible.
- Limit outdoor activity to early morning or evening hours.
- Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing.
- Take frequent breaks in the shade if you must work outdoors.
- Never leave children, adults, or pets in a parked vehicle, even for a short time.
- Check on neighbors, friends, and family members who live alone.

Warning signs of heat illness: Heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps, or weakness may signal heat exhaustion. Hot, dry skin, confusion, or loss of consciousness may signal heat stroke. If you suspect heat stroke, call 911 immediately and move the person to a cooler place.

Cooling centers: Free cooling centers will be open at public libraries and community centers from 10:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. each day of the advisory. For locations or transportation assistance, call 211 or visit the county website.

For updates, follow local news and the National Weather Service.
