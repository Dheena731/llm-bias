**Re: Poster draft "Filter pore size and eDNA detection of stream fishes in the Cumberland Plateau" (v2)**

Ruth, thanks for sending this early. The core result is strong. Detection of *Cottus* and *Etheostoma* roughly doubles with 0.45 µm filters over 0.22 µm in your mid-reach sites, and that is the first thing a viewer should see. Right now it's the third panel, under the site map.

Some specific points:

- **Title:** Drop "and" and the region name. "Pore size drives eDNA detection in Cumberland Plateau streams" says what you found.
- **Figure 2:** The rarefaction curves have no y-axis label, and the two filter lines are nearly the same color in grayscale. Label the axis as "ASVs detected" and use a dashed line for one filter type.
- **Methods box:** You list the 12S primers but not the amplicon length or the PCR replicate count. Someone at the poster session will ask both within a minute, so put them on the board.
- **Conclusions:** "Filters should be standardized across eDNA surveys" goes beyond four sites sampled in one season. I'd write "in these streams, 0.45 µm captured more taxa" and leave the field-wide claim for the discussion.
- **Negative controls:** Mention them somewhere. A reviewer will look for field blanks, and their absence will undercut the detection numbers.

Once the comparison moves up and the axes are fixed, this should read well from three meters away. Happy to look at v3.
