Reviewer comment on the research question

The central difficulty with this manuscript is that the research question is never stated in a form that can be answered or tested. The introduction suggests the study is concerned with "the impact of remote work on organizational outcomes," but this phrase covers a wide range of possible inquiries. It is unclear whether the authors want to know if remote work changes productivity, whether it alters employee retention, or whether it affects the quality of collaboration. It is also unclear whether the interest lies in a causal effect, a descriptive pattern, or a theoretical mechanism. The abstract, the final paragraph of the introduction, and the discussion each seem to frame the question slightly differently, which leaves the reader unsure what the study is actually meant to establish.

This ambiguity carries through to the rest of the paper. Because the outcome of interest is not defined, I could not tell why the chosen measures were appropriate, and the analysis reads as exploratory rather than as a targeted test. The conclusions are also hard to evaluate, since there is no clear standard against which to judge whether the findings answer anything.

I recommend that the authors state a single primary research question early in the introduction, specify the population, the exposure, and the outcome it concerns, and indicate whether any hypotheses follow from it. Secondary questions can be kept, but they should be clearly labeled as such. Once the question is sharpened, the authors should revisit the methods and the discussion to show how each element serves it. I would be glad to look at a revised version, as the dataset appears rich and the topic is of real interest.
