A strong methods section lets another researcher repeat your study without having to guess at anything. As you review your draft, ask whether you have described your participants or materials clearly, including how many there were, how they were selected, and why those criteria suit your question. Check that each procedure is explained in the order it happened, with enough detail on instruments, settings, and timing that a reader could follow the same steps. Where you made a choice, such as a particular measure or statistical test, briefly say why you made it, since reviewers will look for that reasoning.

Also look at the balance between detail and concision. Include what affects the results or how they are interpreted, and cut background that belongs in the introduction. Keep the tense and voice consistent, usually past tense, and avoid mixing in results or discussion. Finally, state how you handled ethical considerations, missing data, and any deviations from your original plan, because readers trust a study more when its limitations are acknowledged openly.

Overall, aim for clarity and reproducibility over length. With a careful revision along these lines, I expect the section will be much stronger. Please come see me if you'd like to talk through any of this.
