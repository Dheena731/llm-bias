Missing citations are one of the most common weaknesses in academic writing, and this essay would benefit considerably from attention to sourcing. Several claims, particularly those presenting statistics, historical dates, and direct interpretations of other scholars' arguments, appear without any indication of where the information came from. Readers cannot verify these statements, and the absence of attribution can make the argument seem less grounded than it actually is. Where an idea has been drawn from a published source, it should be cited, both to give credit and to allow the reader to follow up. Even when the writer is paraphrasing rather than quoting, a citation is necessary. I recommend reviewing each factual assertion and each borrowed idea, adding references in the required style, and checking that the bibliography matches the in-text citations. Doing so will strengthen the essay's credibility and demonstrate the careful scholarship the assignment calls for.
