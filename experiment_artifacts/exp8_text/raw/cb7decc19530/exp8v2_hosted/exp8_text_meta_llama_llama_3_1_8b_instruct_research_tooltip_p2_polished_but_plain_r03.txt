A confidence interval is a range of values within which a population parameter is likely to lie. It is calculated from a sample of data and provides a margin of error around the sample mean or proportion. The interval is typically expressed as a range of values, such as 20-25, and is usually accompanied by a confidence level, for example, 95%. This means that if the same population is sampled multiple times, the calculated interval would contain the true population parameter 95% of the time.
