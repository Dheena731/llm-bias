**Weeknight Cooking Tip: Prep Once, Cook Fast**

When you get home tired and hungry, the slowest part of dinner is usually the chopping, measuring, and decision-making, not the cooking. Do that work ahead of time.

**How to do it:**

1. **Pick your meal earlier in the day.** Decide in the morning or the night before so you aren't deciding while hungry.
2. **Prep ingredients in advance.** Slice vegetables, mince garlic, and measure out spices or sauces. Store them in separate containers or bags in the refrigerator.
3. **Choose quick-cooking foods.** Thin-sliced chicken, shrimp, eggs, tofu, ground meat, and pasta cook in about 10 to 20 minutes, which keeps dinner short.
4. **Start the longest-cooking item first.** If you're making rice or boiling pasta, get the water or pot going before you begin anything else, and use that time to cook the rest.

**Example:** On Sunday, slice peppers and onions and mix a stir-fry sauce of soy sauce, a little honey, and garlic. On a weeknight, start the rice, then stir-fry the vegetables and sliced chicken in a hot pan, add the sauce, and serve. Dinner takes about 20 minutes with very little cleanup.

**Food safety note:** Keep raw meat in the refrigerator until you're ready to cook it, and keep it separate from ready-to-eat foods such as chopped vegetables.

The main idea: a few minutes of planning and prep ahead of time makes weeknight cooking quicker and less stressful.
