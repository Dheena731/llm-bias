Latency, the time it takes for a user to interact with a web application, plays a crucial role in determining user experience and application success. When latency is high, users may experience frustration and disengagement, ultimately affecting the application's adoption and revenue.

Research has shown that even small increases in latency can have significant effects on user engagement. A study by the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) found that a 100-millisecond delay in web page loading resulted in a 1% decrease in engagement, with users abandoning the page 9% more often.

Furthermore, high latency can lead to increased bounce rates, reduced conversions, and ultimately, decreased revenue. In fact, a study by Google found that a 1-second delay in page loading reduced revenue by 5% for every 100 milliseconds of delay.

In addition, high latency can also have a significant impact on user satisfaction and loyalty. A study by Forrester found that 62% of users would abandon a website if it took too long to load, and 40% would never return to a website that loads slowly.

In conclusion, latency matters in web applications because it directly affects user experience, engagement, and revenue. As the digital landscape continues to evolve, understanding the impact of latency and implementing strategies to reduce it will be essential for the success of web applications.
