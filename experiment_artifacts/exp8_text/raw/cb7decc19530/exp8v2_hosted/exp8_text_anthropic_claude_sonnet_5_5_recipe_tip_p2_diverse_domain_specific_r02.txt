**Store soft herbs like flowers, hardy herbs like linens.**

Cilantro, parsley, dill, and mint have tender stems that drink water. Trim the bottoms, stand them in a jar with an inch of water, and loosely tent a produce bag over the top. Keep them in the fridge door. Change the water when it clouds, usually every two or three days. Parsley and cilantro last up to two weeks this way, often longer.

Basil is the exception. Cold turns its leaves black, so leave it on the counter in a jar of water, out of direct sun.

Rosemary, thyme, sage, and oregano want the opposite: dry air. Wrap them in a barely damp paper towel, slip them into a loose bag or container, and refrigerate. If the towel feels wet after a few days, wring it out or replace it, because trapped moisture is what turns woody herbs slimy.

Don't wash any of them until you're about to use them. Water on the leaves speeds up rot.
