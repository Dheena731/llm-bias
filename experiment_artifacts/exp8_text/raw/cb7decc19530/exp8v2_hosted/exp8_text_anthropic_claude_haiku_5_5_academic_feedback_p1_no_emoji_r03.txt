**Overall impression**

The draft shows a clear understanding of the core debates in the field and a sound grasp of the main empirical studies. The thematic structure works well, and the writing is generally clear. The main weaknesses lie in the critical synthesis and the framing of the research gap, which should be strengthened before the next draft.

**Strengths**

- The review is organised thematically (autonomy, work-life boundaries, social isolation), which makes the material easy to follow.
- Key studies are identified and summarised accurately.
- The methodological range of sources is appropriate, including both quantitative and qualitative work.

**Areas for development**

1. **Critical evaluation.** Many paragraphs describe what studies found but do not assess how well they were designed or how far their findings can be generalised. Where studies conflict (for example, on whether flexibility reduces stress), explain why the results differ, considering sample characteristics, measurement of wellbeing, and time period.

2. **Synthesis over summary.** Sections often move from one study to the next without drawing connections. Each section should close with a statement of what the body of evidence collectively suggests and where it remains uncertain.

3. **Research gap.** The gap identified in the concluding section (limited research on hybrid arrangements) is promising but under-argued. Show explicitly that existing studies have not addressed it, and explain why it matters theoretically or practically.

4. **Theoretical framing.** The review would benefit from a clearly stated framework, such as job demands-resources theory, to organise the evidence and justify the thematic structure.

5. **Currency and balance.** Several key sources are more than ten years old. Check whether more recent work has superseded or complicated these findings, and ensure that non-Western and non-professional-sector studies are represented, or that their absence is acknowledged as a limitation.

6. **Referencing and mechanics.** A small number of citations are missing page numbers for direct quotations, and some sentences are overly long and would read more clearly if divided.

**Priority actions for the next draft**

- Add a critical evaluation of study quality and conflicting findings within each theme.
- Rewrite the research gap section with a sharper, better-supported argument.
- Introduce a theoretical framework in the opening section.
- Update the search to include literature from the past three to four years.
