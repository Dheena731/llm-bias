The manuscript does not yet state a research question that I can evaluate. The introduction describes a broad interest in how remote work affects organizational outcomes, but it never specifies which outcomes, which mechanisms, or which population the study addresses. The abstract refers to "the relationship between flexibility and performance," while later sections shift to employee well-being, team cohesion, and retention. As a result, it is unclear whether the study is testing a hypothesis, exploring an open problem, or describing a pattern in the data.

This ambiguity affects the rest of the paper. Without a defined question, I cannot judge whether the sample, the measures, and the analytic approach are appropriate. For instance, the survey instrument captures self-reported satisfaction, which may suit a question about perceived well-being but not one about objective productivity. The conclusions also claim implications for managerial practice, yet it is not apparent which finding supports which recommendation.

I encourage the authors to state one primary question, ideally in a single sentence near the end of the introduction, and to identify the constructs, the expected direction of any effect, and the context in which it is examined. If several questions are intended, they should be ordered by importance and each tied to a specific analysis. The literature review should then be tightened so that it builds toward that question instead of surveying the field in general terms. Finally, the discussion should be organized around the same question, so that readers can see clearly what the study has and has not established.

I believe the underlying data may be valuable, and a clearer framing could make the contribution much easier to recognize. At present, however, the lack of a focused question limits my confidence in the study's design and conclusions, and I recommend major revision.
