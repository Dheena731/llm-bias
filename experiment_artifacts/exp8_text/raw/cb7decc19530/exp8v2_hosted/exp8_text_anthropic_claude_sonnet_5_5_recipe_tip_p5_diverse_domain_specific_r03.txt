**Salt before the oven, acid after.**

Toss your vegetables with oil and a generous pinch of salt before roasting. The salt pulls a little surface moisture, which helps the edges caramelize instead of steam. Hold back anything delicate, though. Garlic, fresh herbs, and spice blends with sugar in them will scorch at 425°F, so add those in the last five minutes or stir them in right when the pan comes out.

Then, while the vegetables are still hot, hit them with something sour: a squeeze of lemon, a splash of sherry vinegar, a spoonful of pickled shallot brine. Roasting concentrates sweetness, and a little acid right at the end keeps carrots, squash, and cauliflower from tasting flat. Heat makes the acid soak in and lose its sharpest edge, so it tastes rounded rather than puckery.

Taste a piece off the tray before serving and adjust. Roasted vegetables often need more salt than you'd expect, since a lot of it ends up on the parchment.
