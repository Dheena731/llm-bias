The synthesis in the first half is in good shape—particularly your handling of the street-level bureaucracy literature (the Lipsky–Bovens lineage) and how you map it against more recent accounts of "system-level" discretion. You’ve avoided the common trap of treating digitalization as a total rupture, which gives your historical framing real weight.

My primary concern is the conceptual drift in Section 3. Right now, you’re using *automated eligibility screening* and *actuarial risk scoring* almost interchangeably. They have distinct administrative genealogies and produce different failure modes: rule-based engines tend to generate catastrophic cliff-effects and mass procedural denials, whereas predictive risk models introduce subtler calibration problems around proxy drift and false-positive distribution. Conflating them blunts the edge of your critique.
