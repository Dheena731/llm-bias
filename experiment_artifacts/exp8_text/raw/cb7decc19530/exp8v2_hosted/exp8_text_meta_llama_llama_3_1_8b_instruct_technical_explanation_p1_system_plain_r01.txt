Latency is a crucial aspect of web applications that can significantly impact user experience and overall performance. In simple terms, latency refers to the time it takes for a user's request to be processed and a response to be sent back. When latency is high, users may experience a delay between when they click on a link or submit a form and when they see the result, making the application feel slow and unresponsive.

High latency can lead to frustration and decreased user engagement. For example, if a user submits a form and has to wait 10 seconds for a response, they may click the "submit" button multiple times, thinking that it's not working. This can result in multiple requests being sent to the server, which can further increase the perceived latency and make the experience even more frustrating.

Furthermore, high latency can also have a significant impact on business and revenue. In e-commerce applications, for instance, a slow response time can lead to cart abandonment and lost sales. In real-time applications, such as online gaming or video conferencing, high latency can result in poor video quality, dropped connections, and a general feeling of instability.

To mitigate these issues, web developers and architects often implement various strategies to reduce latency, such as caching, content delivery networks, and efficient database queries. By optimizing for low latency, web applications can provide a faster, more responsive, and more enjoyable experience for users, ultimately driving user engagement, retention, and revenue.
