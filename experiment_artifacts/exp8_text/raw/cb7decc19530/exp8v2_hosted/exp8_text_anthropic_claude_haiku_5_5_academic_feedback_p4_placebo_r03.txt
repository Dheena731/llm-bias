**Feedback on your poster draft**

Overall, the poster is most effective when a viewer can grasp the main finding within about 30 seconds from a few steps away. Focus your revisions on these points:

1. **Lead with the main takeaway.** Make the central result or question the most prominent element, ideally in the title or a single headline sentence.
2. **Cut text.** Conference attendees skim. Keep each section to a few short bullet points or brief sentences, and move supporting detail to your talk or handout.
3. **Make figures do the work.** Use large, labeled graphs with axes that are readable at a distance. Each figure should have a caption that states what it shows.
4. **Check the reading flow.** Arrange sections in a clear order (for example, left to right, top to bottom) and make sure the path is obvious without arrows.
5. **Confirm readability.** Use a body font size large enough to read from about 1.5 meters, and keep the color scheme high-contrast.
6. **Add a clear contact or next-step element.** A QR code or short URL linking to the paper, data, or your email helps viewers follow up.
