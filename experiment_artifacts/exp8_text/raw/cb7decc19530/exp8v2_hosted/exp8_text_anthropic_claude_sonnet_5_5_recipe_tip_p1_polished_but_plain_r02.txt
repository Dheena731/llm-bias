When you get home on a weeknight, start by putting a large pot of salted water on to boil or a heavy skillet on the stove to heat before you do anything else. Those first few minutes of unattended heating are the most valuable ones, because by the time you have chopped an onion, minced some garlic, or trimmed a piece of chicken, your cooking surface is ready and nothing has to wait. Pair that habit with a simple formula for the meal itself: one protein, one vegetable, and one starch, each seasoned with salt, something acidic like lemon juice or vinegar, and a little fat. A pan of seared chicken thighs, a tray of broccoli roasting alongside, and a pot of rice or pasta can come together in under thirty minutes, and the final squeeze of lemon or splash of vinegar brightens everything so that even a plain meal tastes deliberate. Finally, taste as you go and adjust the salt just before serving, since that last small correction often makes the difference between a dinner that is merely fine and one that feels finished.
