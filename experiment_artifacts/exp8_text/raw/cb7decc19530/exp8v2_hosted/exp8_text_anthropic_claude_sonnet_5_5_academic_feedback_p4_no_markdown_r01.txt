Thanks for sharing the draft. I haven't seen the actual file, so I've written this as a general note on what usually matters most. Please adapt it to your poster, and send the file if you'd like comments on specifics.

The strongest posters can be understood in about thirty seconds from a few feet away. Check that your title states the main finding or question clearly, and that someone walking past could tell what you did and why it matters without reading the body text. A one- or two-sentence takeaway near the top or center would help a lot.

On layout, aim for a clear left-to-right, top-to-bottom flow, with plenty of white space. If the draft feels dense, cut the text by a third and let figures carry more of the story. Body text should be readable from about a meter and a half away, so roughly 24 point or larger, with headings noticeably bigger.

Your figures should each make one point. Label axes legibly, avoid tiny legends, and add a short caption stating what the viewer should notice. Keep the color palette consistent and make sure it works for color-blind viewers.

Finally, trim the methods to what's needed to trust the results, and give the conclusions and next steps more room. Add contact information or a QR code to your paper, data, or slides so people can follow up.

Overall, this is a good start. With a tighter message and less text, it will be much more inviting. I'm happy to look at another round whenever you're ready.
