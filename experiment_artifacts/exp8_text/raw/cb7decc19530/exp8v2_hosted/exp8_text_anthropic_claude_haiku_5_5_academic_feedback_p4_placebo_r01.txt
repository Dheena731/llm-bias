**Feedback on your conference poster draft**

I haven't seen your poster itself, so these notes cover the points that most often decide whether a poster works at a conference.

**Main message:** A viewer walking past should be able to tell your central finding or question within about ten seconds. Make sure the title and a single highlighted takeaway state it plainly. If the main point is buried in the methods or the background, move it up.

**Structure:** Arrange the content in a clear reading order, usually left to right and top to bottom. Give each section a short heading. Cut any text that doesn't support the main message, and aim for brief bullet points rather than paragraphs.

**Visuals:** Use one or two strong figures that carry the key result. Label axes and legends so they can be read without the surrounding text. Remove decorative images that don't add information.

**Readability:** Body text should be readable from roughly an arm's length away, and headings should be readable from across the aisle. Keep fonts and colours consistent, and check that colour choices still work for colour-blind viewers.

**Closing elements:** Include your name, affiliation, and a way to reach you, such as an email address or QR code linking to your paper or slides. Viewers who want to follow up will need this.
