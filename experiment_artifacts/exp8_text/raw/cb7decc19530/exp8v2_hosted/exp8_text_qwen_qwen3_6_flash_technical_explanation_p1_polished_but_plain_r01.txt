Latency refers to the delay between a user initiating an action and the application returning a response, and it directly shapes how people interact with any web service. When pages load slowly or interactive elements take noticeable time to register, users perceive the system as sluggish or unreliable, which quickly erodes trust. This perception translates into measurable business consequences because visitors abandon sites that fail to respond promptly, leading to higher drop off rates and reduced conversion numbers. Beyond immediate satisfaction, latency compounds across the many individual requests that modern web applications make behind the scenes, meaning even small delays accumulate into frustrating wait times. Network conditions vary widely, with mobile users and those on constrained connections experiencing these delays more acutely, so minimizing latency becomes an accessibility and equity concern. Search engines also treat responsiveness as a ranking signal, making speed a direct factor in discoverability. Ultimately, keeping latency low is not merely a technical optimization but a foundational requirement for retaining attention, sustaining engagement, and maintaining competitive relevance in an environment where users expect immediate feedback.
