Since I haven't seen the actual section, this is a model note you can adapt to the student's work.

Dear [Student],

Thank you for your methods section. It is clearly organized, and your description of the overall study design gives a reader a good sense of what you set out to do. The sequence of procedures is easy to follow, and your writing is generally concise and professional.

The main area for improvement is replicability. A reader should be able to repeat your study from your description alone, and at present several details are missing or vague. For example, you mention recruiting participants but do not say how they were selected, how many were included, or what criteria led to anyone being excluded. Likewise, when you describe your measures or instruments, please state their source, what they assess, and any evidence of their reliability or validity. If you adapted an existing tool, explain what you changed and why.

I would also like to see a clearer link between your methods and your research questions. At the moment the procedures are described well, but it is not always obvious why you chose them over alternatives. A sentence or two justifying key decisions, such as your sampling approach or your choice of analysis, would strengthen the section considerably. Your data analysis paragraph in particular should name the specific techniques or software used and explain how you handled missing data, outliers, or any assumptions underlying your tests.

Finally, consider whether you have addressed ethical considerations fully. Mention consent, confidentiality, and any institutional approval you received, even briefly.

On a smaller note, watch for shifts in tense and voice. Methods are usually written in the past tense, and keeping this consistent will make the section read more smoothly.

With these revisions, this will be a strong and credible section. I am happy to look at a revised draft if you would like another round of feedback.

Best regards,
[Instructor]
