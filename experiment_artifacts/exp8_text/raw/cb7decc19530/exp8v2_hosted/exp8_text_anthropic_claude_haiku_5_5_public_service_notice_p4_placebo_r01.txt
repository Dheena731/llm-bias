**PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT**

A heat advisory means high temperatures and humidity are expected to create unsafe conditions, especially for people who are older, very young, pregnant, ill, or who work or exercise outdoors. Heat can cause serious illness, including heat exhaustion and heat stroke.

**To stay safe:**

- Drink water regularly, even if you do not feel thirsty.
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces when you can. If you do not have air conditioning, visit a cool public place such as a library or shopping center.
- Limit outdoor activity, especially during the hottest part of the day, and take frequent breaks in shade if you must be outside.
- Wear loose, light-colored, lightweight clothing.
- Never leave children, pets, or anyone who depends on you in a parked vehicle.
- Check on neighbors, relatives, and friends who may be at higher risk.

**Warning signs of heat illness:** heavy sweating, muscle cramps, dizziness, nausea, headache, confusion, or a body temperature that feels very hot. If someone has confusion, fainting, or stops sweating while hot, call your local emergency number right away. Move the person to a cooler place and cool them while waiting for help.

This advisory remains in effect until it is lifted. Follow updates from your local authorities.
