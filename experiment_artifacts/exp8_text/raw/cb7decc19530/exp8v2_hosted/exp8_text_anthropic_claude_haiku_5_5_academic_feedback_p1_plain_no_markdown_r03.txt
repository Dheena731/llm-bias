Dear Author,

Thank you for sharing the draft of your literature review. The topic is well chosen, and the introduction establishes a clear rationale for examining how sleep interventions affect academic performance among adolescents. Your command of the core studies is evident, and the chronological arrangement in the second section helps the reader follow the development of the field. The draft is at a promising stage, and the revisions I suggest below are aimed at turning a competent survey into a argument-driven review.

The most significant concern is that the review currently describes studies one after another without synthesizing them. Each paragraph summarizes a single paper's sample, method, and findings, but few passages explain how the studies relate to one another or where they disagree. Consider organizing the central section around themes or methodological divides instead, such as differences between laboratory and home-based sleep measures, or between interventions that extend sleep duration and those that improve sleep quality. Doing so would allow you to make claims about the state of the evidence rather than only reporting it.

A related issue concerns the critical evaluation of sources. Several studies are cited with their findings accepted at face value, yet a number rely on small samples, self-reported sleep data, or short follow-up periods. Acknowledging these limitations, and explaining how they bear on the conclusions drawn, would strengthen the review considerably. Attention to the quality of evidence will also make your identification of gaps more persuasive.

The concluding section needs sharper focus. At present it restates findings and then gestures toward future research in general terms. I recommend specifying the particular gap your own study addresses, whether that is a neglected population, an understudied outcome measure, or an absence of longitudinal designs, and then linking that gap explicitly to your research questions.

At the sentence level, the draft would benefit from careful proofreading. There are several instances of article errors and inconsistent citation formatting, and some long sentences would read more clearly if divided. Reviewing the reference list against the in-text citations would also catch a few omissions.

With these revisions, the review can move from a useful summary to a substantive contribution to the field. I would be glad to read a revised version or discuss any of these points further.

Kind regards,
Reviewer
