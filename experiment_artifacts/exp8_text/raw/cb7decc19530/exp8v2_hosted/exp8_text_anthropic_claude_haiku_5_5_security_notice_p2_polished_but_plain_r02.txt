Subject: Protecting Our Team from Phishing Emails

Phishing emails are one of the most common ways attackers gain access to company systems, and they often look routine. They may appear to come from a colleague, a vendor, a bank, or an IT department, and they frequently create a sense of urgency, asking you to act quickly, approve a payment, reset a password, or open an attachment.

Before you click a link or respond to a request, take a moment to check it. Look closely at the sender's full email address rather than the display name, and be wary of slight misspellings or unfamiliar domains. Hover over links to see where they actually lead before opening them. Be cautious with unexpected attachments, especially compressed files or documents that ask you to enable macros. If a request involves money, login credentials, or sensitive data, confirm it through a separate channel, such as a phone call to a known number, rather than replying to the email.

Our IT department will never ask you to share your password by email. If you receive a suspicious message, do not forward it to colleagues or interact with it. Report it to the IT help desk immediately so the team can investigate and warn others if needed. If you have already clicked a link or entered credentials, report it right away. Prompt reporting can make the difference between a minor incident and a serious breach.

Thank you for helping keep our systems and our colleagues safe.
