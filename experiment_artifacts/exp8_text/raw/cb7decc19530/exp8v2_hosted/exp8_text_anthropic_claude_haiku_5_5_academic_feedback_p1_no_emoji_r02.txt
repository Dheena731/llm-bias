**Overall impression**

The draft shows a clear grasp of the core debate between productivity-focused and wellbeing-focused accounts of remote work. The sources are generally current and relevant, and the introduction establishes the review's scope well. In its current form, however, the review reads more as a sequence of summaries than as an argument. It needs stronger synthesis and more critical evaluation before it is ready for submission.

**Strengths**

- The research question is well defined and the scope is appropriately bounded.
- Your selection of sources includes both quantitative studies and qualitative work, which gives the review useful breadth.
- The section on methodological limitations in longitudinal studies is a valuable contribution and shows good awareness of the field.

**Areas for improvement**

1. **Synthesis.** Most paragraphs follow the pattern "Author A found X; Author B found Y." Group studies by theme or finding, and state what the body of evidence collectively suggests. For example, you could contrast studies showing isolation effects with those showing autonomy benefits, and explain what might account for the difference.

2. **Critical evaluation.** Few sources are assessed for quality, sample characteristics, or context. Several rely on self-reported wellbeing measures collected during the pandemic, which may not generalise. Note these limitations explicitly and explain how they affect the conclusions you can draw.

3. **Structure.** The review would benefit from a thematic organisation (for instance, autonomy, social connection, and work-life boundaries) rather than the current chronological order. A short paragraph at the start of each section stating its argument would help the reader follow the logic.

4. **Gaps.** The conclusion identifies a gap in research on non-professional sectors, but the body of the review does not return to this point. Either develop it in the discussion or remove it from the conclusion.

5. **Language.** Some sentences are long and contain multiple claims. Also check consistency in citation format and in the use of "remote," "hybrid," and "flexible" work, which are currently used interchangeably.

**Priorities for revision**

1. Restructure the review thematically and add synthesis statements to each section.
2. Add critical assessment of key studies, especially regarding measurement and sample context.
3. Connect the identified research gap to the rest of the argument.
4. Tighten language and standardise terminology.

With these revisions, the review should move from a competent summary to a persuasive account of the field. I am happy to look at a revised section if that would help.
