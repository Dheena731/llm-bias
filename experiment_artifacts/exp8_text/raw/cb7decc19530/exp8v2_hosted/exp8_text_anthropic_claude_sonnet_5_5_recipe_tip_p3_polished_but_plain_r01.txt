For fluffier, less sticky rice, start by rinsing it. Place the grains in a bowl or fine mesh strainer and run cold water over them, swirling with your fingers, until the water goes from milky to nearly clear. This washes away the surface starch that causes grains to clump together as they cook. It usually takes three or four changes of water.

Next, measure your water carefully. For most long-grain white rice, a ratio of about one and a half cups of water to one cup of rice works well, though basmati and jasmine often need a little less. Once the pot comes to a boil, reduce the heat to its lowest setting, cover tightly, and resist the urge to lift the lid or stir, since stirring releases more starch and makes the rice gummy.

When the water is absorbed, turn off the heat and let the pot sit covered for ten minutes. This resting time allows the steam to even out the moisture in the grains. Finish by fluffing gently with a fork rather than pressing with a spoon, and the rice should come out light, separate, and tender.
