**Salt before the oven, acid after.**

Toss your vegetables with oil and salt (a generous pinch per pound) just before they go onto the pan. The salt helps pull a little surface moisture so the edges can brown instead of steam. Then, once they're out, hit them with something sharp while they're still hot: a squeeze of lemon, a splash of sherry vinegar, or a few drops of pomegranate molasses on roasted carrots or squash.

Heat dulls acidity and bitterness fades into flatness, so vinegar or citrus added before roasting mostly cooks off or scorches. Added at the end, it wakes up the caramelized sweetness and keeps the whole pan from tasting heavy.

Dried spices like cumin, smoked paprika, and coriander can go on with the oil, but save tender herbs (parsley, dill, mint) and anything garlicky-raw for the finish. Minced garlic added at the start will burn by the time cauliflower is browned; toss whole smashed cloves in the pan instead and squeeze them over the top afterward.
