# Feedback on Draft Literature Review

*I haven't seen your draft, so this is a model note with common points. Send the text or topic and I'll tailor it.*

---

**Overall impression**
This is a solid draft that shows broad reading and a clear command of the key sources. The main opportunity for revision is to move from *summarizing* the literature to *synthesizing and critiquing* it, so the review builds toward a clear rationale for your study.

**Strengths**
- The scope is well chosen, and the review covers the major contributors to the field.
- The writing is generally clear, and the section headings help orientation.
- Several paragraphs (e.g., the discussion of methodological approaches) begin to compare studies rather than just describe them. This is the strongest model for the rest of the review.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Much of the review proceeds author by author ("Smith (2018) found… Jones (2020) argued…"). Organize paragraphs around themes, debates, or questions, and place the sources in conversation with each other.

2. **Critical evaluation.** Note the limitations, assumptions, and contradictions in the studies you cite. Consider sample size, context, method, and theoretical stance, and say why they matter for your argument.

3. **Identifying the gap.** The conclusion doesn't yet state clearly what is missing or unresolved in the literature. Make this explicit and link it directly to your research question.

4. **Structure and signposting.** Add a brief introduction that maps the review's organizing logic, and use topic sentences and transitions to show how sections connect.

5. **Sources.** Check for over-reliance on a few authors or on older work. Adding recent literature and perspectives from outside the dominant paradigm would strengthen the review. Also check citation formatting for consistency.

**Suggested next steps**
- Build a synthesis matrix (themes × sources) to reorganize the material.
- Rewrite one section thematically as a trial, then apply the approach throughout.
- Draft a closing paragraph stating the gap and your study's contribution.

I'm happy to look at a revised version once these changes are made.
