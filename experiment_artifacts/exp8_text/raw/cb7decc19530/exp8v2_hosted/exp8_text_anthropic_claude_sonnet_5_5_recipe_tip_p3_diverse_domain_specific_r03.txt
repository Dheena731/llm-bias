**Rinse until the water runs nearly clear.**

Sticky rice is mostly a surface-starch problem. Put the dry rice in a bowl, cover it with cold water, and swirl it hard with your fingers. The water will turn milky. Drain it and repeat, usually 3 to 5 times, until you can see through the water to your knuckles. For long-grain rice like basmati or jasmine, a final 20-minute soak in cold water before cooking helps the grains cook evenly and stay separate.

Two more habits help:

- **Use less water than the bag says.** For rinsed long-grain rice, about 1¼ cups water per cup of rice is plenty. The package ratios assume you skipped the rinse.
- **Don't lift the lid or stir.** When the timer goes off, let the pot sit off the heat for 10 minutes, then fluff with a fork. Stirring during cooking breaks the grains and releases more starch. Fluffing too early, while the grains are still wet and soft, smears them together.

Short-grain and sushi rice are high in amylopectin and will stay clingy no matter what you do. For separate grains, buy long-grain.
