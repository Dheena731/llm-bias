WORKPLACE NOTICE: Protecting Yourself from Phishing Emails

Phishing emails are fraudulent messages designed to trick you into clicking malicious links, opening infected attachments, or revealing passwords and other sensitive information. They often look like they come from a trusted source, such as a colleague, a bank, IT support, or a senior manager.

Watch for these warning signs:

- Urgent or threatening language, such as "Your account will be suspended today" or "Immediate action required"
- Requests for passwords, login details, or payment information
- Sender addresses that look slightly off, such as a misspelled domain name
- Unexpected attachments or links, especially from unfamiliar senders
- Generic greetings like "Dear user" instead of your name
- Requests to change payment or bank details

What to do if you receive a suspicious email:

- Do not click any links or open any attachments.
- Do not reply or provide any information.
- Verify any unusual request by contacting the sender through a known phone number or in person.
- Report the email to the IT Service Desk immediately, using the Report Phishing button in your email client or by forwarding it to [IT security address].
- If you have already clicked a link or entered your password, contact IT right away, even if you feel embarrassed. Quick reporting limits the damage.

Phishing attempts are becoming more convincing, so stay alert. If you are ever unsure, ask IT before you act. Thank you for helping keep our organization and our data secure.
