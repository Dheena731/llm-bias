Wash the dry grains in cold water, rubbing them vigorously between your hands until the drainage runs translucent—this strips away the free surface amylose that hydrates into glue. 

Once cooked, kill the heat and leave the lid on for a full ten minutes without touching it. Stirring hot, freshly gelatinized rice shears the swollen starch walls and forces the grains to fuse; letting them rest allows the surface moisture to redistribute and the starch jackets to firm up before you fluff with a fork.
