**Notice to All Employees: Watch for Phishing Emails**

Phishing emails are messages that look like they come from a trusted source, such as a coworker, a vendor, a bank, or our own IT team. Their purpose is to trick you into clicking a harmful link, opening a dangerous attachment, or giving away sensitive information such as passwords.

**Warning signs**
- The message creates urgency or threatens consequences if you don't act immediately.
- The sender's email address looks slightly wrong or doesn't match the name shown.
- The email asks for your password, payment details, or other sensitive information.
- You weren't expecting a link or attachment, or the greeting and wording seem odd.
- A link's destination looks different from what the text says when you hover over it.

**What to do**
1. Do not click links, open attachments, or reply with any information.
2. If the email claims to be from someone you know, confirm with them using a separate method, such as calling them or speaking in person.
3. Report the email to your IT or security team using the process your workplace provides.

**If you already clicked or shared information**
Tell your IT or security team right away. Do not wait to see whether anything happens. Quick reporting helps limit the damage.

When in doubt, report it. It is always better to check than to risk it.
