**Subject:** Update: Recognizing Current Phishing Patterns and Reporting Procedure

Over the past fortnight, we’ve tracked a shift in phishing campaigns targeting staff. The messages now closely mirror internal workflows—ticketing system alerts, expense approvals, and meeting invitations. Look for these specific markers:

• Envelope sender or reply-to addresses that borrow our naming convention but use alternate TLDs, added hyphens, or third-party domains  
• Links directing to authentication pages outside our corporate SSO provider  
• Attachments featuring double extensions (e.g., `memo.docx.exe`) or prompts to enable macros  
• Unverified web forms requesting credentials, payment details, or approval tokens

If an email demands immediate action or access verification, treat it as untrusted. Hover over links to confirm the destination matches our approved domains exactly. Never enter credentials on a page reached through an email hyperlink.

To report a suspected message, click the “Report Phish” button in your mail client or forward the original item unchanged to security@[yourdomain].com. Preserve the message—full headers are required for threat analysis.

Authentication resets, 2FA verification, and password changes are processed only through the identity management portal or direct contact with IT Support. Email is not used
