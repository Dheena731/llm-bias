Here is a sample feedback note you can adapt. If you share your actual poster draft (as text, or a description of its layout and content), I can tailor the feedback to it.

Thanks for sharing the draft of your poster. The core message of your study comes through clearly, and the research question sits well at the top, which helps viewers decide quickly whether to stop and read. Your results figure is the strongest element on the page because it is simple, labeled clearly, and shows the main comparison at a glance. I would keep it prominent and consider enlarging it if space allows.

The main concern is text density. Several sections, particularly the methods and background, contain full paragraphs that a viewer standing at the poster will not read. I suggest cutting each block to two or three short sentences and moving the detailed methods into a handout or QR code linked to your full paper. The conclusion would also benefit from a single sentence stating the takeaway in plain language.

A few smaller points: the font size in the figure axes looks too small to read from a distance of a meter or two, the color scheme in the second chart may be hard to distinguish for viewers with color vision deficiencies, and the acknowledgments and contact information could move to the bottom corner so they do not compete with the findings. Finally, consider adding a brief line near the title that gives the one-sentence contribution of the work, so passersby understand the value immediately.

Overall, the poster has a solid structure and a clear story. Tightening the text and improving the figure legibility should make it considerably more effective at drawing and holding attention.
