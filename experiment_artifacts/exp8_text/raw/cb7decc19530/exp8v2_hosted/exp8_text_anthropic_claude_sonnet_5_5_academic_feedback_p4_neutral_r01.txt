**Feedback on Poster Draft**

Thanks for sharing the draft. The project is clearly interesting, and with some adjustments the poster will be easier to absorb from a few feet away.

**Strengths**
- The research question is relevant and the topic is easy to place within the field.
- The figures are a good start and support the main findings.

**Suggestions**
1. **Lead with the takeaway.** Add a one-sentence main message near the top, so a passerby understands your contribution in under 10 seconds.
2. **Cut text.** Aim for roughly 300-500 words total. Turn dense paragraphs into short bullets, and save the details for conversation.
3. **Strengthen the visual hierarchy.** Make the title, section headings, and key results clearly larger than the body text. Body text should be readable from about 1.5 meters (≥24 pt).
4. **Improve the figures.** Enlarge them, label axes legibly, and add a brief caption stating what the viewer should notice. Where possible, replace tables with graphs.
5. **Make the flow obvious.** Arrange sections in a clear left-to-right, top-to-bottom order (Background → Methods → Results → Conclusions), with numbered or clearly separated panels.
6. **Be consistent.** Limit the palette to 2-3 colors, use one or two fonts, and leave white space between sections.
7. **Finish with clear conclusions.** Give 2-3 concise points on what the findings mean and what comes next, plus contact information or a QR code for the paper or data.

**Next step:** Try a quick test by showing the poster to someone outside your subfield for 30 seconds and asking what they remember. Revise based on that.

Happy to look at a second draft.
