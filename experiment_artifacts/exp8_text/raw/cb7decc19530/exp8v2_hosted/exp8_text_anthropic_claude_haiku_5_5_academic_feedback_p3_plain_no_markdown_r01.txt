The manuscript's central research question, as stated in the introduction, is whether social media use affects adolescent wellbeing. While the topic is important and well motivated, the question as written is too broad to guide the study design or to be answered by the analyses presented. "Social media use" covers a wide range of platforms, behaviors, and intensities, and "wellbeing" is never defined. The authors measure depressive symptoms and sleep duration in the methods section, yet the introduction suggests a broader construct, and the reader is left to guess which outcomes the study is meant to address. The authors should specify the exposure, the outcome, and the population in a single sentence, and explain how the chosen measures map onto that sentence. It would also help to clarify whether the aim is to establish a causal effect or to describe an association, since the cross-sectional design and the language of "effects" in the discussion point in different directions. A sharper question would make the hypotheses easier to evaluate and would allow readers to judge whether the conclusions follow from the evidence.
