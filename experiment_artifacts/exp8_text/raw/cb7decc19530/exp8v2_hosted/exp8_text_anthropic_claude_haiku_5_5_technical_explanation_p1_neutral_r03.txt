Latency, the delay between a user's action and the system's response, matters in web applications for several reasons:

**User experience:** People expect pages and interactions to feel instant. Even small delays make an app feel sluggish, and users quickly lose patience with slow responses.

**Engagement and conversions:** Slow sites lead to higher abandonment rates. Studies have repeatedly linked added delays to fewer page views, lower conversion rates, and reduced revenue.

**Search rankings:** Search engines like Google factor page speed and responsiveness (e.g., Core Web Vitals) into ranking, so high latency can reduce organic traffic.

**Perception of quality:** Users often judge a product's reliability and professionalism by how responsive it feels. Lag can make an application seem buggy or untrustworthy.

**Scalability and cost:** Latency compounds across multiple service calls, database queries, and network hops. Reducing it can lower server load and infrastructure costs while handling more users efficiently.

In short, latency directly shapes how users feel about an application, how much they use it, and how well it performs as a business.
