Subject: Understanding your end-of-year tax statement

Your end-of-year tax statement summarizes the income you received and the taxes withheld or paid on it during the past year. Depending on your situation, you may receive a form such as a W-2 from an employer, a 1099 from a bank, brokerage, or other payer, or a year-end summary from a retirement plan. You will need this information when you prepare your tax return, so it is worth reviewing as soon as it arrives.

When you open the statement, start by confirming that your name, address, and taxpayer identification number are correct. Then look at the income amounts and the withholding or payment totals. Compare them with your own records, such as final pay stubs or account statements. If a figure looks wrong or a document seems to be missing, contact the issuer promptly, since they can usually send a corrected form.

Keep the statement with your other tax records for at least three years, in case you need to refer to it later. If you plan to work with a tax professional, send them a copy along with any other documents covering deductions, credits, or payments you made during the year.
