Your end-of-year tax statement summarizes the income you earned and the taxes withheld or paid over the past year. It is typically issued by your employer, bank, or other payers, and you will need it when filing your tax return.

The statement usually begins with your total earnings for the year. Below that, you will see amounts withheld for federal and state income taxes, as well as contributions toward programs such as Social Security and Medicare. These withheld amounts count as payments you have already made toward your tax bill. If the total withheld is more than what you owe, you may receive a refund. If it is less, you may need to pay the difference when you file.

Some statements also list deductions or credits, such as retirement plan contributions or health insurance premiums, which can affect how much tax you owe. Please compare the figures on the statement with your own records, and contact the issuer if anything appears incorrect.

Keep this document with your other tax records for at least three years in case you need it later. If you have questions about how to read your statement or how it fits into your return, a tax professional or the tax authority's website can provide further guidance.
