Pick one afternoon, usually Sunday, and spend about an hour preparing the building blocks of your lunches instead of cooking full meals. Cook a big batch of a grain such as rice or quinoa, roast a tray of vegetables, and prepare one protein like grilled chicken, boiled eggs, or a pot of beans. Wash and chop some raw vegetables too, and mix up a jar of dressing or sauce. Store everything in separate containers in the fridge.

Each morning or evening, you can then assemble a lunch in a couple of minutes by combining a grain, a protein, some vegetables, and a sauce. Changing the sauce or adding a different topping, such as feta, nuts, or pickled onions, keeps the meals from feeling repetitive. Since most cooked foods stay fresh for about four days, consider freezing a portion for the end of the week, or plan a simple no-cook lunch like sandwiches or wraps for Thursday and Friday.
