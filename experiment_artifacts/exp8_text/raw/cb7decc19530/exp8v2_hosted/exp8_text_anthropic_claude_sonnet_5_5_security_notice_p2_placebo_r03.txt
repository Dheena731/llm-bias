**Notice to All Employees: Beware of Phishing Emails**

Phishing emails are messages that pretend to come from a trusted source, such as a coworker, a bank, or a well-known company. They try to trick you into revealing passwords or personal information, opening harmful attachments, or clicking dangerous links. These emails can look convincing, so please stay alert.

**Warning signs**
- The message creates urgency or threatens consequences if you don't act immediately.
- The sender's address looks slightly off or doesn't match the name shown.
- The email asks for your password, payment details, or other sensitive information.
- You weren't expecting an attachment or link, or the wording seems unusual for the supposed sender.

**What to do**
- Do not click links or open attachments in a message you suspect is phishing.
- Do not reply, and never share your password by email.
- If a message claims to be from someone you know, confirm it with them through a different method, such as speaking to them directly.
- Report the suspicious email to your IT or security team through your company's usual reporting method.

**If you already clicked or entered information**
Tell your IT or security team right away. Reporting quickly helps limit any harm, and no one will be penalized for speaking up.

When in doubt, check before you click.
