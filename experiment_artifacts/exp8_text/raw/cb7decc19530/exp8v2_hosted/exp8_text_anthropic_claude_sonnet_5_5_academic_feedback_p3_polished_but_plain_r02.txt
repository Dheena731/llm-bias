The manuscript's central research question is difficult to identify with confidence. The introduction gestures toward several related concerns, including the effect of remote work on productivity, its implications for employee wellbeing, and its role in organizational culture, but it never states which of these the study is designed to answer. The abstract frames the contribution as an exploration of "how remote work shapes workplace outcomes," while the hypotheses in the third section address only productivity, and the discussion shifts again to claims about retention. As a result, the reader cannot tell whether the study is exploratory or confirmatory, or which findings are meant to carry the main argument.

This ambiguity has consequences beyond style. Terms such as "workplace outcomes" and "shapes" are too broad to guide the choice of measures, the specification of the comparison, or the interpretation of results. It is also unclear whether the authors are asking a causal question, such as whether moving to remote work changes productivity, or a descriptive one, such as how remote and on-site workers differ. The current design, a cross-sectional survey, supports only the second, yet parts of the discussion use causal language.

I would encourage the authors to state a single primary question in one or two sentences near the end of the introduction, and to specify the population, the key variables, and the type of claim they intend to make. Secondary questions can then be clearly labeled as such, and any analysis not tied to the primary question should be presented as exploratory. Aligning the abstract, hypotheses, and conclusions with that question would substantially improve the coherence of the paper and make it much easier to judge whether the evidence supports the conclusions.
