Thank you for sharing this draft. It shows a solid command of the field, and the effort you have put into locating a wide range of sources is evident. My comments below are meant to help you move from a competent summary toward a more analytical review.

The main concern is structure. At present the review proceeds source by source, so each paragraph tends to begin with an author's name and describe what that study found. This makes it hard to see the shape of the debate. I would encourage you to reorganize around themes or questions, such as competing explanations, methodological approaches, or points of disagreement, and to bring several sources into conversation within each paragraph. Doing so will let you show where scholars agree, where they diverge, and why.

Closely related is the need for a stronger critical voice. You report findings accurately, but you rarely evaluate them. Consider asking of each cluster of studies whether the samples are adequate, whether the methods can support the claims made, and whether conclusions generalize beyond the context studied. Even brief evaluative remarks, such as noting that several studies rely on self-reported data, would add considerable value.

The review would also benefit from a clearer argument. The introduction describes the topic but does not state what the review will conclude about the state of knowledge. By the end, the reader should understand what is established, what remains contested, and what gap your own project addresses. Right now the gap is implied rather than stated, and the final section ends with a summary instead of a justification for your research question.

On sources, the coverage of recent work is good, but several foundational claims rest on secondary citations. Where possible, return to the original studies. I would also check whether you have included work that challenges your preferred framing, since a review is more persuasive when it engages with opposing evidence.

Finally, a few points of style. Some paragraphs are very long and would read better if divided at natural shifts in focus. Transitions between sections are abrupt, and a sentence linking each section to the next would help. Please also check citation formatting for consistency.

With a revision focused on thematic organization and critical evaluation, this could become a strong foundation for your project. I am happy to look at a revised outline before you redraft in full.
