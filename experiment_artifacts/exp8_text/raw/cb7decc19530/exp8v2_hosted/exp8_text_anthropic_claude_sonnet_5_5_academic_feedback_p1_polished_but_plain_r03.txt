Thank you for sharing your draft literature review. It shows a solid command of the field, and your prose is generally clear and well paced. The sections on early theoretical models and on recent empirical work are particularly strong, and you handle the transition between them with care. What follows are the main areas where I think revision would most improve the piece.

The most significant issue is that the review tends toward summary rather than synthesis. Many paragraphs move through studies one at a time, describing each author's aims and findings in turn. This gives the reader a reliable inventory but not an argument. I would encourage you to organize paragraphs around questions, tensions, or themes instead, so that individual studies appear as evidence within a larger line of reasoning. For example, where you discuss the disagreement over how context shapes the results, you might open with the disagreement itself and then show how different studies align on each side, rather than introducing the studies sequentially and leaving the reader to infer the conflict.

Related to this, your evaluative voice is fairly muted. You report methodological choices accurately, but you rarely comment on how they limit or strengthen a study's conclusions. Noting, for instance, that several of the most cited findings rest on small, homogeneous samples would help justify the gap your own project addresses. Right now the rationale for your research question emerges only in the final paragraph, and it would be more persuasive if the earlier sections quietly built toward it.

On scope, the review leans heavily on work from the last decade while giving little attention to foundational sources outside your core subfield. A few well-chosen references to adjacent literatures could strengthen your framing and show that you are aware of competing explanations. Be careful, though, not to widen the review so far that it loses focus.

Finally, some smaller matters. Several sentences rely on phrases such as "a lot of research suggests" or "it is widely accepted," which would be stronger with specific citations. Your citation formatting is inconsistent in places, particularly with multiple-author works, so a careful pass against your style guide is worthwhile. A concise concluding paragraph that states plainly what the literature establishes, where it remains unsettled, and how your study fits would also give the section a firmer close.

With these revisions, the review should move from a competent overview to a focused argument for your project. I am happy to look at a second draft.
