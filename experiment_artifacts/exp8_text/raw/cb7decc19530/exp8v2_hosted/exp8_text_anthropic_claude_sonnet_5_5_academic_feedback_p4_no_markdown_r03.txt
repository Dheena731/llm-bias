Feedback on Conference Poster Draft

Thank you for sharing the draft. I haven't seen the actual poster, so this note covers the points that most often improve a poster. Please adapt it to what applies.

The strongest posters communicate their main message in about ten seconds. Right now, I would make sure the title and a single key takeaway are readable from several feet away. Consider adding a one-sentence conclusion near the top or center so that passersby understand your contribution before reading any details.

On layout, aim for a clear visual flow, typically left to right and top to bottom, with generous white space. If the sections feel crowded, cut text rather than shrinking the font. Body text should be large enough to read from about a meter and a half away, and most of the poster should be figures and visuals rather than paragraphs.

For figures, check that each one has a descriptive title, labeled axes, legible fonts, and a consistent color scheme. Where possible, let a figure make the point on its own, with a short caption stating what the viewer should notice.

For content, the background section can usually be shorter than you think. Give the most space to your methods at a high level, your main results, and what they mean. Finish with a brief conclusion and, if relevant, next steps. Contact information or a QR code linking to the paper or supplementary materials is a helpful addition.

Finally, proofread carefully for typos, inconsistent terminology, and unexplained abbreviations, and consider practicing a thirty-second and a two-minute walkthrough so you are ready for different kinds of visitors.

If you send the specific text or a description of the layout, I would be happy to give more targeted comments.
