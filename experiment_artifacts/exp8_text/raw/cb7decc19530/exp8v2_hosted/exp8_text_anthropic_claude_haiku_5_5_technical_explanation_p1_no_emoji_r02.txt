Latency, the delay between a user's action and the system's response, matters in web applications for several reasons:

- **User experience:** Users expect pages and interactions to respond almost instantly. Even small delays make an application feel sluggish, and users may become frustrated or abandon tasks.
- **Engagement and conversion:** Slow responses reduce engagement. Users are more likely to leave a page before it finishes loading, which can lower sign-ups, sales, or content views.
- **Search ranking:** Search engines consider page speed and responsiveness when ranking results, so high latency can reduce visibility.
- **Perceived quality:** Delays can make a product seem less reliable or professional, affecting user trust.
- **Scalability and cost:** Lower latency often means more efficient use of server and network resources, which can reduce infrastructure costs and improve performance under heavy load.

In short, low latency keeps users engaged, supports business goals, and makes an application feel fast and dependable.
