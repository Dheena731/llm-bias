For the best roasted vegetables, season in two stages. Before roasting, toss the vegetables with enough oil to coat them lightly, along with a generous pinch of salt and some freshly ground pepper. The oil helps them brown, and early salt draws out a little moisture and builds flavor from the inside. Spread them in a single layer with room to breathe, since crowded pans steam rather than caramelize.

Then, once the vegetables come out of the oven, finish them while they are still hot. A squeeze of lemon juice, a splash of good vinegar, a scatter of chopped fresh herbs, or a light sprinkle of flaky salt will brighten the deep, sweet flavors that roasting creates. Delicate ingredients like garlic, fresh herbs, and spice blends with sugar can burn at high heat, so add them in the last ten minutes of cooking or after the pan comes out. This simple habit turns an ordinary tray of vegetables into something balanced and vivid.
