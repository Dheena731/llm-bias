The central objective shifts depending on which section one is reading. In the introduction, the paper is pitched as an empirical test of whether the 2018 Medicaid work-requirement waivers altered labor supply on the extensive versus intensive margin. By Section 3.2, however, the motivation pivots toward measuring administrative spillover—specifically, whether churning was driven by paperwork non-compliance rather than actual employment transitions. 

Because these two questions require fundamentally different identifying assumptions and sample restrictions (e.g., how the authors handle individuals cycling between SSI and Medicaid eligibility), the paper never settles on a clean target estimand. The authors need to decide whether this is an evaluation of labor-supply elasticities or an analysis of administrative burden; attempting to straddle both leaves the main identification strategy without a coherent focal point.
