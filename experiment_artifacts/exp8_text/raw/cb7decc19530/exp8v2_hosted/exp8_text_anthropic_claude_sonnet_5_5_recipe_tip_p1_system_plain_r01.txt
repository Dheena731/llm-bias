When you get home on a weeknight, start the longest-cooking part of your meal before you do anything else. If you are making rice, roasting vegetables, or boiling pasta water, get those going first, and then use the waiting time to chop, season, and prepare everything else. Even ten minutes of overlap makes a big difference, and it keeps you from standing around while one thing finishes before you can start the next.

A related habit that saves a surprising amount of effort is to cook with one pan whenever you can. A sheet pan with chicken thighs, sliced potatoes, and broccoli tossed in olive oil, salt, and pepper will roast at about 425 degrees in roughly 25 to 30 minutes, and cleanup is just one pan. Put the potatoes in a few minutes before the broccoli so everything finishes together, and squeeze a little lemon over the top at the end to brighten the flavors.
