Missing citations weaken an essay in two ways. First, they leave the reader unable to tell which ideas, figures, or quotations belong to the writer and which come from elsewhere. Second, they make the argument harder to trust, because claims that would otherwise be supported by evidence now rest only on the writer's word. Where a statistic, a direct quotation, or a specific interpretation of a source appears without attribution, the reader may reasonably wonder whether the material was borrowed without acknowledgment. Even when no misconduct is intended, the omission can read as carelessness and can undermine confidence in the rest of the work. The fix is usually straightforward: identify each place where the essay draws on outside material, add an in-text citation in the required style, and make sure the full reference appears in the bibliography or works cited list. Doing this consistently not only protects the writer from accusations of plagiarism but also shows the reader that the argument is grounded in a wider body of scholarship.
