Feedback on Draft Literature Review

Overall impression

This draft shows solid familiarity with the field and a clear effort to cover a wide range of sources. The writing is generally clear, and the opening section sets out the scope of the review effectively. At present, however, the review reads more as a series of summaries than as a critical synthesis. The suggestions below are intended to help you move toward a more analytical and cohesive argument.

Strengths

Your coverage of the foundational studies is thorough, and you have identified the key debates accurately. The section on methodological approaches is particularly strong, as it shows awareness of how research design shapes findings. Your citation practice is consistent and generally precise.

Areas for development

1. Synthesis over summary. Many paragraphs move through sources one at a time (for example, "Smith argues... Jones found... Lee suggests..."). Try organizing paragraphs around themes, tensions, or questions instead, and place the sources in conversation with one another. Point out where authors agree, where they diverge, and why.

2. Critical evaluation. The draft tends to report findings without assessing their strength. Consider commenting on sample sizes, theoretical assumptions, contextual limits, and potential bias. This will show that you are judging the evidence rather than only relaying it.

3. Identifying the gap. The conclusion hints at a gap in the literature but does not state it explicitly. Make clear what remains unresolved or understudied, and show how your project responds to that need. This link between the review and your research question should be visible throughout, not only at the end.

4. Structure and signposting. The current sections are somewhat uneven in length, and the transitions between them are abrupt. A short introductory roadmap and brief linking sentences would help readers follow the logic of your argument.

5. Currency of sources. Most of the sources predate 2015. Please check whether more recent work has altered or challenged the earlier positions, and incorporate it where relevant.

Minor points

Watch for occasional overuse of direct quotation; paraphrasing with analytical commentary is usually more effective. Also check for a few inconsistencies in reference formatting.

Next steps

For your revision, I suggest first drafting a brief outline of the three or four main themes, then reorganizing the existing material under them. Add evaluative commentary as you go, and finish with a clearly stated gap. I would be glad to look at the outline before you begin full revisions.
