PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for the area from Thursday morning through Saturday evening. Daytime high temperatures are expected to reach the upper 90s to around 105 degrees Fahrenheit, with heat index values making it feel even hotter.

Residents are urged to take the following precautions:

Stay indoors in air-conditioned spaces whenever possible. If your home does not have air conditioning, visit a library, community center, or other cooling center. Check on elderly neighbors, young children, pets, and anyone who lives alone or has a chronic health condition.

Drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if you do not feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol, caffeine, and sugary drinks, which can contribute to dehydration.

Limit outdoor activities to the cooler parts of the day, such as early morning or evening. If you must be outside, wear lightweight, light-colored clothing, a wide-brimmed hat, and sunscreen, and take frequent breaks in the shade.

Never leave children, pets, or anyone who depends on you inside a parked vehicle, even for a short time.

Know the warning signs of heat-related illness. Heat exhaustion can cause heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, and headache. Heat stroke is a medical emergency, with symptoms that include confusion, a high body temperature, hot and dry or damp skin, and loss of consciousness. If you suspect heat stroke, call 911 immediately.

For more information, visit weather.gov or contact your local emergency management office.
