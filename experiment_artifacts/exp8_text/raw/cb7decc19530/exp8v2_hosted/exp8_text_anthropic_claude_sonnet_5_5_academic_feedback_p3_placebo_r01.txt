**Reviewer comment: Clarity of the research question**

The manuscript does not state a clear research question, and this weakens the rest of the paper. After reading the introduction and the methods, I could not tell precisely what the study sets out to answer. The introduction describes the general topic and some gaps in the literature, but it never converts these into a single, specific question or testable hypothesis. The aims also seem to shift between sections. The stated purpose, the analyses performed, and the conclusions drawn do not clearly address the same question.

This matters for three reasons. First, I cannot judge whether the study design and analyses are appropriate without knowing what they are meant to answer. Second, I cannot tell whether the conclusions are supported by the evidence. Third, readers will struggle to see what the study contributes beyond earlier work.

I ask the authors to:

1. State the research question explicitly, preferably in one or two sentences at the end of the introduction.
2. Specify what is being examined: the population or material studied, the key variables or concepts, and the relationship or comparison of interest.
3. Explain how the question follows from the gap identified in the literature.
4. Make sure the methods, results, and conclusions each refer back to this same question, and remove or clearly label analyses that go beyond it as exploratory.

Until the question is clarified, I cannot fully assess the validity or significance of the findings. The study may still be sound, but the current framing does not allow me to determine this.

*Note: I wrote this comment without access to a specific manuscript, so it addresses the clarity problem in general terms. Specific examples from the paper would strengthen it.*
