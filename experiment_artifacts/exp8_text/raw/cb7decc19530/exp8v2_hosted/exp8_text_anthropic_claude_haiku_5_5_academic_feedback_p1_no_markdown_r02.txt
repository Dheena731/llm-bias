Title: Remote Work and Employee Wellbeing: A Review of the Literature
Date: [Date]

Thank you for your draft. You have assembled a relevant body of work, and the chronological opening gives the reader a clear sense of how research on remote work has developed since the early 2000s. The section on job autonomy as a mediating factor is your strongest, because you connect several studies to a single analytical question rather than summarising each in turn.

The main issue is that the review is currently descriptive. Most paragraphs follow the pattern "Smith (2019) found X; Jones (2021) found Y," with little comparison of method, sample, or context. Please move from reporting findings to evaluating them. Where studies disagree, explain why, for example by considering differences in sample composition, whether wellbeing was measured through self-report or validated scales, or whether the research was conducted before or after the pandemic.

The argument also needs a clearer thread. The introduction promises an examination of how organisational policy shapes the wellbeing effects of remote work, but this question is largely absent from the body. I recommend either restructuring the review around policy or revising the introduction to reflect the broader scope you have actually covered.

Several citations appear without page numbers or are formatted inconsistently. Please check the referencing style against the departmental guide before resubmission.

Finally, the conclusion restates the findings but does not identify the gaps they reveal. A concise statement of what remains unknown, and how your planned study addresses it, would give the review a stronger purpose.

Overall, this is a solid foundation. Focusing on critical comparison and a unified argument should bring it to a strong standard. I am happy to discuss any of these points in a supervision meeting.
