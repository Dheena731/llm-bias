**HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT FOR DUNMORE COUNTY**
*Issued 7:40 a.m., Monday, July 14 · Department of Public Health*

Heat index values of 104 to 109°F are forecast each afternoon from Tuesday through Thursday, with overnight lows staying near 81°F. Nights will not cool off enough to give bodies a break, so the danger builds over several days.

**What to do**

- **Move activity to early morning.** Do yard work, errands, and dog walks before 10 a.m. or after 8 p.m.
- **Check on people who live alone,** especially adults over 65 and anyone on blood pressure, heart, or diuretic medication. Many heat deaths happen indoors in apartments without air conditioning.
- **Never leave a child, pet, or adult in a parked car.** Inside temperatures can pass 120°F in less than half an hour.
- **Test pavement with the back of your hand** before letting dogs out. If you can't hold your hand there for seven seconds, it's too hot for paws.
- **Fans help only up to about 95°F.** Above that, use a cool shower or damp cloths on the neck and wrists.

**Cooling centers**

Open 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily:
- Eastside Branch Library, 418 Mill Road
- Harlan Recreation Center, 92 Orchard Street
- St. Brigid's Parish Hall, 1100 Front Street (free bus route 6 stops nearby)

Transportation to a cooling center is available by calling 555-0148.

**Warning signs**

Heavy sweating, cramps, dizziness, or nausea mean heat exhaustion. Move to shade, sip water, and loosen clothing. Call 911 if someone is confused, stops sweating while hot, has a seizure, or passes out. Heat stroke can be fatal without fast treatment.

Outdoor workers: employers are required to provide water, shade, and scheduled breaks during advisories. Report violations to the county labor office at 555-0177.

Utility assistance for households having trouble paying for air conditioning is available through the Dunmore Energy Relief line at 555-0190.
