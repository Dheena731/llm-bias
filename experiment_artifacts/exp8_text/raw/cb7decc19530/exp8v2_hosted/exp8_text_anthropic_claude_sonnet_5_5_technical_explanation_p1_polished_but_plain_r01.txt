Latency is the delay between a user's action and the application's response, and it shapes nearly every part of how a web application is experienced. When someone clicks a button, submits a form, or loads a page, they expect something to happen almost immediately. Research on human perception suggests that responses within about a hundred milliseconds feel instantaneous, while delays beyond a second begin to interrupt a person's train of thought. Past roughly ten seconds, most users will abandon the task altogether.

This matters first for engagement and revenue. Slow pages lead to higher bounce rates, fewer completed purchases, and lower return visits. Even small delays add up, because a single page often depends on many sequential steps: a DNS lookup, a connection handshake, a server response, and then further requests for scripts, images, and data. Each step adds its own delay, and the total is what the user feels.

Latency also affects how trustworthy an application seems. A sluggish interface can appear broken or unreliable, even when it is functioning correctly. Users may click repeatedly, refresh the page, or submit a form twice, which creates duplicate actions and extra load on the server. Fast, consistent responses, by contrast, signal quality and encourage people to rely on the product.

There is a technical dimension as well. Latency is distinct from bandwidth: a connection can carry a large amount of data per second and still feel slow if each round trip takes a long time. This is why physical distance, network routing, and the number of requests often matter more than raw speed. Techniques such as caching, content delivery networks, connection reuse, and reducing the number of blocking requests all target latency directly.

Finally, search engines and performance standards increasingly treat speed as a measure of quality, so high latency can hurt discoverability as well as usability. Treating latency as a core design concern, rather than something to fix later, helps teams build applications that feel responsive, perform well at scale, and keep users satisfied.
