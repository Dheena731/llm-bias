Rinse the dry grains in a fine-mesh strainer under cold water until the runoff is completely clear. You are stripping away the free surface starch pulverized during the milling process; if left behind, this powder gelatinizes into a gummy slurry the moment the water begins to simmer. 

Once the pot is covered, leave it alone. Stirring creates mechanical agitation that ruptures the softening grain walls, releasing internal starches into the liquid. 

When the cook time is up, cut the heat, lay a clean, folded kitchen towel across the top of the pot, and clamp the lid back down for ten minutes. The fabric traps the rising condensation that would otherwise drip back onto the surface and turn the top layer to mush, allowing the residual steam to redistribute evenly while the starch matrix sets. Fluff with a fork, not a spoon.
