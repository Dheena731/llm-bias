**Reviewer Comment**

The central research question, "How does social media affect well-being?", is too broad to guide the study design or to be answered by the methods described. "Social media" covers many platforms with very different features, and "well-being" is not defined. The manuscript does not specify whether well-being refers to life satisfaction, depressive symptoms, loneliness, or something else, nor does it state the direction or mechanism of the hypothesized effect.

I recommend that the authors:

1. Specify the platform(s) or type of use (e.g., passive scrolling versus active posting) under study.
2. Define the outcome construct and name the measure used to assess it.
3. State a directional, testable hypothesis, along with the mechanism or theory that motivates it.
4. Clarify the population and time frame to which the question applies.

With these revisions, the question would map directly onto the analysis and allow readers to judge whether the conclusions follow from the evidence.
