I don't have the draft in front of me, so this note covers the issues that most often decide whether a conference poster succeeds. If you share the text or a description of the layout, I can make the comments specific.

The most important test for a poster is whether a passerby can grasp the main point in about thirty seconds. Check that your central finding or argument is stated plainly near the top, ideally as a single sentence in the title or in a short summary box, rather than left for the viewer to piece together from the results. A descriptive title that names the topic is fine, but one that signals the conclusion will draw more people in.

Next, look at how much text you are asking people to read. Posters are usually viewed from several feet away, often while the viewer is deciding whether to stop at all, so dense paragraphs tend to be skipped. Where you can, replace blocks of prose with a short statement and a figure, and cut background material to the minimum a newcomer would need to follow your question. Methods in particular can usually be shortened to what is necessary to trust the results, with details saved for conversation.

Figures deserve the most space and the most care. Each one should have a clear takeaway that can be read from its caption or a short heading, labels and axes large enough to read at a distance, and consistent colors and fonts across the poster. If a chart requires a long explanation, it probably needs simplifying or splitting in two.

The reading path should also be obvious. Group related material, leave generous white space, and arrange sections so the eye moves naturally from the question to the approach, the evidence, and the conclusion. If viewers have to guess where to start, many will leave.

Finally, close with a short conclusion that restates the main finding and says why it matters or what comes next, and include contact information so interested readers can follow up. A short, confident ending gives you something to build on when people ask questions, which is where a poster does much of its work.
