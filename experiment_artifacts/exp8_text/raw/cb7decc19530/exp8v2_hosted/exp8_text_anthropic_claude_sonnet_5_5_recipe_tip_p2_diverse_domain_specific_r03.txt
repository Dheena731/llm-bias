**Treat soft herbs like cut flowers, and hardy herbs like linens.**

Cilantro, parsley, dill, and mint do best standing upright in a jar with an inch of water. Trim the stem ends, pull off any yellowing leaves, and set a loose bag over the top. Keep the jar in the fridge door. Basil is the exception: it blackens below about 50°F, so leave it on the counter in water, out of direct sun.

Rosemary, thyme, sage, and oregano want the opposite: dry air with a little humidity buffer. Wrap them in a barely damp paper towel, slip them into a zip bag with the air pressed out, and put them in the crisper. If you see condensation beading inside the bag, the towel is too wet.

Check the jar water every couple of days. Cloudy water means bacteria, and the stems will go slimy fast. Change it and re-trim the ends.

Don't wash herbs until you're ready to use them. Moisture on the leaves is what turns a bunch of chives into green sludge by Thursday.
