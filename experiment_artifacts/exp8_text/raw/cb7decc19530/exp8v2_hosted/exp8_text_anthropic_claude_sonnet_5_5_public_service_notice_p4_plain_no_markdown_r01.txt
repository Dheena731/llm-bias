Public Notice: Heat Advisory

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for our area from noon Thursday until 9 p.m. Saturday. Daytime high temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees, and high humidity will make it feel as hot as 110 degrees. Overnight lows will remain in the upper 70s, offering little relief.

Extended exposure to this heat can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke, which can be life-threatening. Older adults, young children, people with chronic health conditions, outdoor workers, and pets are at greatest risk.

Residents are urged to drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if they do not feel thirsty, and to avoid alcohol and sugary drinks. Limit outdoor activity to the early morning or evening hours, wear lightweight and light-colored clothing, and take frequent breaks in the shade or in air conditioning. Never leave children, pets, or anyone else in a parked vehicle, even for a short time, as interior temperatures can become deadly within minutes.

Please check on neighbors, relatives, and friends who live alone or lack air conditioning. Watch for signs of heat illness, including heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, headache, confusion, and a rapid pulse. If someone shows these symptoms, move them to a cool place, offer water, and apply cool cloths to the skin. If a person has a high body temperature, stops sweating, or loses consciousness, call 911 immediately.

Cooling centers will be open at the public library, the community center, and city hall during regular business hours, with extended hours at the community center until 8 p.m. For questions or transportation assistance to a cooling center, call the city information line during business hours.

Issued by the Office of Emergency Management.
