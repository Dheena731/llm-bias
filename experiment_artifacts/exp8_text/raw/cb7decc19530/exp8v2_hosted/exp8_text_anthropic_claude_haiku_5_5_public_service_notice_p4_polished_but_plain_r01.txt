PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for the county from 11:00 a.m. Thursday through 8:00 p.m. Friday. Afternoon temperatures are expected to reach the upper 90s to near 105 degrees Fahrenheit, and heat index values could exceed 110 degrees. Overnight temperatures will remain high, offering little relief to residents and making extended heat exposure more dangerous.

Prolonged exposure to high heat can lead to heat exhaustion and heat stroke, particularly for older adults, young children, people who work or exercise outdoors, and anyone with chronic medical conditions. Residents are encouraged to limit outdoor activity during the hottest part of the day, typically between noon and 6:00 p.m., and to schedule any necessary outdoor work for early morning or evening. Drink water regularly even if you do not feel thirsty, wear lightweight and light-colored clothing, and take frequent breaks in shade or air conditioning.

Never leave children, pets, or anyone who depends on you inside a parked vehicle, as interior temperatures can rise dangerously in a matter of minutes. Check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and friends who may not have air conditioning or who may need help staying cool and hydrated.

Cooling centers will be open at the public library, the Eastside Community Center, and the county senior center during regular business hours. Residents without reliable transportation may call 211 for information about transportation to a cooling center.

If you experience dizziness, confusion, nausea, rapid pulse, or a body temperature above 103 degrees, seek medical attention immediately or call 911 in an emergency.

For updates, monitor local news outlets and official county channels for the latest information.
