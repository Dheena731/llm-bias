When you get home on a weeknight, start by putting a pot of salted water on to boil or a heavy skillet over medium-high heat before you do anything else. Nearly every quick dinner, whether pasta, seared chicken thighs, or sautéed vegetables, depends on one of those two things being ready, and they take longer to heat up than most people expect. While the pot or pan comes up to temperature, you can slice your vegetables, mince garlic, and gather your seasonings, so no step sits idle waiting on another. Keep a few dependable finishers on hand as well, such as a lemon, a hard cheese, or a jar of good mustard, because a squeeze of acid or a spoonful of something sharp at the end can make a simple meal taste considered rather than rushed. Cooking in this order, with heat first and prep second, will often shave ten or fifteen minutes off your evening and make the whole process feel calmer.
