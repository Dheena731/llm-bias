Stickiness is driven by two factors: free surface starch and excess condensation. 

First, wash the dry rice in cold water, using your fingers to agitate the grains, through at least four changes of water until the runoff is clear rather than milky. 

Second, control the steam after cooking. Once the liquid is absorbed, remove the pot from the heat, drape a clean kitchen towel across the top, and clamp the lid down over it. Let it sit undisturbed for ten minutes. The cloth absorbs the rising steam before it can condense on the lid and drip back down, which prevents the top layer from turning into a wet slurry. 

When you uncover it, use a flat paddle to cut and fold the grains rather than stirring them, which crushes swollen starches.
