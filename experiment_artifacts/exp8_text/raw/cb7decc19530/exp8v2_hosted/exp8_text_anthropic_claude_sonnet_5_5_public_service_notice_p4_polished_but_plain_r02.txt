PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT

The city's Office of Emergency Management has issued a heat advisory for all neighborhoods, effective from noon Thursday until 9 p.m. Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees Fahrenheit, and high humidity will make it feel several degrees hotter. Overnight lows will stay in the upper 70s, which gives the body little chance to recover from the day's heat.

Residents are urged to limit strenuous outdoor activity, especially between 11 a.m. and 6 p.m. Anyone who must work or exercise outside should take frequent breaks in the shade or in an air-conditioned space, wear lightweight and light-colored clothing, and drink water regularly, even if they do not feel thirsty. Alcohol and sugary drinks can worsen dehydration and are best avoided.

Please check on older adults, young children, people with chronic health conditions, and neighbors who live alone. Never leave children or pets in a parked vehicle, even for a short time, as interior temperatures can become deadly within minutes. Pets kept outdoors need constant access to shade and fresh water.

Warning signs of heat illness include heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps, and a rapid pulse. Anyone with these symptoms should move to a cooler place, rest, and sip water. If a person has a high body temperature, confusion, hot and dry skin, or loses consciousness, call 911 immediately and try to cool the person down until help arrives.

Cooling centers will be open at all public libraries and community centers from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. each day of the advisory, with extended hours at the Riverside Recreation Center, which will remain open until midnight. Transportation assistance is available by calling 311. Residents who cannot afford to run air conditioning may also ask about utility assistance through the same number.

Updates will be shared through local radio and television stations and on the city's official website. Please stay alert, look out for one another, and take the heat seriously.
