Thank you for sharing the draft of your literature review. You have assembled a promising body of sources, and the central question of how digital tutoring platforms affect self-regulated learning in first-year university students is clearly motivated. The review also shows genuine engagement with the empirical studies, and several of your summaries capture methodological details, such as sample sizes and measurement instruments, that will be valuable later.

The most pressing concern is the organization. At present the review moves through studies one at a time, so each paragraph reads as a sequence of summaries rather than an argument. I would encourage you to restructure around the themes that emerge from the literature, for example the distinction between platform design features and learner characteristics, or the conflicting findings on whether immediate feedback improves retention. Under each theme, compare and contrast the studies, noting where they agree, where they diverge, and what might account for the divergence. This will transform the review from an annotated list into a synthesis that positions your own study within an identifiable gap.

Relatedly, the introduction promises a gap that the body of the review does not fully establish. You state that few studies examine long-term effects, but several of the sources you cite already report follow-up data at one semester or beyond. Either the gap needs to be narrowed to a more precise claim, such as the absence of longitudinal studies in non-STEM disciplines, or the review should explain why the existing follow-up studies are insufficient.

At the sentence level, the prose is generally clear, though some passages rely on vague evaluative words such as "significant" and "robust" where you appear to mean statistical significance or methodological strength. Please specify which you intend. Citations should also be checked for consistency in format, and a few claims attributed to single studies would benefit from qualification, since those findings have not been replicated.

Finally, the review would benefit from a concluding section that states what the field currently knows, what remains contested, and how your proposed study addresses those open questions. With these revisions, the review should be in a strong position to support your research design.
