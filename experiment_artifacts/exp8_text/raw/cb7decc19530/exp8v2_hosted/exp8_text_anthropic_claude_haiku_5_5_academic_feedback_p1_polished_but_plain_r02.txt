Thank you for sharing your draft literature review on remote work and employee wellbeing. The review has a clear organizational logic, moving from definitions to empirical findings to the policy debate, and your coverage of recent studies is strong. The discussion of the 2020 to 2022 period is particularly useful because it places the literature in a specific historical context.

The main issue is that the review currently reads more as a sequence of summaries than as a synthesis. Many paragraphs follow the pattern of describing one study after another without indicating how the findings relate to each other. Where studies disagree, such as those reporting reduced stress alongside those reporting social isolation, the disagreement should become the focus of the discussion. Ask what explains the divergence. Is it differences in sampling, in how wellbeing was measured, or in the kind of remote arrangement studied? Answering that question would give the review an argument rather than an inventory.

Relatedly, the review would benefit from more critical attention to method. You often report correlational findings as if they showed effects of remote work on wellbeing. A sentence or two noting study design, sample limitations, or reliance on self-report would strengthen your credibility and sharpen the gap you are trying to address.

The final section should state the gap explicitly and link it to your research question. At present the reader has to infer why your study is needed. A brief paragraph that names what the literature has not yet established, and why that matters, would close the review effectively.

Finally, some sentences are long and carry several claims at once. Separating them and tightening transitions between paragraphs will make the argument easier to follow. Once the synthesis and gap statement are in place, I would be glad to look at a revised version.
