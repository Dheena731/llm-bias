Public Notice: Heat Advisory

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for our area from noon Thursday until 8 p.m. Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees, and high humidity will make it feel as hot as 110 degrees. Overnight lows will stay in the upper 70s, which offers little relief for people without air conditioning.

Extreme heat can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke, and it is especially dangerous for older adults, young children, pregnant women, people with chronic health conditions, and outdoor workers. Residents are urged to drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if they do not feel thirsty, and to avoid alcohol and sugary drinks. Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and limit strenuous activity to the early morning or evening hours. Take frequent breaks in the shade or in air conditioning.

Never leave children, pets, or vulnerable adults in a parked vehicle, even for a few minutes. Interior temperatures can rise to deadly levels very quickly. Please check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and friends who live alone.

Cooling centers will be open at the public library, the community center, and the senior center during regular business hours, with extended hours until 8 p.m. on advisory days. Transportation assistance is available by calling the city information line.

Warning signs of heat illness include heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps, and weakness. If someone has a high body temperature, hot dry skin, confusion, or loses consciousness, call 911 immediately and move the person to a cooler place until help arrives.

This notice will be updated if conditions change. Please stay informed through local news and weather alerts.
