PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY

The city health department has issued a heat advisory for all residents, in effect from noon Thursday until 9 p.m. Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach between 98 and 104 degrees Fahrenheit, and overnight lows will stay above 78 degrees, offering little relief from the heat.

Prolonged exposure to these conditions can lead to heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Older adults, young children, pregnant women, people with chronic illnesses, and those who work or exercise outdoors are at the greatest risk. Please check on neighbors, friends, and relatives who live alone.

Residents are urged to drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if they do not feel thirsty, and to avoid alcohol and sugary drinks. Stay indoors in air conditioning whenever possible, and limit strenuous outdoor activity to the early morning or late evening. Wear lightweight, loose-fitting, light-colored clothing, and use sunscreen and a hat when outside. Never leave children or pets in a parked vehicle, even for a moment, as interior temperatures can become deadly within minutes.

Watch for signs of heat illness, including heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, headache, muscle cramps, confusion, or a high body temperature with hot, dry skin. If you suspect heat stroke, call 911 immediately and move the person to a cooler place while waiting for help.

Cooling centers will be open daily from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. at the main public library, the Eastside Community Center, and the Riverside Senior Center. Free water will be available at each location. Residents who need transportation to a cooling center may call the city information line at 311.

For updates and further guidance, contact the city health department or monitor local news and weather broadcasts.

Issued by the Office of Emergency Management.
