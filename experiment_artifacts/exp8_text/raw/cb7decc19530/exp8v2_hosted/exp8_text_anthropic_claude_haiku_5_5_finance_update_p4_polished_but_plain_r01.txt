Your end-of-year tax statement summarizes the income you earned and the taxes paid on it over the past year. It is worth reviewing before you file your return, since the figures on it are what the tax authorities will also have on record. The statement usually begins with your total earnings or income for the year, followed by any amounts already withheld or paid in advance, such as income tax deducted from your pay or estimated payments you made during the year. Those withheld or prepaid amounts are credited against your final tax bill, so if they exceed what you owe, you may be due a refund, and if they fall short, you will need to pay the difference.

Please check that your name, taxpayer identification number, and address are correct, because errors there can delay processing. Compare the income figures against your own records, such as pay stubs, bank statements, or invoices. If anything does not match, contact the issuer of the statement promptly so they can issue a corrected version before you file.

Keep a copy of the statement with your other tax records, generally for at least three to seven years, depending on local requirements.
