**The Secret to Silky Rice: A Pro Tip from a Japanese Chef**

As a chef who's spent years perfecting the art of Japanese cooking, I've learned that the key to making rice that's never sticky lies in mastering the ratio of water to rice. But, there's a lesser-known technique that takes it to the next level.

**The Trick: "Aerating" Your Rice**

When cooking rice, don't overmix the grains as they cook. Instead, use a gentle "stir-frying" motion with a wooden spatula to separate the grains just as they start to set. This simple trick helps to prevent the formation of clumps and sticky patches.

**Why It Works:**

As the rice cooks, starches from the grains start to break down and release excess moisture. If the grains are left to sit undisturbed, this excess moisture can condense, causing the rice to become sticky and clumpy. By aerating the rice with a gentle stir-frying motion, you're helping to distribute this excess moisture evenly throughout the grains, resulting in a silky, separate texture that's perfect for serving with your favorite Japanese dishes.

**Try It Tonight:**

Next time you cook rice, remember to stir-fry the grains gently as they start to set. You'll be amazed at how this simple trick can transform your rice from sticky and clumpy to light, fluffy, and divine.
