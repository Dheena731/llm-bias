Latency in web applications is a critical concern because it directly impacts the user experience and the overall performance of the application. In essence, latency refers to the delay between the time a user interacts with a web page or an application and the time the system responds. This delay can be caused by a variety of factors, including network congestion, server processing time, and the complexity of the application itself.

When latency is high, users are likely to experience frustration, as the application may appear to be unresponsive or slow. This can lead to a negative user experience, resulting in users abandoning the application or reducing their usage. Furthermore, high latency can also have a significant impact on business outcomes, as users who are unable to access or use the application may not be able to complete tasks or make informed decisions.

In web applications, latency can be particularly problematic because users are accustomed to fast and immediate responses. Even small delays can be perceived as slow or unresponsive, and users may not be willing to tolerate significant latency. Therefore, minimizing latency is a key consideration in the design and development of web applications, and it is essential to identify and mitigate the factors that contribute to high latency in order to provide a responsive and user-friendly experience.
