Trim the stem ends on a bias and set the bunch upright in a jar with half an inch of cool water. Loosely drape a plastic produce bag over the crown—not sealed—to stabilize humidity while letting excess moisture escape. Park it in the crisper drawer, far from apples, bananas, or avocados that off-gas ethylene and trigger premature browning. Refresh the water and re-cut the stems every three days; the angled cuts reopen the vascular tissue so uptake doesn’t stall. Leave basil out of the cold entirely. It behaves like a tropical cutting, not a leafy green, and will drop foliage if it meets refrigerator temperatures.
