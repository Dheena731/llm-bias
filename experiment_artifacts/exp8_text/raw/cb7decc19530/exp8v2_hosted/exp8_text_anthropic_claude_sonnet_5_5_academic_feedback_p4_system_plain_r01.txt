Thanks for sharing the draft of your poster. The project is clearly interesting, and the overall structure follows a logical path from question to method to findings. Here are my main thoughts.

The biggest opportunity is to sharpen the central message. Right now a reader walking past has to work to figure out what you found and why it matters. Consider putting a single-sentence takeaway near the top, in larger type than anything else except the title, so that someone can grasp your contribution in about ten seconds. Everything else on the poster can then serve as support for that sentence.

The text is currently dense, especially in the background and methods sections. Most viewers will not read long paragraphs while standing in a crowded hall, so I would cut these down to a few short sentences each and save the detail for conversation. If a point can be shown in a figure instead of described in words, that is usually the better choice.

Your figures are the strongest part of the draft, but a few need attention. Some axis labels and legends are too small to read from a meter or two away, and the color choices in the second graph may be hard to distinguish for viewers with color vision differences. Adding a one-line caption that states what each figure shows would also help people interpret the results quickly.

On layout, the reading order is slightly unclear because the middle columns compete for attention. Clearer spacing, consistent alignment, and a few numbered or clearly separated sections would guide the eye from left to right. There is also a lot of empty space in the lower corner that you could use to give the main result more room.

Finally, a short conclusion and a contact line or QR code for your paper or data would round things off well.

Overall this is a solid foundation, and with some trimming and a clearer focal point it should work very well. I would be happy to look at another version.
