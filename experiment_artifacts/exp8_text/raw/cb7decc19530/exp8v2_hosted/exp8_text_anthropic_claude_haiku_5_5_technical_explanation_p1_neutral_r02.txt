Latency, the delay between a user's action and the system's response, matters in web applications for several reasons:

**User experience:** People expect responses to feel instant. Delays of even a few hundred milliseconds make an interface feel sluggish, and longer waits cause frustration and abandonment.

**Conversion and revenue:** Latency directly affects business outcomes. Studies from companies like Amazon and Google have found that slower pages lead to fewer clicks, lower sales, and reduced ad engagement.

**Search ranking:** Google uses page performance signals, including metrics like Largest Contentful Paint and Interaction to Next Paint, as part of its ranking criteria. Slow sites can rank lower.

**Engagement and retention:** Users who encounter slow experiences are less likely to return. Responsiveness builds trust and habit.

**Scalability and cost:** High latency ties up server resources longer per request, which can increase infrastructure costs and reduce the number of users a system can serve concurrently.

**Accessibility:** Users on slow networks, older devices, or distant geographies are disproportionately affected by latency, so reducing it broadens who can use an application effectively.

In short, latency shapes how users perceive quality, how businesses perform, and how efficiently systems run, making it a core concern in web application design.
