Thank you for sharing your draft literature review. It shows a solid command of the field and a clear effort to engage with a wide range of sources. Below are my main observations, followed by suggestions for revision.

The strongest aspect of the draft is its coverage. You have drawn on both foundational studies and more recent work, and your summaries of individual articles are generally accurate and clear. The opening paragraphs also do a good job of establishing why the topic matters, which gives the reader a sense of purpose early on.

The main weakness is that the review is organized largely as a sequence of summaries. Each paragraph tends to begin with an author's name and describe what that study found, which makes the section read more like an annotated bibliography than a synthesis. A literature review should group sources by theme, method, or point of disagreement, and should show how they relate to one another. For example, the paragraphs on motivation and on institutional context seem to address overlapping questions, yet you do not compare their conclusions or explain why they differ. I would encourage you to reorganize the section around three or four central themes and to use each paragraph to make an argument about what the literature collectively shows.

Related to this, the draft is more descriptive than critical. You rarely comment on the limitations of the studies you cite, such as small samples, narrow geographic focus, or reliance on self-reported data. Pointing out these limits, and noting where findings conflict, would strengthen your case and help you identify the gap your own research addresses. At present that gap is implied rather than stated. A concluding paragraph that explicitly names what remains unknown, and how your project responds to it, would give the review a stronger sense of direction.

On style, your prose is clear, but some sentences are long and carry several claims at once. Breaking these up would improve readability. Watch also for repeated use of phrases such as "it is important to note," which add length without adding meaning. Check that your citation format is consistent, as I noticed a few variations between in-text references and the reference list.

In sum, this is a promising draft with good groundwork in place. Focus your revision on thematic organization, critical evaluation, and a clearly articulated research gap. I would be glad to look at a second version once you have made these changes.
