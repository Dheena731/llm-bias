To keep rice from turning sticky, rinse it before you cook it. Put the dry grains in a bowl or fine mesh strainer and run cold water over them, swirling with your hand, until the water goes from cloudy to mostly clear. This washes away the surface starch that makes the grains cling together. Then use a measured amount of water, usually about one and a half cups for each cup of long grain white rice, bring it to a boil, cover, and turn the heat to the lowest setting. Let it simmer undisturbed for about fifteen minutes, then take it off the heat and leave the lid on for another five to ten minutes. Finally, fluff the rice gently with a fork rather than stirring it, since stirring breaks the grains and releases more starch.
