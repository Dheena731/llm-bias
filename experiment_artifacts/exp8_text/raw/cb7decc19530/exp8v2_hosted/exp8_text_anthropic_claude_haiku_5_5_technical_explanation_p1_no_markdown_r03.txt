Latency matters in web applications because it directly shapes how users experience a product. When a page takes too long to respond or a button press feels sluggish, people perceive the app as slow, broken, or unreliable, and they are more likely to abandon it. Even delays of a fraction of a second can reduce engagement, conversions, and satisfaction, which is why many businesses track response times closely.

Latency also affects search rankings and discoverability, since search engines factor page speed and responsiveness into their evaluations. Beyond user perception, high latency compounds across multiple requests. A page that makes several sequential calls to a server can feel dramatically slower than its individual calls suggest, and this cascading effect makes performance problems harder to diagnose and fix.

Finally, latency influences system design and cost. Developers often add caching, content delivery networks, and asynchronous processing to reduce delays, and these choices carry tradeoffs in complexity and infrastructure spending. Understanding latency helps teams decide where optimization effort will have the greatest impact on the user experience.
