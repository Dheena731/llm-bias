Latency, the delay between a user's action and the application's response, matters in web applications for several reasons:

- **User experience:** Users expect pages and interactions to respond quickly. Noticeable delays make an app feel sluggish and frustrating, and users are more likely to abandon slow experiences.
- **Engagement and conversion:** Even small increases in load time can reduce page views, sign-ups, and purchases. For e-commerce and other revenue-driven sites, latency has a direct effect on the bottom line.
- **Search ranking:** Search engines such as Google factor page performance, including loading speed and responsiveness, into rankings, so high latency can reduce visibility.
- **Perceived quality:** Fast responses signal reliability and professionalism, while slow ones can erode trust in the product or brand.
- **Accessibility and reach:** Users on slow networks, older devices, or mobile connections are affected most by latency. Keeping it low makes an application usable for a wider audience.
- **Real-time functionality:** In applications like video calls, multiplayer games, collaborative editing, and live dashboards, latency directly determines whether the experience works at all.

Reducing latency through techniques such as caching, content delivery networks, efficient queries, and optimized assets is therefore a core part of building a web application that people want to use.
