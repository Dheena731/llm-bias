Latency refers to the time it takes for data to travel between a user and a server, essentially measuring the delay between an action and a response. In web applications, low latency is critical because modern users expect seamless and instant interactions. Even a fraction of a second of delay can make an application feel sluggish, leading to user frustration and lost attention. 

This friction directly harms business performance. Slower response times correlate with higher bounce rates, lower user engagement, and reduced conversion rates, as users quickly abandon applications that fail to keep up with them. Additionally, major search engines factor page speed into their search ranking algorithms, meaning that higher latency can reduce an application's visibility and organic traffic. 

From an infrastructure perspective, high latency strains computing resources. When requests take longer to complete, connections remain open longer, consuming memory and processing power. This makes the entire system more vulnerable to bottlenecks, timeouts, and crashes during traffic spikes. Minimizing latency is therefore essential for maintaining user satisfaction, driving revenue, and ensuring overall technical stability.
