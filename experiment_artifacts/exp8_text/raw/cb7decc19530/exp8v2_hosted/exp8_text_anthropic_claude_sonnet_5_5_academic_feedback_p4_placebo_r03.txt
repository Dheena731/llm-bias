**Feedback on your poster draft**

**Main point:** A viewer should be able to understand your central finding within about 10 seconds from a few feet away. Make sure the title or a one-sentence takeaway states it plainly, and that everything else on the poster supports it.

**What to check:**

1. **Takeaway first.** Put your conclusion where the eye lands first, not at the bottom. Readers shouldn't have to dig through methods to find it.
2. **Cut text.** If a section reads like a paragraph from a paper, shorten it to a few bullets or a figure. Aim for a poster that can be read in a few minutes.
3. **Let figures carry the story.** Each figure needs a clear title stating what it shows, labeled axes, and text large enough to read from about a meter away.
4. **Clear reading order.** Use numbered sections or a left-to-right flow so viewers know where to start and where to go next.
5. **Define terms.** Spell out abbreviations on first use, since visitors may come from neighboring fields.
6. **Visual consistency.** Use a limited set of colors and no more than two fonts, with enough contrast and white space to keep the layout uncluttered.
7. **Contact and credit.** Include your name, affiliation, and a way to follow up, such as an email address or QR code, along with acknowledgments and funding.
