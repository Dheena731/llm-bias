Thank you for sharing the draft of your conference poster. The overall structure is easy to follow, and the central question of the study comes through clearly in the title and opening panel. A reader walking past should be able to grasp the main finding within a few seconds, so I would consider tightening the title to name the population or method directly and moving the key result into a more prominent position near the top of the layout.

The text is currently dense for a poster viewed at standing distance. Most viewers will spend only a minute or two, so each section should carry no more than three or four short sentences, with the rest moved to your verbal explanation or a handout. Where possible, replace long methods paragraphs with a simple labeled diagram or a brief numbered flow of steps, which will also help the eye move through the poster in the intended order.

The figures are the strongest element, but some axis labels and legends appear small enough that they may be hard to read from a distance. Check that every figure can be understood without the surrounding text, that font sizes are consistent, and that colors remain distinguishable for readers with color vision deficiencies. A single clear takeaway caption beneath each figure would help considerably.

Finally, the conclusion section should state what the findings mean and what comes next, rather than restating the results. Adding a clear contact line and a QR code linking to your full paper or data would give interested visitors a way to continue the conversation after they leave. With these adjustments, the poster should communicate your work effectively to a busy audience.
