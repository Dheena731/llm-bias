The manuscript's central research question, as stated in the introduction, is whether community engagement improves health outcomes in rural areas. While the question is important, it is too broad to guide the study design or to be answered convincingly by the data presented. "Community engagement" is used to describe several distinct activities, including town hall participation, volunteer recruitment, and partnership with local clinics, and the authors do not specify which of these they mean or how they were measured. Likewise, "health outcomes" is left undefined, leaving unclear whether the authors refer to clinical indicators, self-reported wellbeing, service utilization, or mortality. The phrase "improves" also implies a causal and directional claim that the observational design described in the methods cannot establish without further justification. I would encourage the authors to narrow the question to a specific form of engagement, a defined outcome, and a population, and to state the expected direction and mechanism of effect. A sharper formulation would make the hypotheses testable, clarify which analyses are necessary, and allow readers to judge whether the conclusions follow from the evidence.
