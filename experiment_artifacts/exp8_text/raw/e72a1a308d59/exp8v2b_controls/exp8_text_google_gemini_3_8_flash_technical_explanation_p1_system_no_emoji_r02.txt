Latency refers to the time it takes for data to travel between a user's device and a web server. In web applications, minimizing latency is critical for several key reasons:

* **User Experience and Retention:** Modern users expect near-instant interactions. Delays as small as 100 milliseconds can make an application feel sluggish and unresponsive. If pages take too long to load or actions lag, users are likely to abandon the site.
* **Business Revenue:** High latency directly correlates with lost revenue. In e-commerce and SaaS, slow response times lead to lower conversion rates, higher cart abandonment, and decreased overall user engagement.
* **Search Engine Optimization (SEO):** Major search engines like Google include performance metrics, such as Core Web Vitals, in their ranking algorithms. High latency hurts these scores, resulting in lower search visibility and less organic traffic.
* **Competitive Advantage:** In crowded markets, performance is a primary differentiator. Fast, low-latency applications provide a smoother experience that builds trust and keeps users from switching to competitors.

Ultimately, low latency is not merely a technical metric—it is a core business requirement that determines how effectively an application attracts, retains, and monetizes users.
