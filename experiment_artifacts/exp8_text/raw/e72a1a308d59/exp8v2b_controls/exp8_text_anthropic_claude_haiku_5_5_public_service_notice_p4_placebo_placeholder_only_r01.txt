**PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT**

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for the surrounding area through the coming weekend. Afternoon temperatures are expected to reach the mid-90s to around 100 degrees Fahrenheit, with heat index values feeling even hotter. Nighttime temperatures will offer little relief.

**Who is most at risk:** Older adults, young children, people with chronic illnesses, outdoor workers, and anyone without reliable air conditioning.

**Recommended precautions:**
- Drink water regularly, even if you do not feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol and heavily sugared drinks.
- Stay indoors in air-conditioned spaces when possible. Public libraries and community centers may serve as cooling locations.
- Limit strenuous outdoor activity to the cooler morning and evening hours.
- Never leave children or pets inside a parked vehicle.
- Check on elderly neighbors, friends, and relatives at least twice a day.
- Wear lightweight, light-colored clothing and a wide-brimmed hat when outdoors.

**Warning signs of heat illness:** Heavy sweating, muscle cramps, dizziness, nausea, headache, or confusion may signal heat exhaustion. Hot, dry skin, a rapid pulse, or loss of consciousness indicates heat stroke. Move the person to a cool place, loosen their clothing, and call 911 immediately if symptoms are severe.

For the latest updates, monitor local news outlets, your local National Weather Service office, and official city or county emergency channels.
