**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

Thank you for sharing this draft. It shows a solid grasp of the field, and you have clearly read widely. The comments below are meant to help you sharpen the review for the next version.

**Strengths**
- The scope is well chosen, and the introduction explains why the topic matters.
- The sources are relevant and mostly current, and they include both foundational and recent work.
- The prose is generally clear, and the section headings help readers find their way.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Several paragraphs describe studies one after another ("Smith found… Jones found…"). Organize them around themes, debates, or methodological approaches instead, and say where authors agree, disagree, or leave questions open.

2. **Critical evaluation.** The review mostly reports findings without assessing them. Comment on sample sizes, methods, theoretical assumptions, and how far the findings generalize, especially for the studies your argument depends on.

3. **Identifying the gap.** The conclusion hints at a gap but does not state it. Say explicitly what is missing or unresolved in the literature and how your research questions address it. This is the main thing that links the review to the rest of the project.

4. **Structure and flow.** Some transitions are abrupt, and the section on [topic] seems to belong earlier. A short roadmap in the introduction and a linking sentence at the end of each section would improve coherence.

5. **Sources and referencing.** Check for over-reliance on a few authors, and consider whether any significant perspectives are missing (for example, work from other regions, disciplines, or competing theoretical traditions). Make sure citation formatting is consistent throughout.

**Suggested next steps**
- Build a short matrix of sources by theme, method, and finding to help you restructure.
- Rewrite one section as a model of thematic synthesis, then apply the approach to the rest.
- Add a closing paragraph that states the gap and your research aims.

This is a promising foundation, and with a stronger analytical focus it will become a persuasive review. I'm happy to look at a revised version.
