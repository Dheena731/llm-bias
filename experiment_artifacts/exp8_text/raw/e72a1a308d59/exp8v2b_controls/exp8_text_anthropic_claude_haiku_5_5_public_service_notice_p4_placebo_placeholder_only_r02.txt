**PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT**

Temperatures are forecast to reach dangerous levels over the next several days, and a heat advisory has been issued for this area. Daytime highs are expected to climb into the upper 90s°F, with heat index values reaching 105°F or higher. Overnight temperatures will offer little relief.

Extreme heat can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke, and it is especially hazardous for older adults, young children, people with chronic illnesses, and anyone who works or exercises outdoors.

**Precautions to take:**
- Drink water regularly, even if you do not feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol, caffeine, and sugary drinks.
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces when possible. If your home lacks air conditioning, visit a library, mall, or community cooling center.
- Limit outdoor activity to the cooler morning and evening hours, and take frequent breaks in shade.
- Never leave children, pets, or anyone who depends on you in a parked vehicle.
- Check on neighbors, relatives, and friends who may be vulnerable to heat.

**Warning signs of heat illness:** Heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, muscle cramps, headache, or confusion. If someone shows signs of heat stroke, such as a body temperature above 103°F, hot and dry or red skin, rapid pulse, or loss of consciousness, call 911 immediately and move the person to a cool place while waiting for help.

The heat advisory will remain in effect until conditions improve. Monitor local weather updates and follow guidance from your local emergency management office.
