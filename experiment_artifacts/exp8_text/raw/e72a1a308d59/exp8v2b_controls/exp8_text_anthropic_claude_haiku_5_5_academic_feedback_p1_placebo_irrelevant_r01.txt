**Feedback Note: Draft Literature Review**

**Overall impression:** The draft shows a clear grasp of the core debate on remote work and employee wellbeing, and the chronological organisation makes the field's development easy to follow. The main weakness is that the review summarises studies one by one rather than synthesising them, so the reader gets little sense of where the evidence converges, where it conflicts, or what gap the present study fills.

**Strengths**
- The introduction defines key terms (e.g., "hybrid work," "wellbeing") early and precisely.
- Sources are recent and relevant, and the mix of quantitative and qualitative studies is appropriate.
- Section 2's discussion of methodological limitations in survey-based research is perceptive.

**Priority revisions**
1. **Shift from description to synthesis.** Group studies by theme or finding (e.g., autonomy, isolation, work-family boundaries) and state what the body of evidence collectively suggests in each case.
2. **Make the gap explicit.** The concluding paragraph should state clearly what remains unresolved and how your study addresses it. At present the reader has to infer this.
3. **Address contradictory findings.** Where studies disagree (as on productivity outcomes), explain why, considering sample, context, or measurement, rather than presenting them side by side.
4. **Tighten the scope.** Section 4 drifts into general organisational behaviour theory that does not connect back to the research question. Either link it explicitly or cut it.

**Minor points**
- Several citations in Section 3 lack page numbers for direct quotations.
- Some sentences begin with "It is important to note that..." which can usually be cut without losing meaning.
- Check consistency in the use of "wellbeing" and "well-being."

**Recommendation:** Revise and resubmit. The foundation is sound, and the changes above are structural rather than requiring new reading.
