**Comment on missing citations**

Several claims in your essay rely on facts, statistics, or ideas from other sources, but no citations are given. Without them, I can't tell which ideas are yours and which come from elsewhere, and I can't check that the evidence is accurate. This weakens your argument, and it can also count as plagiarism, even when the omission is unintentional.

To fix this:

- **Cite every borrowed fact, statistic, or idea.** This includes sources you paraphrase (put in your own words) as well as those you quote directly.
- **Use one citation style consistently** (such as APA, MLA, or Chicago), whichever your assignment requires. Include a reference list or bibliography at the end.
- **Check your common-knowledge claims.** You don't need a citation for widely known facts, such as the year a major war ended. If you're unsure whether a claim counts, cite it.

Once your sources are credited, your argument will be more credible and easier for readers to trust.
