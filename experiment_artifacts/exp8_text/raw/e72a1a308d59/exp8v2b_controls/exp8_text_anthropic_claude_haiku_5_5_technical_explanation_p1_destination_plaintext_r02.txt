Latency is the delay between a user's action and the application's response, and it matters a great deal in web applications. When a page takes too long to load or a button takes too long to respond, users become frustrated and are more likely to leave. Studies across the industry have repeatedly linked even small increases in load time to lower engagement, fewer page views, and reduced conversion rates. Search engines also factor page speed into rankings, so slow applications can lose visibility as well as visitors.

Beyond first impressions, latency affects how usable an application feels. Interactive features such as typing suggestions, live updates, or drag-and-drop interfaces depend on quick feedback to feel natural. Even delays of a fraction of a second can make an interface seem sluggish or unreliable. Latency also compounds: a request that waits on several slow back-end calls can stack delays until the total wait becomes unacceptable.

Reducing latency therefore improves both user satisfaction and business outcomes. Common approaches include serving content from servers closer to users, caching responses, optimizing database queries, compressing assets, and minimizing unnecessary network requests.
