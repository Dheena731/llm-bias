**Feedback on Poster Draft**

Thanks for sharing the draft. Here are my main impressions and suggestions.

**What's working**
- The overall structure follows a logical path from background to methods, results, and conclusions, which makes it easy to scan.
- Figures are the strongest way to carry a poster, so keep them central and generously sized.

**Suggestions**
1. **Lead with the takeaway.** Someone walking past should grasp your main finding within ten seconds. Consider a one-sentence conclusion near the top, in larger type than the body text.
2. **Cut the text.** Aim for roughly 250 to 400 words in total. Convert dense paragraphs to short bullets, and move fine methodological detail to a handout or to conversation.
3. **Make figures self-explanatory.** Each one should have a clear title, labeled axes with readable font sizes, and a one-line interpretation beneath it. Avoid legends that require squinting.
4. **Check readability from a distance.** Titles should be legible from about two meters away, and body text should be no smaller than 24 pt.
5. **Guide the eye.** Use consistent alignment, ample white space, and a restrained color palette. Numbered sections or arrows can make the reading order obvious.
6. **Tighten the conclusions.** Limit them to two or three points that follow directly from your results, and add one line on limitations or next steps.
7. **Add contact details.** A QR code linking to the paper, data, or your contact information makes follow-up easy.

**Final thought**
Treat the poster as a conversation starter rather than a full paper. If it draws people in and leaves them with one clear idea, it has done its job.
