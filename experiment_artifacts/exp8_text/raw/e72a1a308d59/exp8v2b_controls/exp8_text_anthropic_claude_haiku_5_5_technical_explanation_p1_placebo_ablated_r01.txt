Latency is the delay between a user's action and the application's response, such as the time between clicking a link and seeing the next page. It matters because users notice and care about delay. Pages that load slowly or respond sluggishly frustrate people, make the product feel unreliable, and make them more likely to leave before completing what they came to do. For businesses, this translates directly into lost sales, fewer sign-ups, and lower engagement. Search engines also factor page speed into rankings, so high latency can reduce the traffic a site receives.

Latency also compounds. A single page may make many requests to servers, and each one adds delay. Small delays multiplied across dozens of requests can add up to seconds of waiting. Reducing latency, through techniques such as serving content from servers closer to users, caching results, and minimizing unnecessary requests, makes an application feel faster and more dependable.
