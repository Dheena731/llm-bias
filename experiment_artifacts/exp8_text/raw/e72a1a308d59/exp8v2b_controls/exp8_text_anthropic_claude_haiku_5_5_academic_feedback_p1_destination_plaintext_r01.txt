Dear Author,

Thank you for sharing your draft literature review. You have clearly done substantial reading, and the topic is well chosen for the field. The notes below are intended to help you move from a strong collection of sources toward a review that makes an argument.

Strengths

The opening paragraphs establish the scope of the review clearly, and your selection of recent work alongside foundational studies gives the reader a sense of how the debate has developed. Your summaries of individual studies are generally accurate and concise.

Main concerns

First, the review currently reads as a sequence of summaries. Each paragraph describes one or two studies in turn, but the connections between them are rarely made explicit. Consider organizing the review around themes, methodological positions, or points of disagreement, and use each section to synthesize what the sources collectively show rather than what each one says in isolation.

Second, the gap your study addresses is mentioned but not developed. The reader should finish the review with a clear understanding of what is still unknown and why that matters. Stating this gap more directly, ideally near the end of the review, would strengthen the rationale for your own work.

Third, some claims lack support or are stated more strongly than the evidence allows. Where you describe a consensus, please cite the sources that establish it, and where the literature is divided, say so and explain the basis of the disagreement.

Minor points

Please check the consistency of in-text citation format throughout. A few paragraphs contain long quotations that could be paraphrased to keep the review focused on your own synthesis. The transition between the theoretical and empirical sections would benefit from a brief bridging sentence.

Suggested next steps

Begin by drafting a one-paragraph statement of your review's central argument. Use it to test whether each section contributes to that argument. Sections that do not can be shortened or removed.
