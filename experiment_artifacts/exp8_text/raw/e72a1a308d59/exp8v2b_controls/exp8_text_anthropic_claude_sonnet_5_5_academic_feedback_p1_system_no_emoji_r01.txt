**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

Thank you for sharing this draft. I have not seen your specific text, so the note below is a model with the kinds of comments I would typically make. Adapt the points to your own draft.

**Strengths**

The review covers a good range of sources, and the topic is clearly introduced. Your opening section establishes why the question matters, and the writing is generally clear and well organized at the paragraph level.

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Several sections describe studies one after another ("Smith (2018) found... Jones (2020) argued..."). Group sources by theme, method, or point of debate, and show how they agree, conflict, or build on each other.

2. **Critical engagement.** The draft reports findings but rarely evaluates them. Comment on methodological strengths and limitations, sample sizes, contexts, and the strength of the evidence. Where scholars disagree, indicate which position you find more persuasive and why.

3. **Identifying the gap.** The review should lead toward your research question. At present the gap is implied rather than stated. Close each major section with a brief statement of what remains unresolved, and conclude with an explicit rationale for your study.

4. **Structure and signposting.** A short roadmap near the start and clearer transitions between sections would help the reader follow your argument. Consider whether the order of sections reflects a logical progression rather than a chronological one.

5. **Source currency and balance.** Check that recent work (the last five years) is represented and that you are not relying too heavily on a small group of authors or on a single theoretical tradition. Prefer primary sources to secondary citations where possible.

6. **Citation and style.** Check for consistency in referencing format, and reduce long direct quotations in favor of paraphrase with your own interpretation.

**Next steps**

Revise with a focus on synthesis and critique first, then on structure, and finally on style and referencing. A concept map or synthesis table (sources against themes) may help you see patterns and gaps. I would be glad to look at a revised version.
