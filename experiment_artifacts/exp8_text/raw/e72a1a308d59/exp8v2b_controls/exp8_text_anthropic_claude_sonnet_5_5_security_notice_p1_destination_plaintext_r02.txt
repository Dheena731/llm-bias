SECURITY NOTICE: Stop Reusing Passwords

Summary
Using the same password on more than one account is one of the most common causes of security breaches. Please take a few minutes to review your own habits.

Why it matters
When a password is stolen from one website or service, attackers immediately try it on other accounts, including work email, VPN, and company applications. This is called credential stuffing, and it succeeds often because many people reuse passwords. One breach at an unrelated site can expose our systems.

What we ask you to do
1. Use a unique password for every account, especially your work accounts. Never use your work password on personal sites or services.
2. Make passwords long. A passphrase of four or more random words is stronger and easier to remember than a short, complex string.
3. Use the company-approved password manager to create and store passwords so you do not have to remember them all.
4. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) wherever it is available.
5. Change any password right away if you suspect it has been exposed, or if a service you use announces a breach.

What to avoid
- Adding a number or symbol to an old password (for example, Summer2024! becoming Summer2025!)
- Saving passwords in spreadsheets, sticky notes, or unprotected files
- Sharing passwords with coworkers, even people you trust

Need help?
If you think your work account may be compromised, contact the IT Help Desk immediately. Reporting early helps us contain problems quickly, and no one will be penalized for reporting in good faith.

Thank you for helping keep our company and our colleagues safe.
