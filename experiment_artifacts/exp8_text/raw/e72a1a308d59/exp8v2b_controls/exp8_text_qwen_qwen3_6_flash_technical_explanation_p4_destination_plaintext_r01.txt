Caching speeds up a website by temporarily storing copies of frequently used files, such as images, stylesheets, JavaScript, and HTML pages, in locations closer to the user. When a visitor first loads a page, the browser downloads these assets from the origin server. Caching saves them locally in the browser or on intermediary servers. On subsequent visits or when moving between pages, the browser retrieves those files instantly from local storage instead of making new network requests. This eliminates redundant data transfers, reduces server traffic, and cuts page load times dramatically, delivering a faster and smoother experience for users.
