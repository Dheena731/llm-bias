Latency, which refers to the delay between a user's action and the application's response, is a critical factor in the performance of web applications. When an application has high latency, users experience a frustrating delay, often referred to as "slowness" or "lag." This can be particularly detrimental for applications that require frequent interactions, such as real-time collaboration tools, live updates, or online gaming.

High latency can lead to a range of issues, including decreased user engagement, increased frustration, and ultimately, abandonment of the application. For example, in a real-time collaboration tool, high latency can cause users to miss each other's updates, making it difficult to work together effectively.

Furthermore, in today's competitive market, applications with low latency are often perceived as more modern and responsive, giving them a competitive advantage. This is particularly true for applications that require rapid feedback, such as online forms, search results, or live streaming.

To mitigate latency, web developers often employ techniques such as caching, content delivery networks (CDNs), and server-side rendering. These strategies help reduce the load on servers, speed up data transfer, and improve overall performance. As a result, applications with low latency are more likely to retain users, increase engagement, and ultimately drive business success.
