Latency refers to the time it takes for a user's request to be processed and a response to be received. In web applications, latency can have a significant impact on user experience and business outcomes. Here's why latency matters:

1. **User Experience**: High latency can lead to frustration and abandonment. Users expect fast and responsive interactions, and delays can lead to a poor user experience.
2. **Conversion Rates**: Even small increases in latency can lead to decreased conversion rates. A study by Google found that for every 100 milliseconds of latency, conversions can decrease by 1-2%.
3. **Revenue Impact**: In e-commerce, every second counts. A study by Walmart found that for every 100 milliseconds of latency, sales can decrease by 1%.
4. **Competitive Advantage**: With the rise of cloud-based services and mobile apps, latency has become a key differentiator. Companies that prioritize latency and performance can gain a competitive advantage over those that do not.
5. **Scalability**: As web applications grow, latency can become a bottleneck. Optimizing for low latency is essential to ensure that applications can scale with demand.

In summary, latency matters in web applications because it directly impacts user experience, conversion rates, revenue, and competitiveness.
