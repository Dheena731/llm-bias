**Security Notice: Stop Reusing Passwords**

**From:** Information Security Team
**To:** All Employees

**Why this matters**

Reusing the same password across multiple accounts is one of the most common ways attackers gain access to company systems. When a password is exposed in a breach at an outside website, attackers automatically try that same email and password combination on corporate email, VPN, and cloud services. If you have reused it, they get in.

**What we ask of you**

- **Use a unique password for every account.** Never use your work password for personal accounts such as shopping, social media, or streaming services.
- **Make passwords long.** A passphrase of four or more unrelated words is stronger and easier to remember than a short, complex string.
- **Use the company-approved password manager.** It generates and stores unique passwords so you do not have to memorize them.
- **Turn on multi-factor authentication** wherever it is available, especially for email and any system that holds customer or financial data.
- **Change a password immediately** if you suspect it has been exposed, and report it to the Security Team right away.

**What to avoid**

- Adding a number or symbol to an old password and treating it as new
- Saving passwords in spreadsheets, sticky notes, or unprotected files
- Sharing passwords with colleagues, even people you trust

**Reporting concerns**

If you receive a suspicious login alert, a password reset you did not request, or a notice that your credentials appeared in a breach, contact the IT Help Desk or email the Security Team without delay. Reporting quickly limits damage, and no one will be penalized for raising a concern in good faith.

Protecting our systems is a shared responsibility, and unique passwords are one of the simplest and most effective steps each of us can take.

Thank you for your cooperation.

*Information Security Team*
