**Subject: Feedback on your poster draft**

Hi [Name],

Thanks for sharing the draft. The main result is clear: the new sensor reduced the false alarm rate (the share of alerts that were not real events) by about 30% in field tests. Keeping that finding at the top is the right choice, since a visitor walking past has only a few seconds to decide whether to stop.

Three suggestions, in priority order:

1. **State the research question near the title.** At present, a reader has to reach the results panel to learn what problem the work addresses. One sentence under the title would fix this.
2. **Shorten the methods panel.** It runs about 300 words. Keep the three steps a reader needs to judge whether the results are valid, and link to the full protocol with a QR code.
3. **Enlarge the main figure and label the bars directly.** The axis text is small enough to be hard to read from a meter away. Direct labels will work better than the legend.

A smaller point: check that the color scheme still distinguishes the two groups in grayscale, in case the poster is printed in black and white.

The content is strong. The changes are mainly about helping viewers grasp it at a glance. I'm happy to look at a revised version.
