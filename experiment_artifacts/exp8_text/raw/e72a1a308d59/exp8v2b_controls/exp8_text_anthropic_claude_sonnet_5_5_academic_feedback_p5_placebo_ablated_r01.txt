**Comment on missing citations**

Several claims in this essay rely on facts, statistics, or other people's ideas but do not say where they come from. Examples include the statements about [specific claim] in the second paragraph and [specific claim] in the conclusion. Without citations, readers cannot check your evidence, and your argument looks less credible. It may also suggest that you are presenting someone else's work as your own, even if that was not your intent.

To fix this, add a citation whenever you:
- state a statistic, date, or specific fact that is not common knowledge,
- paraphrase another author's idea, or
- quote directly (also use quotation marks).

Use the citation style your course requires, and include a full reference list at the end. If you are unsure whether a claim needs a source, cite it anyway.

Once the sources are in place, your argument will be much easier to trust and evaluate.
