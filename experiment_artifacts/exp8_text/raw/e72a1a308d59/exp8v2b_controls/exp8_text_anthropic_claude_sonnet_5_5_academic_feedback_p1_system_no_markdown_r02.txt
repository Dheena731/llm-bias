Feedback on Draft Literature Review

Thank you for sharing this draft. Since I have not seen the text itself, I have written this note as a model of the feedback a typical literature review might receive. Please adapt it to your own draft or send me the text for specific comments.

Overall, the draft shows a solid grasp of the relevant sources and a clear effort to cover the main strands of research. The writing is generally clear, and the scope seems appropriate to your topic. The main task now is to move from summarizing individual studies to building an argument about the state of knowledge.

The most important issue is synthesis. At present, the review tends to move source by source, describing what each author found. Try organizing the discussion around themes, debates, or methodological approaches instead, and show how studies agree, conflict, or build on one another. Topic sentences that make a claim, rather than name an author, will help with this.

Critical evaluation also needs strengthening. Explain not only what researchers concluded but how convincing the evidence is, noting limitations in sample, design, context, or theoretical framing. Where you cite influential work, consider whether its conclusions still hold.

The review should lead more explicitly to your own project. By the end, the reader should see a clearly identified gap, tension, or unanswered question that your research addresses. A short concluding section that states this directly would give the review stronger purpose.

On sources, check that you rely on recent work alongside foundational texts, and that you are not leaning too heavily on a small number of authors or on secondary summaries. Make sure any major opposing perspectives are represented fairly.

Finally, attend to the details of structure and style. Clearer transitions between sections would improve flow, some paragraphs could be tightened, and citation formatting should be checked for consistency.

With a revision focused on synthesis, critique, and a sharper link to your research question, this could become a strong foundation for your project. I would be glad to look at a revised version.
