HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT

The National Weather Service has issued a Heat Advisory for our area from 11:00 AM Saturday until 8:00 PM Sunday. Daytime high temperatures are expected to reach 100 to 105 degrees, with heat index values up to 110 degrees. Overnight lows will stay in the upper 70s, offering little relief.

Hot conditions can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke, especially for children, older adults, people with chronic health conditions, and pets.

HOW TO STAY SAFE

- Drink plenty of water, even if you do not feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol and sugary drinks.
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces whenever possible.
- Limit outdoor activity to early morning or evening hours. Take frequent breaks in the shade.
- Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing and a hat.
- Never leave children, pets, or anyone else in a parked vehicle, even for a short time.
- Check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and others who may be at risk.

WARNING SIGNS

Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, cool and clammy skin. Move to a cool place, loosen clothing, and sip water.

Heat stroke: high body temperature, hot and dry skin, confusion, or loss of consciousness. This is a medical emergency. Call 911 immediately.

COOLING CENTERS

Public cooling centers will be open during the advisory at local libraries, community centers, and senior centers. Call 211 or visit your city or county website for locations and hours.

For the latest forecast and updates, visit weather.gov or tune in to local news and weather radio.
