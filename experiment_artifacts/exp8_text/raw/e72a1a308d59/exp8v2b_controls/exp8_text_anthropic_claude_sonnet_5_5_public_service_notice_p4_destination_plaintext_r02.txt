HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT

The National Weather Service has issued a Heat Advisory for our area from 11:00 a.m. Saturday until 8:00 p.m. Sunday. Daytime high temperatures are expected to reach 98 to 104 degrees, with heat index values up to 110 degrees. Overnight lows will remain in the upper 70s, offering little relief.

WHO IS AT RISK
Older adults, infants and young children, people with chronic health conditions, outdoor workers, and pets are most vulnerable to heat-related illness.

HOW TO STAY SAFE
- Drink plenty of water, even if you do not feel thirsty.
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces whenever possible.
- Limit outdoor activity to early morning or evening hours.
- Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing.
- Take frequent breaks in the shade if you must be outdoors.
- Never leave children, pets, or anyone else in a parked vehicle, even for a short time.
- Check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and others who may need help.

WARNING SIGNS OF HEAT ILLNESS
Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, cool and clammy skin. Move to a cool place, loosen clothing, and sip water.

Heat stroke: body temperature above 103 degrees, hot and red skin, confusion, or loss of consciousness. This is a medical emergency. Call 911 immediately.

COOLING CENTERS
Public cooling centers will be open during the advisory at local libraries, community centers, and senior centers. Hours and locations are available on the city website or by calling the non-emergency information line at 311.

For the latest weather updates, visit weather.gov or tune in to local radio and television stations.
