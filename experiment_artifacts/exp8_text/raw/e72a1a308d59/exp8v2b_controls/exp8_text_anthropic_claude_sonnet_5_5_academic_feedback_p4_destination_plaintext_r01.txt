Thanks for sharing your poster draft. Here is my feedback.

What works well:
The title is clear and the topic is easy to grasp. The overall layout follows a logical flow from background to methods to results to conclusions, and the main figure is a strong visual anchor.

Suggestions:
1. Lead with the takeaway. Add a one- or two-sentence main message near the top or in a highlighted box so viewers get the point in under 10 seconds.
2. Cut the text. Aim for around 300 to 500 words total. Turn long paragraphs into short phrases, and move detail to what you say aloud or to a handout.
3. Check readability. Body text should be legible from about 1.5 meters away (24 pt or larger), and figure labels and axis text should be bigger than they are now.
4. Simplify the figures. Remove extra gridlines and legends where you can, use consistent colors, and give each figure a short title that states its finding.
5. Tighten the methods. Keep only what is needed to understand the results, and consider a small diagram instead of text.
6. Strengthen the conclusions. Limit them to three or four clear points tied directly to your results, and add one line on next steps or implications.
7. Polish the layout. Align the sections, keep the white space balanced, and make sure the reading order is obvious, with numbers or arrows if needed.
8. Add contact details. A QR code linking to the paper, data, or your contact information would be helpful, along with your affiliation and funding acknowledgments.

Overall, the content is solid. With a sharper main message and less text, the poster will be much easier to take in at a glance. I'm happy to look at another version before you print.
