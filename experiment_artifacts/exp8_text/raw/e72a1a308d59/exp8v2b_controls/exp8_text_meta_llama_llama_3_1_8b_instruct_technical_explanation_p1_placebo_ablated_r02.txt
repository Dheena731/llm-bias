Latency in web applications is a critical factor that affects user experience and ultimately, the success of an online service. In simple terms, latency refers to the time it takes for data to travel from the user's device to the server and back, known as the round-trip time (RTT).

Imagine you're trying to load a webpage or make a payment online. If the latency is high, it means that your request will take longer to be processed, and the response will take longer to arrive. This can cause frustration, particularly if the delay is noticeable or prolonged. For example, a 100ms delay in page loading can lead to a 5% reduction in user engagement.

High latency can also have significant economic consequences. A study by Google found that for every 100ms of additional latency, users are 4.4% less likely to return to the site. This means that even small improvements in latency can lead to significant increases in user engagement and revenue.

Furthermore, latency is not just a concern for users, but also for businesses. A faster and more responsive website can improve conversion rates, reduce bounce rates, and increase overall satisfaction. It's essential for web applications to strive for the lowest possible latency to provide a seamless and enjoyable user experience.

In summary, latency matters in web applications because it directly affects user experience, business outcomes, and ultimately, the success of an online service. By minimizing latency, web applications can provide a faster, more responsive, and more engaging experience for users.
