**Feedback on Your Conference Poster Draft**

I haven't seen your actual draft, so this note covers the points that most often make the difference between a poster people walk past and one they stop to read. Check your draft against each one.

**What to keep**
- A clear, specific title that states the topic or finding.
- Figures and visuals that carry the main results, so viewers don't have to dig through text.

**Main suggestions**

1. **Lead with the takeaway.** A viewer should grasp your main point within ten seconds. Put a one- or two-sentence summary near the top, or make your title state the finding.
2. **Cut the text.** Aim for roughly 300 to 500 words in total. If a paragraph runs longer than four lines, convert it to bullets or a figure.
3. **Check readability from a distance.** Body text should be at least 24 pt, section headings around 36 pt, and the title large enough to read from several meters away. Use high contrast, such as dark text on a light background.
4. **Guide the eye.** Organize the layout into clear columns or blocks, in a logical order such as Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions. Use consistent spacing and alignment.
5. **Make figures self-explanatory.** Each should have a short, descriptive caption, labeled axes with legible fonts, and a limited color palette. Remove chart clutter such as heavy gridlines and unnecessary 3D effects.
6. **Sharpen the conclusions.** State what your results mean and what should happen next, in three bullets or fewer. Avoid restating the results.
7. **Add the practical details.** Include contact information or a QR code linking to the paper, data, or slides, plus acknowledgments and funding, in smaller text at the bottom.

**Final check**
Ask someone outside your subfield to look at the poster for 30 seconds and tell you what it's about. If their answer differs from your intended message, adjust the title and the top section first.
