Latency, the delay between a user's action and the application's response, matters in web applications because it directly shapes how people experience and judge a site. Even small delays are noticeable, and they add up in several ways.

First, users are impatient. Studies have repeatedly shown that pages loading slowly lead to higher abandonment rates, and each extra second of delay can noticeably reduce conversions, sign-ups, and engagement.

Second, latency affects perceived quality. A responsive interface feels reliable and polished, while a laggy one suggests something is broken or poorly built, which erodes trust.

Third, latency has business consequences. Search engines such as Google factor page speed into rankings, and slow sites lose revenue, ad impressions, and customers to faster competitors.

Finally, latency compounds in complex applications. A single user action may trigger multiple network requests, database queries, and service calls, so small delays at each step can add up to a sluggish experience.

Keeping latency low through efficient code, caching, content delivery networks, and optimized queries is therefore essential to a good user experience and to the success of the application.
