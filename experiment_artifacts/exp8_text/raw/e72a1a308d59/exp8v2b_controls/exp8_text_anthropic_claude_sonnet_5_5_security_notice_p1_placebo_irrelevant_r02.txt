**SECURITY NOTICE: Stop Reusing Passwords**

**To:** All Staff
**From:** IT Security Team
**Subject:** Password reuse puts our organization at risk

**What's happening**

When a website or service is breached, attackers take the stolen usernames and passwords and try them on other platforms, including corporate email, VPNs, and cloud applications. This is called *credential stuffing*, and it works because many people use the same password in multiple places.

**Why it matters**

If you reuse your work password on a personal account, such as a shopping site, social media, or a streaming service, a breach at that outside company can give attackers direct access to our systems.

**What we need you to do**

- **Use a unique password for every account.** Never reuse your work password anywhere else.
- **Make passwords long.** Aim for a passphrase of 14 or more characters, such as four or five unrelated words.
- **Use the company-approved password manager.** It generates and stores strong passwords so you don't have to remember them.
- **Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA)** wherever it's available, including personal accounts.
- **Change your password immediately** if you suspect it has been exposed, and let us know.

**What to avoid**

- Adding a number or symbol to an old password (e.g., "Summer2024!" becoming "Summer2025!")
- Keeping passwords in spreadsheets, sticky notes, or unprotected files
- Sharing passwords with coworkers, even people you trust

**Need help?**

Contact the IT Help Desk at helpdesk@yourcompany.com or ext. 1234 for assistance with the password manager or MFA setup. If you believe your account has been compromised, report it right away. Early reporting limits the damage.

Thank you for helping keep our organization secure.

*IT Security Team*
