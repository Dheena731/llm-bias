Thanks for sharing the draft of your conference poster. The central finding is clear and the layout is clean, with a logical flow from the research question to the results. Your color scheme works well and keeps the eye moving.

The main concern is text density. Several panels contain long paragraphs that viewers will not read while walking past. Try reducing each section to two or three key sentences and moving detailed methods into a handout or QR code linked to a full write-up.

The results figure needs a stronger takeaway. Add a one-line headline above it that states the conclusion, such as "Treatment B reduced error rates by 40%," so viewers grasp the point before studying the axes.

Finally, check that the font sizes are readable from about two meters away. Titles should be large enough to read from across the aisle, and figure labels should not be smaller than the body text.

Overall, this is a strong draft. Tightening the text and sharpening the results message should make it much more effective during the poster session.
