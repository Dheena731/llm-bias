**Feedback on Conference Poster Draft**

**Overall impression:** The poster has a clear research focus and a solid foundation. With some changes to hierarchy and concision, it will be easier to read from several feet away, which is how most viewers will first encounter it.

**Strengths**
- The topic and its relevance are clear.
- The logical flow (background, methods, results, conclusions) is easy to follow.
- The figures are relevant to the main findings.

**Suggestions**
1. **Lead with the takeaway.** Add a one-sentence main finding near the top or in a prominent conclusion box. A passerby should grasp your contribution in under 10 seconds.
2. **Cut text.** Aim for roughly 300 to 500 words total. Convert dense paragraphs into short bullets or captions, and keep only the background needed to understand your question.
3. **Strengthen the visual hierarchy.** Make the title, section headings, and key figures clearly larger than body text. Body text should be readable from about 1.5 meters away (24 pt or larger).
4. **Improve the figures.** Enlarge them, label axes legibly, and add a brief caption stating what the viewer should notice. Avoid reproducing full tables when a simple plot would do.
5. **Check layout and spacing.** Leave white space between sections, align elements to a grid, and keep the reading order obvious (for example, left to right in columns).
6. **Limit color and font choices.** Use two or three colors with strong contrast, and one or two typefaces.
7. **Conclusions and next steps.** Keep these to three or four concise points, and add brief contact information or a QR code for the paper or data.

**Next step:** Try a quick test by showing the poster to a colleague outside your subfield for 30 seconds and asking what they remember. Revise based on what they miss.
