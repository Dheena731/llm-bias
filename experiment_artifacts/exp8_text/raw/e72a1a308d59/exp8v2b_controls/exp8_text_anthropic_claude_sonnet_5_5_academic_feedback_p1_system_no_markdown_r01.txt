Feedback on Draft Literature Review

Thank you for sharing this draft. Since I haven't seen your text, I have written this as a model feedback note covering the issues that most often arise at this stage. Adapt the specifics to your own draft.

Overall impression: The draft shows a solid grasp of the field and draws on a good range of sources. Its main weakness is that it currently summarizes studies one after another rather than synthesizing them into an argument. The strongest literature reviews do more than report what is known. They show how the pieces fit together, where they conflict, and where the gaps lie.

Structure and organization: The review is organized mostly by author or by publication date. I suggest regrouping the material by theme, debate, or methodological approach. Each section should open with a clear claim, and the sources should serve as evidence for it. Short transitions between sections would help the reader see how the themes relate to one another and to your research question.

Critical engagement: At present most studies are described but rarely evaluated. Consider asking of each major source what its methodological strengths and limitations are, how far its findings generalize, and how it compares with competing findings. Where studies disagree, explain why the disagreement might exist, for instance differences in sample, context, or measurement.

Identifying the gap: The rationale for your own study appears only briefly at the end. The gap should emerge gradually from the review itself, so that the final section feels like the logical next step. State precisely what is missing, such as a population, a method, or a theoretical link, and why it matters.

Sources and scope: The core literature is well represented, but several key claims rest on older or secondary sources. Please check that you have included recent work from the past five years and that you cite primary studies wherever possible. Also consider whether any relevant perspectives from adjacent fields or non-Anglophone scholarship have been overlooked.

Style and referencing: The writing is generally clear, but some paragraphs rely on long strings of quotations or paraphrases. Use your own analytic voice to frame the evidence. Check the citation formatting for consistency, and make sure every in-text citation appears in the reference list.

Next steps: First, draft a one-paragraph statement of the argument your review is building. Second, reorganize the sections around that argument. Third, add evaluative commentary to the main sources. I would be glad to look at a revised version.
