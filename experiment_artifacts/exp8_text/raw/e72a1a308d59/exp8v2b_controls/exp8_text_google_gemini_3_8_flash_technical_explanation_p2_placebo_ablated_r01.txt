A database index is a specialized structure that significantly speeds up the retrieval of data. 

Without an index, the database must examine every single record from beginning to end to find what you are looking for. An index eliminates this need by keeping a sorted reference list that points directly to the location of the actual data. 

A familiar comparison is the index at the back of a book: instead of reading all hundreds of pages to find a specific topic, you look up the term in the alphabetical index, which tells you the exact page numbers to turn to. 

While an index makes searching and reading data much faster, it comes with a trade-off: it consumes additional storage space and slightly slows down adding or editing records, because the index must be updated whenever the underlying data changes.
