Latency is the time delay between a user’s action and the web application’s response. In modern web applications, minimizing latency is critical for several fundamental reasons:

* **User Retention and Engagement:** Modern users expect real-time feedback. Delays of even a few hundred milliseconds interrupt a user's natural flow, making an application feel unresponsive. High latency increases bounce rates, as users quickly abandon pages that do not render or respond immediately.
* **Conversion and Revenue:** Speed directly drives business outcomes. In e-commerce and software-as-a-service platforms, faster response times correlate with higher checkout completions, increased sign-ups, and greater customer satisfaction. Conversely, sluggish performance directly translates to lost transactions.
* **Search Engine Visibility:** Major search engines use performance and load speed as key criteria in their ranking algorithms. Websites with poor latency receive lower search visibility, reducing organic traffic.
* **Infrastructure and Scalability:** High latency ties up server resources, such as memory and connection pools, for longer periods during every request. By reducing response times, an application frees up capacity faster, allowing the underlying infrastructure to handle far more concurrent users without failing.
