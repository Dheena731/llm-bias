**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall impression**
This draft shows solid engagement with the field and a clear effort to cover the key sources. The main weakness is that it currently reads as a sequence of summaries rather than an argument. With some restructuring and a sharper critical focus, it could become a strong foundation for your project.

**Strengths**
- The range of sources is good, and you include both foundational and recent work.
- The writing is generally clear, and the section headings help orientation.
- You identify the major themes accurately, particularly [theme A] and [theme B].

**Areas for development**

1. **Synthesis over summary.** Many paragraphs describe one study after another ("Smith argues... Jones found..."). Organize paragraphs around ideas, debates, or methodological approaches, and bring several sources into conversation within each one. Ask what the sources agree on, where they conflict, and why.

2. **Critical evaluation.** The review is largely descriptive. Assess the strength of the evidence, the limits of the methods, and the assumptions behind each position. Even brief evaluative comments would strengthen the analysis considerably.

3. **Identifying the gap.** The conclusion does not yet make clear what is missing from the existing literature or how your research responds to it. State the gap explicitly and connect it to your research question.

4. **Structure and signposting.** Add a short introduction outlining the review's scope and organizing logic, and use topic sentences and transitions to show how sections relate. The current order of sections feels somewhat arbitrary.

5. **Scope and selection.** Briefly explain your criteria for inclusion. Some sources appear only loosely relevant, while [specific area or perspective] seems underrepresented.

6. **Citation and style.** Check for consistency in referencing format, and reduce long direct quotations in favor of paraphrase.

**Suggested next steps**
- Create a simple matrix of your sources by theme, method, and position to help reveal patterns and disagreements.
- Rewrite one section as a model of the synthesized, critical approach, then apply it to the rest.
- Draft a concluding paragraph that states the gap and leads directly into your research question.

I would be glad to look at a revised version once these changes are made.
