**Feedback on Conference Poster Draft**

Thanks for sharing this draft. The project is interesting, and there's a solid base to build on. My suggestions:

**What's working**
- The topic and research question are clear and relevant.
- The structure follows a logical flow (background → methods → results → conclusions), which makes it easy to navigate.

**Suggestions for improvement**
1. **Lead with the takeaway.** Someone passing by should grasp your main finding in about 10 seconds. Consider a one-sentence headline or a prominent "Key Finding" box near the top.
2. **Cut the text.** Aim for roughly 300-500 words total. Convert paragraphs to short bullets and trim the background and methods in particular.
3. **Make the figures carry the story.** Enlarge your key figures and make sure axes, labels, and legends are readable from about 1.5 meters. Each figure needs a brief title stating its conclusion, not just what it shows.
4. **Check visual hierarchy.** Use consistent font sizes (title > headings > body) and generous white space. Limit the palette to 2-3 colors, with strong contrast between text and background.
5. **Sharpen the conclusions.** Limit them to 3 or so concise points that tie directly to your research question. Add a line on limitations or next steps if there's room.
6. **Add contact and reference details.** Include a QR code linking to the paper, data, or your contact info, and keep citations minimal.

**Overall**
With some trimming and a stronger focus on the central result, this could be a very effective poster. Happy to look at another round once you've revised.
