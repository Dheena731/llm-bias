Latency is the time it takes for a request to travel from a user's device to a server and return with a response. In web applications, low latency is critical for three primary reasons:

**1. User Experience and Engagement**
Users expect modern web applications to respond almost instantaneously. Delays as short as 100 milliseconds can disrupt a user’s sense of seamless control. High latency leads to perceived sluggishness, frustration, and higher bounce rates, causing users to abandon the application for a faster alternative.

**2. Business Revenue and Conversions**
Speed directly correlates with business performance. Numerous industry studies demonstrate that every fraction of a second added to page load or transaction time decreases conversion rates, reduces cart sizes, and hurts customer retention. For e-commerce and SaaS platforms, latency translates directly into lost revenue.

**3. Search Visibility and Compounding Bottlenecks**
Search engines like Google factor page speed and responsiveness into search ranking algorithms; high latency negatively impacts organic traffic. Additionally, modern applications rely on microservices, third-party APIs, and databases. In these distributed systems, small latencies compound quickly, turning minor delays into severe performance bottlenecks.
