**Feedback on Your Methods Section**

Thank you for your draft. The section is organized in a logical order, and the overall design is easy to follow. Below are the strengths and the areas that need revision.

**What works well**
- The sequence of procedures matches the sequence of your research questions, which helps the reader.
- Your writing is mostly clear and uses past tense consistently.

**What to revise**

1. **Justify your choices.** You describe what you did, but not always why. For each major decision (sample, instrument, analysis technique), add a sentence explaining why it suits your research question.

2. **Add detail for replication.** Another researcher should be able to repeat your study from this section alone. Check that you have stated how participants were recruited, how many took part, what materials or software you used (with versions), and the exact steps in your procedure.

3. **Describe your analysis plan more fully.** Say which statistical tests or coding procedures you used, what thresholds you applied, and how you handled missing data or outliers. For qualitative work, explain how themes or codes were developed and checked.

4. **Address limitations and ethics.** Briefly note any methodological constraints that affect interpretation, and confirm ethical approval and informed consent where relevant.

5. **Keep results out of this section.** If any sentences report findings, move them to the Results section.

Revise with these points in mind, paying particular attention to items 1 and 3, then send it back. I'm happy to look at it again.
