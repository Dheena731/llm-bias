**Feedback on Draft Literature Review**

**Overall impression:** The draft shows a clear grasp of the core debates in the field and a sensible selection of key sources. It is currently closer to an annotated list of studies than a synthesized review, so the next revision should focus on building an argument across the sources.

**Strengths:**
- The opening clearly defines the scope and motivates the review.
- Sources are recent and mostly drawn from high-quality peer-reviewed venues.
- The section on methodological approaches is well organized and easy to follow.

**Areas for development:**
1. **Synthesis over summary.** Many paragraphs proceed study by study ("Smith (2019) found... Jones (2021) argued..."). Group studies by theme, claim, or methodological tradition, and state what the body of work collectively shows.
2. **Critical evaluation.** The review describes findings but rarely assesses them. Consider sample sizes, designs, contexts, and limitations, and note where studies conflict and why.
3. **Identifying the gap.** The concluding section should connect the review to your research question. State explicitly what remains unexamined and why your study is positioned to address it.
4. **Organization.** The transition between the second and third sections is abrupt. A brief signposting sentence at the start of each section would help the reader follow the logic.
5. **Citation consistency.** A few references are missing page numbers or use inconsistent formatting. Please check against the required style guide before submission.

**Suggested next steps:**
- Restructure Section 2 around two or three thematic claims, each supported by multiple sources.
- Add a short paragraph after each theme noting limitations or unresolved tensions.
- Revise the conclusion to state the gap in one or two precise sentences.

**Summary:** A solid foundation. With a shift from description to thematic synthesis and critical evaluation, the review will present a much stronger argument for your study.
