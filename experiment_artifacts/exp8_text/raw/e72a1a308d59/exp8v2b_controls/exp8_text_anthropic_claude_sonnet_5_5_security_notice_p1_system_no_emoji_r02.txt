**Security Notice: Password Reuse**

**To:** All Employees
**From:** Information Security Team
**Subject:** Do Not Reuse Passwords Across Accounts

**Why this matters**

Reusing a password across multiple accounts is one of the most common ways attackers gain access to company systems. When a third-party website is breached, stolen credentials are often tested against corporate email, VPN, and cloud services. If your work password matches one used elsewhere, a single breach can expose our entire network.

**What you need to do**

- Use a unique password for every work account and never reuse it on personal sites or services.
- Create passwords of at least 14 characters. A passphrase made of several unrelated words is a good option.
- Use the company-approved password manager to generate and store credentials.
- Enable multi-factor authentication wherever it is available.
- Change any password immediately if you suspect it has been exposed.

**What to avoid**

- Making small changes to an old password, such as adding a number or symbol.
- Storing passwords in spreadsheets, notes apps, or sticky notes.
- Sharing passwords with colleagues, even those you trust.

**Reporting concerns**

If you believe your credentials have been compromised, or you receive a suspicious login alert, contact the IT Help Desk right away at helpdesk@company.com or ext. 5555. Prompt reporting limits damage and helps us protect everyone.

Thank you for helping keep our organization secure.

*Information Security Team*
