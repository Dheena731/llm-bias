**Latency**—the time it takes for a request to travel from a user’s device to a server and back with a response—is a critical factor in the success of any web application. In simple terms, it dictates how fast an application feels.

Here is why latency matters:

### 1. User Experience and Perception
Human perception operates on tight timelines. Delays under 100 milliseconds feel instantaneous, but once latency exceeds 300 to 500 milliseconds, users perceive the interface as sluggish. High latency disrupts a user's flow, leading to frustration, mistakes, and a general impression that the service is unreliable.

### 2. Conversions and Revenue
Speed directly impacts the bottom line. Numerous studies from tech giants like Google and Amazon have demonstrated that even small delays (e.g., 100ms) result in measurable drops in sales, page views, and user engagement. If an e-commerce checkout or SaaS dashboard lags, users are much more likely to abandon the task or look for a faster alternative.

### 3. Bounce Rates and Retention
Users have little patience for slow-loading pages. If an application takes several seconds to respond, a large percentage of visitors will leave immediately (bounce). Consistent low latency builds trust and encourages repeated use, whereas high latency drives users away permanently.

### 4. Search Engine Optimization (SEO)
Search engines, particularly Google, incorporate performance metrics into their ranking algorithms (such as Core Web Vitals). Applications with high latency—specifically slow Time to First Byte (TTFB) or slow interactions—rank lower in organic search results, reducing overall visibility and traffic.

***

In modern software development, **speed is a core feature, not an afterthought.** Keeping latency low ensures that users stay engaged, businesses retain revenue, and applications remain competitive.
