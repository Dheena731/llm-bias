**Comment on Missing Citations**

Several claims in this essay rely on statistics, historical events, and expert opinions, but none of them are attributed to a source. For example, the statement that most students perform better with later school start times is presented as established fact, yet the reader has no way to verify it. Without citations, even accurate information can appear unsupported, and the essay's credibility suffers.

To strengthen the work, add in-text citations wherever you draw on outside research, quote another writer, or paraphrase someone else's ideas. Then include a matching reference list at the end, formatted consistently in the style your assignment requires, such as MLA, APA, or Chicago. Doing so will show that your argument rests on reliable evidence, give readers a path to explore the topic further, and ensure you receive proper credit for responsible research rather than risk concerns about plagiarism.
