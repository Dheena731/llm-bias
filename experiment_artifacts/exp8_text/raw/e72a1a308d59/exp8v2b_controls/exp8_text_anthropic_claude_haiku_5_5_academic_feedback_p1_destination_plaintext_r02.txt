Feedback on Draft Literature Review

Overall impression
The draft shows a clear grasp of the core debate in the field and a sensible selection of sources. However, the review currently reads more as a sequence of summaries than as a synthesis, and the link between the literature and your research question needs to be made much more explicit.

Strengths
The introduction sets out the scope of the review clearly, and the chronological ordering of the early studies makes the development of the field easy to follow. Your summaries of individual papers are accurate and concise.

Main areas for revision
1. Move from summary to synthesis. Many paragraphs follow the pattern "Author A found X. Author B found Y." Instead, group studies by theme, methodology, or point of disagreement, and state what the collective evidence suggests. Each section should end with a short statement of what is established, what is contested, and what remains unexplored.

2. Make the gap explicit. The research gap is implied but never stated directly. Identify the specific limitation in the existing work (for example, a population, context, or method that has been neglected) and show how your study addresses it. This should appear clearly in the final section of the review.

3. Strengthen critical evaluation. Assess the strengths and weaknesses of the key studies, including sample size, methodological rigour, and generalisability. Phrases such as "a limitation of this approach is..." show the reader that you are weighing the evidence rather than accepting it at face value.

4. Check currency. Several central sources date from before the last decade. Please confirm whether more recent work has challenged or extended these findings and include it where relevant.

5. Tighten the structure. Some subsections overlap, particularly the two discussions of theoretical frameworks. Consider merging them to reduce repetition.

Minor points
- Define key terms at first use and apply them consistently.
- Check that all in-text citations appear in the reference list and follow the required citation style.
- Some sentences are overly long; breaking them up will improve clarity.

Next steps
I recommend restructuring around three or four themes, drafting a one-paragraph summary at the start of each section, and revising the conclusion to state the research gap and your contribution. I would be glad to review a revised draft.
