An API rate limit is a restriction that controls how many requests you can send to a service within a defined time period, such as per minute or per hour. It exists primarily to protect backend servers from overload, ensure equitable access for all users, and maintain consistent performance during high traffic. When you exceed the allowed number of requests, the service will temporarily block additional calls until the time window resets, typically returning a clear error response indicating the limit was reached. Rate limits enable providers to balance resource allocation and system reliability while preventing abuse or monopolization. For developers and application integrators, understanding and planning around these limits is essential for building stable workflows, implementing appropriate request pacing, and avoiding unexpected service interruptions.
