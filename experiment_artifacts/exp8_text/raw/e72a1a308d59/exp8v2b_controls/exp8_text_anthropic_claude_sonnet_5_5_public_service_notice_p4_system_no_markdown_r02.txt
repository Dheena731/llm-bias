PUBLIC NOTICE: HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT

Issued by the City Office of Emergency Management

A Heat Advisory is in effect from 11:00 a.m. Thursday until 9:00 p.m. Saturday. Daytime temperatures are expected to reach 98 to 104 degrees Fahrenheit, with heat index values up to 110 degrees. Overnight lows will stay in the upper 70s, offering little relief.

Who is most at risk: older adults, infants and young children, people with chronic health conditions, outdoor workers, and anyone without air conditioning. Pets are also vulnerable.

What you should do:

Drink plenty of water throughout the day, even if you do not feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol and sugary drinks.

Stay indoors in air conditioning whenever possible. Limit outdoor activity to early morning or evening hours.

Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing and use sunscreen and a hat outdoors.

Never leave children, adults, or pets in a parked vehicle, even for a short time.

Check on elderly neighbors, relatives, and others who live alone.

Know the warning signs of heat illness: heavy sweating, muscle cramps, dizziness, nausea, headache, and weakness. If someone has a high body temperature, hot dry skin, confusion, or loses consciousness, call 911 immediately and move them to a cooler place.

Cooling centers will be open daily from 10:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. at the Main Library, the Eastside Community Center, and the Senior Services Building. Water and seating are available at no cost. For transportation assistance to a cooling center, call 311.

For updates, visit the city website or tune in to local radio and television broadcasts.
