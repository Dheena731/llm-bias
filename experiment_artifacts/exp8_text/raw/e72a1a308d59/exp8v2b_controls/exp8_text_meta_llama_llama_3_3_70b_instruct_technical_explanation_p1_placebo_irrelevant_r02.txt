Latency matters in web applications because it directly impacts the user experience. Latency refers to the delay between the time a user interacts with a web application (e.g., clicks a button, submits a form) and the time the application responds. High latency can lead to frustrating delays, causing users to wait for pages to load, responses to appear, or actions to be processed. This can result in a range of negative consequences, including:

* Increased bounce rates: Users may abandon a slow-loading website or application, leading to lost engagement and potential revenue.
* Decreased conversion rates: Delays can disrupt the user's workflow, causing them to lose focus or abandon a task, such as filling out a form or completing a purchase.
* Poor user satisfaction: High latency can lead to a perception of a poorly performing or unresponsive application, eroding trust and loyalty.
* Competitive disadvantage: In today's fast-paced digital landscape, users expect rapid responses and seamless interactions. Web applications with high latency can be at a competitive disadvantage, driving users to alternative, faster solutions.

By minimizing latency, web developers can create a more responsive, engaging, and user-friendly experience, ultimately driving business success and user satisfaction.
