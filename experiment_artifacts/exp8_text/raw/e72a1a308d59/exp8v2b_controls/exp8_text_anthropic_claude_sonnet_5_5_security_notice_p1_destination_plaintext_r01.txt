SUBJECT: Security Notice: Stop Reusing Passwords

Team,

We are reminding everyone that using the same password across multiple accounts is one of the most common ways attackers gain access to company systems.

WHY IT MATTERS
When a password is exposed in a breach at one website, attackers automatically try that same email and password combination on other services, including work accounts. This technique is called credential stuffing, and it succeeds more often than most people expect. One reused password can put company data, customer information, and your colleagues at risk.

WHAT WE ASK YOU TO DO
- Use a unique password for every work account. Never reuse a work password on personal sites or apps.
- Make passwords long. A passphrase of four or more random words is stronger and easier to remember than a short, complex string.
- Use the company-approved password manager to create and store your passwords so you do not have to memorize them.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) wherever it is available.
- Change your password right away if you suspect it has been exposed, and report it to IT Security.

WHAT TO AVOID
- Adding a number or symbol to an old password (for example, Summer2024! becoming Summer2025!)
- Saving passwords in spreadsheets, sticky notes, or unprotected files
- Sharing passwords with coworkers, even people you trust
