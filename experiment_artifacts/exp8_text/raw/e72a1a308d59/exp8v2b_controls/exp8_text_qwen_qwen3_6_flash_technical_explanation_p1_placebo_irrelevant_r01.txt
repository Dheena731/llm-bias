Latency is the delay between a user initiating an action and receiving a response, usually measured in milliseconds. In web applications, even small amounts of latency matter because they directly dictate perceived performance. Human cognition registers delays above ~100 ms as noticeable friction, which triggers impatience, abandonment, or switching to a competitor. This UX impact translates to hard business metrics: studies show that each additional second of load time can reduce conversions by 7% and increase bounce rates significantly. 

Technically, latency compounds. A single page view often chains multiple network round-trips, database queries, and client-side render cycles; optimizing one link doesn't fix bottlenecks elsewhere. Finally, search engines use page speed as a ranking signal, meaning high latency silently erodes organic visibility. Ultimately, latency isn't just a backend metric—it's the bridge between functionality and user trust, directly influencing engagement, revenue, and competitive survival.
