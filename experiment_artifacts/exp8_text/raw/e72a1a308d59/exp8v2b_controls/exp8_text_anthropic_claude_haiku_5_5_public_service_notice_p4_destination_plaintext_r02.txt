HEAT ADVISORY

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for our area. Temperatures are expected to reach dangerous levels over the next several days, with heat index values climbing above 100 degrees Fahrenheit.

Heat can cause serious illness, especially for infants, young children, older adults, people with chronic medical conditions, and those who work or exercise outdoors. To stay safe:

- Drink water regularly, even if you do not feel thirsty.
- Stay in air-conditioned spaces when possible. If you do not have air conditioning, visit a library, mall, or community cooling center.
- Never leave children, pets, or anyone who depends on you inside a parked vehicle.
- Limit strenuous outdoor activity to the cooler parts of the day, such as early morning or evening.
- Check on friends, neighbors, and relatives who may be vulnerable to heat.
- Wear lightweight, light-colored clothing and a wide-brimmed hat when outdoors.

Recognize the warning signs of heat illness. Heat exhaustion can cause heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, and headache. Heat stroke is a medical emergency and may cause confusion, fainting, hot dry or red skin, or a body temperature above 103 degrees Fahrenheit. If you notice these symptoms, move to a cool place, loosen clothing, and seek medical help immediately. Call 911 for heat stroke or any person who is confused or unconscious.

For updates, monitor local news and the National Weather Service at weather.gov.
