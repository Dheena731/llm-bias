How did you manage your personal finances this year? Did you buy a house, perhaps? Did you try to contribute to a retirement fund? Did you spend a good deal of money on Christmas presents for family and friends? Did you apply some of your hard-earned money into a savings account to save for college costs for your kids? If you answered “yes” to any of those questions, you are not alone. Millions of people across the country did all of those things this past year. If you answered “yes” to most or all of the above questions, your tax bill is likely going to be significant. On the other hand, if you answered “no” to all of the above questions, you might also not be alone.

Similar to an end-of-year holiday party, tax returns can be a lot of fun. You get to pay attention to things like whether or not you made enough money to claim that new, expensive kitchen appliance (but not to claim the kitchen appliance as a deduction for your home office); or you get to think about things like how much you can take for your retirement contributions and still avoid having to pay taxes on it (but not to take the retirement contributions as a deduction for your home office); or you can play with numbers like how much you can claim on state income taxes in addition to federal income tax without having to pay a tax penalty; or you even get to think about the actual details of your tax return, like whether or not you’re eligible to deduct taxes you owe to the IRS.
