Cookies are small text files that websites send to your browser to store information on your computer. They can be categorised into three types, cookies used to send advertisements to your browser, those used to improve your experience browsing a website, and third-party cookies, which are cookies that are not set by the browser itself.


Describe potential effects of a change to use of the cookies.

Describe how long the cookies have a shelf life and whether they can be erased, deleted, or stored differently. For example, in the case of advertising, the "lifetime" of a cookie is usually the length of time the ad is intended to be displayed. If a cookie is set from a third-party ad server, the "lifetime" of the cookie will vary depending on how often it is refreshed.

Describe how the use of the cookies will affect the security of your computer or browser.
Describe the impacts on the privacy of data storage on your device from each type of cookie. For example, if the cookies are used to track your browsing history, are you aware of that use? How will your privacy be affected?

Give a detailed description of the benefits to the user of accepting cookies.

Explain how the user can refuse the cookies and exactly what the consequences will be of their refusal.

Questions for Verification

  1. Provide a detailed explanation of the cookie policy, including what types of cookies are used, how long they are stored, the types of information they store, and their intended purpose.
  2. What is your website's financial impact from the use of cookies? Provide figures when possible.
  3. Provide a detailed description of the use of cookies in your website. What are their intended purposes? Why are they used? What are their effects?
  4. How much do you know about the use of cookies on your site?
  5. What measures will you take to prevent the use of cookies?
  6. What are the cookies used on your site and where can you find them?
  7. Why do you need web cookies?
  8. What will happen if the user refuses the use of cookies?
  9. How will you validate a user's refusal?
  10. Provide evidence (such as screenshots, screenshots of the cookie pop-up, sample cookies, etc.) for the use of cookies on your site.

Be sure to include in your answer any of the following:

  • Links to your website's privacy policy.
  • Links to a third-party cookie disclosure policy.
  • Links to third-party online privacy policies.

Be sure to also include in your answer any of the following:

  • References to existing content on your website that can be referenced to provide further information.
