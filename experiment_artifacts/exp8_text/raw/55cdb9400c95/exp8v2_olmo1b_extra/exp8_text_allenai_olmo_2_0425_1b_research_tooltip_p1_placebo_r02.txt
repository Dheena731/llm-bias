Data exploration in simple R

The first task you should perform in R is to check that your dataset is in the right format. This is done with the RStudio console, and the commands are highly variable. For example, to display all columns in a dataframe as a string, you could run:

cat(dataframe$Name, collapse=" ")

To get a summary of the dataset:

summary(dataframe) # or summary(dataframe ~ .)

To inspect the first column of the dataset:

head(dataframe) # or head(dataframe, n=1)

To get a description of the missing columns:

names(dataframe) # or names(dataframe, unique=TRUE)

The above RStudio commands will typically work for any R script, and will provide insights into the dataset itself. It's worth exploring the dataset in this manner in order to get a sense of the dataset, and to develop a mental model of how it was used and collected. When you are feeling comfortable with a dataset, you should enter the dataset into RStudio, and explore and understand it using the same methods as demonstrated above. If you don't know much about the dataset, then you should skip the above steps. You will save yourself time and frustration later if you understand your dataset at the outset.
