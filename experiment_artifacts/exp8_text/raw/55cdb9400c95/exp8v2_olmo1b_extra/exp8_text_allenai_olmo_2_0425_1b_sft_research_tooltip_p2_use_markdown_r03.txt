# Confidence Interval in a Chart

**A confidence interval** is a statistical method used to estimate the unknown parameter of a population, in this case, the average age of people aged 18-65 from a specific city. Unlike a simple interval, a confidence interval provides a range within which the true population parameter (in this case, the average age) is assumed to lie with a certain level of confidence (commonly 95%). This helps gauge the reliability and precision of our estimates. Confidence intervals are crucial for understanding the variability and uncertainty in our data, allowing for more informed decision-making and analysis.

- **Grapses the parameter's range** within a specific confidence level.
- **Provides a basis for assessing the reliability** of population estimates.
- **Encourages a deeper understanding** of how data is sampled and estimated.
- **Facilitates the comparison** of different samples or groups against each other.
