  1. Written language, or a written script, whose sounds can be represented by letters, a letter combination or other grapheme is called a phonetic alphabet or alphabet in English. Arabic, Mandarin Chinese, Japanese and Vietnamese, for example, use a phonetic writing system. The Roman alphabet, which we use for English, has it roots in ancient Greek and has been adjusted for use in other languages. It’s derived from the Phoenician alphabet. All of these scripts are phonetic. In other scripts, which are called non-phonetic, we see a few examples of sounds represented, but only a few.

  2. The Latin alphabet was the first alphabet to be used by a significant number of people. Other scripts go back much longer. Many of these scripts are oral, like Chinese and Arabic. It’s written in human beings, so it makes sense that people could memorize the characters and imitate them when they speak, but when reading they wouldn’t write out the characters, because that would require a great deal of time. The earliest document we know of that was written in Chinese, for example, is a set of rules. Chinese writing was also oral until the 7th century.

  3. Modern languages have developed from phonetic scripts. English is a world language based on the Latin script, but English grammar and spelling have changed over time. The most obvious difference is that there is an alphabet but no alphabet. English, Spanish and French both have pronunciation, but their written forms are different. One of the biggest differences is that English uses a certain spelling rule called a silent e. When the vowels a, e, i, o, u, or y come after a consonant, the next vowel (or the vowel before it in the case of y) is pronounced. The word red is pronounced /rɛd/, but the word beet /bɛt/. The silent e keeps the word beet spelled the same, even though the pronunciation has changed. Languages of the other major world language families don’t use silent e, and only use silent e when it is a part of the name of a person or place, like Boston or Leiden. The only exception are the Slavic languages and the Baltic languages, which use silent e on the end of common nouns. The Baltic languages are two languages that are not closely related to English, but they have some words in common.

    The same spelling rule applies to the Latin script. In English, we use “ea” in the word “weigh” and “re” in the word “earth”. We also use “ea” and “re” in words of Greek origin (tear and tear) and Latin origin (air and hair). In Latin, the silent e makes a difference in pronunciation, while in other languages the silent e additionally makes a difference in spelling.
