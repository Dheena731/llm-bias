Just right, for some background.

The balance is very close to just right. The most important factors are the language of the podcast and the audience you are writing the launch note for.

The language of the podcast

For a famous podcast such as TED, the language will be mostly academic and formal (because it is intended for academics and experts). For a podcast aimed at the general public, the language is likely to be more conversational. For the podcast you are writing the launch note for, use a language that is most appropriate for your audience.

The audience of the podcast

On the one hand, it should be clear from the topic of the podcast whether your audience is academic or nonacademic. On the other hand, you may not know the audience well enough to be sure it is academic or not. If you are not sure that the audience is academic, consider writing the launch note in a more formal style.

Avoid jargon

Jargon is the language that is used by a special group of people. An example of jargon is the term "simultaneous interpretation." It is a technical term used by some people in some jobs. A nonacademic audience is likely not to be familiar with jargon.

Avoid slang

Slang is the language used by a special group of people. A slang term such as "really" is not likely to be understood by a general audience. You should also avoid slang when you are writing a launch note. In the launch note you are writing for a general audience, an audience that is not familiar with slang, you should use formal language instead of slang.
