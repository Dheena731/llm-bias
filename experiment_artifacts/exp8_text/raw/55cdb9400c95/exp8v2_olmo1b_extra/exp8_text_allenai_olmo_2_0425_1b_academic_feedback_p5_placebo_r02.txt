Use the right discipline for your course. Do not use a different discipline for the purpose of convenience. Do not cite your expository or persuasive essay as a scientific essay.

Make the essay relevant. Show how the "argument" contributes to the discipline's body of knowledge. If not, then you should not be writing an essay.

Use relevant evidence. Cite facts, references, and quotations only when they help you make your point. Even if they do not, they should be in the essay to conform to your style guide.

Ensure that you have cited all significant evidence. A substantial work may have literally thousands of references. If you forget one, your essay will not be as good as it could be.

Do not include a bibliography unless your essay requires it. You will not need to include a bibliography if you are using common style guides such as MLA or APA. If you are writing for a class on research methods or qualitative methods, you will need a bibliography.

If you use an essay as evidence, cite the essay as text. In some disciplines, such as history, it may be standard to use archival evidence as text. In any case, you will need to cite the source of the text. This might include a reference to a book or journal, or a citation to a specific page in a book or journal. You will not need to cite the essay if you are using primary sources (such as a personal interview) or secondary sources (such as an encyclopedia).

If you use a book, always cite the title, the author(s), and the publisher. For the publisher, cite the name of the book, the date of publication, and the place of publication. In most disciplines, you will not need to cite the edition.

If you use a journal article, cite both the title and the author. If the journal has an index of articles, and the name of the author is listed, cite the author's name. If not, cite the name of the journal, the volume number, and the page number, and the date of publication. In most disciplines, you will not need to cite the edition.
