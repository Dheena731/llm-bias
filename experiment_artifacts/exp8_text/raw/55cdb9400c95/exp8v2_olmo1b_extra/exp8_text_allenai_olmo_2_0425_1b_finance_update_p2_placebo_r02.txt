A company that periodically sends you a friendly reminder that you might want to update your account, including the fee for doing so, has a history of writing and endorsing faux-news articles about the company in which it bills itself. This is an example of fraudulent writing.

This post was written for a small number of people who use a card to buy diapers or groceries. It was written with a very specific audience and purpose. It was also carefully checked for errors in spelling and grammar. I could’ve written something shorter, or less specific, or about a different audience, but my decision to write this post at this length was based on the facts I had at hand and the purpose I had in mind. At the same time, it’s possible that the post would still make sense if I had adapted it to appeal to a broader audience.

Finally, this post was not written with the intention of informing every single person who might ever read it. Instead, it was written for a select group of interested readers who will be able to use it as a helpful guide to evaluating news sources.
