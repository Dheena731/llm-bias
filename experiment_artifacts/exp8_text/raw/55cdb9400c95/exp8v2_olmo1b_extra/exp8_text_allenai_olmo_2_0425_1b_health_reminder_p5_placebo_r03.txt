Working with Groups

Cooperative learning:  Cooperative learning enables students to build their skills on a shared basis, as they work together in groups.  So this kind of work involves people interacting with other people.  In a large lecture course, students interact with the entire class, but in a group course, they interact with one another.  In group-work, you usually have a teacher who is more involved with the students in small groups than with the entire class.  Both the students and the teacher have the same goals.

Learning communities:  Assisted by a teacher, students meet to discuss learning goals, evaluate themselves, and collaborate on their studies.  Students in learning communities share a specific learning goal that guides their discussions.

Small groups:  Small groups for collaborative work are usually smaller than a learning community.  Small groups have a limited range of students and are guided by a teacher.  The groups may or may not be pre-established.

Peer and self-assessment:  You can find opportunities for students to share ideas in groups, create assignments that encourage students to peer-assess their work, or use a variety of different assessment tools such as peer assess or self-assesses for extra credit.

Group work:  You can arrange for students to collaborate on group projects, or you can have groups of students working on a project and then evaluating each other’s work.  Groups may also meet as teams that represent a particular type of research or problem.  You can also use forms of self-assessment to evaluate student work and to guide the teaching-learning process.

Group learning:  Group learning includes cooperative learning and peer assessment.
