Latency in web applications is a critical factor that can significantly impact the user experience and the operational efficiency of a service. Here are several specific reasons why latency matters in web applications:

1. **Real-time Interaction and Updates:** Web applications thrive on instant responsiveness. Whether it's real-time chat, online gaming, real-time analytics, or any application where users expect actions to be immediate, low latency is essential. High latency can lead to a feel of lag, freezing, or buffering that frustrates users and breaks immersion in the application.

2. **Customer Expectations:** In today's fast-paced digital world, consumers have high expectations for real-time engagement and seamless updates. A one-second delay can be noticeable enough to lose a customer who is used to instant services.

3. **Data Consistency and Real-Time Insights:** Web applications often rely on real-time data and analytics for decision-making. High latency can make these services either slow to respond or outdated before they can be utilized by the application or its real-time processing capabilities. This could lead to incorrect decisions or updates that are not in sync with the constantly changing environment, severely impacting application performance and user trust.

4. **Server-Load and Scalability Issues:** Latency can be a direct result of the server's capacity to handle incoming requests. As the number of users increases or when load peaks, high latency can become a bottleneck, gnawing away at the overall responsiveness and scalability of the application.

5. **Mobile vs. Desktop Applications:** Mobile users often experience higher latency due to network conditions even if they are in the same geographic vicinity as the server. Desktop applications can often afford to have higher levels of latency because their users are less likely to experience jitter or heavy latency on a consistent basis.

6. **User Experience Design:** Designers pay close attention to latency because users literally feel it. Slow loading pages or delayed responses can animate the user's experience into negative territory, leading to dissatisfaction and potential abandonment of the application.

In summary, latency is a critical metric not only because it impacts user experience directly but also because it presents practical challenges to application scalability, user satisfaction, and operational efficiency. Minimizing latency is a critical component of a high-performance web application strategy.
