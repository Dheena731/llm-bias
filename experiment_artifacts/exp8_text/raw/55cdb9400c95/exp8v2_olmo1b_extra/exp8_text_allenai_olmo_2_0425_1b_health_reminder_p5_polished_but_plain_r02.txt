Ensure your prescription is complete, clear, and concise

Try to include as much information as possible in the prescription. Your prescriber may ask for information about your medical history. For example, if you have a history of asthma or allergies, your prescriber may ask for these items to be included in your prescription. Worksheets can be useful tools for checking your prescription.

Be a good patient

Include any information the prescriber may request on the prescription. Do not assume your prescriber will have all the information they need. This is known as "double filling" and is a problem that can lead to medication mistakes.


Be aware that allergies, and medicines that may cause allergies, should always be included with your prescription, even if it is for the same medication. These may include the following:

  • Medicines that can cause allergies.
  • Foods that can cause allergies.
  • Plants, trees, flowers, and seeds that may cause allergies.
  • Insect stings, bites, or skin contact.
  • Certain drugs or medicines that can cause allergic reactions.
  • Over-the-counter medicines.
  • Foods to avoid, including certain foods that may cause allergic reactions.

If you have an allergy, discuss your medications with your prescriber before starting the prescription. This will help prevent an allergic reaction, which can make treatment difficult or even dangerous. Your prescriber may recommend other medications to replace your allergies medicines. Be sure to tell your prescriber about any side effects of your new medication.

Consider the risks of any new medications

Your prescriber will review the potential risks of taking your medication. Therefore, be sure to discuss your concerns with your prescriber. This will give you the opportunity to ask questions and make sure your concerns are addressed. Ask them about the risks for each medication they prescribe. It is not always possible to completely avoid risks, but you should be aware of these, if your prescriber is aware.

The following are some examples of risks that may be discussed:

  • Risks to your health.
  • Risks to your finances.
  • Risks to the quality of your life.

The following are some examples of benefits that may be discussed:

  • Benefits to your health.
  • Benefits to your finances.
  • Benefits to your quality of life.

Keep your healthcare professionals informed

The more information you provide, the better you will be cared for.

If you are not sure what medications you should take for certain conditions, or if you have questions about what the medications mean, ask your prescriber. They may answer your questions or refer you to other healthcare professionals who can help.

If you are not getting the care you need, ask your prescriber about the reasons why and how to improve it. Follow your prescriber's instructions and take your medications as prescribed.

When you are not sure what to do

If you are not sure about side effects from one medication that you are taking, ask your prescriber or pharmacist how they compare to the benefits and risks of taking other medications.

Sometimes it is difficult to make choices, or the risks and benefits of medications may not be clear. Your prescriber can help you understand these concepts. They can also help you make decisions about which medications to take.

If your prescriber prescribes a medication that you do not like, do not think it works, or feel it is too expensive, ask your prescriber for the following:

  • Information about the medication.
  • Information about how to take the medication.
  • Information about why the prescriber is prescribing the medication.
  • Information about what the medication does or how it works.
  • Information about the risks, benefits, and side effects of the medication.
