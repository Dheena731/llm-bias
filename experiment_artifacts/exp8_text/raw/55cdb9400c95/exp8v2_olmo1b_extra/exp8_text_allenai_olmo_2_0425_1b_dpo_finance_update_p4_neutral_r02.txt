Here is a short explanation of an End-of-Year tax statement:

The end-of-year tax statement is a summary document that informs you of your tax liability for the current year. It outlines any tax deductions and credits you claimed during the year, showing your overall tax bill before any credits are applied. It's essential because you can use this statement to plan your tax strategy for next year, such as potentially taking advantage of tax credits you've applied for.

Key elements typically found on an end-of-year tax statement:

1. **Total Tax Calculation**: Simply shows the total amount of tax you owe before any adjustments or credits are applied.

2. **Tax Deductions and Credits**: Details all the expenses and credits you've claimed that reduce your tax bill, like medical expenses, charitable donations, education expenses, and income tax credits like the Child Tax Credit, Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC), etc.

3. **Statement of Changes from Year Before**: Highlights any changes from the previous tax year, such as adjustments for retirement savings contributions or changes in adjusted gross income.

4. **Estimated Tax Payments**: Reminds you to have paid estimated taxes throughout the year, ensuring you do not owe a significant amount come April 15th.

5. **Instructions for Refund Application**: If you're eligible for a refund, this section provides guidance on how to submit your return and Form 843 to contest any overpayment.

6. **Other Information**: Sometimes, it includes links to online tools for calculating estimated taxes, forms you need to file, and details on any notices or payments already filed.

Remember, it's crucial to look at your tax statement before filing your tax return to ensure accuracy and that you are taking advantage of any tax deductions or credits you're eligible for.
