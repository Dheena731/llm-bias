An API rate limit is a mechanism built into many web services and APIs to control how many requests can be made in a certain time frame, typically per minute, hour, day, or month. It's an essential security feature that helps protect the services from being exhaustively abused by automated or unscrupulous applications, saving the service from overloading and potentially leading to data loss, service downtime, or increased response times for legitimate users.

Here's an example of an abstract explanation that doesn't introduce any undefined jargon and sticks to the essential concept of API rate limits:

Imagine you have a super popular blog platform, and your API is responsible for managing blog posts. If too many blog posts are submitted at once, your servers could become overwhelmed and slow down. To prevent this, you might have a rate limit in place. This limit could be 100 requests per minute. If someone sends 101 requests in one minute, they are hitting the limit. Your system might then throttle back to 50 requests per minute for that IP address for the next few minutes to give your servers a breather before the next burst of requests.
