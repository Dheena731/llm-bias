Missing citations in an essay can significantly detract from the credibility and persuasiveness of the work. When you rely on someone else's ideas without properly citing them, it indicates that you have not conducted your own research or that the research has not been sufficiently peer-reviewed. This can weaken your argument and potentially harm your academic standing or professional reputation if the essay is part of an academic course or professional portfolio.

Here's a brief suggestion for addressing the issue of missing citations:

"Furthermore, the lack of citations in this essay underscores a critical absence in thorough academic research which is expected in contemporary essays. It is imperative to include proper citations when drawing on the works of others, not only for academic integrity but also to provide readers with accessible references they can research further if warranted. Without these citations, the essay lacks the supporting evidence necessary to substantiate the author's claims."

Note that this comment is short and intentionally avoids including invented facts or contradicting itself, both of which would undermine its intended message. It is targeted at an audience that includes educators, peers in academic writing seminars, and most importantly, the original essay's audience seeking to understand the importance of citing sources in academic writing.
