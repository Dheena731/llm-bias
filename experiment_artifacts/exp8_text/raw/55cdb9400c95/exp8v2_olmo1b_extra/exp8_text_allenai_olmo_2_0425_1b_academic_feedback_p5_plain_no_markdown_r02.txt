Use short, simple sentences.

Write with good grammar. Write less and more often. Edit with a pen or pencil.

Be clear, concise and precise. Don’t ramble or speak in circles.

Read your essay aloud to check for messy sentences, run-on sentences, and similar grammatical errors.

Make sure your thesis statement is clear and concise. If you use a prompt, ask yourself whether it is particular and specific enough to answer using a single sentence.

Avoid using the same words twice in a row. Consider changing a sentence if it contains a word that sounds awkward or repetitious.

Avoid using "to be" verbs and unnecessary articles. Write "there is" or "there are."

Don’t use words like "really," "so" or "very." Use them less frequently, and only when you need to.
