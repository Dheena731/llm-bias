The lowest value is IQR-3σ, but is that more significant than the 4.8σ? Why can't we say that there's 95% confidence that the average of the numbers is not more than 4.8σ? And what about including the interval, or at least a reference to why it's a confidence interval?

The confidence interval is calculated as
$$ IQR = \sqrt{\frac{s^2}{n-1}} $$

with the standard deviation calculated as $$ s=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{n-1}} $$, where n is the number of observations and σ the standard deviation. This gives us an IQR of 3.56 and a standard deviation of 0.016.

Obviously, we know that the 4.8σ is highly significant compared to 3.56σ, so we can say that there is 95% confidence that the average of the numbers is at most 4.8σ. For the interval alone, the best we can say is that there is 95% confidence that the average of the numbers is at least 4.8σ, but is that more meaningful than saying that the average of the numbers is at most 4.8σ?

And how do we combine these two pieces? Which sentence is more specific? Which is easier to understand?
