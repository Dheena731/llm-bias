Customer benefit

The first paragraph of an elevator pitch should focus on the customer benefit. This is sometimes called the CTA (call-to-action).

Customer benefit, also called "benefit" or "promise", is an essential part of a brand's identity and brand promise.

It's worth noting that there are often multiple benefits in a product or service that a customer will take advantage of, and these should be aligned with the combined value proposition.

For example, a customer benefit for a camera might be "clear pictures", and a customer benefit for a travel booking app might be "no booking fees".

Customer benefit examples

Here are some examples of customer benefits (along with the corresponding benefit statements and promises):

  • "The best camera for clear photos" (Promise). This is a benefit statement. It's a promise: you will not only get clear photos, but you will not have to pay for them.
  • "The hotel reservation app with no booking fees" (Promise). This is a benefit statement. You will not have to pay for the hotel reservation service.
  • "This travel app makes all travel planning easy" (Promise). This is a benefit statement. You will not have to worry about planning your next trip or paying for any of your trips.

In a customer benefit, the customer benefit is the most important part of the argument. The promise is also very important, but it should usually be mentioned at the end of the argument.

You can use different types of benefits to appeal to different customer segments, such as celebrities or ultra-rich people.

Customer benefits are used in many different contexts, such as advertising, sales, and marketing. It would be very difficult to write a good pitch without writing a good customer benefit, so it's worth a lifetime of effort.

Customer benefit development

In order to come up with a useful customer benefit, you need to dig deep into the customer's needs. The best way to do this is to understand what motivates each customer.

When you do this, you will find that there are many different types of needs that customers may have, such as:

  • Gamify your customer experience
  • Showcase your customer's achievements
  • Achieve the most incredible comparison

It's also important to try to understand what motivates the customer:

  • Their motivation to buy
  • Their motivation to retain the benefits
  • Their motivation to go back to the company

For example, one of the common motivations of customers is to save time.

This means that you should ensure that you show customers that you can save them time, whether it's by making your product or service easier to use, or by giving them a more affordable alternative.

By understanding what motivates each customer, you will be able to create more effective customer benefits.
