When studying an essay, a conspicuous absence of citations can significantly weaken the argumentation and lend a misleading impression to the material presented, potentially leaving the reader with false confidence in the author’s claims. Citations are crucial for several reasons: they provide credit to the original sources that inform and support the arguments, they help ensure that authorship and integrity are properly attributed, they aid in the reader’s ability to verify the information, and they establish a clear line of scholarly discourse.

A lack of citations can obscure the original authors' views, potentially misrepresenting the essay’s perspective and making it seem more original than it actually is. It can also mislead readers about the extent of research conducted by the author and the strength of the evidence supporting their claims.

To enhance credibility and depth, it's essential to integrate appropriate citations within an essay whenever facts, ideas, statistics, or quotes are drawn from other sources. This practice not only adheres to academic standards and professional guidelines but also enriches the essay by enriching the reader's understanding and providing a scaffold for evaluating the claims made. Accurate citations reveal the intellectual debt owed to others and ensure that the discussion is grounded in verifiable information.
