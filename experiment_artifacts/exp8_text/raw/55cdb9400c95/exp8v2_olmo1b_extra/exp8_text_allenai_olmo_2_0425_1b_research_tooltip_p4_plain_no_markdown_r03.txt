What is a tooltip?

A tooltip is a small box that appears above an element on a webpage to provide additional information. They're often used to clarify what an icon or button means, or what a specific link refers to.

In contrast to other rich-media forms of conveying information (such as screenshots, text descriptions, or fully interactive experiences), tooltips provide only very limited information about what it is that the user is viewing or experiencing.

How can I create a tooltip?

There are many ways to generate tooltips, depending on the tool you're using and the purpose at hand. In general, though, there are a few common elements that all good tooltips share:

1. **Content**: The first thing that should be considered is the content of the tooltip. What exactly does the user need to know? How will they benefit from knowing this particular piece of information? It's highly important that the content is clear, concise, and easily understood.

2. **Highlighting**: Another important aspect of a good tooltip is its appearance. How can it be made visually appealing? Is there a way to emphasize the important details? Using a combination of colors, shapes, and fonts can go a long way towards making a tooltip stand out from the rest of the webpage.

3. **Size**: The last thing to consider when creating a tooltip is its size. What is the optimal size for the text, image, or icon being displayed within it? While many tools offer different sized options, it's often best to choose a size that allows the content to be read easily without being too small or too large.
