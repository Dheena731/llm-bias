An API rate limit is a condition imposed by a service or platform on the number of requests an application can make within a certain time frame. It's a mechanism designed to prevent a single application from overwhelming the infrastructure used by the API, ensuring fair access and preventing Denial of Service (DoS) attacks. 

Rate limits are important from a security and fairness perspective. They help maintain a healthy balance between concurrent users or requests coming in from different sources, which is crucial for the reliable and efficient operation of APIs. Different APIs may have different limits based on the volume of requests, the frequency of requests, or specific conditions like time-of-day limits. It's a form of rate management, allowing developers to manage the scale of their applications and ensuring that APIs remain responsive and secure.
