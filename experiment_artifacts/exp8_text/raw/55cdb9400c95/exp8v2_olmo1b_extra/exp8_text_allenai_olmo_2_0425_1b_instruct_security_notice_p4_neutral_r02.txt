Enable two-factor authentication for your online accounts. Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds an extra layer of security by requiring not only a password and username but also a second form of verification, such as a code sent to your mobile phone or generated by a hardware device. This significantly reduces the likelihood of unauthorized access even if your password is compromised.

Several websites and online platforms, including Gmail, Facebook, and many banks, offer 2FA as an optional security feature. Turning it on enhances your online security by making it harder for attackers to gain unauthorized access to your accounts. Remember that even if you're not concerned about severe breaches, protecting your personal information and complying with security best practices are always a good idea.

Here are some steps to enable 2FA for your accounts:

1. Log into the account with your username and password.
2. Look for an option to enable 2FA.
3. Follow the on-screen instructions to set up the required code generator, which most commonly includes a link sent to your phone or a authentication app.
4. Test your 2FA setup by sending a verification code to your phone number or device to ensure it works correctly.

By enabling 2FA, you're not only strengthening your personal online security but also protecting your accounts against a broader range of threats.
