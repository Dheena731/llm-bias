For storing fresh herbs, it’s all about freshness and freshness equals longevity. Here’s a short tip: 

* Color Matters: Keep herbs in their natural color if possible. Remove leaves that have turned brown or wilted, as this signifies they’re no longer fresh. Store herbs in a dark, cool place like a pantry or refrigerator where it’s not too humid or too cold.

* Think Fungus: Herbs like mushrooms and certain types of onions can still be stored for a couple of weeks after opening due to their protective mycelium and cell walls, but for most herbs, aim for an expiration date of 1-2 weeks. 

* Wrap it up: Use breathable cloth or paper bags to keep herbs dry and fresh. Avoid storing them in plastic or sealed airtight containers as this can lead to mold and excessive moisture. 

* Rinse: Before storing, rinse herbs in a kitchen sink or a colander to remove any surface dust or bugs. 

* Trim: Shorten stems; shorter stems have a higher surface area for gases to escape, improving freshness.

* Use It Fast: Fresh herbs are best consumed within a couple of days of purchase. If you notice any signs of wilting or mold, it’s best to use those herbs rather than trying to store them longer.

Storing herbs this way ensures you get the most from your fresh herbs while keeping them fresher for longer.
