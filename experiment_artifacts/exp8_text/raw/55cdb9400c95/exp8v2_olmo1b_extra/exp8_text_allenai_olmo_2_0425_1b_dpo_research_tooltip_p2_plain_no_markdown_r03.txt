A confidence interval provides a range of values that is likely to include an unknown population parameter with a certain degree of confidence, often 95%. It helps to describe how sure we are about our estimate of a population characteristic. For example, in a chart illustrating sales over time, a confidence interval for the average sales per week could tell us how closely the observed sales data align with the true average. It acknowledges the variability within the data and suggests that if we were to collect more data, the actual average sales per week would fall within this interval far more often than not.
