When analyzing an essay lacking citations, it's evident that the author has not conducted thorough research or acknowledged the sources of their information. Proper citation is not only a matter of academic integrity but also serves as a vital link to the specific knowledge and evidence presented in the text, providing readers with the opportunity to explore more about the claims or arguments made.

In the domain of historical analysis, for instance, citations should reference historical documents, scholarly articles, scholarly books, and other primary and secondary sources with detailed authors' names, titles, publication years, and publication venues. For instance: "According to Smith, 'Ancient Rome' (2015),..." or "In line with Jones' research, see 'Journal of Ancient History,' vol. X (2012)." Without these, readers lack the necessary context to evaluate the validity and source of the author's claims.

Moreover, standard citation styles (e.g., APA, MLA) indicate how information is organized and referenced, so the absence of citation markers undercuts the essay's scholarly rigor. It's important for such an essay to be accompanied by a bibliography or list of cited works, allowing for an exhaustive exploration by other scholars and readers of the foundational texts mentioned.

Removing citations not only makes the essay incomplete but also undermines its scientific or academic merit, potentially leading to misunderstanding or misinterpretation of the author's argument. In academia, accuracy, transparency, and the ability to support claims with evidence are paramount; citation repair is a fundamental step toward rectifying an oversight that could have significantly impacted the quality of the paper.
