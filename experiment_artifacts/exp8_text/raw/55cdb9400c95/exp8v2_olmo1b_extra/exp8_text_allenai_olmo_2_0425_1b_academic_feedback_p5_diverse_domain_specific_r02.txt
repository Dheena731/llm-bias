Do not use this POST in your writing unless

  • your paper is about a real life situation,
  • the situation and consequences are not fictional,
  • you mention the real life situation prominently in the paper,
  • the situation is causal, and
  • the causal relationship is well understood.

A good question is framed in the form of a problem where the answer will help with a known problem. It should focus on a known problem and there should not be any obvious solutions. Often we define the problem as a user asking for a solution. Sometimes we define it as a team asking for a solution. You will need to explore the problem, solve it, and report some results.

A bad question is factual in tone. It does not have a problem. It should be a fact that one of the following has happened: the OP passed the exam with flying colors; a team failed to pass the exam; a team passed the exam with flying colors; the etiquette for addressing an in-class exam was wrong; the size of the exam room was too small; the exam room was too loud; the exam had too many questions; the exam had too few questions; it was too easy; it was too difficult; the exam was graded on a curve; the exam was ungraded; the grade was a fail; the exam was never graded; it was a tossup; the exam was cancelled; the exam was held in a computer lab; the exam was held in a teacher office; the exam was held outside; the exam was held in a classroom; the exam had no questions; the exam had too many questions; the exam had too few questions; the test was random; the test was capricious; the test was unfair.

You can always change an answer to a question to a different question by using the question tag and adding another question at the end. Example: Would you say this question or this question? (which is the same as What question? or Which question?).

You should always answer directly to the question. Do not give an indirect answer such as No, it is not true that…, No, we don’t have to do that, No, I have already answered that question, etc. You will not be penalized for including an answer that was not asked. If you have an interesting suggestion that is not directly related to the question, you can write a comment below the question, but this should be rare.

For a general discussion of good and bad questions, please see our general guidelines for questions.

It is important to include some references to the literature on the problem. You should always include a bibliographical entry and a reference list at the end of your question so that it can be used by someone as a research starting point.
