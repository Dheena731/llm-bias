First steps

The goal is to start building habits. Each week we will create three habits, each with three subgoals. First we will create an associated daily log, where we track our progress. Like a blog, a log is a place for personal writing and reflection. It can also just be a place for storing your thoughts and goals for later.

You can use any method to start your own habit tracker, whether that is a journal, a log or a calendar. A log is a tool for tracking actions and reflecting on them over time to help you learn from your mistakes and develop habits. A log can also be used to communicate and collaborate with others.

Day 1

The first day will be special, the starting day. On this day, you can set up your habit tracker. It is highly recommended that you create your habit tracker on the first day and then always refer to it from this point forward. The log is the place where you will record progress.

As mentioned, you can use a journal or any tool of your own invention, such as a calendar or a Google calendar. A good way to start is to simply write down your habit name and some basic information about it. You can also list your subgoals, the connection between them and your timeline.

Day 2

Day 2 is the day to start tracking your progress. It is highly recommended that you track your progress every day until you have reached your goal.


Do not start with deadline-driven (i.e. goal driven) tasks. If you are supposed to start a task after a certain date, set that date in your log.

Day 3

This is the day to check your progress. Use your log to track your progress.

Progress is the ability to do something well.

Practice the art of mindfulness

Learning to keep track of our habits will begin with tracking our progress. We will focus on tracking our mental progress, because being mindful is key to being and staying successful.

Freethinking and practicing self-acceptance are two of the best ways to start training yourself to be mindful. Freethinking is the ability to question things and not believe you know everything without researching or questioning it.

When trying to attain a goal, you can apply freethinking to every step towards your target. Freethinking does not mean not accepting reality, but rather not believing you know everything and everything is simple. Freethinking is about learning to question and not simply taking things for granted.

You can use freethinking to question and question your habits. Are you doing your habit to be mindful of your health? What does mindful mean? Are you asking yourself, am I being mindful? Can you be more mindful?

Freethinking does not mean being a negative person. Freethinking is about being aware of what is going on around you, in yourself and in the world. Freethinking can help you find answers and solutions to your problems. Freethinking helps you solve problems and not to blame others for them.

Freethinking also helps you deal with things that are complex. Freethinking helps you learn and be humble. Freethinking is a way of life that helps you understand what is going on around you and in yourself. Freethinking is the first step towards being successful.
