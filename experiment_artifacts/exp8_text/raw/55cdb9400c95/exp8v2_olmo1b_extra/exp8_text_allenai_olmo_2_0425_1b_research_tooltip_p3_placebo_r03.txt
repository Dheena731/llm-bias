An example:

                      <date-range>December 30 - January 4</date-range>
            <data>January 1</data>
            <data>January 2</data>
            <data>January 3</data>

Use anchor links to link to the date ranges used in the header:

            <link rel="next" href="#January">Next</link>
            <link rel="prev" href="#December">Previous</link>
            <link rel="date-range" href="#January">January</link>
            <link rel="date-range" href="#December">December</link>

Your markup should be structured so that the date ranges are encased in <date-range> tag, and the anchor links are outermost tags.

If you are looking for inspiration, take a look at the markup for articles on the Stack Exchange network.

Do not hard-code date ranges

If you hard-code date ranges, they will break when an article is updated on the server.

Use the date-range templates

The date-range templates are really easy to use. Since they're templates, you don't have to write it all yourself. This will let you concentrate on the details.

    <date-range>December 30 - January 4</date-range>

Use an output filter

The HTML output for a question, answer, or comment must be output using a template. In doing so, it's easy to output date ranges. By default, the date-range templates are not rendered by default. This means they must be output using an output filter.

You can find an example output filter for the date-range templates on GitHub, here.
