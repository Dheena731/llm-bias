Making the point clear is absolutely essential to writing a good abstract, but equally important is making the point understandable to the reader. It is often difficult to hit the sweet spot between these two extremes, but this is an area with a lot of room for improvement.

Summary (250 words)

Summary is the most important part of your proposal, though many people forget to write it. Its purpose is to explain your research in terms that a judge can understand.

What to Write

Write about what you plan to do, and how you will do it. Make a list of the most important points you want to make. If you cannot explain it in one paragraph, you are probably going to have to write an entire paper. Make sure you write enough so that a judge can easily read it.

Avoid Using jargon

A lot of times when someone writes a summary they use technical language that only someone who knows the research can understand. Your summary should, however, be accessible to everyone who reads it. We have a guide to jargon and how to avoid it. One way to help you avoid jargon is to read up on the field and see how other scientists have written about the same subject.
