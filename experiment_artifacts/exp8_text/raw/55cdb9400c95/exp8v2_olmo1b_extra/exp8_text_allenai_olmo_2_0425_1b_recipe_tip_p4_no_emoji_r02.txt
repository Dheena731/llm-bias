The secret to making good calls? Don't be a cow

It's easy to believe that if you want good results when making calls, you have to be the best communicator you can be. It makes sense. You have to be compelling, detailed, and concise. You have to be an expert and take the time to fully prepare.
Well, maybe not.

Much of the time, your success is directly proportional to what the person you are calling knows about you. If you are already known to them, as a trustworthy, reliable, and fair person, they'll be far more likely to listen to what you have to say. If you are not known to them, they will be at best curious, and at worst suspicious, negative, or a little hostile.

So the secret to success? Be as much of an unknown as possible. That will make it easy for them to believe what you are saying.

In order to be an unknown, you have to be:

  • consistent;
  • predictable;
  • trustworthy;
  • reliable;
  • fair;
  • empathetic;
  • consistent.

If you are consistent, they'll know you pretty well. They'll pay attention to the details -- the way you dress, the way you talk, the way you carry yourself. They'll notice you for the same reasons they notice the sales clerk they go to every Thursday: you're reliable. You've been in that office -- now working for that date for at least a year. You're consistent. And you're fair.

Everyone knows you found a way to keep at work, so they know you'll make the same kind of promises and keep the same kind of deadlines to them. And they're also going to notice the consistency with you at work. They'll notice that you're always on time. You're always on task. You're always on the ball.

When you show up for work at 9 a.m. on Monday, 8 a.m. on Tuesday, and 9 a.m. on Wednesday, that shows them that you are reliable. You'll be there every day, doing the quality work needed to keep your job.

Consistency is important when you have a reputation at work, too. If you are consistent with your work attitude, work ethic, and work style -- you're fair, as well. If you are consistent with your work tasks, deadlines, and work style, they'll notice that you are trustworthy. And consistency makes them comfortable with you, so they'll listen to you. They'll know that you're the kind of person who can be depended upon to deliver exactly what you promise: quality work, on time, in the budget.

When you show up for work, make a detailed plan of your day ahead each morning, and stick to it. When you come in, take a couple of minutes to review your plan so that you can be consistent. If you review your plans each morning, you'll be sure that your focus is the same in different areas, every day. You'll keep to the same plan and schedule.

For example, if you are the only person in the office, and you come in at 8 a.m. every morning, but the office closes at 4 p.m., you know that you will have to stay until 6 p.m. every day. You review your plan. You know that you are responsible for all the work from 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. that day.
