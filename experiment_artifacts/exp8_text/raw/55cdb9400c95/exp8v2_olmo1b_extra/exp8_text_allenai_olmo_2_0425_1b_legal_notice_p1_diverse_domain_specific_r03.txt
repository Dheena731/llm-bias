What is an "account change"?

A bank account change is the term used to describe your account being altered in some way. This could be the account balance, balance transactions, funds transfer between accounts, or a change in the account owner. Often, the account balance is the most commonly associated with this phrase.

Here's an update on the bank's terms:

"This bank account will be closed in 2023, and new funds will not be available to you until 2024."

This notice provides plenty of explanation and context to serve as a reminder that the bank account will be closed, but that the funds will not be available to you until the end of 2024.

Let's say the bank goes out of business and your account changes:

"This bank account will be closed in 2022, and all funds in the account will be considered lost."

In this example, the notice becomes much longer and less effective at communicating the message. However, there are also good reasons to go beyond a single notice. A series of written notices offers a more organized way to communicate about an account change while also letting the account holder know when the account will be closed.

What are some known risks that should be clarified in the notice?

There are many types of account changes that may be related to transactions fees increases, late fees, a new minimum account balance, and other changes. Whenever a bank adds a new fee, they should provide a detailed notice to the account holder outlining what a fee is, how it is calculated, and the new fee schedule. This notice should be made available at least 30 days in advance.

What is the notice of a new account, or a new account term?

This type of notification is used when a new type of deposit-type account is added to the bank's portfolio. The new account will have different terms than the old account, and it's important to explain the change to the account holder. The details should be communicated in a clear and concise manner, include dates, and describe the new account options.


"Starting January 1st 2020, you will have access to unlimited online access to your account at this bank. This account will have a one-time account maintenance fee of $10/month."

This notice details the account change and dates when the account will be opened. It provides clear information on how the fee will be calculated, and how the fee will be applied.

What is an account change notice?

An account change notice is a notification that a change in account has been made to a bank's account holder. This change might be due to a bank policy, or a transaction with the bank. Account change notices typically include dates, names, and the amount of the change.

The notice gives account holders the information they need to understand the change and prepare for its impact. The notice also gives account holders an opportunity to ask questions before the change is implemented.

Example: "Starting today, your account has been upgraded from an hourly-rate account to a monthly rate account."

This notice might be used to inform account holders that the bank has changed the way their account is paid into or paid from. It might also be used to notify account holders that they must deposit payments to their accounts every month.
