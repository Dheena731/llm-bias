 • If there is more than one thing to do, make sure you do it in the order of priority.
  • If you’re adding a tip to a calendar, think about how often the task is repeated. If it’s every 2 weeks, you should consider breaking it into two shorter chunks and adding a note to remind you when the next time is.
  • If you’re writing tips for your co-workers, you have to think about how you want them to read it. Does a typical user want instructions? Are they looking for tips? Do they need to be filled with pictures to better engage them? What sort of tone is better for them to read? These are important questions to answer when you start writing. For instance, if you’re writing for a graphic designer, you should make sure the tips are clear, concise, and easy to follow. If you’re writing for the marketing team in your office, you should think about how they might read it and what tone they would be most receptive to.

  • Did you know that you can write tips in free business writing software like Notion, Google Docs, and Microsoft Word? Try using those tools if you ever get stuck on Tips 1-8. But if you’re writing for an audience that has a specific skill set, free business writing software is probably not going to get you very far. You might need to use a paid service like Text Expander, or even pay a professional to write your tips for you.
