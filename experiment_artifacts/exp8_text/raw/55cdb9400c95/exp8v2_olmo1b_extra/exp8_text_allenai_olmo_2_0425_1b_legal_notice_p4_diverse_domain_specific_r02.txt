Create a High-Level Executive Summary
The executive summary provides a concise overview of the next-line goals and objectives. The executive summary should be:

  • Short, interesting, and to the point.
  • Written in the third person.
  • Not written solely using the first person or in the passive voice.
  • Written without capitalization or use of negative words (e.g., not, can’t, won’t, shouldn’t).

Write a three to five line summary of the following sections.

Key Takeaways
In this section, cover the most important things of the paper you wrote. You want to briefly mention the following: the problem, what you did, where you did it, what you found, and what you are doing next, next steps, and possible solutions.
Write a three to five sentence summary of the following sections.

Problem in Brief
In this section, briefly describe the problem you are trying to solve or the issue you want to address. Provide a short summary of the problem(s) and any other context which might be relevant, such as:

  • What is the problem?
  • Why is the problem important?
  • What are the key factors that lead to the problem(s)?
  • Who is affected by the problem?
  • What are the key concerns?

What Did You Do?
In this section, describe the steps you took to either solve the problem or approach the issue. This includes:

  • Describe your approach to solving the problem.
  • What data and resources did you use?
  • How did you measure your results?

Where Did You Do It?
In this section, briefly describe the place where you conducted the research or performed the experiments. For example:

  • Where was your research conducted?
  • What was the general location?
  • What was the specific location?
  • Who was the specific location?
  • What was the date?

What Did You Find?
In this section, describe what you found and included any other relevant information. For example:

  • What was the nature of the research?
  • What were your key findings?
  • What does your research suggest?

Next Steps
In this section, describe what you plan to do next. For example:

  • What are your next steps?
  • What are your next ideas?
  • What are the next steps in your research?
  • What could you do next?
  • What are your next steps for the project?

Next Steps for Future Research
In this section, describe what other future studies could be performed or what results could be generated. For example:

  • What are the next steps in your research?
  • What does this research model suggest?
  • What are the next steps for your research?
  • What other outcomes have been reported in other literature?
  • What are the next steps?

Case Study
In this section, briefly describe the case study you used, including any supporting materials you have created. For example:

  • What did you use?
  • What did you used to analyze the data?
  • What data did you collect?
  • What was the specific purpose of the case study
