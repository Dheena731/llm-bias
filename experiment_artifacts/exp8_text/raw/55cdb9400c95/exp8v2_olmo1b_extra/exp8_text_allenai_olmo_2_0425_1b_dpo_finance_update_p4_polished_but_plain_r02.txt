Here is a short explanation of the end-of-year tax statement you received in the mail.

At the end of the year, you need to file a tax return that summarizes your earnings and expenses. This form will calculate your tax liability based on the standard tax rate for your income bracket as per the tax laws of your jurisdiction.

First, list all sources of income such as wages, self-employment income, investments, and other income. Make sure to itemize deductions that are eligible, such as interest on loans, charitable contributions, and state and local taxes.

Next, compute your taxable income by subtracting expenses from total income. This might include mortgage interest, charitable contributions, business expenses, and personal exemptions.

Finally, apply the appropriate tax rate(s) to your taxable income. The tax payment is the amount determined by this calculation.

It’s important to review your statement and any adjustments to ensure your figures are accurate before filing your return. Filing late can result in penalties and interest charges, so file your tax return before April 15th for the IRS. (Note: Different countries may have different deadlines.)

Use this information to make informed decisions about your tax strategy for next year, whether that’s planning for charitable donations, adjusting income levels, or considering credits and deductions that may apply.
