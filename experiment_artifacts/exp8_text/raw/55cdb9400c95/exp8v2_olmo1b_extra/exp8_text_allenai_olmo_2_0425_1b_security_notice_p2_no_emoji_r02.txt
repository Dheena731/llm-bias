Phishing is the practice of sending emails that appear to be from a legitimate source with the intention of obtaining personal and financial information from the victim. The email is often tailored to the person addressed and the request is for personal or financial information. While the person may be convinced to provide information, they are not aware that the email contains a virus or that they have provided their personal information to a fraudulent website. This is why getting even the smallest bit of information is so important – many potential victims have stopped providing information or have altered their passwords or usernames because they had no other proof of the legitimacy of the message.

It is important for all workplaces to have a policy in place to deal with phishing attempts and to educate staff about the issue. This could include implementing a checklist of what to do if an email looks like it is phishing. Some examples of what to do include:

  • Determine if the email address is different to the address of the company from which the email is purported to be coming from.
  • Check the email address to see if the business is familiar to you and what services they provide.
  • See if the email is being sent from a number of different computers.
  • Double check the URL of the email to see if it is the same as the address that you believe it should be.
  • Get further information from the sender by phone or through a known social network or trusted website.
  • If in doubt, delete the email and advise the sender that you are suspicious of the message.

It is also important to consider educating staff about the dangers of phishing attacks. Although this is likely to be geared towards staff that handle email on a daily basis, any staff that may be interested should be educated on the issue so they can help others to spot phishing emails. This could include:

  • Educating staff about the dangers of phishing emails. A phishing scam is not always obvious, but educating staff can help them to avoid many of the scams.
  • Creating a list of known phishing websites and what staff should do if they visit them. Educating staff about where to go to report a phishing site to help them to identify them on the fly.
  • Thoroughly explaining what to do if a staff member is the victim of a phishing attack in their position.
  • Creating a checklist of how staff can identify a phishing email. This could be a simple list of things to do, or it could be a more comprehensive list with examples of various phishing types.

Be aware of spoofing emails.

Spoofing is the practice of sending emails that appear to come from a legitimate source, but are actually from a malicious website or an attacker. This email may be sent to the person you think is someone else, or it may appear to be from a legitimate source. The email is designed to trick you into clicking a link or opening an attachment. Spoofing emails are often very effective because they are designed to look legitimate.

It is a good rule of thumb to never execute any links or actions from emails that look like they may be phishing attacks or spoof emails. In particular, links to websites that are not legitimate may harm your computer or put your personal data at risk.

It is also important to exercise caution with emails that appear to be from trusted sources. Do not just assume that an email from your boss is legitimate. Old fashioned paper emails can be important too, especially if you receive a letter from a manager or supervisor that you have not seen recently. A letter that seems to sense something is wrong, as well as a letter that does not have a return address are both good reasons to be suspicious.
