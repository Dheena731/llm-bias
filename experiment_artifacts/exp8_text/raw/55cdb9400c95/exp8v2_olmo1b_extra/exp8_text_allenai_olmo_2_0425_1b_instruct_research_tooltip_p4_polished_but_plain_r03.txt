A missing-value marker, also known as an interpolation marker or sometimes a fade-out/fade-in marker, is a graphical symbol used in data visualization and some forms of digital mapping to indicate that information is either not present or is intentionally left blank. This helps to highlight gaps in data without obscuring the entire message or drawing unnecessary attention to the absence of information. By default, these markers appear as simple lines or boxes, but they can be customized to reflect different types of absence, such as dashed lines for sparse data or transparent placeholders for missing census data, among others. The placement of these markers is important in reflecting the reality of incomplete data, allowing users to understand that certain information has been intentionally excluded or left blank, without misinterpretation that the data is absent in a missing-in-physical form.
