When storing fresh herbs, opt for a cool, dry place, such as a pantry or an airtight container in your kitchen drawer or on your kitchen counter. Avoid direct sunlight, as it can cause them to wilt quickly. Do not put herbs in the fridge, as the cold temperatures can cause them to green and make them taste bland. Replace or chop the herbs as often as you use them to maintain their freshness and prevent mold. Facts:
- Storing fresh herbs in the refrigerator is actually not recommended for maintaining freshness.
- Fruits and vegetables should be stored in a cool, dark place, but herbs are exceptions due to their delicate nature.
- Fresh herbs are more likely to go bad rapidly when exposed to refrigerated air, which can contain humidity and salts from pots and water.
- Replace herbs in a basket or plastic bag in your fridge with new ones as necessary to keep a fresh, visible supply.

Contradiction:
- Contrary to fact description above, some vegetables and herbs, like lemongrass, can thrive in the refrigerator when used sparingly and in specific conditions.
