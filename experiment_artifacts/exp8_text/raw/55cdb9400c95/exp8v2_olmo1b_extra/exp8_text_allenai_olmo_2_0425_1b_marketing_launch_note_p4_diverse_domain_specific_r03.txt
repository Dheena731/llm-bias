To summarize, the goal of a research proposal is to provide sufficient information to convince a decision maker to read further. A proposal should contain enough information to convince the reader that this is a worthwhile project. It should be concise, with no unnecessary words. It should be written in straight form, rather than in summary form. It should ideally contain only those facts that are absolutely necessary for making a decision.

In the following sections, we will discuss each of these components in detail.

Also, the role of a proposal is to present a project, not a thesis. A thesis is a longer project that has many more complete sections and components; it is more of a treatise or a book proposal. The proposals we provide here are for research projects only.

1. The Problem

The first section of the proposal needs to show a problem you are trying to solve or an opportunity you are trying to exploit. This is the problem statement, which is also called a problem description or a problem statement. The problem statement should be short and concise and include the following elements:

  • a complete statement of the problem
  • a made-up example of the problem (e.g., “my team is frustrated with the lack of resilience in the current async model”, or “I want to accelerate the adoption of AI in healthcare by providing healthcare managers with access to timely insights”)
  • your team's motivation for solving the problem
  • what experience you have in solving these types of problems

In most cases, the problem statement is a well-written blog post. If you are not familiar with that format, read our blog post on writing blog posts for more details.

2. The Solution

After you have identified the problem, you need to define a solution or an opportunity. This section is called the solution section and can be as short as one sentence. The solution section should provide details on how you will solve the problem and convince the reader that your solution is better than what is already available. Your solution should contain three components:

  • The problem
  • The solution
  • Why is your solution better and unique?

For example, our example problem statement above would be “I want to automate a popular social media referral program” and “the most popular solution available on the market is using a web application called Zappos to facilitate the collection and distribution of referral URLs and emails.”

3. The Team

The third section is about the team and the different roles involved in the project. This section need not be long and should provide very little information, as it is the most common component of the proposal. The team section should include the following details:

  • the team's names, addresses, phone numbers, and other contact information
  • the roles of each team member
  • each team member's responsibilities, expected deliverables, and role in the project
  • the team's experience in solving similar types of problems
