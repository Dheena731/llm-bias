The question

Here is the question that the authors asked in the abstract:

[...] we investigated the potential for a shift in the endometrial phenotype during the menstrual cycle, based on gene expression data, and whether this could be modulated by the activity of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis, the endotherm model of the female reproductive tract, and immune tolerance.

What is an "endometrial phenotype"? The authors don't say.

We cannot evaluate the authors' hypothesis that the endometrial phenotype can "shift" during the cycle. An endometrial phenotype is the set of patterns of gene expression (or anything else) that characterize the different phases of the cycle. The authors do not explain what they mean by this, and do not describe the experiment, or state a starting hypothesis about the phenomenon they are trying to demonstrate.

The authors make a unsupported claim that the endometrium is an "endotherm". What is an "endotherm"? Endotherms are animals that maintain their core body temperature at a constant level. The authors provide no evidence, or hint as to what "endotherm" might mean, in terms of the endometrium.

The authors state that the "endometrium is an endotherm". Endotherms include animals in the Chordata division, as well as humans. This is not what an endotherm is: an endotherm is an animal who maintains its internal temperature. There are a lot of other ways that the endometrium can be regulated, and their implication that the endometrium is an endotherm is not explained.

The authors then state that the endometrium is not an "endotherm". This is not true. The authors say this to make it seem like the authors are putting forward a hypothesis, but in fact they have not demonstrated anything.

The authors state that the endometrium is an "ovum recipient device". What is an "ovum recipient device"? The authors don't explain what this means. A "recipient device" is something that receives something else. This is not a useful scientific term.

The authors state that the endometrium is "immune tolerant". What is "immune tolerant"? The authors state this as if it were a variable and a well understood concept. This is not what they are claiming. They do not provide an explanation of what the authors mean by this term.

The authors state that the endometrium is not an "immune tolerant device". What is an "immune tolerant device"? This again is not a useful scientific term. The authors do not explain what it means.

The authors state that the endometrium is a complicated system. What does this mean? This is not really an explanation.

The authors provide ten hypotheses in the Introduction.

The authors state that "endometrial epithelium is implicated in a variety of physiological processes, including the maintenance of endometrial receptivity, the initiation of implantation, and the control of ovulatory functions." What do these things have to do with each other? The authors do not explain. The authors do not state what "endometrial receptivity" means, or why it might be important.
