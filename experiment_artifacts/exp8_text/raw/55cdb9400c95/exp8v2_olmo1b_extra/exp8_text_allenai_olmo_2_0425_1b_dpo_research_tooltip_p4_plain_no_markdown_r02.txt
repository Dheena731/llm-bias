A missing-value marker, often denoted by an X, Missing, NULL, NaN, or an empty spot on a graph or data table, is a point where the value is not available or undefined. This concept is crucial in statistics and data analysis where understanding missing information is essential for making accurate conclusions and drawing meaningful insights. When data is missing at a certain point, it indicates that the corresponding value was not recorded or is not accurately represented. It's a signal that some data points need attention or handling appropriately, whether by interpolation (filling in with estimated values), exclusion (removing those points), or by including (retaining those points with appropriate handling), depending on the context and the precision needed for analysis.
