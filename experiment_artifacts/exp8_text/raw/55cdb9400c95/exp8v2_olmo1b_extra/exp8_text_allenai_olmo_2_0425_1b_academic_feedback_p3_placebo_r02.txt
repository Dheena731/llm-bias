Avoid writing to much. Write carefully, avoid verbosity, and stick to the most important.

Be sensitive to the question. A research question should, at most, describe a hypothesis. You might find that the question is too vague or simple, or that you have already answered the question. You might find that the question is too broad, and that a more focused one is needed.

Try to ask a very specific research question, although the most common choice may be the question stated in the assignment.

It is not a good idea to ask the same question than the one stated in the assignment, although it might seem that way. If the question does not make sense or sounds too similar or the same it is likely that the question is too broad and you should ask a more focused one.

If you find yourself in this situation, you might ask the teacher whether it is a good idea to ask more focused questions. You might also ask the teacher to clarify the question or replace it with a more focused one.

Cite some sources in your research question

Cite some sources in the research you perform. This will allow you to better understand what is going on.

For example:

There exist interesting links between people who have a critical attitude towards mass media and the influence of the same on their lives and on the perception of society. (H, M, D) C. (2012). Criticality of mass media and its consequences for society: A study with a critical attitude. Journal of Critical Media Practice, 2(3&4), 158-167.

This statement can be expanded to:

Hidi Hidary (2011) The Role of Critical Mass Media in the Public Order Analysis and Regulation. Journal of Critical Media Practice, 2(3&4), 158-167.

At best, it includes the citation of the article in which a very specific question from a very general one is being answered.

Avoid vague statements

Avoid vague statements or vague questions. They are hard to answer, and those who do try to answer them might have to put a lot of work into it.

For example:

What is the importance of mass media in today's society?

The research question above can be better phrased as:

What is the role of mass media in today's society?

The research question above does not answer the actual question. It does detail what mass media is, but does not detail what its importance or role in contemporary society is nor whether this is relevant or not.

Annotated bibliography

Make an annotated bibliography. An annotated bibliography is an (optional) subsection of an annotated paper that lists the different sources that are used.

The compilation of an annotated bibliography is not optional for most assignments. It is a very useful tool for understanding what others have done in the past, and it is a very good way for making connections between diverse and otherwise unrelated research.

An annotated bibliography is a 'bibliographical essay' that lists all the sources that are relevant for the research question. Each source is described briefly (title, author, year, main theme/topic, structure and content, etc.), and an evaluation about whether the source is useful or not is made. The evaluation can make reference to the source's relevance, quality, reliability, etc.

It is not a good idea to just type up search results in a browser and list it as a source. The same can be done for a thesis. It is recommended that you use specialised tools to generate a bibliography and to evaluate the sources.
