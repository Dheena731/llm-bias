In the feedback note, take into consideration the following:

1) Are the questions of the literature clearly stated? If not, ask for clarification. Are the questions open-ended or closed-ended? Are they asking for definite answers? What type of information should you provide (e.g., do you need to only provide the names of articles, the data, or more detailed comments)?

2) Are the questions formulated in a way that make them specific and measurable? This means that they have a beginning, a middle, and an end. If they are too general, then you will need to specify them better so that your feedback note has a beginning and an end.

3) Are the conclusions clear? Are you satisfied with their ability to answer the question of the literature review? Do they provide basic information? Are your readings sufficiently different from each other? Are they compatible with one another? Does your feedback note reflect that relationship? If your answer to the first question is yes and to the second is no, then revise the literature review and ask the instructor to go over it again. Be sure to include a copy of the literature review with your feedback note.

4) Does the literature review seem to be a coherent whole? Is it well-organized? Is it organized by a clear and logical sequence of topics? What type of organization do you think it could use?

5) Is it necessary to read the literature review again? Are the questions clear? Are the conclusions clear? Does the literature review seem well organized? Does it seem cohesive? Is it coherent? Does it appear to be well-supported? It seems clear that you need to revise your literature review and ask the instructor to go over it again.

6) Are the conclusions developed clearly? The conclusions are the last part of your literature review. They should summarize what you have learned, state your opinion(s), and provide a justification of your opinion(s). Are they clearly stated? Are they supported? Are they supported by evidence?

7) Does the literature review have a purpose? Does it have a problem, a question, a hypothesis, or a gap (uncertainty)? Does it have a reason for being written? Is there a point to it? Be sure to provide the instructor with a copy of the literature review with your feedback note.

8) Was the process of writing the literature review successful? There are many things to measure in a literature review. Here are a few indicators that you have done a good job:

a) Was the literature review well organized?

b) Was the literature review coherent?

c) Was the literature review cohesive?

d) Was the literature review well-organized?

e) Was the literature review well-supported?

f) Was the literature review well-developed?

g) Was the literature review well-written?

h) Was the literature review easy to read?

If you can answer yes to all of these questions, then you have succeeded in writing a well-organized, well-supported, well-written literature review.
