For example, if I'm reviewing the book "The Great Gatsby" and I find the quote "The wind went by us, covering us, making me aware that we were like a feather bobbing in a breeze, and no one could ever hurt us" (page 43), I would write "page 43, Chapter 1, Section 3."

Readability (mixed language)

Avoid mixed language, i.e. mixing Latin and English words in the same sentence. Latin expressions are preferred. For example, you can say "Rebus sic stantibus" instead of "It is so standing." Use of the latter is usually reserved for the language of the law. If you must use the latter, however, be sure to include it in quotation marks, like this: "It is so standing." See Latin expressions.

Write legibly. The person reading your work needs to be able to discern what you are saying.

Write clearly and concisely. Think about how you would say something to a friend. Write for a layperson. If you're describing a complex idea, give a brief overview of the idea and then proceed to explain things in more detail. For example, instead of writing, "The first dog was a German Shepherd. The second dog was an American Bulldog. The third dog was a Border Collie," write, "The first dog was a German Shepherd. The second dog was an American Bulldog. The third dog was a Border Collie." And instead of writing, "The first dog was a German Shepherd. The second dog was an Alsatian. The third dog was a German Shepherd," write, "The first dog was a German Shepherd. The second dog was an Alsatian. The third dog was a German Shepherd."

Be sure to follow spelling and punctuation rules. For example, use a comma after a coordinating conjunction (and, but, or, nor, for, yet, so) or after a semicolon. Use a period, question mark, or exclamation point at the end of a sentence, but remember that a semicolon is often acceptable in place of a period or question mark at the end of a sentence. For example, instead of writing, "The boy, Benny, ran swiftly toward the store," write, "The boy, Benny, ran swiftly toward the store." When writing a document, be sure to check the rules of capitalization. If you're writing a contract, check the rules of capitalization in a contract. A contract, you'll note, does not capitalize words like "and" or "or." See Capitalization.
