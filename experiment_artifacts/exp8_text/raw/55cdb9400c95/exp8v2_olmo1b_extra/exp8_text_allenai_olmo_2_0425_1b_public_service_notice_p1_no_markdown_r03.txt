Herd immunity

It is not entirely clear how the herd immunity in the US will be reached, whether it will be reached by vaccination or by a milder form of infection. The recommendation is not to go to large gatherings and not to get together in groups. Many people, especially children, might not understand and follow these recommendations.

A study by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) showed that if 90% of the population were vaccinated, the chance of the virus spreading will be greatly reduced. If less than 50%, it will still increase but to a lesser extent.

However, if the virus spreads, we do not know how long immunity will last. On the one hand, short-term immunity may be longer, but on the other hand, it will be shorter. If no immunity has developed at that point, the risk of infection will be high and the possibility of the disease spreading is very high.

There is some evidence that the vaccines that have been proven to be effective against COVID-19 do not protect against all variants of the virus. There is also evidence that the immune response in the vaccinated people is not as strong as in the unvaccinated. Both indicate that the problem is not yet solved.

Herd immunity

The aim of the vaccination program is to create a herd immunity of 75% to 80% of the population.

A vaccine removes the virus from the body after inoculation and gives the immune system a chance to prepare to defend itself from the virus. If a sufficient number of people are vaccinated, a certain number will have the necessary immunity and it will be almost impossible for the virus to spread. The virus will die without hosts, so vaccination is a weapon against the virus. Natural immunity erases fear.

It is very difficult to determine herd immunity, because we are still in the process of creating it. And the faster we can do it, the better.
