Impoverishment and Hunger
When a person is unable to continue working or supporting themselves due to age, disability, or other reason, they are considered poor.  This may also be called poverty.  This person may be considered “poor” for a period of time while their situation is being addressed.  For example, while they are seeking work, they may be considered impoverished.  Hence they may receive food stamps or similar benefits until they are able to secure work.  Temporary impoverishment is the way to describe a situation where a person is unable to work or support themselves due to circumstances which are not permanent.

Unemployment and Welfare
Sometimes, in addition to temporary impoverishment, there will be a period of time when a person may be out of work and requiring food assistance.  This may last a number of months, or even a year or more.  During this time, the person may receive benefits from the state to help them eat.  They are not considered impoverished during this time, but are considered to be “unemployed” during this time.  The services available to the unemployed may be similar to food stamps, as well as other services which are available to assist with housing or other expenses.  If the time is expected to be much longer, a person may be given the term “long term unemployed” if they have been out of work for six months.

Permanent Impoverishment
There are some people who are never able to work again.  They are considered totally disabled.  If they have no family members to help support them, they may receive the term “dependent” and receive benefits from the government.  There are many reasons why people become disabled, and not all of them are poor prior to becoming disabled.

Totality of Circumstances
When discussing the poverty of a person, it is important to look at the total picture.  For example, a person who is disabled may have worked as a bus driver and may have been able to support themselves during this time, but may have suffered a serious injury which prevents them from continuing to do so.  It would be unfair to consider this person impoverished since they are working, when they have suffered a disability which permanently keeps them from being able to support themselves.  It would not be considered poverty if an individual has a job, but does not have any means to pay for rent or food.  This is an example of the word “totality” used in government policy.  It means that the government will not consider the “poor” if there are alternatives to them.
