The Gulf Coast Privacy Policy, our updated Terms of Use and our updated Privacy Policy are all the same in spirit, but all three are different in their detail. There is a way to write a good privacy policy, and our Privacy Policy is that way, but there are still some important differences between each type.

From a legal perspective, we are very careful to avoid generic language like, “We will not use your personal information for any other purpose, unless you have previously given us explicit permission to do so” or “We do not use your personal information for any purpose, unless you have previously given us permission to use it.” While these phrases are legalistic and non-intrusive, they do not make the reader feel confident that the company has thought about the unique privacy risks of their product or service.

In contrast, a good privacy policy will be clear and easy to understand, without excessive legalese. In particular, it should describe what personal information the company does and do not collect from users, and where that information goes. A good privacy policy will avoid vague language, like “we will not use your personal information for any purpose” or “we do not use your personal information for any purpose.” A good privacy policy will also avoid language that can be misleading, such as “we will not use your personal information for any purpose unless you have explicitly consented to use it” or “we will not use your personal information for any purpose unless we have first obtained your explicit consent to use it.”

Good privacy policies should also be consistent with the user experience. They should not contradict the company’s mission or goals, and they should not create unnecessary friction for the user.
