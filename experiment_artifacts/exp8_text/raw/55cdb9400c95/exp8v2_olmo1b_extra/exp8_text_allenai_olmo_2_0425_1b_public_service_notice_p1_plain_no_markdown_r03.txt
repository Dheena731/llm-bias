Avoid technical terms except in the most technical context. Do not use acronyms or initialisms except in the most formal context.

Use active voice. Write in the present tense.

Write in a clear, friendly style. Avoid jokes and puns unless they are highly appropriate.

Avoid abstract, obscure, or academic language.

Do not use contractions. If you can avoid it, spell out the names of drugs and medical devices.

Do not use slang or jargon, unless you’re writing for an appropriate audience.

Do not use emoticons, exclamation marks, or other informal punctuation.

Do not use symbols, emoji, or other non-textual elements. In general, you should not use images at all in your writing.

Avoid profanity or offensive language.

When writing or speaking, do not refer to the user of libraries or other services as “you.”

Do not use capital letters unless they are at the start of a sentence.

If you’re using headings, do not use the first letter of the first word. For example, don’t write “This is a Test.”

Use correct spelling, grammar, and punctuation, and avoid run-on sentences.
