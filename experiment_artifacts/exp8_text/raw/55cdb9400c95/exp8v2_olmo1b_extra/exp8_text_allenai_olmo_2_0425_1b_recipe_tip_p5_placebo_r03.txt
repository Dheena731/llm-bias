  1. Give a description of your topic (at least two sentences)
  2. Describe your research method (at least one sentence)
  3. State your findings (at least one sentence)
  4. Give a commentary on your results
  5. Give your conclusions (at least one sentence)

The first sentence should describe the topic, the second sentence describes the method used to collect data, and the third sentence should summarize the results of your analysis. Here are some examples:

  1. In this article I will explore the relevance of information technology (IT) to scientific and technical problem-solving.
  2. In this study, I investigated how IT affects students in their learning process.
  3. Students will learn how IT affects them in learning processes.

The next step is to introduce your work. One way to do that is to begin with a description of a problem or research question. This serves as a basis for your next sentences. In a similar way, you can introduce your findings and conclusions by saying: “The results of this study show that….”, “These results show that….”, etc.

When you have completed your introduction, write a conclusion. It is usually a good idea to end your introduction with a summary of the main points. The disadvantage of this approach is that you may be tempted to repeat yourself in the conclusion. You can avoid that by writing a brief summary of your findings.
