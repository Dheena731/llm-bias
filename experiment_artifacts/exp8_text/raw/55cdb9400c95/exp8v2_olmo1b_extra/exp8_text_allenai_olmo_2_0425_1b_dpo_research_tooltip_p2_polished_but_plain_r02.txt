A confidence interval (CI) in a chart represents the range within which a population parameter is estimated to fall, given a certain level of confidence. For instance, if you're plotting company sales data and calculate a 95% confidence interval, it suggests that if multiple samples were taken and their mean values were averaged, about 95% of those intervals would contain the true sales figure for the entire population. The confidence interval takes into account both the variability of the data and the sample size, thereby offering a range of plausible values that estimate the unknown population mean.
