Don't be vague or ambiguous. For example, don't say "make sure you incorporate this into your workflow" unless you’re prepared to explain exactly what you mean.

The message should be concise and to the point.

Step by Step

  1. Research and collect information in order to understand the new workflow.
    • What are the current, current, and historic processes?

    • What are the problem areas of the current process, and how does the new workflow address those issues?
    • Which aspects of the existing processes do you want to keep, and which ones do you want to discard?
    • How do these processes affect other processes?
    • Who are the people who will be affected by this change?

Step 2: Write a newsletter announcement

Write a newsletter announcement.

According to the U.S. Government, a newsletter announcement is "a written account of a past event, action, or meeting."

Your announcement should be in a medium like a newsletter or a blog post. A classic example of this would be a newspaper or magazine, but you can use any format you choose.

You may be asked to write a newsletter announcement for a specific reason. For example, you could write one for a new product launch or an organizational event that has recently taken place.

You may not know what you should include in your announcement until it is written. To help you get started, you can look at other examples.

Before you begin writing, you should probably define your topic in detail in advance.

Step 3: Put the announcement to the test

Put the announcement to the test.

Your announcement has to be tested for accuracy and completeness.

Test your announcement by providing a critical audience of people the announcement. Ask a few people to read the announcement and give their feedback before you make any edits to the announcement.

 Make sure to look for spelling and grammar mistakes. Also look for typos and formatting issues in your announcement.

Step 4: Deliver the announcement

Deliver the announcement.

Make sure that you deliver your announcement in a meeting setting.

Bring enough copies of your announcement so that everyone can easily read it.

After delivering your announcement, sit back and relax. Your work is done.
