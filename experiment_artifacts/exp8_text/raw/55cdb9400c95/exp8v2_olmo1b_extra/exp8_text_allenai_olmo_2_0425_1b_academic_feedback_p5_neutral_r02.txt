Citing works is a good habit. If you do not do it, you may end up with the works of other people that you are not aware of, but which have been cited by others in your work. Not all the works that are cited in your paper are necessarily from the same author. Sometimes, the citation might be made to a work by a different author, but which follows the same topic. At that time, it is perfectly acceptable to say that you are citing a work by another author, saying whether or not it is a critical work, or a work that should be read or not.

Citations require extensive reading of the work in question. You should familiarize yourself with it and its author. You should be able to accurately transcribe from the original sources. If you do not know how to cite your sources correctly, you can ask your professor or a friend to help.

You should compare the source you are citing with the ones you have read in your essay. Compare it to the paper you have already read. You should note the features that are common to it and to the source you are citing. Make a list of the commonalities you find, and consider what you have learned from them. Do not simply copy the citations you read, but use them as a basis for your analysis. The main thing is to have a sense of what you are writing about, and to build your work around this theme.

Citations are essential in writing a successful essay. If you do not cite what you read, you will lose the opportunity to demonstrate your analysis and your understanding of the works you read.
