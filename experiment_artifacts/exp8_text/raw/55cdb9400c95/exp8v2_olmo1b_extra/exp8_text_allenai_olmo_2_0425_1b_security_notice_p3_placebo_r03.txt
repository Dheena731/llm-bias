How do I do it?

First, decide what you want to write a letter about. What is your primary concern? Next, decide on the key points that you want to address. This may be a letter after an annoying comment or a letter to complain about a particular issue. Make your letters short and simple, and single-spaced.

1. List your key points in a table or a matrix, like this:

Points of concern points

Next, list the details that support your main points. For example, if you are writing a letter about a complaint, you might list the details on each of your key points. Make sure your list is accurate and complete, but do not include information that is not relevant to your letter.

2. Write your letter. Leave a few spaces between each point and detail. This will help your letter flow. Start with a broad introduction about your experience or concern. Label the introduction. For example,

This letter is being written about ...

This letter is about ...

Next, write your main points. For each point, write a sentence or two. For example:

  1. This is a letter about being late for work. I usually leave in plenty of time for work, but this morning I did not leave on time. I arrived at my job 25 minutes late. This was my first time being late for work after several months of no tardiness.
  2. And this is a letter about being late after work. I always leave on time to catch my train to work. However, this morning I left on time and met my train. Once I got on the train, however, it was not traveling anywhere. It was stuck in traffic for 20 minutes and then arrived four minutes late.

3. State the primary concern of your letter. This will summarize your main points. For example:

The primary concern of this letter is:

Write the rest of your letter. Use a simple but interesting style. Remember to use simple, declarative sentences that are plain and straightforward. Use short, simple words.
