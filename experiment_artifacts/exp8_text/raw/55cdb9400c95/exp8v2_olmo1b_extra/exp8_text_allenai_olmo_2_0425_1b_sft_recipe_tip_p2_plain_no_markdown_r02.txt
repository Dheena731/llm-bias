Storing fresh herbs is simple when you follow these tips. Let's start with the most popular and easy-to-grow herbs: basil, mint, and cilantro. Basil is a perennial herb and can be grown from seed any time of the year. Mint is a temperamental plant that prefers full sun and well-draining soil. Cilantro is an annual herb with a unique flavor that pairs perfectly with seafood, so once you've harvested it, it's best to use it before it bolts and goes to seed. 

For basil, you can dry your leaves with patience, and it will store up to 12 months in an airtight container on a window sill. For mint and cilantro, you can dry your leaves slightly but remember to wash them first to remove dirt before drying. Once dried, store them in a sealed container and keep them in the fridge to preserve their freshness.  

Remember, herbs will live as long as you do, so start drying and storing them as soon as you pick them so they can mature to their full potential.
