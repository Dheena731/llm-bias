Title: Delayed Payments Processing Scenarios

Description: When a client does not pay on time, the bank is faced with a delayed payment processing scenario. While the bank can make a decision either to accept or reject the transaction, it is important to understand the implications of either decision for the bank’s relationship with the client.

  2. Pre-Approved Payment

  3. Post-Approved Payment

  4. Acceptance of Delayed Payment

  5. Rejection of Delayed Payment


Pre-Approved Payment Process

  1. A bank has a client named Bob Maloney. Bob initially makes a deposit with an amount of $50,000, but the bank notices that Bob’s income is only $20,000 annually. The bank sends a pre-approved payment request to Bob’s fiscal agent. Bob’s fiscal agent, in turn, informs Bob.

    Bob is aware of the transaction and agrees to it. He makes a deposit with the same amount of $50,000. Afterward, Bob’s initial account balance is $40,000.

  2. Post-Approved Payment Process

  3. After Bob makes the deposit, the bank processes the payment immediately. Bob receives his paycheck and transfers $50,000 to the bank. The bank processes the payment and debits Bob’s account balance by $50,000.

    After six months, Bob makes another deposit of $50,000 to Bob’s account.

  4. Acceptance of Delayed Payment

  5. After six months, Bob receives his paycheck and makes a deposit of $50,000 to his account. The bank processes the payment immediately and debits Bob’s account by $50,000. The bank also makes the transfer to Bob’s bank account. Bob receives only $40,000 from the bank. Bob is aware of the transaction and decides to keep the extra money for later needs.

  6. Rejection of Delayed Payment

  7. After six months, Bob receives his paycheck and makes a deposit with the same amount of $50,000. The bank processes the payment immediately and debits Bob’s account by $50,000. The bank refuses the payment and transfers the money back to Bob’s bank account. Bob is also made aware of the transaction.
