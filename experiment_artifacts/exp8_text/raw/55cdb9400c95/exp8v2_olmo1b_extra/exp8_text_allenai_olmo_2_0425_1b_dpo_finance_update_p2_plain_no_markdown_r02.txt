Card transaction fees have traditionally been charged by banks as a percentage of each transaction amount. However, recent changes have started to shift the way these fees are implemented, aiming to simplify the process and reduce costs for both consumers and banks.

Instead of being a flat percentage on every purchase, many banks are now introducing a flat fee plus an additional percentage on top of that. This means that for certain transactions, consumers might not see a direct percentage charge on their purchases. Instead, the total cost, which includes the flat fee plus a percentage on the whole amount, is clearly communicated upfront and can be budgeted accordingly.

This shift is part of an effort to streamline the card transaction system and make it more transparent for consumers. It’s also intended to encourage more financial inclusion by making it easier for individuals and small businesses to manage costs without being overwhelmed by frequent and obscure fees.

The implementation varies by bank and country, so specifics can vary. Some banks have also started offering more straightforward options, such as prepaid card services, which eliminate the need for regular charged transactions and thus reduce the pressure on traditional card fees. The goal of this change is to balance the need for banking profits with consumer convenience and cost-effectiveness.
