Citations are crucial as they ensure the reader can verify the sources you referred to, check the accuracy of your information, and understand the context of your argument. When citations are missing, it hobbles the credibility and persuasiveness of your essay. Each time you refer to a source outside your own words, you must provide the appropriate details like the author's name, title of the work, publication details (like the title of the journal or book), and the date of publication where possible.

Here are some common reasons for missing citations and associated issues:
- Lack of awareness: Failing to include citations can stem from a lack of educational background in referencing or a lack of understanding of the essay's primary and secondary sources.
- Poor academic standards: Poor referencing can reflect on the author's credibility and can damage the university or institution's reputation.
- Authoritative sources: Citations are especially critical when referencing authoritative sources that are central to your argument or that the reader is unlikely to have access to.
- Lack of familiarity with the citation style: Different citation styles (such as APA, MLA, Chicago) require specific formats, so those unfamiliar with them may leave them out intentionally or incorrectly.
- Carelessness or oversight: Sometimes, missing citations can be an oversight resulting from careless writing or a lapse in attention during the editing process.

Incorporating citations properly ensures a robust, academically sound, and truth-based work that encourages readers to delve deeper into your sources and to support your argument with evidence.
