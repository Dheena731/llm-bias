Two-factor authentication (2FA) is an extra layer of security that requires you to provide another form of verification aside from just your password to gain access to your accounts on websites and services. This method ensures that even if someone gets access to your password, they still need both to complete the authentication process, significantly reducing the risk of unauthorized access.

Enabling 2FA is a wise decision for several reasons. First, it adds an extra barrier between you and potential cyber threats. If your password is somehow compromised, the attacker might still need the second factor to gain entry. This can significantly delay or prevent their unauthorized access, protecting your important accounts.

Second, it enhances the security of your online accounts against lost, stolen, or hacked passwords. Many services offer an optional 2FA feature that can send you a text message or email with a unique code to enter after logging in with your credentials. This additional step can prevent accounts from being compromised if your password is guessed.

Finally, 2FA is a good practice to maintain for the overall security of your digital identity. By opting into 2FA, you're helping reinforce the security of your online presence, making it harder for attackers to breach your accounts, thus safeguarding your personal information and data.
