When evaluating an essay for any academic or scholarly intent, one of the critical findings is the absence of proper citations. Citations are essential to provide attribution to the original sources of ideas, facts, and arguments used within the essay. Whether it is direct quotations, paraphrased information, or data drawn from specific studies or texts, citations serve as a check against plagiarism and ensure that the work is grounded in verifiable reality. An essay lacking proper citations, particularly in formal or scholarly writing, immediately raises concerns about the author's credibility and adherence to academic standards.

Therefore, a short comment on missing citations in an essay is straightforward yet potent: "A well-cited essay is the hallmark of academic integrity and thoughtful argumentation."

Be mindful that the interpretation of "missing citations" can vary based on the citation style required by your assignment guidelines (e.g., APA, MLA, Chicago) or the specific academic context, as different styles have varying requirements for citing sources. If you encounter an essay that seems incomplete with citations, it would be prudent to request clarification or check for the correct citation format before assuming or stating the essay lacks them.
