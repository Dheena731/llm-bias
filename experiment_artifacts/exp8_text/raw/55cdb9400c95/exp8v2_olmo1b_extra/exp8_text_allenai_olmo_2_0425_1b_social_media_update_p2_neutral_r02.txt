While searching for a location for the site, Thorvaldsen struck upon an unusual site in the hills just outside of Copenhagen. Sunlight bathes the castle in a natural golden glow and it seemingly floats on a pedestal, creating a dazzling sight. The castle was founded by the local bishop, and was a place for visitors to come and see the famous 19th century architect.

As this stunning castle is far away, Thorvaldsen wasn't able to show it to his friends. Instead he decided to film the views from a nearby hill. He took the footage home with him and the selfie was taken by photojournalist Joey Elwood. The resulting picture was shared on the Photojojo website.

"Seeing the view from the castle window, Joey Elwood had a moment," the website said. "He finally decided to head out to the castle with his camera and the result is this spectacular image."

As not many people had been to the castle, it went viral, becoming the most popular selfie in history.

"This little castle is an absolute gem and the view is amazing," Elwood said of the selfie. "No wonder people have been flocking to see it."

While the castle is famous, it was built for a reason. It was built for religious purposes, specifically to give tourists a glimpse of the church and the chapel inside.

The view goes both ways. While Thorvaldsen's castle tower looms above the castle, the chapel inside is accessible to visitors. (Photo: Facebook)

"The castle was built as a house for the church and chapel of the town because the town was getting a lot of travellers," Thorvaldsen's grandson, Christoffer Thorvaldsen, told the Copenhagen Times. "The chapel was also a meeting place for the public, where people could come and listen to the pastor. Today, tourists can view the two in one place."

The tower is open to the public, and can be climbed. While the chapel can't be visited, Thorvaldsen's descendants have let it be used to hold events and lectures.

"Olaf Thorvaldsen started the old chapel in 1807 and made it into the new one in 1855," Christoffer told the Copenhagen Times. "The church has hosted many events and the current one was opened in 2016."

The castle is currently closed to the public, but the grounds are still open to visitors.
