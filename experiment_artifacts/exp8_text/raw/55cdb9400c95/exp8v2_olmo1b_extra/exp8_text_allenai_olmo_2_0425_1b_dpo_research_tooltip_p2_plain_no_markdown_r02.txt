A confidence interval in a chart provides a range of plausible values for an unknown population parameter, based on the sample data. It indicates how sure we can be that the real value of that parameter falls within this range. The confidence interval reflects the uncertainty inherent in our estimate from the sample data. For instance, if we calculate a 95% confidence interval for a mean such as the average test score of a group of students, it suggests that if we were to take multiple samples and estimate their means repeatedly, 95% of those estimated means would fall within the actual mean score of the population. This range is useful for assessing the accuracy and reliability of our data-sampling method and the parameters we estimate from it.
