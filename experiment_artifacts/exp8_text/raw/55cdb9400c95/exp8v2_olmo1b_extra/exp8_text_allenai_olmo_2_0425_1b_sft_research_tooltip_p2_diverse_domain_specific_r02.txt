"Here's a concise tooltip explaining a confidence interval in a bar chart. Each bar in the chart represents a unique data point from a larger dataset, with the total data points being represented by the overall shape of the chart. A confidence interval is a range of values that is given as an estimate for the true value of a population parameter, commonly a measure of central tendency such as the mean or median. The width of the confidence interval is determined by the confidence level specified in the chart's tooltip, with higher confidence levels typically resulting in wider intervals. In this bar chart, the width of the intervals is depicted by the thickness of the lines connecting the points, with thinner lines indicating narrower confidence intervals indicative of greater confidence in the estimated value."
