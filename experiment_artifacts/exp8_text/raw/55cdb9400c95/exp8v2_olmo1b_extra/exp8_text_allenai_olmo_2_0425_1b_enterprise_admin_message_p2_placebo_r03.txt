Writing for non-native speakers

Writing for a global audience can be a challenging task. Not only must you take into account differences in the target language, but you must also consider the difference in the cultural "style" and language used by non-native speakers of the target language.

Tips for writing for a non-native audience:

  • Check your text thoroughly for spelling or grammatical inaccuracies.
  • Use formal, neutral language.
  • Use short sentences and simple words.
  • Avoid idiomatic expressions.
  • Avoid verbal cues, such as prepositions and word order.
  • Use a neutral tone.
  • Avoid colloquial speech.
  • Be careful when using metaphors, acronyms, and abbreviations.
  • Be careful when using words that have possible multiple meanings.

For example, "I will write to you as soon as possible" is a common phrase that means "I will be in touch with you as soon as it is possible for me to have a conversation with you," whereas "I will write to you as soon as possible" means "I will send you an email as soon as I have finished my work." The expression "I will write to you as soon as possible" is neutral, while "I will write to you as soon as possible" will sound condescending and demanding to a non-native English speaker.

Tips for writing for a technical audience

Writing for a technical audience can be challenging, but following these tips can help you create clear, accurate, and easy-to-understand content.

  • Write in a clear, concise, and precise manner.
  • Provide clear, easy-to-follow instructions for users.
  • Use simple, clear language that is easy to understand and logical.
  • Avoid technical jargon, acronyms, and abbreviations.
  • Use active, engaging language that resonates with the target audience.
  • Be mindful of cultural differences and take them into account when writing for a technical audience.
  • Be sure to test your content with a variety of target audiences to ensure that it is accessible and effective for all users.
