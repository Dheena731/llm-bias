The body

The body of the essay is where you have room to make your case, and the reader has room to think. Whether you are writing an essay on the best way to make your email more useful, or your essay is on how to help kids learn better, the body is where your main points are put forth. Don't lead with an introduction, which is really just a required first sentence, and then spend all of your time on an introduction. Write your essay as if it were a stand-alone document. Don't put up roadblocks or just dump information into the essay. Make it useful and easy to understand.

We ask the reader to follow a set of rules in the introduction. The body is the place where we can be clear and specific about how to follow those rules. In the introduction, we say that an essay is all about "how to," and we say what the essay should do.

In the body, we can be more specific about what the essay does and how it does it. Writing without context is too vague and confusing. The body is the context. You create context ("how to") by explaining what you are doing (the activities and rules you are using), and how you are doing it (what you say and why you say it). Your essay is in context when it explains how and why you are doing things. The body is where the context is set up.

The body is all the activity. The rules apply to the activity. The reader is presented with the activity and can't do anything. They can't do anything without the rules because the rules are too large for them and they are too small for the rules. The rules are all the big stuff.
