Be as specific as possible. Do not tell; show. For example, instead of writing, "I feel better now," write a description of your current state of health. Instead of saying "I've been thinking about this a lot," write a few sentences about how you arrived at this conclusion. Also, do not repeat yourself. Reiterating the same information in different ways is not a good strategy for writing.

Avoid writing long sentences. Writing long sentences tends to make it harder to understand and can be confusing to read. Instead of writing a sentence with lots of information in it, write two or three shorter sentences that together explain the same thing.

Avoid using formal language. Writing formally can make you appear stiff and uninterested. Stick to simple, clear language. If you are writing about your day, try to use everyday language.

Include examples. When you're writing, make sure that your ideas are concrete and specific. For example, instead of writing, "I think that people should recycle more," write, "I've noticed that I can save more money by recycling instead of throwing away food that I don't need."

Avoid using passive voice. Passive voice makes your writing sound more formal and distant. Instead of writing, "The dog chased the cat," write, "The cat was chased by the dog."

Avoid using technical or scientific words. Technical or scientific words can seem confusing and may make your writing sound boring. Instead of writing, "The stomach is the muscular organ that eats and digests food," write, "The stomach is a muscular organ that eats and digests food."

Use clear and simple language. Use simple words and phrases when writing. Avoid using jargon or other complex language unless it is necessary. Writing that is too complicated can confuse readers and make it harder for them to understand your argument.

Check your work for errors. When you're finished writing, go back and look for any errors. Check spelling and grammar throughout the document, and make sure that your punctuation is correct. Editing your work before you send it can help you avoid making mistakes that could confuse your readers.

Be concise. Writing that is too long can seem boring or overwhelming. Keep your writing short and to the point. Avoid writing unnecessary details or information.

Be intentional. When writing, think about what you want to say. What is the purpose of your writing? What is your message? Who is your audience? What is your main point? Use these questions to guide your writing.

Avoid using cliches. Clichés are overused phrases that seem familiar but don't really add anything new to your writing. Examples include "I could hardly wait," "It was a long time coming," or "It blew my mind." Instead, try to avoid clichés and look for ways to write in a more concrete and specific way.

Be aware of overgeneralizing. Writing that is too general can seem vague and unclear. Try to avoid making broad statements that don't give specific details. Instead, try to be more specific and concrete when writing.

Be aware of assumptions. Writing that assumes things that aren't true can make you seem unreliable or uninformed. Before you start writing, make sure that your assumptions are based on facts and research. Also, remember that your readers may have different opinions than you do, so be sure to consider their perspectives as well.
