The Charge

The only time a bank is notified of a purchase is when the card is used. For transactions that fall below $5, the card holder is responsible for the transaction fee. For larger transactions, the merchant pays the fee.

This means every time you swipe a card, you are paying hidden fees. Online transactions have been the worst, with a close to 2% charge, but even your home transactions have been racking up fees.

According to the U.S. Federal Reserve, Americans swipe their credit cards an average of 11,000 times a year. That’s a decent chunk of change, and it’s why credit cards are so expensive.

There are ways to save money on these fees though. First, follow the trend of checking your account balance before you leave home. If you can only use a portion of your funds, you can avoid paying higher fees. If you have a card with a rewards program, you can redeem it for rewards or cash back. 

If you have a card with no points or rewards, you can pay with cash instead. It won’t save you money on fees, but you won’t be paying for your purchases with a credit card.

You can also check for cheaper options for making purchases. Sometimes, it’s cheaper to charge your purchases at a larger retailer, so head to the neighborhood store and pay in cash.
