The short tip is 200 characters or less. Write the tip so it's easy to scan and remember and sounds good. If you include any links, make sure they're not broken. If this is a tip for a recipe, include the name of the recipe. If you can, target your tips at the most common users—people like you, people like me, people who use your program—instead of the general public.

A good tip is often a single sentence. You might think that you must have it long, but it doesn't have to be. Many popular tips are very short.

What makes a good tip?

  • It works. Put it to the test.
  • It's unique. If everyone else does it, what's the point?
  • It's actionable. Why should I read this? Does it help me do something?
  • It's creative. How can I make this more interesting for people? Humor? a real-life example?

How to write a short tip

  1. Collect ideas. I collect tips from the community, from my inbox, and from blog posts. For this exercise, pick one and do it. If you have a particularly good tip, write about that one. If you found a really good tip, like the one below, share it in the comments.
  2. Write it. Don't look away from your computer. Write it the way you envision people seeing it. It's not like you're writing a novel. This is for the web, not your favorite magazine.
  3. Review it. Does it work? Is it unique? Is it actionable? Is it creative?
  4. Optimize it. Is it too long? Too short? Too wordy? Too passive? Can you cut other words or phrases? Do you need to be more succinct?
  5. Post it. Include the link to your post and share it on social media.

Tips for writing better tips

  • There's more than one way to skin a cat. Don't be afraid to try different approaches.
  • Try something new. Even if you think it won't work, write it anyway. It will at least help you see it from a different angle.
  • Short is good. About 500 characters works great for tips.
  • Don't edit while writing. It's better to write it exactly how it should be. You'll thank yourself later.
  • Mix techniques. You can include links, a short story, and a picture.
  • Pick a target audience. For example, I recently wrote this tip for a new user of Sublime Text (a great text editor). The tip below is aimed at people who edit code for a living, but it's still really simple.
  • Don't be afraid of brevity. There's no rule that says a tip has to be long. In fact, short is usually better. For a tip to work, it has to be easy to read.
  • Use tips as a checklist. Just like the checklist you use to prepare a house for an inspection, use a checklist to make sure your tip is complete and has all the basic elements.
