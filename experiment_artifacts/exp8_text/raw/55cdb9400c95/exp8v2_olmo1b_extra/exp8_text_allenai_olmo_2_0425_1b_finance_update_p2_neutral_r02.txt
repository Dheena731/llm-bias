It is also worth reflecting on the impact of the changes on small business. We have been covering the concerns of trade and business all year, but we have also been keeping a close eye on what will happen to the small businesses in the UK. This is the biggest change to the system since the 1972 Consumer Credit Act. There is no easy answer to this issue, but it is worth reflecting on the following aspects that impact on small businesses:

  • The reduction in the threshold from £20,000 to £25,000, leaving one third of all small business transactions impacted by the rate change.

  • The cost of paying the fee is a concern for smaller businesses. Some businesses are assisted by the Small Business Loans Partnership and this is being reviewed.

  • There are currently 46,000 businesses who use the 125% Cash Discount scheme. This may be changed in the future.

  • The cap on fees for the most common payment types will likely be higher for smaller businesses.

  • It is worth remembering that the initial impact of the change will be felt by larger businesses, but in the long term, the impact is likely to be more significant for small business.

  • The impact on fees may differ between the various regions of the UK.

  • Small and medium-sized businesses will continue to benefit from the Relentless Client Discount scheme for the majority of transactions, though the cap will be revised.

  • The Great Firewall is potentially causing concern for financial professionals. On the surface, it will appear that it could be a barrier for businesses that are supposed to be exempt from the rate change. However, it is likely to fall into the category of routine business like the rest of the income that is not impacted.

  • It is also worth remembering that it is likely that the number of small businesses that fail is low compared to the number of businesses that fail overall and so the impact on small businesses will be less than you might expect.

  • It is worth considering the way in which the questions that people ask have changed in recent years. The question that people ask is not about the rate change. It is about the red tape that is now involved in the process.
