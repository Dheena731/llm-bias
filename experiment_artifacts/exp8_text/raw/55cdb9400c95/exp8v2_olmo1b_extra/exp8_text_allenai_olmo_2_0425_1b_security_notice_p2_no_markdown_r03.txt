What is a phishing email?

Phishing emails are a type of scam that entails a fraudulent attempt to get your personal information, such as bank account or credit card details or passwords. The sender pretends to be someone you know, such as a trusted business or a colleague. 

Phishing emails come in different forms, including text messages, emails, and phone calls. These are often disguised as emails from trusted organizations such as banks. Phishing emails can be used to steal your bank details, credit card information, or passwords. Hackers can also use phishing emails to distribute malware and steal other sensitive information. 

The basic goal of a phishing email is to get you to share your personal information or money with them.

How can I tell if an email is a phishing email?

It is crucial to detect any suspicious emails. The best way to avoid phishing emails is to be vigilant. When you receive an email from someone you don’t know, do not click on any links or attachments before you confirm their identity. 

You can do this by hovering over the link to see where it actually leads. If the link leads to a malicious site, there’s a very high chance that you’ll be redirected to a phishing website. 

It is also a good practice to look for a trusted business name in the email, such as Amazon or PayPal. If the email seems too good to be true, it probably is. 

The best way to prevent phishing scams is to avoid accessing your financial accounts on suspicious websites. 

Also, if you suspect a phishing email, do not hesitate to contact the company directly and ask for verification. 

Finally, be aware that if you have an email account, you are the only one who can access it. Do not give anyone else your login details. 

How to respond to phishing scams in the workplace?

For the most part, the best way to respond to phishing emails is to ignore them. However, if they become a problem for an employee, they may need to follow up the email with the recipient’s supervisor. 

The best response is to send an example of a potential phishing email to your employees. This will encourage them to pay closer attention to their email and stay alert for any suspicious emails. 

Finally, the best way to respond to a phishing scam in the workplace is to curb the problem before it happens. 

You can do this by creating a company-wide email policy that details what to do if you receive a phishing email. Everyone in your company should know what to do when they receive a phishing email. 

What should you do if you get a phishing email?

First, before opening the email, hover over the link to see where it actually leads. If it leads to a malicious site, there is a very high chance that you will be redirected to a phishing site.

If the link leads to a site that you trust, the email might not be a phishing scam. You should check the email address to see if it matches the address of the website you’re visiting. legitimate addresses tend to have the 'www' part before the domain name. 

If the link is on a site that you don’t trust, do not click on the link and continue reading the email. If you open up the link, you might be taken to a malicious site that will steal your information or install malware on your computer.
