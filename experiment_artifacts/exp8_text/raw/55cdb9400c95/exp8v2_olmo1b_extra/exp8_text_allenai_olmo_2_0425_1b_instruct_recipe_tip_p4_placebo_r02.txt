Tip: Make planning lunch meals for the week as easy and efficient as possible by creating a weekly menu spreadsheet. 

Here’s how you can do it:

1. Weekday 1: Start with your go-to lunch option. You know it works well: a sandwich with a side salad or a wrap with a side of carrots and hummus. Put this together in your lunch planner and assign it a color code that matches your outfit for the day.

2. Weekday 2: Move on to another staple. If you enjoy a nutritious vegetarian option, then go for it! Perhaps a granola bowl with berries and a yogurt dip.

3. Weekday 3: Deviate from routine for fun. Choose a new food group, like trying a new salad dressing or a new kind of cheese.

4. Weekday 4: Reflect on upcoming events or meetings. Plan a lunch that’s versatile enough to work with various outfit choices for different days.

5. Weekday 5: Clubbing it in? Choose something fun and festive that you won’t regret eating again later. Avoid anything overly spicy or dairy-heavy.

6. Weekend: On Saturdays, say yes to a brunch that doubles as lunch. If brunch is your jam, consider a tofu scramble with fresh berries and a side of granola.

7. Weekday 6: Plan for leftovers. Take-home lunches can save you time and reduce food waste, and leftovers are a budget-friendly way to keep ingredients from going bad.

8. Tailor to dietary restrictions with labels and meal prep suggestions attached to each lunch option.

Remember to justify your meal choices with reasons that align with your lifestyle, such as a fast-food fix on a night out, or a home-cooked meal on a busy day.

By doing this, you'll ensure that your lunches are ready to go and less of a whirlwind of decisions, keeping your busy week stress-free and your diet enjoyable.
