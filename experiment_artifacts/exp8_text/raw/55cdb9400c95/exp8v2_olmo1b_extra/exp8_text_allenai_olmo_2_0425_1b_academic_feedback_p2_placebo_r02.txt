Write a short feedback note on a student's methods section. Writing feedback on the methods section can be tricky. The best way to do this is to avoid the temptation of getting into a lot of detail about the technical aspects of an experiment, and let the student demonstrate their understanding of the methods section by paying attention to style and clarity. This also allows them to practice reading, which is a skill that we have to build for students to do well in research.

It is actually easier for the student to present the information in the methods section than to present it in a research presentation. Methods sections need to be clear and easy to read, and there should be enough detail in them to be accurate. Also, comparing and contrasting ideas from different sources is easier to do in a methods section than in a research presentation.

When preparing your feedback, be clear about what the student has done right in the methods section. What made this a good example of writing a methods section? If there were any mistakes in the methods section, what are they and why did the student make them? Finally, do any of the methods that you have read need modifications?

When writing a feedback letter to a student, leave a copy of it for the student before you send it. Then later on, you can ask for a second opportunity to comment.
