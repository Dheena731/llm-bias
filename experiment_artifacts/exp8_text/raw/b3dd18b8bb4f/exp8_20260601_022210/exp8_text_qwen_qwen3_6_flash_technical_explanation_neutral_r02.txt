Latency is the delay between a user's action and the web application's response. It matters because human perception of speed is highly sensitive: delays under ~200 milliseconds feel instantaneous, while anything over 300ms becomes noticeable and starts to frustrate users. This directly impacts business outcomes; even 100ms of added latency can reduce conversion rates, increase bounce rates, and hurt user retention.

Technically, high latency compounds in modern web architectures. Single-page apps, microservices, and third-party APIs chain multiple requests, so each millisecond of delay adds up. It also ties up server connections and resources longer, limiting scalability and making it harder to optimize for concurrent traffic. Ultimately, minimizing latency isn't just about raw speed—it's about maintaining responsiveness, preserving user trust, and keeping infrastructure efficient.
