Latency is a critical factor in web applications, and its impact cannot be overstated. In essence, latency refers to the time it takes for a user's request to be processed and for the response to be delivered back to the user. This delay can have a significant effect on the overall user experience and can ultimately lead to a decrease in engagement and revenue.

When a user interacts with a web application, they expect a near-instant response. Any delay can be perceived as slow, unresponsive, or even broken. This is particularly true for real-time applications, such as live updates, chat functionality, or video streaming. In these cases, even a few milliseconds of latency can make the difference between a seamless experience and a frustrating one.

Furthermore, high latency can also have a negative impact on the application's performance and scalability. When requests are delayed, it can lead to a buildup of queued tasks, causing the system to become overwhelmed and reducing its ability to handle incoming traffic. This can result in increased resource utilization, longer response times, and ultimately, a degradation in the overall quality of service.

To mitigate the effects of latency, web developers and architects often employ various techniques, such as caching, content delivery networks, and load balancing. These strategies can help reduce the time it takes for requests to be processed and responses to be delivered, ultimately leading to a better user experience and improved application performance.
