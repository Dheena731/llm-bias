Latency in web applications refers to the delay between the time a user interacts with a website or application (e.g., clicks a button, submits a form) and the time the application responds. This delay can significantly impact the user experience, as high latency can lead to frustration, abandonment, and a negative perception of the application.

Latency matters for several reasons:

1. **User experience**: High latency can make an application feel slow, unresponsive, and unreliable. Users expect fast and seamless interactions, and delays can erode trust and satisfaction.
2. **Conversion rates**: Studies have shown that even small increases in latency can lead to significant decreases in conversion rates, sales, and revenue. For example, a 1-second delay can result in a 7% reduction in conversions.
3. **Competitive advantage**: In today's fast-paced digital landscape, users have numerous alternatives and will quickly switch to a competitor's site or application if they experience delays or frustration.
4. **Search engine optimization (SEO)**: Google and other search engines consider page load times and latency when ranking websites, so high latency can negatively impact search engine rankings and visibility.

In summary, minimizing latency is crucial to delivering a positive user experience, driving engagement, and ultimately, achieving business success in web applications. By optimizing for low latency, developers and organizations can improve user satisfaction, increase conversions, and stay competitive in the digital marketplace.
