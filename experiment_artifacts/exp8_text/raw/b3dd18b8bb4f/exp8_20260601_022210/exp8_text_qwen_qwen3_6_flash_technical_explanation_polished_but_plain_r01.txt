Latency refers to the time delay between a user's action and the application's response, a metric that profoundly influences both user experience and technical performance. Users have grown accustomed to instantaneous interactions, and even minor delays can disrupt their flow, leading to frustration or abandonment. When a web application responds slowly, visitors are more likely to navigate away, which directly impacts bounce rates and conversion metrics. Search engines also factor page speed into their ranking algorithms, meaning that high latency can reduce visibility and organic traffic over time.

From a technical perspective, latency affects system efficiency and scalability. High latency often indicates bottlenecks in database queries, network transmission, or server processing, which can throttle throughput and increase resource consumption. This not only degrades the experience during peak usage but can also inflate infrastructure costs as more resources are required to maintain acceptable performance levels. For features that rely on real-time interaction, such as collaborative editing or live communication, minimizing latency is critical to maintaining functionality and user trust. Ultimately, optimizing latency is essential for building applications that feel responsive, retain users, and operate efficiently at scale.
