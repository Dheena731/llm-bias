Latency refers to the delay between a user's action and the application's response. In web applications, latency can have a significant impact on user experience and overall performance. When a user interacts with a web application, such as clicking a button or submitting a form, they expect a timely response from the application. If the application takes too long to respond, the user may perceive it as unresponsive or even frozen.

Latency can lead to a range of issues, including frustration, abandonment, and lost productivity. Users may become impatient and leave the application if it takes too long to load or respond to their actions. This can result in a significant loss of potential revenue and business opportunities. Furthermore, high latency can also have a negative impact on search engine rankings, as search engines often consider page load times and responsiveness when evaluating website quality.

Reducing latency is therefore a critical consideration for web application developers. By minimizing the delay between user actions and application responses, developers can create a more responsive and user-friendly experience that meets the needs and expectations of their users. This can involve optimizing application code, reducing the number of requests made to the server, and leveraging caching and content delivery networks to improve page load times and responsiveness.
